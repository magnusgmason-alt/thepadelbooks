Why do my ankles hurt after padel? Causes, self-care and prevention

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 15 Nov 2026

Padel puts your ankles through constant sideways steps, quick stops and changes of direction, often on sand-filled artificial grass. So it's no surprise that ankle pain is one of the most common complaints after a match. Most of it comes from a handful of causes: a rolled ankle, an irritated Achilles, overuse after a busy week, or the wrong shoes. Here's how to tell them apart, what to do at home, and how to keep your ankles strong.

General information, not medical advice

This page explains common causes and general self-care. It can't diagnose your pain. If pain is severe, follows a fall or a loud pop, stops you putting weight on the leg, or isn't improving after a couple of weeks, see a doctor or physiotherapist.

The common causes

Where it hurtsLikely causeTypical story
Outside of the ankle, swollen, bruisedAnkle sprainRolled it landing, stepping on a ball, or on a sharp change of direction
Back of the ankle, above the heelAchilles tendon irritationStiff and sore in the morning or at the start of play; often after a jump in matches
Under the heelPlantar heel painSharp first steps in the morning
General ache, both anklesOveruse or unsuitable shoesMore matches than usual, a new surface, or running shoes on court
Ankle that keeps giving wayWeakness or poor balance after an old sprainRepeated small sprains on the same side

Research on padel injuries backs this up. A 2023 systematic review (Dahmen and colleagues) lists the ankle among the regularly injured areas, alongside the knee, elbow, shoulder, back and calf.

Why padel is hard on ankles

  • Lots of short side steps and sudden stops, especially at the net.
  • Sand-filled grass gives a different grip from a hard court, and worn soles slip more.
  • Loose balls on court: stepping on one is a classic way to roll an ankle.
  • Turning to chase lobs, then braking hard near the back glass.
  • The calf and Achilles take a big share of every push-off.

What to do at home

NHS advice for ankle pain and sprains is to rest the ankle at first, use an ice pack wrapped in a towel for up to 20 minutes every 2–3 hours, raise it when you can, and use a bandage or support if it helps. Painkillers such as paracetamol can help; avoid heat and heavy massage for the first couple of days. As the pain settles, start gentle movement rather than keeping it completely still.

When to see someone

Get urgent help if you heard a crack, can't put any weight on the leg, the ankle looks deformed, or your foot is numb, pale or cold. Book a GP or physiotherapist if the pain isn't improving after about two weeks, keeps coming back, or the ankle feels unstable. A sudden pain at the back of the ankle, as if you've been kicked, can be an Achilles tendon rupture and needs to be seen the same day.

Exercises to strengthen your ankles

Once walking is comfortable, these build the strength and balance that protect the ankle on court. Balance training on its own reduced repeat ankle sprains in a 2009 BMJ trial (Hupperets and colleagues).

ExerciseDose
Single-leg balance, progress to eyes closed3 × 30 s each leg
Calf raises, both legs then single leg3 × 12–15
Bent-knee calf raise (targets the soleus)3 × 12
Band ankle eversion (push the foot out against a band)2 × 15
Small side hops over a line, once pain-free3 × 20 s

Prevention on court

  • Wear proper padel shoes with a sole suited to your court surface, and replace them when the grip wears.
  • Keep loose balls in your pocket or at the back, never in the middle of the court.
  • Warm up with side shuffles and calf raises before you play. See our warm-up.
  • Build up playing time gradually, especially after a break.
  • If you've sprained before, consider a brace or tape while you rebuild strength.

Frequently asked questions

Why do my ankles hurt after playing padel?

The most common causes are a rolled ankle (sprain), Achilles tendon irritation, plantar heel pain, overuse after more play than usual, and shoes that don't grip or support lateral movement.

Should I play padel with ankle pain?

Not if it's swollen, you're limping or it gets worse while you play. Let it settle, then rebuild strength and balance before going back to full matches.

How do I strengthen my ankles for padel?

Single-leg balance, calf raises (straight and bent knee), band exercises and, once pain-free, small side hops. Do them two or three times a week.

Do padel shoes prevent ankle injuries?

Shoes with the right sole for your surface give better grip and lateral support than running shoes, which lowers the chance of slipping or rolling over.

When should I see a doctor about ankle pain?

If you heard a crack, can't bear weight, the ankle looks deformed, your foot is numb, or it isn't improving after about two weeks.
