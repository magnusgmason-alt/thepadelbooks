Padel workout at home: 25 minutes with a band and your bodyweight

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 15 Nov 2026

You don't need a gym to get fit for padel. A resistance band, a step and 25 minutes twice a week will build the legs, calves, trunk, shoulders and forearms that padel loads hardest. Here's a complete home workout, with an easier version for beginners and a harder one once it gets comfortable.

General information, not medical advice

This page explains common patterns and general training principles. It can't diagnose you or replace an assessment. If you have a heart condition, a recent injury or any medical concern, check with a doctor or physiotherapist before starting a new programme.

What you need

  • A light-to-medium resistance band (a loop band and a long band are both useful)
  • A stair or sturdy step
  • A chair or sofa
  • A water bottle or light dumbbell for the forearm exercises

The 25-minute workout

Start with three minutes of marching, side steps and arm circles. Then work through the circuit twice, resting about a minute between rounds.

Add a padel finisher

If you have space, finish with three minutes of footwork: split step, two quick side shuffles, back to the middle, repeat for 20 seconds with 40 seconds rest. It trains the same first step you need on court. For more, see padel plyometrics.

How to progress

When you can do the top of the rep range with good form on every exercise, move to the harder version or add a third round. The WHO recommends muscle-strengthening activities on at least two days a week; two home sessions meets that. Strength training also has the best evidence of any exercise type for reducing sports injuries, according to a 2014 meta-analysis (Lauersen and colleagues).

Common mistakes

  • Rushing the reps. Slow, controlled lowering does most of the work.
  • Letting the knees cave in on squats and lunges.
  • Skipping the calf raises. The calf and Achilles take a beating in padel.
  • Doing it the morning of a match. Give your legs a day.

Frequently asked questions

Can I train for padel at home?

Yes. A resistance band, a step and your bodyweight cover legs, calves, trunk, shoulders and forearms, which are the areas padel loads most.

How long should a padel home workout be?

About 25 minutes, twice a week, is enough to build strength for most club players.

What equipment do I need for a padel workout at home?

A resistance band and a step are enough. A light dumbbell or a water bottle helps for forearm exercises.

Is a home workout enough for padel?

For most recreational players, yes. Competitive players benefit from heavier gym work as they progress.
