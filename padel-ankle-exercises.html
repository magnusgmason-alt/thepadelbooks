Padel ankle exercises: balance and strength to stop repeat sprains

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 17 Nov 2026

The biggest risk factor for an ankle sprain is a previous ankle sprain. The ligaments heal, but the ankle's sense of position and its quick reactions often don't come back on their own. That's what balance training fixes, and it takes ten minutes a few times a week.

Not medical advice

This guide explains common patterns in plain language. It can't diagnose you. If the pain is severe, came from a fall or twist, or isn't settling, get it assessed.

The evidence: balance training works

In a 2009 randomised trial published in the BMJ (Hupperets and colleagues), an unsupervised, 8-week home balance programme reduced repeat ankle sprains in athletes who had recently sprained an ankle. The 2018 evidence-based ankle sprain guideline in the BJSM (Vuurberg and colleagues) also prefers exercise-based programmes to passive treatments, because they help restore the ankle's functional stability. In padel, ankle sprains appear regularly in injury studies, with around 8% of injuries in two of the studies in the 2023 systematic review.

The exercises

ExerciseWhy
Single-leg balanceRetrains the ankle's position sense and reactions
Calf raiseStrength for push-off and landing
Band eversionStrengthens the muscles on the outside of the ankle that resist rolling over
Single-leg Romanian deadliftBalance and hip control on one leg
Lateral band walkHip control for side steps, so the ankle isn't doing it alone
Side hops and split-step hopsPadel-speed reactions, added last

The 8-week plan

Weeks3 times a week, about 10 minutes
1–2Single-leg balance 3 × 30 s each, eyes open · Two-leg calf raise 3 × 15 · Band eversion 2 × 15
3–4Balance with eyes closed, or while swinging the other leg 3 × 30 s · Single-leg calf raise 3 × 10–12 · Band eversion 3 × 15
5–6Balance on a cushion or folded towel, or catching a ball · Single-leg Romanian deadlift 3 × 8 · Lateral band walk 2 × 10 steps each way
7–8Small side hops and forward-back hops 3 × 20 s · Split-step hops landing on one leg · Keep one balance drill

Hops come last, once single-leg balance and calf raises are pain-free. After the eight weeks, keep a short balance drill in your warm-up. You can build most of these into a PDF in our free workout builder.

Make it part of padel

  • Do single-leg balance while you wait for your court.
  • Warm up with side shuffles, split steps and calf raises; see our padel warm-up.
  • Clear loose balls off your side of the court; landing on one is a classic sprain.
  • Consider a brace while you rebuild; see ankle brace or tape for padel.

If you've just sprained it

Start gently: NHS advice is rest, ice, support and raising the ankle for the first days, and most sprains feel better after about two weeks. Begin with ankle circles and two-leg balance, and read can I play padel with a sprained ankle? before you go back on court.

NHS advice is to call 999 or go to A&E if you heard a crack at the time of injury, the ankle has changed shape or points at an odd angle, it's numb or tingling, or the skin looks blue or grey or feels cold; don't drive yourself. Get help from NHS 111 (or your local urgent care) if it's very painful or getting worse, the swelling or bruising is large or increasing, or you can't put any weight on it or walk more than a few steps. See a GP or physiotherapist if it isn't getting better with self-care.

Frequently asked questions

What are the best ankle exercises for padel?

Single-leg balance progressions, calf raises, band eversion, single-leg Romanian deadlifts and, once pain-free, side hops and split-step hops.

Do balance exercises prevent ankle sprains?

A 2009 BMJ trial found an 8-week home balance programme reduced repeat sprains in athletes who had recently sprained an ankle.

How often should I do ankle exercises?

About three times a week for eight weeks, then a short balance drill in your warm-up.

When can I start ankle exercises after a sprain?

Gentle movement such as ankle circles can start in the first days. Add balance and strength as pain allows, and hops last.

Why do I keep spraining my ankle in padel?

A previous sprain is the biggest risk factor, often because balance and reactions weren't retrained. Balance work and a brace help.
