Padel blisters on your hands and feet: prevention and treatment

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 15 Nov 2026

Blisters are a small problem that can ruin a weekend of padel. On the hand they usually come from a worn grip or a new racket; on the feet from new shoes, damp socks or lots of quick stops on sand-filled grass. Most are easy to prevent. Here's how.

General information, not medical advice

This page gives general information. It can't diagnose you or replace an assessment. If you have a health condition, a recent injury or symptoms that worry you, check with a doctor or physiotherapist.

Why padel causes blisters

WhereCommon cause
Palm and base of fingersWorn or slippery grip, a grip that's too thin, sweaty hands, a new racket
Thumb and index fingerSqueezing the racket too tightly
Toes and ball of the footNew shoes, shoes too big or small, lots of braking and pivoting
HeelShoe slipping at the heel, damp socks

Prevent hand blisters

  • Replace your overgrip regularly: when it's shiny, smooth or sticky with sweat.
  • Check your grip size. Too thin and you squeeze harder.
  • Hold the racket more loosely between shots.
  • Use a wristband or towel to keep sweat off the grip.
  • Tape hot spots before they become blisters.

Prevent foot blisters

  • Wear padel shoes that fit, and break new ones in with shorter sessions.
  • Use sports socks that wick moisture; avoid cotton.
  • Lace firmly so your heel doesn't slide.
  • Put tape or a blister plaster on hot spots before you play.

How to treat a blister

NHS advice is not to pop blisters, because the skin protects against infection. Cover the blister with a plaster or a soft dressing to protect it; blister plasters are useful in shoes. If it bursts, don't peel off the skin; let the fluid drain, wash with water and cover it. Avoid the activity that caused it until it has healed if you can.

When to see a doctor

The NHS advises seeing a doctor or pharmacist if a blister looks infected (hot, red, painful, or filled with yellow or green pus), keeps coming back, is very painful, or if you have diabetes or problems with circulation.

Frequently asked questions

How do I stop getting blisters from padel?

For the hands, keep your overgrip fresh, use the right grip size and hold the racket more loosely. For the feet, wear well-fitting padel shoes, moisture-wicking socks and tape hot spots.

Should I pop a padel blister?

No. NHS advice is not to pop blisters. Cover it with a plaster or blister plaster and let it heal.

Can I play padel with a blister?

Yes, if it's small and well covered with a blister plaster or tape. Stop if it's very painful or shows signs of infection.

How often should I change my padel overgrip?

When it becomes shiny, smooth or sticky with sweat. Regular players often change it every few sessions.
