Neck pain from padel: why overheads and lobs strain your neck

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 15 Nov 2026

Padel has more overheads than most racket sports. You tip your head back to track lobs, smash, play bandejas and look up at balls coming off the glass. If your upper back is stiff, your neck ends up doing most of that extension, and after a long session it can feel tight, sore or stiff. Here's why it happens and what helps.

General information, not medical advice

This page explains common causes and general self-care. It can't diagnose you. If pain is severe, follows an injury, or comes with symptoms such as numbness or weakness, see a doctor or physiotherapist.

Why padel strains the neck

  • Repeated looking up for lobs, smashes and bandejas.
  • A stiff upper back (thoracic spine) makes the neck bend backwards more to compensate.
  • Long hours at a desk before playing, with the head forward.
  • Sudden turns to follow the ball off the back glass.

What you can do

NHS advice for neck pain includes carrying on with your normal activities as far as possible, taking paracetamol or ibuprofen if suitable, using a heat pack or a hot bath to relax the muscles, sleeping on a low, firm pillow, and checking that your posture is good when sitting. Avoid wearing a neck collar. Most neck pain gets better within a few weeks.

On court

  • Turn your body and step back for lobs, rather than only tipping your head back.
  • Include upper-back rotation and arm circles in your warm-up.
  • If your neck is sore, use more bandejas and fewer full smashes for a while.
  • Look after your shoulder too: see padel shoulder pain.

When to see a doctor

The NHS advises seeing a GP if neck pain doesn't improve after a few weeks, painkillers aren't helping, or you're worried. Get urgent help if neck pain comes with a high temperature and feeling unwell, numbness, tingling or weakness in the arms or legs, problems with balance, or follows an injury such as a fall or a collision.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my neck hurt after padel?

Looking up repeatedly for lobs and overheads loads the neck, especially if your upper back is stiff. Long days at a desk before playing make it worse.

What exercises help neck pain from padel?

Chin tucks, gentle neck mobility, thoracic extension and rotation for the upper back, and band pull-aparts and Y raises for the shoulder blades.

Can I play padel with a stiff neck?

If it's mild, gentle activity usually helps. Use fewer full smashes until it settles and see a GP if it hasn't improved in a few weeks.

When is neck pain serious?

Get urgent help for neck pain with fever and feeling unwell, numbness or weakness in the arms or legs, balance problems, or after a fall or collision.
