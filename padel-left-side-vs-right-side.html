Padel left side vs right side: which side should you play?

6 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 23 Oct 2026

Padel left side vs right side is really a question of roles: for two right-handers, the right-side player traditionally builds the point and the left-side player finishes it. Neither side is better. The best pairs combine one patient player and one finisher, and many coaches now say the roles are less fixed than they used to be.

How sides work in padel

In doubles, each player usually keeps the same side for the whole set and receives serve from the same box every time. That isn't just habit. Under FIP Rule 8.2, the receiving pair decides in the first game of each set who receives first, and that player receives the first serve of every game in the set. Rule 8.3 keeps the receiving order fixed for the set. So you can swap sides at the start of a new set, but not halfway through.

During a rally you'll cross over when you need to, but your home side stays the same. That's why players talk about being a "right side player" or a "left side player".

Padel left side vs right side at a glance

This is the traditional split for two right-handed players:

Right side (drive / forehand side)Left side (revés / backhand side)
RoleBuilds the point, controls the rallyFinishes the point
Typical profileConsistent, patient, good defenderAggressive, athletic, strong overhead
The middleBackhand faces the middleForehand and overhead face the middle
Lobs down the middleUsually leaves them to the partnerUsually covers them
Key shotsLob, backhand off the glass, controlled volleyBandeja, víbora, smash

The Padel School and All For Padel both call this the traditional view, and both add that players can now dictate play from either side. All For Padel's coach puts it simply: there's no longer such a marked difference between the sides.

The right side player

The Padel School calls the right-side player the strategist of the pair, and All For Padel calls them the "brain". Your job is to keep the ball in play, vary speed and angle, read the rally and set up your partner.

Right side tactics

  • Be the steady one. Fewer errors, deep lobs, low balls at the feet.
  • The Padel School notes that from the right most lobs come over your left shoulder, so your choice of overheads is different and the bandeja becomes important.
  • Work on your backhand off the glass. The backhand corner is where right-side players get into trouble.
  • Use the víbora less. The Padel School says it's harder to hit from the right side.

The left side player

For a right-hander on the left, the forehand and the overhead face the middle of the court. That's why the left-side player usually takes the lobs and smashes through the centre and is expected to finish points. HEAD describes the left player as typically more aggressive and better at aerial shots.

Left side player tips

  • Own the middle. Your forehand covers it, so call and take the middle balls you can reach.
  • Pick the right ball to attack. Being the finisher doesn't mean smashing everything. Wait for the ball your partner has set up.
  • Develop the bandeja and víbora. The Padel School argues the bandeja is far more of an advantage from the left than a high backhand volley is from the right.
  • Be ready to cover a little of your partner's side, as All For Padel notes left-side players sometimes do.

Where left-handers play

A left-hander's forehand points to the middle from the right side. The Padel School says left-handers almost always play on the right for that reason, and All For Padel notes that a right-hander and left-hander together have an advantage: both forehands cover the centre, where many shots are played.

The flip side is lobs. With a lefty on the right and a righty on the left, both backhands face the side walls, so agree who takes the high balls down the middle before you start. A left-hander on the right also has a different role from a right-handed drive player: The Padel School says they should look to attack through the middle with smashes and bandejas.

Padel racket for the left side

Some retailers suggest power-oriented rackets for left-side players and control rackets for right-side players. Padel Market, for example, says the backhand-side player usually takes more smashes and finishing shots and often chooses a power or all-round racket, while the forehand-side player builds the point and tends to suit control.

The same source adds the important caveat: it isn't a fixed rule, and if your technique isn't yet solid, control will win you more points even on the left. Choose your racket for your level and style first, then for your side. Our guide on how to choose a padel racket explains shapes and balance.

Which side should I play?

The Padel School's main advice is not to choose your side too early, calling it one of the biggest mistakes new players make. Play both, so you understand your partner's job too. Then use these questions:

  1. Which hand do you play with? Left-handers usually go right.
  2. What is your strongest shot? A reliable overhead points left; a reliable backhand and lob point right.
  3. What is your temperament? Patient builders often suit the right; natural finishers the left.
  4. Where do you feel comfortable? All For Padel's coach says personal feeling matters more than the traditional profiles. You'll usually find a side where you miss less and read the game better.
  5. Who is your partner? Professionals switch sides depending on their partner. Fit the pair, not just yourself.
Short answer

New to padel? Play both sides for a few months. After that, the more consistent player usually goes right and the stronger overhead goes left, unless one of you is left-handed.

Making the partnership work

Whichever side you play, The Padel School stresses that learning to move together as a pair matters more than the side choice itself. Agree who takes the middle, call every shared ball, and move up and back as one. Our guides to positioning in doubles and padel doubles strategy cover how.

Frequently asked questions

Is the left side harder in padel?

It's different, not harder. The left side usually carries more overheads and finishing shots; the right side carries more defence, backhands and point-building. Both are demanding.

Should the better player play on the left?

Traditionally, the player with the stronger overhead and attacking game plays left. But if the better player is a left-hander or a superb defender, the right side may suit them more.

Can you switch sides during a padel match?

Not in the middle of a set. The receiving order is fixed for the set (FIP Rules 8.2 and 8.3), so pairs usually swap, if at all, at the start of a new set.

Which side should a left-handed padel player play?

Usually the right side, so the forehand covers the middle of the court.

Do I need a different racket for the left side?

Not necessarily. Some players on the left prefer more power, but level and technique matter more than side when choosing a racket.
