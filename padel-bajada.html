The padel bajada: attacking off the back glass

6 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 23 Oct 2026

The padel bajada, or bajada de pared, is an attacking shot you play from the back of the court after a lob has bounced and come high off your back glass: instead of defending, you hit it firmly from around shoulder height or above. It turns a defensive moment into an attacking one. Here is what the bajada is, when it's on, how to hit it and how it differs from the bandeja.

Bajada meaning: what the word tells you

Bajada is Spanish for a descent or a drop, and pared means wall. So bajada de pared is, roughly, the ball coming down off the wall. Spanish padel glossaries define it as a high attacking shot from the back of the court, played against the glass. In English you'll also see it called the off-the-wall smash, which is the term NOX and the research literature use.

The idea is simple. An opponent lobs you. The ball bounces in your court, hits the back glass and comes out high. Most club players let it drop and play a defensive lob. A player who can hit the bajada attacks it instead, while it's still high, and puts the opponents under pressure.

Bajada vs bandeja: what's the difference?

Both are answers to a lob, and both are usually hit side-on with slice. The difference is when and where you hit them.

BandejaBajada
WhenBefore the bounce, in the airAfter the bounce and after the back glass
Where you areNear the net or moving back from itAt the back of the court, near the glass
PurposeKeep the net positionTurn defence into attack and win back time
Typical contactAround head height, in frontAs high as the rebound allows, shoulder height or above, in front

Put simply: the bandeja stops you losing the net, the bajada starts the fight to get it back. If you're at the net and can reach the lob, play a bandeja. If the lob has beaten you and is coming high off the glass, the bajada is the attacking option.

When the bajada shot is on

  • The ball comes out high. NOX suggests the ball should be at least around waist height after leaving the glass, and the higher the better. Shoulder height or above gives you the best angle down into the court.
  • You've got behind the ball. You need time to get between the ball and the wall. If you're still chasing it, play safe.
  • You're balanced. A bajada hit while falling backwards usually becomes an error.

If the ball comes out below waist height, it's a normal back-glass ball. See how to play off the back glass for those, and consider a lob instead.

Padel bajada technique, step by step

  1. Read the lob early. As soon as it goes over you, move back fast and judge where it will come out of the glass.
  2. Get behind the ball. Your body should end up between the ball and the wall, a little further back than you'd stand for a volley. The Padel School's main warning: most players wait for the ball to come to them off the glass and end up hitting it behind their body, which makes it almost impossible to attack.
  3. Turn fully side-on. Shoulders and feet face the side wall, with your weight on the back leg.
  4. Bring the racket up early. Arms up over the shoulders, much like a high volley or a bandeja.
  5. Contact high and in front. Let the ball leave the glass, then hit it as high as you comfortably can, with the arm close to full extension.
  6. Cut through the ball. Swing forward and slightly down with slice. Control matters more than raw pace; on the backhand side especially, NOX notes you can't generate much power, so rely on spin and direction. Adjust your pace to how comfortable your position is.
  7. Step forward. Move your weight onto the front leg through the shot and keep moving towards the net with your partner if the reply is weak.

Forehand and backhand bajada

Most bajadas are forehands. In the study of professional players below, around 90% were hit from the centre and right of the court and on the player's dominant side. The backhand bajada exists, and NOX has a guide to it: same set-up, contact around shoulder height with the arm nearly straight, and a cut under the ball. NOX calls it a medium-to-advanced shot and suggests mastering the lower backhand off the glass first.

Where to aim the bajada

Your partner can help by calling where the opponents are. Then choose from three options, safest first:

  1. Down the middle, between the opponents. Safest, and it can cause hesitation over who takes it. The Padel School also favours an aggressive bajada down the middle.
  2. At an opponent's body, if they're close to the net. A ball into the body is hard to volley well.
  3. Into a gap, for example towards the opponent who is further back. Most skilful, least margin.

If both opponents are at the back, there's no need to take risks. If they're tight on the net, the body or a lob may be the better choice.

What the research says about the bajada

A 2024 study in Perceptual and Motor Skills (Martín-Miguel and colleagues) analysed 441 off-the-wall smashes from the 2022 World Padel Tour Final Master. Its findings give a realistic picture of what the shot does:

  • Women hit twice as many bajadas as men.
  • About 90% were played from the centre and right side of the court, on the dominant side.
  • In about 73% of cases the rally simply carried on with the opponents volleying.
  • About 21.5% won the point.

The lesson for club players: even at the top level, the bajada is mostly a pressure shot rather than a winner. Aim to make the opponents uncomfortable, not to end the point every time.

Common bajada mistakes

  1. Contact behind the body. Usually from waiting for the ball. Get back early and behind it.
  2. Trying it on low balls. If the rebound isn't high, play a normal glass shot.
  3. Too much power. A hard, flat bajada tends to fly long or hit the opponents' glass on the full, which loses the point (FIP Rule 13g).
  4. Standing still afterwards. A good bajada creates time. Use it to move forward.
  5. Crowding the glass. Leave space so the ball can come out to you.

How to practise the bajada

Have a partner stand at the net and feed lobs that bounce deep and come high off your back glass. Start with ten forehands aimed down the middle, with slice and moderate pace. Count how many land deep without hitting the opponents' glass first. Then add a target, such as the feet of a net player, and finally play the point out. Frequently asked questions

What is a bajada in padel?

An attacking shot played from the back of the court after the ball has bounced and come high off the back glass. You hit it from around shoulder height or above, usually with slice, to put the opponents under pressure.

What does bajada de pared mean?

Literally "descent from the wall": bajada means descent or drop, and pared means wall. In English it's often called the off-the-wall smash.

Is the bajada the same as the bandeja?

No. The bandeja is hit in the air before the bounce, to keep the net. The bajada is hit after the ball has bounced and come off the back glass, to turn defence into attack.

Is the bajada a beginner shot?

Not really. It needs good reading of the glass, and NOX rates the backhand version a medium-to-advanced shot. Learn to play comfortably off the back glass first, then add the bajada on high rebounds.

Where should I aim my bajada?

Down the middle is the safest choice. At an opponent's body works when they're close to the net, and into a gap is the most ambitious option.
