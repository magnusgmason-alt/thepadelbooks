Padel spin cheat sheet: slice, topspin, flat and side spin

6 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 8 Nov 2026

Padel spin comes in four kinds, slice (backspin), topspin, flat and side spin, and each one changes how the ball behaves after the bounce and off the glass. Slice stays low and slow, flat comes off the back glass higher, topspin dips and climbs the glass faster, and side spin skids into the side glass. This cheat sheet puts it all in short tables, then explains where the spin really comes from.

How to generate spin with a padel racket

Padel rackets have no strings, and many are sold with rough or sanded faces for extra "bite". The evidence on that is thin. In a physics test by Tennis Warehouse University, three rackets with very different surface friction produced the same spin across most of the impact angles tested, and the author concluded that perceived spin differences come mainly from the incoming ball's speed, spin and angle and from how the player swings, not from surface roughness. It was a small test of three rackets, so treat it as a strong hint rather than the final word.

What you can control:

  • Swing path against the face angle. Spin comes from the racket moving across the back of the ball rather than straight through it: down and under for slice, up for topspin, around the outside for side spin.
  • The wrist. Babolat's coaching points are a relaxed wrist for the bandeja's slice and a quick pronation (turning the forearm) for the víbora.
  • A light brush, not a big chop. For topspin, brush lightly up and over the back of the ball: enough for it to dip at the opponents' feet, not so much that it kicks up off their back glass.
  • Contact point. Slice needs the ball above the waist; topspin needs it in front and a little lower; side spin needs it in front and slightly away from the body.

Slice or flat? Where coaches disagree

A lot of padel coaching treats slice as the default: it's controlled and keeps the ball low off the glass. The Padel School pushes back. In its article on hitting flat it argues that a flat ball goes down earlier than a sliced one, reaches harder places, is easier to disguise and is simpler to hit, while too much slice gives the opponents time. A separate Padel School article adds that from the back, slice works best on balls above chest height; on a low ball it travels slower than a flat shot and tends to float up.

Slice when

The ball is above chest height, you want a low ball off the opponents' back glass, you need control more than pace (bandeja, deep volley), or you're buying time.

Go flat when

The ball is low, you want it to drop at the opponents' feet sooner, you need pace to finish a volley, or the opponents have learned to handle your slice.

The Padel School's view on volleys is the sensible middle: decide from the ball you receive and where the opponents stand, and remember that speed matters more than spin.

Three spin drills

  • Spin ladder (2 players, 10 min). Rally cross-court from the back. Five balls slice, five flat, five topspin. Your partner names the spin they received; score a point each time they get it right.
  • Low off the glass (2 players, 10 min). Volley deep from the net into your partner's back glass. They call "low" if the ball comes out below the knee. Target 7 of 10 lows with slice, then compare with flat volleys.
  • Side-glass skid (2 players, 12 min). From a fed lob, play víboras into the far side glass. Count how many stay below waist height after the glass.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a rough padel racket for spin?

Probably less than marketing suggests. A Tennis Warehouse University test found that surface roughness made little difference to spin on most padel shots; swing path and contact matter far more.

What spin is used on a bandeja?

Slice. The racket cuts under and behind the ball, so it travels with control and stays low off the back glass.

What spin does a víbora have?

Mostly side spin with some slice, from brushing around the outside of the ball. Some brands describe it as lateral topspin. Either way, the aim is a ball that skids low off the side glass.

Is topspin used in padel?

Yes, but less than in tennis. It's useful for dipping passes and attacking lobs, chiquitas and topspin smashes. Heavy topspin from the back can bounce high off the opponents' glass and give them an easy ball.

How do I return a sliced ball that stays low off the glass?

Get low early, stand a little closer to the glass than usual, open the racket face and lift the ball, often as a lob. Don't try to hit it hard from below knee height.
