Padel smash drills

10 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 31 Oct 2026

The best padel smash drills teach you which ball to smash as much as how to hit it, because a smash that comes back off the glass is often worse than a bandeja. These eight drills cover x3 and x4 practice, the topspin rulo, choosing between smash and bandeja, and the part most players skip: getting back in position after the smash.

All eight are adapted from The Padel Drill Book: Intermediate & Advanced. The code after each name is its number in the book.

Before you start: the smash in padel

In padel a hard, flat smash that stays in the court usually bounces off the back glass and comes out again at a comfortable height. So a padel smash either has to leave the court, land somewhere awkward, or force a weak reply that you then finish. Our padel smash guide explains the types:

  • Por tres (x3): the ball bounces, comes off the back glass and goes out over the side, named after the 3 m side enclosure.
  • Por cuatro (x4): a flat smash from close to the net that bounces and goes out over the back, which is 4 m high.
  • Rulo: a topspin overhead, lower risk, that kicks awkwardly off the glass.

Coaching sources agree on the basics. Bounce advises smashing only when the ball is above your head and you are close enough to the net to control it, and NOX says the x3 should be struck as high as possible from as near the net as you can get, never from a medium-height ball.

What the rules say

A ball that bounces correctly and then goes over the back wall cannot be played, so a clean x4 wins the point. A ball that leaves over the side wall or through a door may be chased outside only where out-of-court play is authorised; otherwise the point is over (FIP Rule 13). See out-of-court play.

x3 and x4 smash practice

1. Por tres or bandeja (O6 in the book)

Players: 3–5 · Time: 15 min · Level: 5–6

Set-up: You at the net on the right, your partner on the left. A cone marks your service line. A target near the opponents' side glass, towards the back, shows where the x3 should bounce.

How it works: The feeder alternates short, high lobs that drop inside your service line with deeper, flatter ones. Short and balanced: go for the x3, bouncing near the target so the rebound off the back glass carries it out over the side wall. Pushed behind the line, or rushed: deep bandeja. Check your feet against the line before you swing. The feeder marks the choice and the result.

Pass mark: Ten right choices in twelve, and half your x3 attempts leaving over the side.

Make it harder: Add two defenders. On courts where out-of-court play is authorised, they can run out after the x3.

2. Por cuatro and the follow-up (O7)

Players: 3 · Time: 12 min · Level: 5–6

Set-up: You at the net on the right, about two metres back. The feeder stands beside the far net post and sends short, high lobs. C stands by the far back glass holding spare balls. Mark a zone around the opponents' service line for the bounce.

How it works: Smash flat and hard into the zone from well inside your service line. As you land, C acts: if the smash cleared the back wall, C throws in a new lob; if it stayed in the court, C plays it back for real. Either way you recover and play the next ball under control, a volley or an overhead, with no second smash unless the ball is short again.

Pass mark: Six of eight follow-up balls controlled, whatever happened to the smash.

Make it harder: C lobs straight back over you off the glass, so you defend with a bandeja from deeper.

3. Rulo off the back glass (O4)

Players: 2–3 · Time: 12 min · Level: 5–6

Set-up: You at the net, inside the service line. The feeder supplies medium-height lobs from the other end. Mark a landing zone two to three metres short of the opponents' back glass. C can defend.

How it works: Take the lob in front of you, a little higher than an ideal bandeja. Swing quickly and brush up and over it, racket face slightly closed, so it lands in the zone with heavy topspin and jumps forward off the back glass. Recover to the net after each ball. In the last set the feeder calls "rulo" or "bandeja" as you prepare.

Pass mark: Five of eight land in the zone and come off the glass above head height.

Make it harder: C defends the rebound and the rally continues for another two shots.

Overhead drills for shot choice

4. Late-call overhead (O9)

Players: 2 · Time: 15 min · Level: 4–6

Set-up: You at the net, the feeder at the far baseline. Mark three zones: deep middle, cross-court corner and open court.

How it works: The feeder lobs a ball where any overhead is possible. Turn, racket up, free arm pointing at the ball. Once you are set, the feeder calls the shot: bandeja to the deep middle, víbora to the corner, smash to the open court. Opponents read smashes from the preparation, so keeping one neutral set-up until late is what makes your smash a threat.

Pass mark: Twenty of twenty-seven correct shots in the right zone.

Make it harder: The call comes just before your forward swing.

5. Pounce on the second ball (T5)

Players: 4–5 · Time: 15 min · Level: 4–6

Set-up: Your pair holds the net, with C and D at the back and a feeder behind them who opens each rally with a medium-paced ball to you.

How it works: Hit a firm first volley, either deep into D's corner or down at C's feet. Judge the reply as it comes off their racket. Short lob: smash it. Floating ball: volley it firmly. Good ball: stay patient. The smash ball usually comes from a defender who is stretched or hitting from behind the body, so move as they make contact, not after the bounce.

Pass mark: Eight of ten weak replies attacked early, and at least six of those points won.

Make it harder: The defenders score double for any point won after you attacked a reply that was actually good.

6. Set up the revés finisher (P1)

Players: 4 · Time: 15 min · Level: 4–6

Set-up: A (right) and B (left) start at the net. C and D defend from the back, and every point begins with C lobbing towards A.

How it works: A builds with deep volleys into the corners and the middle, never short and never forced. B is the finisher. Given a lob on the left half that is short enough to attack, B plays a víbora into the corner or an x3; otherwise a deep bandeja. A point won by B's overhead counts two. Play to eleven, then swap roles with C and D.

Pass mark: Get to eleven first, and win four or more points with B's overhead.

Make it harder: The defenders may now lob to any part of the court, so B has the middle to cover as well.

Padel smash drills for recovery

7. Smash and reset (O8)

Players: 3 · Time: 12 min · Level: 4–6

Set-up: You and your partner at the net, each with a cone marking your net position, about two and a half metres from the net. The feeder stands beside the far side wall, behind the opponents' service line.

How it works: The feeder lobs to come down around your service line. Move back and smash flat and firm through the middle of their court. As you land, your partner edges towards the middle. Then run forward to your cone and split level with your partner. About two seconds after the smash, the feeder drives hard at one of you, and that volley has to go deep. Pick a smash that lets you recover in time; a jump smash at full stretch that leaves you off balance is too slow to reset.

Pass mark: Seven of eight sequences with both of you split at the cones and the volley deep.

Make it harder: After the smash, the feeder picks randomly between a drive, a chiquita and another lob.

8. Earn the smash (M2)

Players: 4 · Time: 15 min · Level: 4–6

Set-up: You and your partner start at the net, the opponents behind their service line, and C lobs to start each point.

How it works: Play the point out and count every bandeja your pair hits. After the third, any overhead is allowed, including the x3 and x4. A smash before then loses the point on the spot. Even once the smash is earned, ask whether this lob really is a smash ball. Play to eleven and swap ends.

Pass mark: Win a game to eleven without a single early smash.

Make it harder: Four bandejas earn the smash.

A 60-minute smash session

  1. 0–10 min: warm-up. Volleys, then easy lobs and shadow overheads to warm the shoulder. Our warm-up guide has a routine.
  2. 10–22 min: Late-call overhead. Two sets of nine.
  3. 22–34 min: Smash and reset. Sets of eight, swapping smasher and cover.
  4. 34–46 min: Por tres or bandeja. Twelve lobs each.
  5. 46–60 min: Earn the smash. One or two games to eleven.

Rotate the hitter every eight to ten balls rather than smashing a whole basket in one go, so the contact point stays in front instead of drifting as you tire.

Common smash mistakes

  • Smashing from too deep. From behind the service line the ball rarely leaves the court and the glass hands it back. Bounce suggests a practice rule: no smashes from behind the service line.
  • Standing still to watch. Land, split and expect the ball to come back.
  • Smashing medium-height balls. If you can't reach up and hit it high, play a bandeja or víbora.
  • Going for x3 and x4 too soon. Learn to place a controlled smash first.
  • Losing your partner. While you smash, your partner covers the middle; afterwards you level up together.

Frequently asked questions

How do I practise the x3 smash in padel?

Start from a fed short, high lob close to the net, aim the bounce around the opponents' service line, close to the side glass, and hit with topspin from as high as you can reach. Then add the decision drill, Por tres or bandeja, so you only try it on the right ball.

What is the difference between an x3 and an x4?

An x3 goes out over the side after coming off the back glass. An x4 is a flat, powerful smash that bounces high and goes out over the back of the court, which is 4 m high.

Can the opponents return an x4?

No. Under the FIP rules a ball that bounces correctly and then goes over the back wall cannot be played, so the point is lost for the side that had to return it.

Can you chase an x3 outside the court?

Only where out-of-court play is authorised, which depends on the court's exits and safety area and on the competition. If it isn't, a ball that leaves over the side wins the point for the smasher.

What are good overhead drills in padel for intermediate players?
