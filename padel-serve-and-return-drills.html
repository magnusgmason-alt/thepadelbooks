Padel serve and return drills

10 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 31 Oct 2026

Padel serve and return drills turn the first two shots of every point into habits: a deep, reliable serve that takes you to the net, and a return that stops the serving pair from attacking. Below are ten of them, from solo serve targets to four-player games, each with a court diagram and a pass mark, plus the serve rules every drill has to respect.

Every drill on this page is adapted from The Padel Drill Book, which has a full chapter on the serve and return among its 100 drills.

The serve rules your drills must follow

Practise the serve exactly as it has to be hit in a match. Under the FIP rules (Rule 6):

  • Stand with at least one foot behind the service line, inside your service box, without touching the service line or the centre line.
  • Bounce the ball behind the service line in your own box before you hit it.
  • Strike it at or below waist height, with at least one foot on the ground.
  • Serve diagonally into the receiver's box. Lines are good. A serve that bounces in the box and then hits the glass is fine; one that bounces and then hits the metal mesh is a fault (Rule 7). Two faults in a row lose the point.

The receiver must let the serve bounce before returning it (Rule 8.1). Our guides to the waist-height serve rule and service faults go into the detail.

One practical point for wide serves: on standard courts the side glass only covers the last four metres before each back wall, and the rest of the side is mesh (LTA court guidance). The service line is 6.95 metres from the net, so a serve aimed at the side glass has to bounce deep in the box to reach glass rather than mesh.

Padel serve practice drills

Coaches agree on the job of the serve: it doesn't need to win the point, it needs to start it on your terms. The LTA recommends serving as deep into the box as you can and using the side glass; The Padel School favours a reliable, mostly sliced serve at medium-to-fast pace, followed straight away by a move to the net.

1. Serve to the T

Players: 1 · Time: 12 min · Level: 1–4

Set-up: Put a towel about a metre from the T (the point where the centre line and service line cross) in the diagonal box.

How it works: Use the same routine on every serve. Bounce the ball behind the line, meet it beside your front hip at or below waist height and push through it with a little slice. The serve should land near the towel and carry on into the back glass, arriving low. Three sets of 12 from each side.

Pass mark: 6 of 12 into the target from each side.

Progression: Ask a partner to stand in and return, so you serve with someone watching the ball.

2. Serve to the side glass

Players: 1 · Time: 12 min · Level: 2–5

Set-up: Stand closer to your side wall than usual to open the angle. Mark a target about a metre in from the side wall, deep in the diagonal box.

How it works: Add a little side slice so the ball drifts towards the wall after the bounce, hits the side glass and comes back low and quick. The ball must bounce in the box first: hitting the glass directly is a fault, and so is a bounce followed by the mesh. Two sets of 12 from each side.

Pass mark: 6 of 12 that bounce in the target and then hit the side glass.

Progression: Alternate T serves and side-glass serves without changing your stance, so the receiver can't read you.

3. Body serve

Players: 2 · Time: 12 min · Level: 2–5

Set-up: The receiver (C in the diagram) stands in a normal return position.

How it works: Aim the serve so that, after the bounce, it arrives at the receiver's hip. Few receivers can step away from a ball at the hip in time. The serve still has to be deep: a body serve that lands short sits up and is easy to punish. The receiver tries to return deep. Twelve serves each, then swap.

Pass mark: 5 of 12 returns that drop short or find the net.

Progression: The receiver returns live and you play the point out.

4. Serve and follow

Players: 2 · Time: 12 min · Level: 2–4

Set-up: A cone three metres from the net on the server's side.

How it works: Serve to the T, move straight up to the cone, and split step as the receiver hits. Then volley the return deep through the centre. Don't run past the cone: if you are still moving when the return comes, it goes past you. Ten serves, then swap.

Pass mark: 6 of 10 first volleys landing deep.

Progression: The receiver lobs every third return, so you also have to be ready to turn.

Return of serve drills

The Padel School's advice on where to return depends on the serve: block a hard serve off the glass back towards the server, lob a good serve down the T to buy time, and attack an easy serve, low at the server's feet or with a lob that lets you take the net. The next three drills train those options.

5. Deep lob return

Players: 2 · Time: 12 min · Level: 2–5

Set-up: One player serves at medium pace, the other returns. T1 is the deep zone behind where the server's partner would normally stand at the net.

How it works: Lift the return high and deep into T1, bending low and standing up through the shot. Height matters more than pace. A good lob gives you time, so move forward as soon as you have hit it. Twelve returns each.

Pass mark: 7 of 12 lob returns in T1.

Progression: The server comes forward and smashes any lob that falls short. Our lob guide covers the technique.

6. Return to the net player's feet

Players: 3 · Time: 12 min · Level: 3–5

Set-up: One player serves, their partner stands at the net, the third player returns.

How it works: Return with a flat, firm ball that dips at the net player's feet. The net player must volley every return; anything above the waist, they attack. The target is low, not wide: a high return to the net player is a free point for the serving pair. Rotate roles after 12 returns.

Pass mark: 7 of 12 returns that force the net player to volley up.

Progression: Every return is volleyed live by the net player and the point is played out.

7. Chiquita return

Players: 3 · Time: 12 min · Level: 3–5

Set-up: The server serves and moves forward towards the net, as in a match. Their partner is at the net.

How it works: The receiver takes the pace off with an open face and plays a soft, slow ball that drops in front of the incoming server. The server, still moving, has to play it from below the net and can only volley up. Twelve returns each.

Pass mark: 7 of 12 chiquitas the server has to lift.

Progression: The server must stop and split step before the ball lands, which makes the chiquita harder to pull off.

Serve and return games

8. The ball after the serve

Players: 4 · Time: 15 min · Level: 3–5

Set-up: Normal doubles positions with A serving.

How it works: After the serve and the return, the serving pair plays the third ball as a building shot: a deep volley through the middle, or a lob, when the return was itself a lob. Then play the point out. A point won by the serving pair counts double if their third ball was a deep volley. Play to 11.

Pass mark: 7 deep third balls from 10 serves.

Progression: The serving pair has to win the point within six shots, so building can't turn into waiting.

9. Return under pressure

Players: 3 · Time: 12 min · Level: 3–6

Set-up: Treat every serve as a golden point. As in a real golden point, the receiving side chooses who returns.

How it works: Decide on your return before the serve and commit to it: a deep lob, or a low ball through the middle. Never down the line. A missed return costs five split-step jumps before the next point. Ten returns each.

Pass mark: 9 of 10 returns in play.

Progression: The serving pair chooses which receiver has to return.

10. Serve-return rotation game

Players: 4 · Time: 15 min · Level: 2–4

Set-up: A normal game of doubles with one change to the serve.

How it works: Every player gets one point on serve before it passes to the next player clockwise. There is only one serve per point, so a fault loses it. Play every point out, to 11, then change partners. With one serve, depth and placement beat pace.

Pass mark: Track the points you win on your own serve and beat that number next time.

Progression: Servers must follow their serve to the net or lose the point.

Common serve and return mistakes in practice

  • Practising an illegal serve. Hitting above the waist or letting the bounce drift over the service line in practice builds a habit that will be called in a match.
  • Serving hard and flat. A deep, sliced serve you can repeat beats a fast one that keeps finding the net.
  • Standing still after the serve. The serve is the first step to the net. Move as soon as you have hit it.
  • Returning high to the net player. It is the easiest volley they will get all game. Go low or go over them.
  • Changing your mind mid-swing. Choose the return early and commit to it, especially on big points.

Frequently asked questions

How do I practise my padel serve alone?

Book a court, take a basket of balls and two towels as targets: one near the T and one near the side wall, deep in the box. Serve sets of 12 from each side with the same routine every time and count how many land on target.

Where should I aim my padel serve?

The three classic targets are the side glass, the T and the receiver's body. Mix them so the receiver can't settle, keep the serve deep and use slice to keep it low after the bounce.

What is the best return of serve in padel?

It depends on the serve. Against a hard serve into the glass, block it back safely. Against a good serve down the T, a deep lob buys time. Against an easy serve, attack: low at the server's feet or a lob that lets you take the net.

Can you volley the return of serve in padel?

No. The receiver must let the serve bounce in the box before playing it (FIP Rule 8.1). If the serve hits the receiver or their partner before bouncing, the point goes to the server.

Can you serve overarm in padel?

No. The ball must be bounced and struck at or below waist height, with at least one foot on the ground (FIP Rule 6).
