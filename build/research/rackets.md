## General guidance on choosing a racket (with sources)

### By level: weight, shape and balance
Decathlon's buying guide ([decathlon.eg](https://www.decathlon.eg/en/content/196-how-to-choose-your-padel-racket)) gives these bands:

| Level | Weight | Shape | Balance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beginner | Light, under 340 g | Round | Low (weight in the handle) |
| Intermediate | Medium, 340–360 g | Teardrop | Middle (neutral) |
| Advanced | Heavy, over 360 g | Diamond | High (head-heavy), with rigid fibre and hard foam |

- **Round:** wide sweet spot, forgiving, easy to control.
- **Teardrop:** smaller sweet spot and middle balance. A hybrid shape for intermediate to advanced players.
- **Diamond:** the sweet spot sits near the top, so it needs more skill but gives more power.
- **Under 340 g** suits newer players and people with tennis elbow (epicondylitis). **Over 360 g** suits bigger players or anyone looking for maximum power. (Same source.)

Central Sports UK's 2026 guide ([centralsports.co.uk](https://centralsports.co.uk/pages/best-padel-rackets-uk-2026)) gives a second, heavier view of the bands:
- Beginner: round, soft EVA, about 355–370 g.
- Intermediate: teardrop, medium EVA, about 365–375 g.
- Advanced: diamond, hard EVA, about 375–385 g. It adds that most players don't need a diamond.
- Balance point by shape: round under 260 mm, teardrop 260–275 mm, diamond over 275 mm.
- Women usually prefer the lighter end of each band. Replace a racket every 12–24 months of regular play.

The two sources disagree on weights, and this matters for the website. Decathlon's bands are about 20–30 g lighter than Central Sports'. Most adult 2026 rackets in this file are listed at 345–375 g.

### Carbon vs fibreglass
- **Fibreglass** adds comfort and elasticity. **Carbon** is durable and rigid, and suits expert rackets built for power on hard hits ([Decathlon](https://www.decathlon.eg/en/content/196-how-to-choose-your-padel-racket)).
- Padelful's intermediate guide says softer cores and frames are kinder to the arm, while stiffer rackets reward clean technique but forgive less. It also says head-heavy or medium-hard rackets need solid positioning and technique ([padelful.com](https://www.padelful.com/en/best-padel-rackets-intermediate)).

### Core hardness
From [Decathlon](https://www.decathlon.eg/en/content/196-how-to-choose-your-padel-racket):
- **Soft:** comfortable, reduces vibration and eases wrist strain. Gives more rebound on slow shots and less on hard ones.
- **Medium:** a balance of power, rebound and comfort.
- **Hard:** maximum power on hard shots, plus control, at the cost of comfort.
- Soft foam helps on shots from the back of the court. Hard foam adds speed on hard hits.

### Juniors: weight by age
**HEAD (a brand source)** ([head.com kids guide](https://www.head.com/en/rs/stories/how-to-choose-a-padel-racquet-for-kids)):
- Children can start from about **age 3**.
- **Under 10:** a shorter racket with a **33 mm profile**, about **300 g**.
- **Over 10:** about one finger shorter than an adult racket, with an adult **38 mm profile**, about **330 g**.
- **Transition, around age 12–13:** a light adult racket at the full **455 mm** length, for example the Zephyr UL at **340 g**. Later, move up to **355–375 g** depending on playing style.
- The age cutoffs aren't strict. Strength, height and ability matter more, and a coach can judge the fit. A racket that's too big or heavy raises the risk of injury.
- Adults shouldn't use junior rackets.

**Padelbrowser (a blog, so less authoritative)** ([padelbrowser.com](https://www.padelbrowser.com/blog/best-junior-padel-rackets-2026)):

| Age | Height | Target weight | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3–6 | under 120 cm | 210–260 g | foam or ultralight mini, round |
| 7–9 | 120–140 cm | 260–300 g | light junior, round, soft core |
| 10–12 | 140–155 cm | 300–330 g | junior or light adult, round or teardrop |
| 12–14 | over 155 cm | 330–355 g | junior pro frame or light adult |

- Round shapes, fibreglass faces and soft EVA suit most juniors. Stiff carbon frames are for stronger teens.
- Many rackets labelled "junior" weigh 330–350 g, which is heavy for a child under about 10, so check the actual weight.
- ⚠ This blog's own per-model weights are sometimes wrong. For example, it gives the Bullpadel Vertex Jr as 330–350 g and the Nox AT10 Genius Jr as 300–330 g. The model specs below come from retailer pages instead.

### FIP racket rules (relevant to juniors)
From the FIP Rules of Padel (Dec 2025 PDF, [padelfip.com](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Rules-of-Padel.pdf)):
- Total length: **no more than 45.5 cm**. Handle: at most 20 cm long and 50 mm thick.
- Head: at most **26 cm wide** and **38 mm thick**, with a **2.5% thickness tolerance** when measured.
- Holes: 9–13 mm across in the central area. Larger holes, up to 20 mm, are allowed only within 4 cm of the edge.
- Wrist strap: **mandatory**. It must be a non-elastic cord of at most **35 cm**.
- **The rules set no separate limits for junior rackets.** Junior rackets are simply shorter, thinner or lighter versions that stay within the adult maximums. Rackets must also follow the FIP Rackets Homologation Annex, which isn't part of this PDF. I didn't verify the annex.
- A Padel Point shop page mentions "FIP 2026 regulations" for equipment, but its text gives no details ([tiendapadelpoint.com](https://www.tiendapadelpoint.com/en/renew-padel-equipment-fip-2026-regulations-en)). Not verified.
