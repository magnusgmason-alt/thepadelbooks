Padel injuries after 40: what changes and how to stay ahead

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 17 Nov 2026

Padel is popular with players in their forties and fifties for good reason: it's sociable, tactical and doesn't require youth to enjoy. But the injury picture changes after 40. Knowing which injuries rise, and why, tells you where to put your prevention time.

Not medical advice

This guide explains common patterns in plain language. It can't diagnose you. If the pain is severe, came from a fall or twist, or isn't settling, get it assessed.

Is the risk really higher?

In the 2025 survey of 645 French players by Lecler and colleagues, being over 40 was associated with about four times the odds of injury. Not every study agrees: a 2025 survey of 364 Chilean amateur players (Belmar-Arriagada and colleagues) found similar injury rates across age groups. Either way, injuries were common in both, so prevention pays at any age, and it pays most where the evidence is consistent.

The calf and Achilles stand out: they were the most injured area overall in the French survey.

Why the body changes

Muscle strength declines faster than muscle size with age (Mitchell and colleagues, 2012), and power, the ability to produce force quickly, declines earlier and more steeply still (Reid and Fielding, 2012). Padel is full of fast push-offs, so a calf that has lost power but is asked for a 25-year-old's sprint is a classic setup for a tear. Tendons also become less stiff with age, but they respond to strength training: 12 to 14 weeks raised tendon stiffness in older adults about as much as in younger ones (McCrum and colleagues, 2018).

The four habits that matter most

  1. Strength twice a week, with calves non-negotiable. A 2014 meta-analysis of 25 trials found strength training cut sports injuries to less than a third. See our workout over 40.
  2. A real warm-up, not three minutes of rallying. The Aspetar review lists inadequate warm-up among padel's injury risk factors. See our warm-up over 40.
  3. Add sessions slowly. Padel New Zealand stresses gradual increases and rest days, particularly for older adults and new players.
  4. Act on early signals. A tight calf on the first sprint or an Achilles that's stiff in the morning is easier to fix in week one than in month three.

Play smarter

Experienced players can beat fitter ones with lobs, the walls and good positioning, which also means fewer flat-out sprints. Our 10 rules for padel over 40 cover the tactical side.

Check first if you need to

If you've been inactive for a long time or have a heart, joint or other medical condition, check with your doctor before ramping up. Stop and get urgent help for chest pain, severe breathlessness or feeling faint during exercise.

Frequently asked questions

Are padel injuries more common after 40?

A 2025 French study found about four times the odds of injury over 40, though a Chilean study found similar rates across ages. Injuries were common in both.

What is the most common padel injury over 40?

Calf and Achilles problems stand out: tennis leg is most common in poorly conditioned men over 40, and the calf-Achilles area was the most injured overall in a large French survey.

Why do older padel players get calf tears?

Strength and especially power decline with age, while padel demands explosive push-offs. Calf strength work and a proper warm-up reduce the risk.

Can strength training reduce injuries after 40?

Strength training substantially reduces sports injuries in general, and older adults still respond well to it, including their tendons.

Should I stop playing padel after 40?

No. Train twice a week, warm up properly, build up gradually and act early on niggles.
