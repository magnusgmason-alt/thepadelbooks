Hamstring strain in padel: recovery, rehab and the Nordic curl

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 15 Nov 2026

A sprint forward for a drop shot or a deep lunge for a low ball can strain the hamstring at the back of your thigh. You feel a sudden sharp pain, sometimes a pop, and it hurts to straighten the leg or walk quickly. Hamstring strains are common in sports with sprinting and they have a habit of coming back, so a good rehab matters as much as the rest. Here's what to do.

General information, not medical advice

This page explains common causes and general self-care. It can't diagnose you. If pain is severe, follows an injury, or comes with symptoms such as numbness or weakness, see a doctor or physiotherapist.

Symptoms

  • Sudden sharp pain in the back of the thigh during a sprint or lunge
  • Sometimes a popping or tearing feeling
  • Pain on straightening the leg, bending forward or walking fast
  • Swelling or bruising appearing over a few days

First steps

NHS advice for hamstring injuries is to rest the leg and avoid putting weight on it as much as possible, use ice packs wrapped in a towel for 15–20 minutes every 2–3 hours, use a compression bandage, and raise the leg when you can. Paracetamol can help with pain. After a few days, start gentle stretches and exercises as the pain allows.

How long does it take?

The NHS notes that a mild hamstring strain may take a few days to recover, while a more severe one can take weeks or months. A complete tear can need surgery. Going back before the muscle is strong is the main reason hamstring injuries recur.

Prevention: the Nordic hamstring curl

Kneel with a partner holding your ankles, keep your hips straight and lower your body forward as slowly as you can, then push back up with your hands. A 2019 meta-analysis (van Dyk and colleagues) found that injury prevention programmes including the Nordic hamstring exercise reduced hamstring injury rates by about half, though the authors also noted the overall quality of evidence was limited. Start with 2 × 4 reps once a week and build to 2–3 × 6–8, ideally not right before playing.

  • Warm up with leg swings and gradually faster sprints before matches.
  • Strength train the hamstrings with Romanian deadlifts and bridges as well as Nordics.
  • Increase match time gradually and avoid playing very tired.

When to see a doctor

Get urgent help if you heard a pop and can't walk, there's a dent or lump in the back of the thigh, or the bruising is extensive. See a GP or physiotherapist if it isn't improving after a week or two.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a pulled hamstring take to heal?

A mild strain may need a few days to a few weeks; a more severe strain can take weeks or months. Go back to padel only once you can sprint and lunge without pain.

How do I prevent hamstring injuries in padel?

Warm up with gradually faster sprints, strength train the hamstrings with Nordic curls, Romanian deadlifts and bridges, and increase playing time gradually.

Are Nordic curls good for padel players?

Yes. Programmes including Nordic curls roughly halved hamstring injury rates in other sports, though the evidence quality is limited.

Should I stretch a pulled hamstring?

Gentle stretching can start after a few days as pain allows, but strength work is the key part of rehab.
