Can you leave the court to play the ball in padel?

8 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 23 Oct 2026

You can only leave the court to play the ball if out-of-court play has been authorised, which depends on the court and its surrounding safety area meeting FIP specifications (FIP Rule 16). If it hasn't been authorised, hitting the ball with any part of your body outside the court loses the point (FIP Rule 13p). Many club courts are played without it, so check before you sprint for the door.

The short answer

Only on courts where out-of-court play is authorised. There, you may chase a ball that has gone out over the side wall or through a door and return it before it bounces again, but a ball over the end wall is always lost. Everywhere else, a ball that bounces in and then leaves the court is the opponents' point, and you can't play from outside.

What the rules say

The condition: Rule 16

Rule 16 ties out-of-court play to the court meeting the FIP safety-area specifications. The rulebook doesn't say who authorises it; in competitions the organiser normally announces whether it applies. Either way, it isn't something players decide mid-rally, and an opening in the side wall doesn't automatically mean you can run through it.

The rulebook's safety-area specifications for out-of-court play are:

  • Access points: 2 on each side of the court.
  • Clear space outside: an area free of obstacles, no less than 3 m wide (4 m recommended), 4 m long on either side and at least 3 m high.
  • Door openings with one access per side: at least 1.05 × 2.0 m and at most 2.20 × 2.20 m.
  • Door openings with two accesses per side: at least 0.72 × 2.00 m and at most 1.10 × 2.20 m.

If your club court doesn't have that clear space around it, out-of-court play isn't on, whatever the doors look like.

When out-of-court play is not authorised

  • Your body must be inside. If the player hitting the ball has one foot, both feet or any part of the body outside the court, the point is lost (FIP Rule 13p).
  • A ball that leaves the court is finished. If the ball bounces correctly and then crosses the marked outer limit of the court, at the side or the end, or goes out through a gate, the pair that had to return it loses the point (FIP Rule 13d). So a ball that bounces in your court and flies out is the opponents' point.
  • Serves through the gate. On a court without a safety zone, a serve that lands in the box and leaves through a gate is a fault (FIP Rule 7f).

When out-of-court play is authorised

  • Shots from outside are valid. A ball hit from outside the court is a correct return as long as the other conditions for a correct return are met (FIP Rule 14j).
  • The end wall is still final. If the ball bounces correctly and then goes over the end wall, the point is lost for the side that had to return it, and the ball can't be played (FIP Rule 13e).
  • Side wall and door balls can be chased. If the ball goes over a side wall or out through a door, the point is only lost if the ball bounces a second time or touches an element unrelated to the court (FIP Rule 13e).
  • The neutral post. The vertical post dividing the door openings is a neutral zone above 0.92 m, and any player may touch or hold it (FIP Rule 13b). Normally, touching the net, posts or the opponents' court during a point loses it (Rule 13a); this is a specific exception for out-of-court play.

Allowed

On an authorised court: running out through the door to return a ball that went over the side wall or through a door, before it bounces a second time, and holding the neutral post above 0.92 m (Rules 13b, 13e, 14j).

Not allowed

Playing from outside on a court where out-of-court play isn't authorised (13p), chasing a ball over the end wall on any court (13e), or letting the ball bounce a second time outside (13e).

Real match situations

The smash that flies out of a club court

Your opponent smashes, the ball bounces in your court and sails over the side glass. You run out through the door and somehow hit it back in. On a court where out-of-court play isn't authorised, your shot doesn't count: the point ended when the ball left the court, and it goes to the smasher (FIP Rule 13d).

The same smash on an authorised court

Now the court and event allow out-of-court play. You sprint out, reach the ball before its second bounce and send it back so it lands in the opponents' court. That is a correct return (FIP Rules 13e and 14j), and the rally continues.

Over the back wall

The smash bounces and goes over your end wall instead. Even with out-of-court play authorised, the point is lost for your side and the ball can't be played (FIP Rule 13e). Save your legs.

One foot through the doorway

On a non-authorised court, you stretch for a ball by the open doorway and plant your foot outside the court as you hit. Verdict: point lost (FIP Rule 13p). It doesn't matter that the ball was still inside; your body was not.

The ball hits you outside

You're outside on an authorised court and the opponents' shot hits your body instead of your racket. Verdict: point lost (FIP Rule 13k). Rule 13k makes no distinction between inside and outside the court, so it applies wherever you are standing.

Grabbing the post to get back in

On an authorised court, you return the ball from outside and grab the vertical post between the door openings to swing yourself back in. Above 0.92 m that post is a neutral zone, so it is allowed (FIP Rule 13b).

Common misconceptions

  • "If I can reach it, I can play it." Not unless out-of-court play is authorised for that court (Rule 16). Otherwise, Rule 13d ends the point when the ball leaves.
  • "A ball that goes out of the court is replayed." No. After a correct bounce, a ball leaving the court is a point for the hitter (13d), or playable on an authorised court (13e). Neither is a let.
  • "An open door means out-of-court play is on." Doors exist on every court. What matters is the court and safety area meeting specifications and the play being authorised (Rule 16).
  • "Outside, you get one bounce." The ball has already bounced once in your court. Outside, you must reach it before a second bounce (13e).
  • "End-wall balls can be chased too." Never. Rule 13e makes those unplayable.

Social games vs official matches

The rulebook doesn't say who authorises out-of-court play; in competitions the organiser normally announces whether it applies, and the umpire then applies Rules 13d, 13e and 13p. In club and social games, the convention at most venues is to play without it, often because there isn't safe space around the court or the next court is right there. Agree before the first point. If anyone in your four is unsure, play it as not authorised: that way a ball leaving the court is simply the hitter's point, and nobody runs into a fence or a neighbouring game.

Practical tips

  • On a non-authorised court, stop chasing. Once the ball has left the court after a bounce, the point is over. Running after it only risks injury.
  • On an authorised court, read the smash early. Out-of-court play usually starts with a hard smash that bounces and kicks high. Start moving towards the door as soon as you see the bounce, rather than waiting under the ball.
  • Aim your return deep and safe. From outside you have little control. A high, deep ball into the opponents' court gives you and your partner time to get back into position.

Frequently asked questions

Is out-of-court play allowed in amateur padel?

Only when the court and safety area meet specifications and play outside has been authorised (FIP Rule 16). Check the event's rules. In casual club play the common convention is that it isn't allowed.

What happens if the ball bounces and goes out of the court?

If out-of-court play isn't authorised, the pair that had to return it loses the point (FIP Rule 13d). If it is authorised, a ball over the side wall or through a door can still be played before its second bounce, but a ball over the end wall can't (FIP Rule 13e).

Can I touch the door post when playing outside?

Yes, when out-of-court play is authorised, the vertical post dividing the doors is a neutral zone above 0.92 m that any player may touch or hold (FIP Rule 13b).

Which door do I have to use to go out?

The FIP rulebook doesn't specifically say. What it does cover is that touching the net, posts or the opponents' court during a point loses it (Rule 13a), so make sure you never step into their side on the way out or back.

What if the serve goes out of the door?

On a court without a safety zone, a serve that bounces in the box and leaves through a gate is a fault (FIP Rule 7f). More in our guide to service faults.
