Heel pain from padel: plantar fasciitis, Achilles pain and what helps

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 15 Nov 2026

Sharp pain under your heel with the first steps in the morning, or an ache at the back of the heel after a weekend of matches, is common in padel players. The quick push-offs and stops load the heel and the tissues around it, and sudden increases in play or worn-out shoes often tip it over. Here's how to tell the main causes apart and what usually helps.

General information, not medical advice

This page explains common causes and general self-care. It can't diagnose your pain. If pain is severe, follows a fall or a loud pop, stops you putting weight on the leg, or isn't improving after a couple of weeks, see a doctor or physiotherapist.

Under the heel or behind it?

Plantar heel pain (plantar fasciitis)Achilles tendon pain
WhereUnderneath the heel, sometimes into the archBack of the heel or a few cm above it
WorstFirst steps in the morning or after sittingStart of activity and the morning after playing
Often linked toMore time on your feet, worn or flat shoes, tight calvesSudden increase in jumping, push-offs or matches

What you can do

NHS advice for plantar fasciitis includes resting your feet and raising them when you can, wearing shoes with cushioned heels and good arch support, using insoles or heel pads, gentle stretches, painkillers such as ibuprofen if suitable, and an ice pack wrapped in a towel on the heel. It also suggests losing weight if you're overweight, and avoiding walking or standing for long periods and high heels. It can take a while to settle, so patience matters.

For Achilles pain, the general approach is to reduce the load that aggravates it (fewer matches, less jumping) without stopping all activity, then build calf strength gradually.

Exercises that help

ExerciseDoseNotes
Calf stretch against a wall (straight and bent knee)3 × 30 s eachGentle, several times a day
Rolling a bottle or ball under the arch1–2 minCan ease morning pain
Calf raises, slow lowering3 × 12–15Start on two legs, progress to one
Calf raises with a towel under the toes3 × 12Used in plantar heel pain rehab to load the arch
Bent-knee calf raise3 × 12Strengthens the soleus, important for push-offs

Pain during these exercises should be mild and settle within a day. If it keeps getting worse, reduce the load.

Playing with heel pain

  • Cut back on match time rather than stopping completely if pain is mild and settles quickly.
  • Check your shoes: worn heels and flattened midsoles are common culprits. See padel shoes.
  • Warm up the calves before playing with calf raises and light skipping.
  • Avoid sudden jumps from one or two matches a week to five.

When to see a doctor

The NHS advises seeing a GP if heel pain hasn't improved after two weeks of treating it at home, if it's severe or stops you doing normal activities, or if you have diabetes. Get urgent help if you felt a snap in the back of the heel, or the pain started after a fall or injury.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my heel hurt after padel?

Often plantar heel pain (plantar fasciitis) under the heel, or Achilles tendon irritation at the back of the heel, usually after an increase in play, worn shoes or tight, weak calves.

Can I play padel with plantar fasciitis?

Some people can keep playing a little if pain is mild and settles quickly, but cut back, check your shoes and do calf and foot exercises. Stop if it's getting worse.

What shoes are best for heel pain in padel?

Padel shoes with a cushioned heel, good arch support and a sole that suits your court surface. Replace them when the midsole flattens.

How long does plantar fasciitis take to heal?

It often improves within several weeks to months with self-care. See a GP if it's not better after two weeks of home treatment.
