How to beat hard hitters in padel

6 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 8 Nov 2026

The way to beat hard hitters in padel is to stop competing on pace: let the glass absorb their power, block their drives at the net, and give them slow, high or low balls they have to create speed from. Power players win when you feed them the ball they like. Below, find the symptom you're seeing on court, its likely cause and the fix, then run the self-test to see which part of your game is letting them in.

Why raw power is beatable in padel

In tennis, a big hitter can blast through you. In padel the walls bring most hard shots back into play, and a ball that rebounds off the glass with pace often arrives at a comfortable height for the defender. That's why our padel tactics for beginners guide says position beats power more often than you'd think.

Hard hitters also have a pattern: they love a ball that arrives at waist height with some pace on it, because they can swing through it. Take that ball away and many of them start overhitting. The Padel School's advice on slow balls is built on this idea: change the rhythm instead of answering speed with more speed.

Fix 1: block, don't swing, at the net

When a hard hitter has time and a sitting ball, expect the fast one. The Padel School and Ely Padel Club's coaching notes make the same points about the block volley:

  • Read it early. Watch their body and their earlier choices. If they hit hard whenever they have time, prepare before they swing.
  • Stay compact. No big backswing. Ely's coaches suggest beginners and lower intermediates keep a little space between racket and chest for quicker reactions, while stronger players can hold the racket tighter to the chest, especially on the backhand.
  • Use their pace. Present the racket face and let the ball rebound. Aim into open space or the corners and don't take unnecessary risks. You're neutralising the attack, not winning the point with one shot.

A split step as they start their swing gets you balanced in time to block in either direction.

Fix 2: let the glass take the sting out

A hard ball that bounces and hits your back glass loses a lot of its speed and gives you time. The Padel School recommends letting the back glass do this work: it gives you an extra bounce and time to play a controlled shot. The keys are space and preparation.

  • Start about a metre or more in front of the glass, not pressed against it.
  • Get your racket back as the ball is about to hit the glass, as The Padel School advises, then move so you contact it in front of you.
  • Match the shot to the height: low balls get a soft defensive reply, waist-to-chest balls a controlled shot, and only balls above the shoulder are for attacking.

More on this in our guide to playing off the back glass.

Fix 3: give them nothing to hit

Padel Browser's plan against stronger pairs applies perfectly to bangers: don't match their pace. Hit higher over the net, deeper and slower, with more margin, and make them generate every bit of speed themselves. A slow ball that lands deep is harder to crush than a quick one at mid height.

Your two main slow balls are:

  • The high lob. The Padel School calls it the easier slow ball to execute, and it forces the hitter into an overhead from deep. Aim for depth; Padel Browser stresses that depth matters more than height.
  • The chiquita. A soft ball that dips at the feet of a net player. A big hitter can't drive a ball that's below the net, so they have to lift it, and you can step in.
One exception

If the hard hitter's best shot is the smash, Padel Browser's advice is to lob less and keep the ball low. Lob the partner instead, or lob only when it's deep and well disguised.

Fix 4: aim low, and aim at the feet

Hard hitters want to hit down or flat. Make them hit up. From the middle of the court, Babolat's three-zone model says a low ball calls for a patient shot to the opponents' feet. That ball either forces a weak volley or tempts the banger into a low-percentage swing that finds the net.

Self-test: why are the hitters beating you?

Answer yes or no after your next match against a power pair.

  1. Did you stand within a metre of your back glass for most rallies? If yes, you gave their pace no space to die. Move forward.
  2. Did you try to out-hit them from the back? If yes, you played their game. Count how many of your hard drives came back.
  3. Were your lobs landing short of their service line? If yes, you fed their overhead. Lob higher and deeper, or switch to low balls.
  4. Did you take a full swing at volleys aimed at your body? If yes, practise the block until it's automatic.
  5. Did their errors rise as the match went on? If not, your balls were too comfortable. Slow down and go lower.

Two or more yes answers on the first four points tell you where to start.

Practise blocking against pace

A feeder drives hard from behind the far service line at you and your partner at the net. Block every ball deep into a corner target without a backswing. When you land seven out of ten, the feeder mixes in lobs and slow balls so you can't settle into a block-only stance. Frequently asked questions

How do you play against someone who hits really hard in padel?

Don't match their pace. Let hard balls come off the glass, block their drives at the net, and give them slow, high and low balls so they have to generate all the speed themselves.

Is it legal to hit the ball at an opponent's body?

During a point, yes: hitting at a player is a normal tactic, and a ball that touches an opponent's body loses them the point (FIP Rule 13k). The FIP's etiquette guidance bans hitting the ball at opponents when it's not in play.

What is a block volley?

A volley with almost no swing, where you hold the racket firmly in front of you and let the opponent's pace rebound off it. It's the safest answer to a hard drive at the net.

Should I lob a hard hitter?

Usually yes, deep and high, because it forces them to hit an overhead from far back. If their smash is their best shot, lob less and keep the ball low at their feet instead.

Why do my hard shots keep coming back?

Because the glass returns them. A hard ball that bounces into the back glass slows down and gives the defender time. Placement and depth beat pace in padel.
