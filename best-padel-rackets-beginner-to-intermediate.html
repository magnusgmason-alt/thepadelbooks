Best padel rackets for beginner to intermediate players (2026)

5 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 23 Oct 2026

Your first racket got you playing. Now you're playing every week, your technique is settling and you want a racket that gives you a little more: better feel, more pop on overheads, a sweet spot that rewards good contact. The trick is to upgrade without jumping to a racket that's too demanding.

The short answer

Stay with a round or soft teardrop shape and a low to medium balance, but look for better materials: a carbon frame, a quality fibreglass or glass-carbon face and a comfortable EVA core. Keep the weight close to what you're used to, roughly 340–375 g for most adults.

Are you ready to upgrade?

Upgrade when at least two of these are true:

  • You play at least once a week and can keep a rally going from the back and at the net.
  • You can hit a controlled bandeja and play off the back glass.
  • Your current racket feels "dead" on volleys or lacks pop on overheads.
  • Your racket is worn: cracked face, damaged frame or a core that feels soft and lifeless.

If none of those apply, keep your racket and spend the money on lessons. A new racket won't fix technique.

What to change, and what not to

Two buying guides we checked differ on exact weights: Decathlon suggests under 340 g for beginners and 340–360 g for intermediates, while Central Sports suggests bands about 15–25 g heavier. In practice, most 2026 adult rackets weigh 345–375 g. The safest rule is not to jump more than about 10–15 g heavier than what you use now, especially if you've ever had arm trouble.

  • Keep: a forgiving shape (round or soft teardrop) and a low to medium balance.
  • Upgrade: the materials. A carbon frame adds stability; a better face and core improve feel.
  • Avoid for now: diamond shapes, hard cores and very head-heavy rackets.

Shape and balance, explained

Balance describes where the weight sits along the racket. Low balance (weight towards the handle) makes the racket quicker at the net and easier on the arm. High balance (weight towards the head) adds power on overheads but needs more strength and timing. For players in this stage, low to medium balance is usually the sweet spot.

Common upgrade mistakes

  1. Buying a pro's racket. Professional models are usually stiffer, heavier in the head and far less forgiving.
  2. Going much heavier. A big jump in weight often brings arm trouble. See our guide to padel elbow.
  3. Choosing by colour or price alone. Shape, weight and balance matter far more.
  4. Never trying before buying. Demo rackets at your club are the best test.

Frequently asked questions

How long should my first racket last before I upgrade?

There's no fixed time. Central Sports suggests replacing a racket after roughly 12–24 months of regular play. Upgrade sooner if it's damaged, or if your game has clearly outgrown it.

Should my second racket be carbon?

A carbon frame is a sensible upgrade. A full carbon face is stiffer and less forgiving, so many improving players are happier with a fibreglass or mixed face for now.

Round or teardrop for an improving player?

Round if control and comfort matter most. A soft teardrop if you want a bit more power and your technique is consistent.

Is a heavier racket better?

Not necessarily. A heavier racket can give more stability and power, but it's slower to move and harder on the arm. Choose the heaviest racket you can swing comfortably for a whole session, not the heaviest you can lift.
