What age can kids start padel?

6 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 8 Nov 2026

What age can kids start padel? Children can make their first strokes from about age three, HEAD says, as long as the racket is light and short enough; in practice junior programmes usually take children from around five, with mini-padel games, and Padel Browser's parents' guide calls nine to twelve the sweet spot for starting properly. Below, parents' most common questions, answered by age, with the equipment, safety and competition details you need.

What age can kids start padel?

Earlier than most parents think. HEAD's guide to junior rackets says children can start as soon as they're strong enough to hold the racket, and could make their first strokes from the age of three. At that age it's play with a racket and a ball rather than padel as adults know it.

Organised coaching usually starts a little later. The USA Padel Association's guide for clubs starting a junior programme suggests beginning with an under-12 group for ages 7 to 12, and later splitting off an under-8 group for ages 5 to 8. Padel Browser's parents' guide describes ages 5 to 8 as the age for fun or mini-padel focused on coordination, and calls 9 to 12 the sweet spot for starting properly.

What is the best age to start padel?

There isn't a single best age. What matters more is whether your child can track a moving ball, enjoys hitting it, and has a racket and ball suited to their size. The USPA guide makes the same point about progress: children should move through stages based on demonstrated ability, not age or time enrolled.

If your child loves it at five, start at five, with games. If they come to it at eleven or twelve, they'll pick it up fast: the underarm serve, the smaller court and the slower game make padel one of the easier racket sports to start.

Padel for 5- and 6-year-olds

At this age, think games, not technique.

  • Slower balls. The USPA recommends foam or low-compression balls for young beginners, and for the youngest even rolling the ball along the court with the racket.
  • Short sessions. Padel Browser suggests about 30 to 45 minutes for ages 5 to 8, mostly games.
  • Lots of hits. The USPA warns against line drills where children queue for one turn each. Mini-rallies of three to five shots, races and target games keep everyone moving.
  • Walls as play. In the USPA's first stage, wall play is "exploratory, not technical". Let them discover the bounce.

Padel for 8-year-olds

Most eight-year-olds can rally over the net with a slow ball, serve underarm and start to understand where to stand. A one-hour session is realistic: the USPA calls 60 minutes the standard in well-run programmes and says 90 minutes is typically too long for beginners or under-12s. Keep scoring simple (longest rally, first to five) rather than full adult scoring, and add a few doubles points each session so they learn to play with a partner.

Padel for 10-year-olds and older

From about ten, children can move towards adult-style practice: cross-court rallies, letting the ball come off the back glass, volleys at the net and simple doubles tactics. This is also the age when a slightly heavier junior racket makes sense. What racket and ball does a child need?

A junior racket, not a cut-down adult one. HEAD's guide suggests a shorter, lighter model of about 300 g for children under ten and about 330 g for older juniors, then a light adult racket of around 340 g as a transition. A racket that's too big or heavy makes the game harder and, HEAD says, increases the risk of injury. Strength and height matter more than age, so let your child try a racket before you buy. Our guide to the best padel rackets for kids compares current junior models.

The FIP rules have no separate junior racket rules, so junior rackets stay within the adult limits, including the compulsory wrist strap. For balls, start with low-compression or foam balls and move to standard padel balls as rallies get faster.

How long should a junior padel session be?

  • Ages 5 to 8: about 30 to 45 minutes, mostly games (Padel Browser).
  • Ages 9 to 12: a full hour is usually fine (Padel Browser; the USPA's standard class length is 60 minutes).
  • Teenagers: similar to adults, with rest and water breaks.

Stop while they're still enjoying it. A child who leaves wanting more will come back.

Is padel safe for kids?

Padel is a fairly gentle racket sport, but three points matter.

Eyes. Researchers at the University of Gothenburg described three padel eye injuries that all needed surgery, noted that a smash can reach up to 130 km/h, and said it's a good idea to wear goggles, which can cut the risk of eye damage by up to 90 percent when used correctly. Children at the net in mixed-age games are exactly the situation to think about. Our guide to padel eye protection covers the options.

Collisions. Padel Browser's parents' guide makes calling "mine" before swinging the biggest safety habit in doubles, and adds a rule worth repeating: never run through a glass wall to chase a ball.

Behaviour and supervision. The FIP's etiquette rules ban hitting the ball at opponents when it isn't in play; with children, ban smashing at anyone at all. The USPA guide adds no unsupervised court access and no running on wet courts.

What age can kids compete in padel?

At international level, the FIP's junior circuit, FIP Promises, runs Under 12, Under 14, Under 16 and Under 18 categories from 2026, organised as continental circuits plus a global Under 18 tour. Nationally, federations and clubs run their own junior competitions and set their own age groups, so ask at your club. Padel Browser suggests a gentle route: group clinics first, occasional private lessons after a few months, and local junior tournaments in the second year.

Frequently asked questions

Can a 4-year-old play padel?

They can play with a racket and a soft ball. HEAD says first strokes are possible from about three, but most coaching groups start around five, with games rather than technique.

Is padel good for a 6-year-old?

Yes, as long as sessions are short and game-based, with a light junior racket and a slow, low-compression ball.

Is padel or tennis better for young children?

Both can work. Padel is often easier to start because of the underarm serve, smaller court and slower ball, so young children tend to have rallies sooner.

What age groups are there in junior padel?

The FIP's junior circuit uses Under 12, Under 14, Under 16 and Under 18 categories. National federations and clubs set their own groups, which can start younger.

Should children wear goggles for padel?

It's worth considering. University of Gothenburg ophthalmologists recommend goggles for padel and say protective eyewear can reduce the risk of eye damage by up to 90 percent when used correctly.
