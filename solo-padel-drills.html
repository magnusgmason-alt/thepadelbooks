Padel drills to do alone

6 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 23 Oct 2026

Yes, there are plenty of padel drills to do alone: ball-control taps at home, wall rallies against any flat wall, back-glass rallies on an empty court and serve practice with a basket of balls. Here are eight solo drills, from the living room to the court, with targets to beat and progressions.

Padel drills to do alone: what solo training can and can't do

Training alone won't teach you to read an opponent or work with a partner. It is, though, the best way to get thousands of touches on the ball, groove your contact point and build fitness and footwork. Two short solo sessions a week add up to a lot of extra repetitions over a season.

All you need is a racket, a few balls and, for most drills, a wall. A garage door, a sports-hall wall or the glass of an empty padel court all work. Check with the owner or club before you start banging balls against a wall.

Padel drills at home

1. Face taps

Players: 1 · Time: 5 minutes

Setup: A racket, a ball and a bit of space indoors or outside.

How it works: Tap the ball up into the air with one face of the racket, keeping it at about head height. Then switch to the other face, then alternate faces every touch. NOX suggests this as a home drill for getting comfortable with the whole hitting surface.

Goal: 30 taps on one face, then 30 alternating, without the ball dropping.

Progression: Add touches on the frame between face taps, or walk slowly around a room while tapping.

2. Split-step and shadow

Players: 1 · Time: 6 minutes

Setup: A space of about three metres square. A phone with an interval timer helps.

How it works: Stand in a ready position with your racket up. Do a small split-step hop, then move two steps to a forehand volley shadow and recover. Repeat to the backhand, then backwards for an overhead shadow. Turn side-on for every shot. The Padel School puts the split step at the heart of good footwork; this drill grooves it without a ball.

Goal: Six rounds of 30 seconds on, 30 seconds off, staying light on the balls of your feet.

Progression: Use a footwork ladder or a chalk grid, with quick in-and-out steps before each shadow shot.

Wall drills

3. Volley wall

Players: 1 · Time: 8 minutes

Setup: Stand about two and a half to three metres from a solid wall, the distance NOX recommends for this exercise.

How it works: Volley the ball against the wall without letting it bounce, alternating forehand and backhand. Keep the swing short and the contact in front of your body.

Goal: 20 volleys in a row, then 40.

Progression: Move sideways along the wall as you volley, or aim at a taped square at net height.

4. Bounce rally

Players: 1 · Time: 8 minutes

Setup: Step further back from the wall, enough to let the ball bounce once before each shot.

How it works: Hit forehands and backhands against the wall, letting the ball bounce once each time. Turn side-on, take the racket back early and finish towards the wall.

Goal: Your longest unbroken rally. Note it and try to beat it next time.

Progression: Alternate a firm drive with a softer shot so you practise changing pace.

Solo drills on an empty court

5. Back-glass rally

Players: 1 · Time: 10 minutes

Setup: Stand three to four metres in front of the back glass, facing it.

How it works: Hit the ball into the back glass so it bounces on the way back to you, then hit it again. Alternate forehand and backhand. This builds racket control and rhythm; the progression below practises the real back-glass shape.

Goal: 20 in a row, then 50, then 100.

Progression: Add a side step between shots, or turn around and play the rebound properly: drop the ball in front of the glass, let it come out, and send it over the net. The Padel School's key point applies: step back, then step forward into the ball rather than hitting it behind you.

6. Corner rebound

Players: 1 · Time: 8 minutes

Setup: A basket of balls in a back corner.

How it works: Throw a ball so it bounces and then hits the side glass and back glass. Read the rebound, move to it with small steps, and play it over the net cross-court.

Goal: Seven out of ten balls returned over the net past the service line.

Progression: Throw harder and lower so the ball comes out faster, or vary which wall it hits first.

7. Serve targets

Players: 1 · Time: 10 minutes

Setup: Put two targets in each service box: one near the centre line and one near the side glass, both deep.

How it works: Serve ten balls at each target, using a legal serve: ball bounced behind the service line in your own box, struck at or below waist height with at least one foot on the ground (FIP Rule 6). A serve that bounces in the box and then hits the mesh is a fault (Rule 7), so aim for the glass.

Goal: Serves in out of 40, plus target hits. Record both.

Progression: Call your target aloud before each serve and only count it if it goes where you said.

8. Self-fed overheads

Players: 1 · Time: 8 minutes

Setup: Stand around the service line with a basket.

How it works: Toss the ball high in front of you, turn side-on, and play a controlled bandeja deep into the opposite court. Recover two steps towards the net after each one, as if your partner were beside you.

Goal: Ten bandejas that land past the opponent's service line without hitting the back glass on the full.

Progression: Toss the ball slightly behind you so you have to move back with side steps first.

A 20-minute solo session

MinutesDrill
0–3Warm-up and split-step shadow
3–8Back-glass rally (or bounce rally at home)
8–12Serve targets
12–16Volley wall
16–20Self-fed overheads or face taps
Keep a log

Write down one number per drill: consecutive back-glass hits, serves in out of 40, longest volley chain. Solo training lives or dies by whether you can see progress.

Solo padel training mistakes

  • Standing too close to the wall. It forces a flick of the wrist. Give yourself room for a proper short swing.
  • Hitting everything hard. The wall gives power back. Control is the point.
  • Forgetting your feet. Small adjustment steps before every ball, even when alone.
  • Same drill every time. Rotate drills so you cover volleys, the glass and the serve each week.

Frequently asked questions

Can you practise padel alone?

Yes. Wall drills, back-glass rallies, serve practice and footwork work well alone. You can't practise tactics or teamwork solo, but you can build technique and fitness.

What padel drills can I do at home?

Face taps, split-step and shadow footwork, and wall volleys if you have a suitable outside wall such as a garage. NOX also suggests a bounce rally if you have a patio or garage with more space.

Can I hit against the glass on an empty padel court?

Usually, if you've booked the court. It's the best place for solo training, because the glass behaves exactly as it does in a match. Check the club's rules first.

How often should I train alone?

Two short sessions of 15–20 minutes a week on top of your normal games is a realistic, useful amount for most players.

Do I need special equipment for solo padel drills?

No. A racket, a handful of balls and a wall are enough. A ball basket and a few cones or towels for targets make court sessions easier.
