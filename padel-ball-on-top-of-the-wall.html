Ball on top of the wall, stuck in the fence or in the corner: what the padel rules say

9 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 23 Oct 2026

If your shot bounces in your opponents' court and then gets stuck on the flat top of the wall, wedges in the metal fence or escapes through a hole in it, you win the point (FIP Rules 15a and 15b). A ball that lands in the corner where the wall meets the floor is also a good ball (FIP Rule 14i). The catch is the bounce: all of this only works in your favour once the ball has bounced on the other side first.

The short answer

After a correct bounce in the opponents' court, a ball that gets stuck on top of the wall, stuck in the mesh or goes through a hole in the mesh wins the point for the player who hit it. A ball landing in the floor-wall corner is good. If the ball reaches the wall or fence before it bounces, the hitter loses the point instead.

What the rules say

Everything in this article hangs off one principle from FIP Rule 12. Once your shot has bounced on the opponents' ground, it may touch any part of the court and stays in play, and the opponents must return it before it bounces a second time. The rulebook lists the parts of the court as the inside of the walls, the metal mesh fence including its frame, the ground, the net and the posts.

So after the bounce, glass and mesh are both fair game. The question is what happens when the ball doesn't come back out again. That is where Rules 15 and 14i come in.

Stuck on the flat top of the wall (Rule 15b)

If the ball bounces in the opponents' court and then comes to rest on the flat top of the wall, the point goes to the player who hit it (FIP Rule 15b). The opponents can't reach it to play it, so the rulebook simply awards the point.

Stuck in the mesh or through a hole (Rule 15a)

If the ball bounces in the opponents' court and then gets stuck in the metal fence, or leaves the court through a hole in the mesh, that is also a point won for the hitter (FIP Rule 15a). Rule 15a covers a ball that goes through, or sticks in, a hole in the metal fence itself. A gap under the fence is not explicitly covered, so agree how to treat it before playing.

The corner where wall meets floor (Rule 14i)

A ball that bounces right in the corner where the wall meets the ground in the opponents' court is a correct return (FIP Rule 14i). These balls often skid out flat and are nearly impossible to play, which is exactly why the rule exists: it settles the argument in favour of the hitter.

Why the bounce decides everything (Rule 13g)

Now flip it round. If your shot strikes the opponents' walls, their metal fence or any element not part of their court without bouncing first, you lose the point (FIP Rule 13g). That covers a volley straight into their mesh where it sticks, and a smash that hits their glass full. The same ball that would have won you the point after a bounce loses it before one.

Point won

Your shot bounces in the opponents' court, then sticks on top of the wall, sticks in the mesh, slips through a hole in the mesh, or lands in the floor-wall corner (Rules 15a, 15b, 14i).

Point lost

Your shot hits the opponents' wall or mesh before bouncing, wherever it ends up, including stuck in the fence or on top of the wall (Rule 13g).

Real match situations

The lob that parks on the back wall

You play a high, deep lob. It bounces near the opponents' baseline, kicks up off the back glass and settles on the flat top of the wall. Verdict: your point (FIP Rule 15b). Nobody needs to climb anything to keep the rally going.

The smash that wedges in the side fence

Your smash bounces in mid-court, flies sideways into the side mesh and jams in the wire. Verdict: your point (FIP Rule 15a). It bounced first, so the fence was a legal part of the court, and the opponents can't return a ball that is stuck.

The volley that goes straight into the mesh

At the net, you punch a volley that never touches their floor and buries itself in their side fence. Verdict: you lose the point (FIP Rule 13g). The ball hit their fence before bouncing, and getting stuck doesn't rescue it. For more on how the fence behaves, see what happens when the ball hits the fence.

The ball that dies in the corner

Your drive lands exactly where the back glass meets the floor and skids out without a playable rebound. The opponents say it hit the glass first. If it landed in the corner, it is a good ball (FIP Rule 14i). If it clearly struck the glass before touching the ground, it falls under Rule 13g instead. That judgement is the whole dispute, which is why calling it honestly and quickly matters.

The ball that clips the top edge and drops back in

After bouncing in the opponents' court, the ball catches the top edge of the glass and falls back into their court. The FIP rulebook doesn't specifically address the top edge of the wall. What it does say is that after a correct bounce the ball stays in play when it touches the court, and must be returned before the second bounce (FIP Rule 12). The usual club convention is to treat this like any other wall contact: play on. If it stays on top, Rule 15b applies; if it leaves the court, the out-of-court rules (13d and 13e) decide it.

The serve that sticks in the mesh

Your serve lands in the right box and then jams in the side fence. Verdict: fault, not a winner (FIP Rule 7e). On the serve, touching the metal fence before the second bounce is a fault, even though the glass is fine. Rule 15a is about rallies, and the serve has its own rules.

Common misconceptions

  • "A stuck ball is always a let." No. After a bounce in the opponents' court it is a point for the hitter (Rules 15a and 15b). Before the bounce, it is a point for the opponents (13g). Nothing gets replayed.
  • "The frame of the fence is out." The rulebook counts the metal mesh fence including its frame as part of the court (Rule 12), so after a bounce it is in play like the rest of the mesh.
  • "A ball in the corner hit the wall first, so it's out." A ball that bounces in the floor-wall corner is good (Rule 14i). Only a ball that clearly hit the wall before the floor loses the point.
  • "The opponents can knock the ball off the top of the wall and keep playing." Rule 15b awards the point when the ball is stuck there, so the rally is over.
  • "If the serve sticks in the fence, the server wins." Serves follow Rule 7e: mesh before the second bounce is a fault.

Social games vs official matches

With an umpire, these calls are the umpire's to make. In social and club games, players call their own side, and the corner ball is the classic flashpoint. A good habit: the pair on the receiving side calls it, and if nobody saw it clearly, agree beforehand whether you replay the point. That is a convention, not an FIP rule, but it saves a lot of arguments.

It is also worth agreeing before you start how you treat the top-edge clip, since the rulebook doesn't spell it out. And when a ball lodges high in the fence or on top of the wall, finish the point first, then fetch it safely. Climbing the fence mid-game is how people get hurt.

Practical tip: don't stop playing early

The most common lost point in this area isn't a rule dispute. It is a player who assumes a ball heading for the corner or the top of the glass is dead and stops moving, then watches it pop back out. Unless the ball is actually stuck, keep playing until it bounces twice. Defending deep balls off the glass is a skill in its own right; our guide to playing off the back glass covers the footwork.

Frequently asked questions

Who wins the point if the ball gets stuck in the fence in padel?

If the ball bounced in the opponents' court before sticking in the mesh, the hitter wins the point (FIP Rule 15a). If it went into the mesh without bouncing, the hitter loses it (FIP Rule 13g).

What happens if the ball lands on top of the wall?

After a correct bounce in the opponents' court, a ball that stays on the flat top of the wall is a point won for the hitter (FIP Rule 15b). If it lands there straight from the racket, it struck the opponents' wall without bouncing, which loses the point (Rule 13g).

Is a ball in the corner good in padel?

Yes. A ball bouncing in the corner where the wall meets the ground in the opponents' court is a correct return (FIP Rule 14i).

What if the ball goes through a hole in the fence?

If it bounced in the opponents' court first, it is a point for the hitter (FIP Rule 15a).

What if my serve gets stuck in the fence?

It is a fault. A serve that touches the metal fence before its second bounce is a service fault (FIP Rule 7e). See every way a serve can be a fault.

Does the ball have to come back out of the corner to be good?

No. Rule 14i treats the corner bounce itself as valid. It is then up to the opponents to return it before the second bounce, which is often impossible.
