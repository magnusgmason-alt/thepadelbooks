Soft vs hard padel racket: myths and truths

6 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 8 Nov 2026

The soft vs hard padel racket choice is about the core: a soft core gives the ball an easy push on slow swings and absorbs more vibration, while a hard core gives more control and pays off when you swing fast. Most of the confusion comes from half-truths, like "hard means power". Here are six common myths, what the sources actually say, and how to choose the right hardness for your level.

What makes a padel racket soft or hard?

Under the faces, every padel racket has a foam or rubber core. Padel USA explains that brands mainly use two materials, EVA (a rubber, the most common) and polyethylene foam (softer, less common), each made in different densities. When a racket is called soft, medium or hard, it's mostly the core density people mean, though a stiff carbon face adds to the hard feel.

Some rackets now mix densities: Padel USA mentions multi-density cores designed to balance feel on fast and slow shots.

Myth 1: hard rackets always hit harder

Truth

A hard core gives the most power on fast swings. On slow swings, a soft core gives more.

Myth

"Hard rackets are the power rackets, full stop."

Decathlon's buying guide puts it clearly: a soft core gives more rebound on slow shots and less on hard ones, while a hard core gives maximum power on hard shots. Think of a soft core as a trampoline: the ball sinks in and springs back even when you barely swing. A hard core gives the ball little help unless you bring the speed. The Padel School describes hard foam as giving "less automatic power" for the same reason.

So a hard racket only hits harder if you swing hard enough to use it. For a player who mostly blocks and places the ball, a soft racket often sends it deeper.

Myth 2: soft rackets are only for beginners

Truth

Soft cores suit anyone who values comfort, easy depth from the back or plays in the cold.

Myth

"Once you're decent, you should move to a hard racket."

Soft rackets are generally recommended for beginners, and hard ones for advanced players, as Padel USA and The Padel School both say. But that's a starting point, not a ladder. Decathlon notes soft foam helps on shots from the back of the court. Plenty of good defenders and control players stay on soft or medium cores.

Myth 3: hard rackets give you less control

Truth

Hard cores generally give more control and precision to players who swing consistently.

Myth

"Hard means wild; soft means accurate."

It's usually the other way round. The Padel School says hard foam gives more control and precision. Padel USA says hard rackets favour cleaner, more precise shots and stability. A soft core holds the ball longer, which helps a developing player, but the extra rebound can also send the ball long when you swing fully. The catch is that a hard racket's control only shows up with consistent technique.

Myth 4: a soft racket will fix your elbow

Truth

A softer core absorbs more shock and is the usual recommendation for sensitive arms, but it's one factor among several.

Myth

"Buy a soft racket and the elbow pain will go away."

Padel USA calls soft rackets the safer choice for tennis elbow because they absorb more shock, and Decathlon says soft cores reduce vibration and ease wrist strain. Justpadel also links a soft core to comfort for players with arm or elbow pain. But weight, balance, grip size, technique and how much you play all matter too. If you have pain, see our padel elbow guide and get it checked; a racket change supports recovery, it doesn't treat it.

Myth 5: a racket feels the same in every season

Truth

Cores get harder in the cold and softer in the heat.

Myth

"Hardness is fixed when the racket leaves the factory."

Padel USA says foam gets harder in cold weather and softer in heat, and NOX says materials harden in the cold and soften in the heat, so rackets feel more controlled but less powerful in winter and softer and less controllable in summer. NOX suggests intermediate-to-soft rackets for cold conditions and intermediate-to-stiff for heat, and recommends two rackets of different hardness if your seasons vary a lot. Some brands even sell summer and winter versions of the same model. Balls change too: NOX notes they lose pressure in the cold and gain it in the heat. For outdoor tips, see playing padel outdoors.

Myth 6: EVA and foam are the same thing

Truth

EVA is a rubber; "foam" usually means softer polyethylene. They feel different.

Myth

"Every core is foam, so the label doesn't matter."

Padel USA describes polyethylene as softer, more comfortable and better at absorbing vibration, but less precise and powerful than EVA. Justpadel calls foam the softest option, with a large sweet spot that helps beginners, but less durable and becoming less common. EVA itself comes in soft and hard versions: soft EVA for comfort and control, hard EVA for speed and explosiveness, at the cost of comfort.

Beginner (first year)Soft EVA or foamEasy depth on short swings, comfort, forgiveness

This follows the general pattern in Decathlon's, The Padel School's and Padel USA's guidance. Brands don't use a shared hardness scale, so one brand's "medium" can feel like another's "hard". How to test hardness on court

  • Block a volley from about two metres off the net with almost no swing. A soft racket sends it noticeably deeper.
  • Hit a full bandeja or smash. A hard racket feels more solid and goes where you aim if your contact is clean.
  • Play a defensive lob from the back glass. If you have to force it to reach the back of the court, the racket may be too hard for you.
  • Notice your arm after an hour. Extra fatigue or buzzing is a reason to go softer.

Frequently asked questions

Is a soft or hard padel racket better?

It depends on your swing. Soft cores give more rebound on slow swings and more comfort; hard cores give more control and power when you swing fast. Most beginners do better with soft, and many advanced attackers prefer hard.

Does a hard padel racket give more power?

Only with a fast swing. On slow or short swings, a soft core actually gives the ball more rebound.

Which padel racket hardness is best for tennis elbow?

Soft cores absorb more shock and are the usual recommendation, ideally with a lighter weight and lower balance. A racket change isn't treatment, so see a professional if pain persists.

Does cold weather make a padel racket harder?

Yes. Brands and retailers say cores harden in the cold and soften in the heat, so a racket feels less powerful in winter and less controlled in summer.

What is the difference between EVA and foam in a padel racket?

EVA is a rubber and the most common core; it comes in soft and hard versions. Foam usually means polyethylene, which is softer and more comfortable but less precise and generally less durable.
