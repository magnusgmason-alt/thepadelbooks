Best padel rackets for intermediate players (2026)

5 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 23 Oct 2026

Intermediate players have the widest choice of rackets, and the easiest time picking the wrong one. You're strong enough to use more power, but most of your points are still won with control, placement and patience. The right racket rewards that, rather than punishing every off-centre hit.

The short answer

Most intermediate players are best served by a teardrop or round racket with a medium balance, a medium or soft core, and a weight of roughly 355–375 g. Choose a fibreglass face for comfort, or a carbon face if your technique is solid and you want more power.

What "intermediate" means here

We mean club players who play regularly, can hold their own at the net, hit bandejas and play off the walls, and are working on consistency and tactics. On many club scales, that's roughly level 3 to 4. If you're still learning the walls, see our beginner-to-intermediate picks.

What to look for

  • Shape: teardrop is the classic intermediate choice, mixing control and power. Round still suits control players.
  • Balance: medium. A medium-high balance can work if you play mostly at the net and like to attack.
  • Face: fibreglass for comfort and touch; carbon for stiffness and power. Mixed faces sit in between.
  • Core: soft to medium. Hard cores need clean, fast swings to work well.

As a rule of thumb, softer cores and frames are kinder to the arm, while stiffer rackets reward clean technique but forgive less.

Left side or right side?

Your position in the pair can guide the choice. Players on the right (the forehand side for right-handers) often defend more and build the point, so control rackets suit them. Players on the left tend to take more overheads and finishing shots, so a little more balance in the head can help. Left-handers usually play the right side. This is a tendency, not a rule. See our guide to padel positioning for how the two sides work.

Balance in practice

Mistakes to avoid

  1. Jumping straight to an advanced diamond. You'll gain a little power on smashes and lose control on everything else.
  2. Ignoring your arm. If you've had elbow or wrist pain, stay with softer cores and medium or low balance. Read our padel elbow guide.
  3. Buying the current year only. Last year's model is often almost identical and much cheaper.

Frequently asked questions

What weight should an intermediate padel racket be?

Buying guides differ: Decathlon suggests 340–360 g, Central Sports roughly 365–375 g. Most players do well somewhere around 355–375 g. Choose the weight you can swing comfortably for a full session.

Is a teardrop racket good for intermediate players?

Yes. It's the most popular shape at this level because it mixes the control of a round racket with some of the power of a diamond.

Carbon or fibreglass for an intermediate player?

Both work. Fibreglass faces are softer and more forgiving; carbon faces are stiffer and give more power on fast swings. If in doubt, choose a mixed face or a carbon racket with a soft core.

How do I know when I'm ready for an advanced racket?

When your contact is consistent, you hit overheads with confidence and you want more power without losing control. Our intermediate-to-advanced guide covers that next step.
