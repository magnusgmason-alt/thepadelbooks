How often should you play padel? A guide to safe weekly load

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 15 Nov 2026

Padel is addictive, and many players go from once a week to five times in a single month. That's exactly when calves, elbows and knees start to complain. How often you can play depends on your fitness, age and how quickly you build up. Here's a practical guide.

General information, not medical advice

This page gives general information. It can't diagnose you or replace an assessment. If you have a health condition, a recent injury or symptoms that worry you, check with a doctor or physiotherapist.

Why sudden jumps cause injuries

Muscles, tendons and bones adapt to the load you give them, but tendons adapt slowly. A narrative review of padel injuries in the Aspetar Sports Medicine Journal lists overuse, poor conditioning and an inadequate warm-up among the main risk factors, and padel's most common problems, such as padel elbow and calf and Achilles issues, are often overuse injuries. The pattern behind them is usually the same: too much, too soon.

A sensible frequency

PlayerMatches per weekNotes
New to padel1–2Add a third after a month or two without niggles
Regular club player2–4Plus two short strength sessions
Competitive player3–5With planned easy days and strength training
Over 40 / over 602–3More recovery between matches; see padel over 40

These are rough guides, not rules. Your body's response matters more than any number.

How to increase safely

  1. Add one session at a time. Keep a new level for 2–3 weeks before adding more.
  2. Mix intensity. Not every session needs to be a hard match. Drills and relaxed games count as easier days.
  3. Watch holiday weeks. Padel holidays and tournaments are classic triggers for calf, elbow and Achilles problems.
  4. Keep strength training. Two short sessions a week make you more robust. A 2014 meta-analysis found strength training reduced sports injuries to less than a third.
  5. Take at least one full rest day. Two if you're over 40 or playing hard matches.

Warning signs of overuse

  • Pain at the start of play that "warms up" and then returns worse afterwards
  • Morning stiffness in the Achilles, heel or elbow
  • Pain that is worse each week rather than better
  • Shots feeling weaker or slower for several sessions in a row

If you notice these, cut back for a week or two rather than pushing on. Pain that persists needs a physiotherapist or doctor.

Is it OK to play padel every day?

Some experienced players do, but most recreational players will feel better and get injured less with at least one or two rest days a week. If you want to play daily, keep some sessions light (drills, relaxed games) and be alert to the warning signs above.

Frequently asked questions

How many times a week should I play padel?

For most club players, two to four times a week works well, with at least one rest day. Beginners should start with one or two sessions.

Is it OK to play padel every day?

Some experienced players manage it, but most recreational players are better off with one or two rest days a week and a mix of hard and easy sessions.

How do I avoid overuse injuries in padel?

Increase playing time gradually, mix intensities, keep up strength training, warm up properly and cut back early when you notice warning signs.

How much rest do I need between padel matches?

Most players are fine with a day between hard matches. Over 40, or after a tournament, you may need two.
