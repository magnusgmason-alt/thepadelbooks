Padel americano: rules, scoring and how to organise one

7 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 30 Oct 2026

A padel americano is a social doubles format where you play a series of short matches, change partner every round and keep your own running total of points, so the winner is the individual with the most points at the end. It's the most popular way to run a club social night, because everyone plays the whole time, nobody is knocked out, and you get to play with (and against) everyone in the group. Here is how the rules and scoring work, and how to organise one yourself.

What is padel americano?

In a normal match you pick a partner and stay with them all evening. In a padel americano you are on your own. The partners and opponents change every round, and every rally you win adds to your personal score. You might win a round with one partner and lose the next with someone else, but your points keep counting.

The schedule is set in advance so that, as far as the numbers allow, everyone partners everyone and plays against everyone. The event ends when that rotation is complete or when the court time runs out. Whoever has collected the most points wins.

No federation owns the americano. It's a club format, so details vary from venue to venue and from app to app. What follows is the version most clubs and americano apps use, with the common variations pointed out.

Americano padel rules

Inside each match you play normal padel: underarm serve, one bounce, walls in play, as in the official FIP rules. Only the scoring and the rotation change.

  1. Players in multiples of four. Four players per court: 8 players on 2 courts, 12 on 3, 16 on 4. You need at least four.
  2. New partner every round. The schedule rotates partners and opponents. With 8 players there are 7 rounds, and each player partners each of the other seven exactly once.
  3. Every rally is a point. There's no 15, 30, 40. Each rally won is one point for the pair that wins it.
  4. Play to a fixed total. The match stops when the agreed number of points has been played, for example 24. The result can be 13–11, 18–6 or even 12–12.
  5. Both players keep their pair's points. If you and your partner win 18–14, you each add 18 to your total and your opponents each add 14. You still score in a match you lose.
  6. Highest individual total wins. After the last round, rank everyone by points.
Agree the details first

Because the americano isn't an official format, announce the details before the first serve: the points total, how often the serve rotates, whether you play one or two serves, and how ties at the top are broken.

Americano scoring: how many points per match?

Clubs and apps usually offer 16, 21, 24 or 32 points per match. Some play timed rounds of 10 to 20 minutes instead and count however many points have been played when the buzzer goes.

The time estimates come from americano app publishers (PadelOS for 24 points, Elite Padel for 32) and depend on level and rally length. Treat them as a guide, not a promise.

How the serve rotates

Because there are no games, the serve can't simply change every game. The common solution is that a player serves a block of points, often four, then the serve passes to the other pair, and so on around all four players. Some apps use blocks of two or three.

That is why 16, 24 and 32 are so popular: they divide by four, so every player serves the same number of points. With 32 points and four-point blocks, each player serves twice, eight points in all. With 21 points, someone always serves more than the others.

A worked example

Eight friends book two courts. Round 1 on court 1 is Ana and Ben against Cara and Dan, playing to 24. Ana and Ben win 15–9. Ana and Ben each score 15, Cara and Dan each score 9. On court 2, Eva and Finn beat Gil and Hana 13–11.

In round 2 everyone gets a new partner: perhaps Ana with Cara, Ben with Dan. After seven rounds, each of the eight has partnered all seven others once. If Cara has 101 points and Ana 99, Cara wins, even if Ana won more of her matches.

How to organise an americano

  1. Fix the numbers. Get a multiple of four players and one court per four. If someone drops out, either find a replacement or rotate a rest round evenly so nobody sits out twice before others sit out once.
  2. Book enough court time. A full 8-player americano is 7 rounds. At around 10 to 12 minutes a round plus changeovers, plan for about one and a half to two hours. Shorten the points total if you only have 90 minutes.
  3. Generate the schedule. Use an americano app or a club booking system with americano tools, such as Playtomic's. They create the rotation and track the scores.
  4. Set the rules out loud. Points total, serve blocks, tie-break rule, and a clear "last point" signal for timed rounds.
  5. Warm up before round one. Rounds are short, so start ready. A proper ten-minute warm-up pays off in round one.
  6. Collect scores quickly. One player per court reports the result straight after each match, so the next round can start on time.

On ties, there's no standard rule: tie-breakers are set by the club. Head-to-head result and matches won are common choices. Announce yours before you start.

Americano vs mexicano

The mexicano uses the same individual scoring, but the pairings aren't fixed in advance. After a random first round, players are ranked and the next round is drawn from the standings, so the top four play together on court 1, the next four on court 2, and so on. Apps differ on the exact split within each court. Visu's generator, for example, pairs 1st and 3rd against 2nd and 4th.

Team americano

In a team americano you keep the same partner all event and only your opponents rotate. The pair's points are added up as a team total, and the pair with the most points wins. It suits people who arrive as a pair, and it's a gentle introduction to playing as a team before you try a league.

Mixed americano

A mixed americano has an equal number of men and women, and every pair on court is one man and one woman. Partners still change each round, so you play with each player of the opposite sex in turn. Scores are individual, and the event often crowns both a top-scoring man and a top-scoring woman. You need at least four of each, eight players in total.

Tips for playing an americano well

  • Every point counts. There is no "it's only 15–0". A sloppy point at 3–1 is worth exactly the same as one at 20–20.
  • Adapt quickly to new partners. Agree who takes the middle and who plays which side in the first ten seconds. See left side vs right side.
  • Keep your errors down. Short matches reward consistency. The pair that misses least usually wins.
  • Call the score. Say the score before every serve, so nobody argues at the end.

Is americano good for beginners?

Yes, as long as the group is roughly the same level. Losing a match costs you nothing except a few points, and you play a lot of padel in a short time. If you're new, look for americanos labelled with a level range, and see padel levels explained to pick the right one.

Frequently asked questions

How does padel americano scoring work?

Every rally is one point. Matches are played to a fixed total, often 16, 24 or 32 points, and each player adds their pair's score to their personal total. The highest total at the end wins.

How many players do you need for an americano?

At least four, ideally a multiple of four: 8 players on 2 courts is the classic size. With odd numbers, players take turns to rest, spread evenly.

How long does a padel americano take?

A 24-point match takes around 10 minutes and a 32-point match around 15. A full 8-player americano of 7 rounds usually fits in about one and a half to two hours.

What is the difference between americano and mexicano?

In an americano the partner rotation is fixed in advance. In a mexicano each round is drawn from the current standings, so players of a similar level end up together.

Do you play deuce or golden point in an americano?

Not in the usual points-based americano. It counts single points up to a fixed total, so there are no games and no 40–40. A few clubs run americanos with short sets instead, and then they agree a deuce rule. Deuce and golden point only matter in normal padel scoring.
