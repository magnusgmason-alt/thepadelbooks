Can I play padel with wrist pain?

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 17 Nov 2026

A wrist that aches after padel from overuse can often keep playing with a few changes. A wrist that hurt after a fall, won't move properly or has changed shape cannot. The first job is to work out which you have. Then, if it's the overuse kind, you change the load so the wrist can settle while you strengthen it.

Not medical advice

This guide explains common patterns in plain language. It can't diagnose you. If the pain is severe, came from a fall or twist, or isn't settling, get it assessed.

First: is it an injury or an overload?

The NHS says not to try to treat a possible broken wrist yourself. Contact NHS 111 (or your local equivalent) if you have:

  • severe wrist pain, or you feel faint, dizzy or sick from the pain
  • you heard a snap, grinding or popping noise when it happened
  • you can't move your wrist or hold things
  • the wrist has changed shape or colour
  • a very painful, hot or red lump on the wrist
  • you've lost feeling in part or all of your hand

A sudden, sharp pain with swelling and a pop or snap during a fall is a classic sign of a fracture. Don't play on it.

Overuse ache: the next-morning rule

Most padel wrist pain builds slowly; in one 2025 study of amateur players, 81.8% of wrist and hand injuries came on gradually. For that kind of ache, use a simple rule from tendon rehabilitation:

During padelNext morningVerdict
Little or no painNormalCarry on and keep strengthening
Moderate, up to about 5 out of 10Back to its usual levelAcceptable, don't add more playing time
Strong or rising painWorseToo much: cut back or rest it

Changes that take load off the wrist

  • Loosen the grip. Firm only around contact. Poberaj's review of padel upper-limb injuries reports that a looser grip in the follow-through reduces force at the elbow by nearly 30%, and a relaxed hand helps the wrist too.
  • No wrist flicks. Volley with a quiet wrist; make spin on the víbora with the swing path, not a snap.
  • Turn your body on overheads so the trunk, not the wrist, does the work.
  • Check the racket. Rehaboteket's physio guidance lists a heavier racket and a grip size that doesn't suit your hand among the triggers. An overgrip can build up a thin handle.
  • Play less, for now. Shorter sessions, a rest day in between, no tournaments until it settles.

Self-care between sessions

For mild wrist pain, the NHS suggests resting the wrist when you can, ice in a towel for up to 20 minutes every two to three hours, gentle exercises to keep it moving, painkillers such as paracetamol or ibuprofen gel, and a splint for support, especially at night. It also says not to grip anything too tightly or lift heavy objects, and not to use heat in the first two to three days after an injury. When it's calm, start our 6-week wrist plan, and see which support to use.

When to see a GP

The NHS says to see a GP if wrist pain stops you doing normal activities, is getting worse or keeps coming back, hasn't improved after two weeks of home treatment, or comes with tingling or loss of feeling; also if the wrist is painful, warm, swollen and stiff, or you feel unwell with a high temperature. Pain on the little-finger side that clicks or catches deserves a check too: see TFCC wrist pain.

Frequently asked questions

Can I play padel with a sore wrist?

If it's a mild overuse ache with no red flags, often yes, at reduced load. Keep pain moderate during play and make sure it settles by the next morning.

When should I not play padel with wrist pain?

After a fall with sudden pain and swelling, if you heard a snap or pop, can't move the wrist, it has changed shape or colour, or you've lost feeling in the hand.

Should I wear a wrist support for padel?

A support can ease pain short term. The NHS suggests a splint, especially at night, for mild wrist pain. It doesn't treat the cause.

Should I use ice or heat on my wrist?

The NHS suggests ice wrapped in a towel for up to 20 minutes every two to three hours, and no heat in the first two to three days after an injury.

How long should I rest my wrist from padel?

If it hasn't improved after two weeks of home treatment, see a GP rather than waiting longer.
