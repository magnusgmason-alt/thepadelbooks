What to wear for padel

5 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 30 Oct 2026

What to wear for padel is simpler than it looks: comfortable, breathable sportswear you can lunge and twist in, plus court shoes with good grip and side support. There's no uniform, and most clubs only ask for appropriate sports clothing. This guide covers the details that make a difference, from shoes and fabrics to dress codes, outfits for men and women, and dressing for heat and cold.

What to wear for padel: the quick checklist

  • A breathable sports top in a moisture-wicking fabric.
  • Shorts, a skort, leggings or a sports dress that let you lunge and crouch freely.
  • Court shoes with lateral support, ideally padel or clay-court tennis shoes. Not running shoes.
  • Sports socks, cushioned at the heel and forefoot.
  • Optional extras: a wristband, headband, cap or visor, and a light layer for cold days.

The Padel School's advice for beginners is that no special uniform is needed and any comfortable sportswear works. Freedom of movement is the key, because you'll turn, lunge and pivot constantly.

Padel clothing: what to look for

You don't need branded padel clothing, but the features that brands build in are worth knowing. The LTA's clothing guide highlights three:

  • Stretch fabrics for unrestricted movement on fast points.
  • Moisture-wicking material that keeps you dry through long rallies.
  • Breathable layers, which matter most outdoors and in warm weather.

One padel-specific detail: when you serve, you'll often want to hold or pocket the second ball. Macron's guide recommends shorts with a secure pocket for exactly this reason. Skorts and leggings with a ball pocket do the same job.

Avoid heavy cotton, which holds sweat, and anything loose enough to catch on your racket. Under the FIP rules you lose the point if the ball touches anything you're wearing, so baggy clothing doesn't help.

Padel shoes vs tennis shoes

Shoes matter more than anything else you wear. Padel is mostly side steps, split steps, sprints and lunges, usually on sand-filled artificial grass. The LTA's guide says padel shoes are designed for fast side-to-side movement, with textured herringbone or mixed-pattern soles for traction and cushioning to protect knees and ankles during quick pivots.

  • Padel shoes are the best option if you play regularly.
  • Tennis shoes are close enough to start in. The Padel School recommends padel-specific or clay-court tennis shoes; a clay-court sole with a herringbone pattern is a reasonable stand-in.
  • Running shoes are the ones to avoid. The Padel School says they don't give the right grip or stability and increase injury risk.

For indoor courts, the LTA's guide suggests non-marking, lightweight soles. Our full guide to padel shoes explains herringbone, omni and hybrid soles and how to choose between them.

Padel dress code at clubs

Padel is relaxed compared with tennis. Most clubs simply ask for sports clothing and proper court shoes. Two real examples from UK clubs:

  • Roehampton Club in London asks tennis players to wear tops at least 90% white, but for padel it asks only for sports clothing and footwear.
  • West of Scotland Padel's club rules say players must wear appropriate clothing and footwear.

That's typical. The rule that's most often enforced is footwear, because the wrong soles can damage the turf or mark indoor floors. If you're visiting a private members' club, a hotel or a tennis club with padel courts, check its website first: some traditional clubs have stricter general dress codes for their grounds.

What to wear for padel: women

Macron's guide suggests technical tops, tanks and singlets, paired with shorts, skirts with built-in shorts, leggings or padel dresses. The key is a fit that stays put when you stretch for a low ball or reach up for a smash. Practical tips:

  • A skort or dress with ball pockets saves you juggling the second ball on serve.
  • A supportive sports bra matters: padel involves constant short sprints and jumps.
  • Leggings or longer shorts are a good choice if you're worried about grazes when you stretch low near the walls.

What to wear for padel: men

Macron recommends technical T-shirts or polo shirts in moisture-wicking fabric, and shorts with a comfortable waistband, a practical length and a secure pocket for the second ball. Mid-thigh to just-above-knee shorts give the most freedom for lunges. Base-layer shorts or compression shorts underneath are a personal choice, not a requirement.

Padel in hot weather

Outdoor padel in summer, or under a hot canopy, needs a bit of planning.

  1. Dress light. Macron suggests sleeveless or short-sleeved technical tops and mesh panels for airflow.
  2. Shade your eyes. A cap or visor helps on lobs and smashes into the sun, and Padel Point notes that visors, sports sunglasses and wristbands absorb sweat and protect from the sun.
  3. Use sunscreen. The NHS recommends at least SPF 30 with at least 4-star UVA protection, reapplied every two hours and after sweating or towel drying. In the UK, it says the sun is strongest between 11am and 3pm from March to October.
  4. Book smart. Early morning or evening slots avoid the worst of the heat.
  5. Drink plenty of water and bring more than you think you'll need. Macron makes the same point.
  6. Pack a spare top and a wristband. A wristband keeps sweat off your grip, which matters more as the session goes on.

Padel in cold weather

Winter padel is very playable if you layer. The LTA's guide suggests lightweight warm-up layers in winter to keep muscles loose. Padel Point recommends thermal layers, long-sleeved shirts and long trousers in stretchy fabrics, plus a hat or neck warmer that doesn't restrict movement. Start in layers, remove them as you warm up, and put them back on between sets. A proper padel warm-up matters even more when it's cold.

Accessories worth wearing

  • Wristband and headband: Macron's guide notes a wristband keeps sweat off your hands and a headband keeps it out of your eyes.
  • Technical socks: cushioning at the heel and forefoot helps with friction and moisture on long sessions.
  • Eye protection: worth considering. See the eye-injury research in our padel equipment guide.
  • Leave jewellery at home, or keep it minimal. Rings and bracelets can catch on the racket or the wrist cord.

Frequently asked questions

What should I wear to play padel for the first time?

Comfortable, breathable sportswear and court shoes with good side support. Tennis shoes are fine to start. Avoid running shoes and heavy cotton.

Can I wear tennis shoes for padel?

Yes, especially clay-court tennis shoes with a herringbone sole. If you play regularly, padel shoes give better grip on sand-filled turf.

Is there a dress code for padel?

Usually only appropriate sports clothing and court shoes. Some traditional clubs have stricter rules for their grounds, so check before you visit.

What do women wear for padel?

Technical tops or tanks with shorts, skorts, leggings or padel dresses. Pockets for the second ball and a fit that stays put matter most.

What should I wear for padel in hot weather?

Light, sleeveless or short-sleeved technical tops, a cap or visor, sunscreen of at least SPF 30 with 4-star UVA protection, a wristband and plenty of water.
