Padel defence drills

12 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 31 Oct 2026

The best padel defence drills put you at the back of the court against a real attack and train three habits: let the glass slow the ball down, pick the reply that pushes the net pair back, and move with your partner. Below are nine drills, from defending the smash to surviving long rallies, each with a court diagram, a pass mark and a way to make it harder.

All the drills below are adapted from The Padel Drill Book: Intermediate & Advanced, reworded and shortened for the web.

How to use these padel defence drills

The pair at the net has the easier job: they hit downwards and get more chances to finish. Your job at the back is not to hit winners but to make their next ball harder than the last one.

These drills suit levels 3 to 6 on the 0–7 scale used in the book (see padel levels explained). Most need a feeder who can hit firmly: build the pace up until the ball arrives as it would in a match.

  • Stand off the glass. Coaches recommend starting a metre or a metre and a half from the back glass rather than pressed against it, so you have space to play the rebound.
  • Height and depth beat pace. A deep lob that lands beyond the net pair's service line pushes them back. A hard drive usually comes straight back at you.
  • Do both sides. Run each drill on the drive side and the revés side, and move on to the progression once you reach the pass mark twice in a row.

Drills for defending the smash

1. Glass first, then lift (D1 in the book)

Players: 3–4 · Time: 12 min · Level: 4–6

Set-up: You and your partner stand side by side about a metre and a half in front of your back glass. The feeder, holding a basket, stands about three metres back from the net on the far side.

How it works: The feeder smashes at three-quarter pace towards you. As their racket comes forward, turn sideways and take a step into the court so the rebound has space. Let the ball come off the glass and drop to a comfortable height, then lob high and deep cross-court. Your partner stays slightly behind and to the side, ready for a smash through the middle. After ten balls, swap. Then add a second player at the net and play every point out.

Pass mark: 7 lobs out of 10 bounce beyond the feeder's service line without handing them a second overhead.

Make it harder: The feeder adds the odd soft drop and some flat smashes aimed at the side glass, so you can't commit early.

2. Chase the x3 outside (D10 in the book)

Players: 2–4 · Time: 15 min · Level: 5–6

Set-up: Run this only on a court whose doors and safety area meet the FIP specifications, and only if the events you play in permit it. Our guide to out-of-court play explains the conditions. Move bags and benches well away from the exits first.

How it works: The feeder hits a por tres (x3): it bounces in your half, comes off the back glass and carries over your side wall. As soon as you see the steep bounce, run for the door on that side instead of backing towards the glass. Outside, play the ball before it bounces again and send it high back over the net, then come back in through the same door while your partner covers the court. Walk the route slowly a few times before adding pace.

The rule behind it: Where out-of-court play is authorised, a ball over the side wall or through a door stays in play until it bounces a second time or touches something outside the court; a ball over the end wall can't be played (FIP Rule 13). Without authorisation, the point is simply lost.

Pass mark: You return 4 of 8 x3s from outside the court.

Make it harder: The feeder alternates x3s with bandejas, so you have to read each ball before you commit to the door.

Drills against the net pair's attack

3. Rescue the corner pocket (D3 in the book)

Players: 2–4 · Time: 12 min · Level: 4–6

Set-up: You start on the right half, about two metres off the back glass, with your partner on the left. The feeder is at the opposite net, just left of centre, and hits sliced balls with side spin, the way a víbora arrives.

How it works: The feeds land near your side wall and run into the corner. If it skids low and will die on the side glass, move in and play it before the wall. If it sits up enough to come off the side glass around hip height, let it come out and lob cross-court. After eight balls, the feeder adds balls that reach the back glass as well, so you have two walls to read.

Pass mark: 7 of 10 replies land deep or at the feeder's feet, with nothing left stuck in the corner.

Make it harder: The feeder mixes víboras with soft balls short of the service line, so you can't stay deep.

4. Make room at the hips (D4 in the book)

Players: 2–3 · Time: 10 min · Level: 4–6

Set-up: You stand about two metres off the back glass on the right half. The feeder is two metres from the net on the far side. Your partner collects balls and calls targets.

How it works: The feeder drives flat balls at your body in a set order (right hip, left hip, chest) four times through. On every ball, shuffle out of its line so you meet it in front of you, not beside your stomach. Send hip balls back low and slow to the feeder's feet; take chest balls on the backhand and lift them into a high, deep lob. For the second round, the order is random and your partner shouts which corner to aim for.

Pass mark: 8 of 12 returned with control and none off the frame.

Make it harder: The feeder steps in by a metre, and your replies must stay lower than the net tape.

5. Low and slow from the back (D5 in the book)

Players: 4–5 · Time: 12 min · Level: 3–5

Set-up: Your pair stands 1.5–2 metres from your back glass, the opponents at the net. A fifth player, standing at the side, starts each point by feeding one of the net players; with only four, that net player starts from the hand.

How it works: The net player volleys deep to either of you. You may use the glass, but every reply has to be a soft block or a chiquita aimed low at the volleyers' feet, dropping just after the net. No lobs. Play the point out. After six points, each of you may use one lob per point, whenever you choose.

Pass mark: Your pair wins 4 of 10 points, or forces 6 volleys that have to be hit upwards.

Make it harder: The volleyers start nearer the net, which leaves less room for the low ball.

Defending as a pair and under pressure

6. Your partner's voice (D8 in the book)

Players: 3–4 · Time: 12 min · Level: 3–5

Set-up: You and your partner defend near your back glass. The feeder at the far net mixes deep balls with some that will hit your glass on the full.

How it works: Before each ball bounces, your partner gives one call: glass (let it come off the wall), out (it will hit the wall without bouncing, so leave it) or yours (take it before the wall). You act on the call alone. Under FIP Rule 13, a ball that hits your walls or fence without bouncing in your court first is a point for your side, so a correct "out" wins the point without a swing. Swap after ten feeds, then use the calls in live points.

The key read: If the ball is still climbing when it crosses your service line, expect it to strike the glass on the full. If it is already falling, it will bounce first.

Pass mark: 9 of 10 calls are right, and the player on the ball follows every one.

Make it harder: The feeder adds balls through the middle, so the caller also has to decide who takes them.

7. Alone at the back (D6 in the book)

Players: 3 · Time: 15 min · Level: 4–6

Set-up: One defender starts in the middle of the back court, about two metres off the glass, against two players at the net. The defender begins each point with a lob.

How it works: The net players may volley or play bandejas and víboras; flat, all-out smashes are banned. The defender may only lob. The defender scores whenever a lob forces the net pair to turn round and leave the ball for their own back glass; the net pair wins on any error or clean winner. Play eight points, then rotate so everyone takes a turn alone.

Pass mark: The defender wins 3 of 8 points, or sends the net pair back to their glass 4 times.

Make it harder: The net pair may smash once per point, and the defender must play it off the glass.

8. Survive ten (D9 in the book)

Players: 4–5 · Time: 15 min · Level: 4–6

Set-up: Your pair starts near your back glass, the opponents at the net. A feeder beside the net post starts each rally to one of the net players (with four, that player starts from the hand).

How it works: The net pair attacks and you defend, and your partner counts aloud each of your defensive balls that gets over the net. Before ten, you may only lob, block or reset; going for a winner costs the point. If the attackers miss before ten, you score. Ball eleven onwards is a normal rally, and you may move forward once a lob really gets past the net pair.

The key read: When you're tired, choose the safe ball: late means a high lob, balanced means low at their feet.

Pass mark: Reach ten defensive balls in 3 of 5 rallies.

Make it harder: Make it fifteen balls, or allow the net pair one smash per rally.

9. Glass first (M3 in the book)

Players: 4 · Time: 15 min · Level: 3–6

From the book's pressure-games chapter: a scoring game built on the habits above.

Set-up: A normal 2 v 2 on the full court. Your pair starts at the back, the opponents at the net, and they open each point with a deep volley.

How it works: Any ball you take before it has touched your glass may only be lobbed. Once a ball has come off your back or side glass, you may play anything: lob, chiquita, low drive or passing shot. Attack a ball that has not touched the glass and you lose the point. First to 11, then change roles.

Pass mark: In a game to 11, win at least 5 points by attacking a ball that came off your glass.

Make it harder: The side glass no longer counts; only a back-glass rebound lets you attack.

A 60-minute padel defence session

This session uses four of the drills above for a pair plus one feeder. If you have four players, the fourth joins as the second net player in the live parts.

0–8Warm-up: mini-rallies, volleys, a few lobs and some easy balls off the back glassEasy rallies

For a level 3 pair, swap the corner pocket for Your partner's voice and play Survive ten at its easier setting (six balls, attackers at three-quarter pace).

Common mistakes in padel defence

  • Standing with your back on the glass. The rebound jams you. Start a metre or more off the wall and move with the ball.
  • Hitting hard to get out of trouble. The Padel School lists hitting too hard as one of the most common tactical errors. From the back, height and placement do more than pace.
  • Short lobs. A lob that lands in front of the service line is a free smash. LTA Padel's advice is to land it beyond the opponents' service line but before their back glass, usually cross-court.
  • Defending as two individuals. If your partner is pushed back, you go back too. A diagonal gap between you is where the next ball goes.
  • Trying to win from the back too early. Patience is the theme of every drill here. HEAD's tactical advice is the same: be patient and as consistent as possible.

Once your defence holds, the next step is turning it into attack. Our padel lob drills cover the lob that lets you take the net, and the back glass guide covers the technique of playing off the wall.

Frequently asked questions

What are the best defensive drills in padel?

Drills that put you against a real attack and make you choose: glass or early, lob or low. Glass first, then lift (the smash), Low and slow from the back (volleyers) and Survive ten (long rallies) cover the three most common situations.

How do you practise defending the smash in padel?

Have a feeder smash at controlled pace while you start about a metre to a metre and a half off the back glass. Let the ball come off the glass, wait for it to drop, then lob deep cross-court. Raise the pace and mix in side-glass smashes once you can land 7 lobs out of 10 deep.

Should I lob or play low when defending?

It depends on where the net players stand: lob a pair crowding the net, and go low at the feet of a pair waiting near the service line. Drill 5 trains this read.

Can I play the ball from outside the court?

Only where out-of-court play is authorised, which FIP Rule 16 limits to courts whose safety area meets the specifications. A ball over the end wall can never be played. At many clubs it isn't authorised, so the point ends when the ball leaves the court.

Can I do padel defence drills with only two players?

Yes. Rescue the corner pocket and Make room at the hips work with one feeder and one defender. The live drills, like Survive ten and Glass first, need four players.
