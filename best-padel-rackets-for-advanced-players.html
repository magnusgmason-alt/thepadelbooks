Best padel rackets for advanced players (2026)

5 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 23 Oct 2026

At an advanced level, the racket becomes a precision tool. You hit the sweet spot almost every time, so you can choose a stiffer, more powerful frame, and small differences in balance and feel really matter. Here are five top rackets for 2026 and how to choose between them.

The short answer

Advanced players usually choose a carbon racket with a medium-firm to hard core. Pick a diamond with a high balance if you finish points with overheads, or a teardrop if you want power with more control. Most top models weigh 360–375 g.

Who these rackets are for

These are demanding rackets for players who play often, compete, and have consistent technique on every shot: volleys, bandejas, víboras and smashes. If you're still building that consistency, an advanced racket will make padel harder, not easier. Our intermediate-to-advanced picks are a better fit.

What matters at this level

  • Shape and balance define the racket's character. Diamond and high balance mean more power on overheads; teardrop and medium balance mean more control.
  • Carbon type: brands use 3K, 12K, 18K, 24K and other carbons. The number is how many thousand carbon filaments are in each strand (3K = 3,000). Higher numbers usually feel firmer, but the whole build decides the feel.
  • Core hardness: hard cores give maximum power on fast swings but less comfort; medium cores are more versatile.
  • Adjustable weights: some 2026 models let you fine-tune balance with small counterweights.

Shape for advanced players

Even at the top level, plenty of players choose a teardrop or round racket. As a general tendency, players on the right side build points, so control often wins them more matches than raw power; players on the left finish more points with overheads, so a diamond can make more sense.

Power or control? A quick self-test

  1. How do you win most of your points? If it's with overheads and smashes, lean towards power. If it's by forcing errors with placement, lean towards control.
  2. Which side do you play? Left-side players tend to want more power; right-side players tend to want control. It is a tendency, not a rule.
  3. How is your arm? Any history of elbow or wrist pain points towards a medium core and a less head-heavy balance.
  4. How often do you play? Hard, head-heavy rackets suit players who train often enough to keep their timing sharp.

Looking after a high-end racket

  • Use edge protector tape to protect the frame from scrapes on the glass and floor.
  • Keep it out of hot cars, which can affect the core and face.
  • Replace the overgrip often for a secure, consistent feel.
  • Replace the racket when it loses its response. Central Sports suggests roughly every 12–24 months of regular play.

Frequently asked questions

What weight do advanced players use?

Most advanced rackets weigh around 360–375 g. Guides like Decathlon's suggest over 360 g for advanced players, but the right weight depends on your strength and swing speed.

Is a harder core always better for advanced players?

No. Hard cores give more power on fast shots, but less on slow ones and less comfort. Many advanced players prefer a medium core for versatility.

Do pros use the same rackets you can buy?

Brands sell signature models named after their players, but professional players may use customised versions. Treat the shop model as the same design, not necessarily an identical racket.

Should I buy the 2027 model?

Only if the changes matter to you. The 2026 versions of many top rackets are now discounted and remain excellent.
