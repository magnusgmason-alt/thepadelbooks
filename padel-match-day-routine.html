Padel match preparation: your match day routine, hour by hour

6 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 8 Nov 2026

Good padel match preparation starts the night before, not in the car park: a packed bag, a proper night's sleep, a meal timed so you're fuelled but not full, an early arrival, a real warm-up and a two-minute plan with your partner. Here's a timed routine you can follow for a league match or a tournament, with what the evidence says about each step.

The times below assume a match at around midday. Shift them for your own start time. They are a sensible template, not a rule; if something already works for you, keep it.

The night before

Pack everything

The Padel School's preparation advice starts with the bag: a main racket and a backup, wristband, headband, towel, spare overgrips, balls if you need them, extra socks and shoes you have checked. Packing early shows you what's missing while there is still time to fix it. Our padel bag checklist has the full list.

Sleep

The same advice recommends avoiding late nights and aiming for at least eight hours of sleep. There's some evidence that more sleep helps racket-sport performance: in a small study of five Stanford women's tennis players, reported by the American Academy of Sleep Medicine, extending sleep for several weeks was linked to more accurate serves and faster sprints. It was a tiny, preliminary study, but the direction is clear and the cost is zero. One bad night before a match is common and not a disaster; it is the weeks of short sleep that matter most.

The morning

Keep it calm. The Padel School suggests avoiding work meetings and other commitments that morning if you can. Don't do a hard training session; a short walk or some mobility work is plenty. Drink normally through the morning: the American College of Sports Medicine's position stand on fluid replacement recommends starting exercise properly hydrated, with the amount individualised to you.

What to eat before a padel match, and when

For sessions over an hour, the US Department of Defense's Human Performance Resource Center (HPRC) suggests 1 to 4 grams of carbohydrate per kilogram of body weight, 1 to 4 hours before exercise, with more digestion time for bigger meals. If you only have 30 to 60 minutes, it suggests a small carbohydrate-rich snack of about 200 to 300 calories.

In practice: a familiar carbohydrate-based meal such as porridge, rice or pasta with something light, three or four hours before; then a banana or similar if you're hungry closer to the start. Match day is not the day to try a new food. Our guide on what to eat before padel has meal ideas and timings.

Arrival

Aim to arrive about 45 minutes before your match. The Padel School recommends leaving a time margin and arriving early to sort out the details, and there are always details: parking, check-in, which court, a toilet queue, a delay on the schedule. Under FIP Rule 2, a pair that is not ready 10 minutes after the scheduled start can lose by walkover, so lateness is a real risk in competitions.

If you're playing outdoors, use the time to look at the court: where the sun is, which way the wind blows. Our outdoor padel cheat sheet covers the adjustments.

The warm-up

The on-court courtesy rally is only 3 minutes (FIP Rule 2), and you will spend it feeling the balls, not warming your hamstrings. So warm up before you step on court:

MinutesBlockExamples
0–5RaiseEasy jog, side steps, skips
5–10MobiliseShoulder circles, trunk rotations, walking lunges with a twist, ankle and wrist circles
10–15ActivateSquats, calf raises, split steps, short accelerations and stops
15–20RehearseShadow swings: volleys, bandejas, a few serves; easy hitting if a court is free

The two-minute plan with your partner

Five to ten minutes before you go on, agree on:

  1. Sides and the middle. Who plays where, who takes the middle and lobs down the centre.
  2. The golden point. If you're playing it, who receives.
  3. Two patterns. For example, "lob when they're both at the net" and "everything through the middle when in doubt".
  4. Calls and signals. The words and the serve signals you'll use. See our padel communication cheat sheet.
  5. One process goal each. "First serve in", "split step on every hit". Something you control.

On court: the first points

Use the 3-minute rally to check the bounce off the back glass, the light and the speed of the balls. Hit a few overheads; you'll want them later. Then settle into your between-point routine from the very first point. The Padel School's mental toughness advice is to use a breath, eye contact with your partner or a short cue word to reset between points. Our padel mental game guide has a simple 20-second routine.

Between matches at a tournament

  • Refuel soon after you come off: water and some carbohydrate, especially if you have another match the same day.
  • Stay warm: put a layer on, keep moving lightly, and do a shorter warm-up before the next match.
  • Review briefly: one thing that worked, one change. Then switch off until the next warm-up.

Build your own routine

A routine only helps if it's familiar, so rehearse it before ordinary league matches, not just finals. Keep the same warm-up, meal timing and partner plan for a few weeks, then adjust one thing at a time. Frequently asked questions

How should I prepare for a padel match?

Pack the night before, sleep well, eat a familiar carbohydrate-based meal 3 to 4 hours before, arrive about 45 minutes early, warm up for 15 to 20 minutes off court and agree a simple plan with your partner.

What should I eat before a padel match?

A familiar meal that is mostly carbohydrate, 1 to 4 hours before, then a small snack of about 200 to 300 calories in the last hour if you're hungry. Avoid new foods on match day.

How early should I arrive for a padel match?

About 45 minutes for a competition, enough to check in, find the court and warm up properly. In FIP-rules events, a pair not ready 10 minutes after the start time can lose by walkover.

Is the 3-minute rally enough of a warm-up?

No. It's for getting used to the court and balls. Do 15 to 20 minutes of movement, mobility and shadow swings before you step on court.

What should we discuss as a pair before a match?

Sides, who takes the middle, who receives on golden point, two simple patterns, your calls and serve signals, and one process goal each.
