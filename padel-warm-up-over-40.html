Padel warm-up over 40: a 12-minute routine

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 17 Nov 2026

Three minutes of gentle rallying is how most club players warm up, and after 40 it's the easiest injury risk to fix. Calves, hips and shoulders need more preparation for the first explosive sprint or smash than they did at 25. This routine takes 12 minutes and needs nothing more than a resistance band.

Not medical advice

This guide explains common patterns in plain language. It can't diagnose you. If the pain is severe, came from a fall or twist, or isn't settling, get it assessed.

Why a longer warm-up after 40?

The Aspetar review of padel injuries lists inadequate warm-up among the sport's injury risk factors, and the injuries that rise with age, calf tears and muscle strains, are exactly the ones a cold start provokes. The Aspetar authors note muscle strains are more common in players over 35, and that "tennis leg" is most common in poorly conditioned men over 40. There's no study on the ideal warm-up length by age, so the extra minutes here are coaching advice, but they cost you nothing.

The 12-minute routine

MinutesWhat
0–3Easy jog and side shuffle around the court, gradually faster
3–5Leg swings, front-back and side-to-side, 10 each · Walking lunge with rotation, 6 each side
5–7Mini-band glute activation · 10 calf raises · ankle bounces
7–9Arm circles and band pull-apart · band external rotation 10 each side
9–10Skips · shadow swings, including overheads
10–12Split steps and sprint starts at 50%, 70%, then 90%

Then rally from the service line, then the back, and play a few practice overheads before the first point. Our workout builder has the "10-minute warm-up" set as a printable PDF.

Short on time? The 4-minute version

  1. One minute of jogging and side shuffles.
  2. Ten calf raises and ten ankle bounces.
  3. Six walking lunges with rotation each side.
  4. Arm circles and ten band pull-aparts.
  5. Five split steps into short sprints at increasing speed.

Do this even if your partner is already rallying; it's the calves and the first sprint you're protecting.

Warm-ups don't replace strength

A warm-up prepares the body you have; strength training changes it. A 2014 meta-analysis of 25 trials (Lauersen and colleagues) found strength training reduced sports injuries to less than a third. Pair this routine with our twice-a-week workout.

After the match

Research suggests a cool-down doesn't reliably reduce next-day soreness, but a few minutes of walking and gentle stretches help you leave the court relaxed. Our cool-down stretches have a short routine. If something felt tight during the warm-up, especially the calf, don't test it with a flat-out sprint; ease into the match or sit it out.

Check first if you need to

If you've been inactive for a long time or have a heart, joint or other medical condition, check with your doctor before ramping up. Stop and get urgent help for chest pain, severe breathlessness or feeling faint during exercise.

Frequently asked questions

How long should I warm up for padel over 40?

About 10 to 12 minutes is a sensible target. Even a 4-minute routine is far better than rallying alone.

What should a padel warm-up include?

Gentle jogging and shuffling, hip and leg mobility, calf raises, shoulder band work, shadow swings, then split steps and short sprints at rising speed.

Why do older players need a longer warm-up?

The injuries that rise with age, such as calf tears and muscle strains, are the ones a cold start provokes. The extra minutes are coaching advice rather than a research finding.

Is rallying enough of a warm-up?

Not really. It rarely prepares the calves, hips and shoulders for the first sprint or smash.

Should I stretch before padel?

Use movement-based drills like leg swings and walking lunges before playing, and keep longer static stretches for after.
