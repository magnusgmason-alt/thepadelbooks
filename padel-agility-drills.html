Padel agility drills

11 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 31 Oct 2026

The most useful padel agility drills copy what a real point asks of your legs: a small split step, a short burst, a balanced stop, the shot, and an instant move back into position. Below are ten on-court drills that train exactly that, from ladder and cone work to volley recovery and sprint intervals, each with a set-up, a target score and a way to make it harder.

All ten drills are adapted from The Padel Drill Book, mostly from its fitness and footwork chapter. We have rewritten and shortened them for the web; the book has the full version of each, with a court diagram and a score sheet.

Why padel agility drills should be short

Padel is a stop-start sport. A 2023 review of the research in Frontiers in Sports and Active Living found that points in professional men's padel last roughly 9 to 13 seconds on average across studies, with longer points in women's padel, and that players work at around 70 to 80% of their maximum heart rate. The FIP rules allow a maximum of 20 seconds between points (Rule 2).

So the movement you need is short, sharp and repeated: a few quick steps, a stop, a shot, a recovery, then a brief pause. Long, steady runs build general fitness but they don't train that pattern. The drills below keep every effort short and finish most of them with a shadow shot or a real ball, because on court footwork always ends in a hit.

How to use these drills

  • Warm up first. Ten minutes of easy movement before any sprinting or jumping. Our padel warm-up has a full routine.
  • Quality beats tiredness. Stop a set when your feet get sloppy. An agility drill done tired trains slow habits.
  • Face the net. Almost every drill here keeps your chest towards the net, as in a match.
  • Count something. Each drill has a target. Write it down and try to beat it next week.
  • Fresh legs. The book's advice is to do this work on a rest day or before a light technique session, not straight after a hard match.

Looking for strength and gym work instead? That lives in our guide to padel exercises. This page is about movement on the court itself.

Padel speed drills: first step and footwork

1. Agility ladder for padel

Players: 1 · Time: 10 minutes · Level: 1–6

Set-up: A flat agility ladder on the floor, running from near your back glass towards the net.

How it works: Run three patterns, three passes each. First, two feet in every square. Second, side-on to the ladder: step in with both feet, then out with both, travelling sideways (do both directions). Third, the "icky shuffle": two steps into a square and one out to the side, moving forwards. At the end of every pass, split-step and shadow a volley.

Goal: Nine clean passes out of ten without touching a rung. Eyes up, as if you were watching the ball.

Progression: A partner feeds a real volley at the end of each pass.

2. Cone star drill

Players: 1 · Time: 10 minutes · Level: 1–6

Set-up: One cone in the middle of your half and four cones in a square around it, each about 3.5 metres away.

How it works: Begin in a ready stance on the middle cone. Go to the front-right cone and back, then back left, front left and back right, returning to the middle after each. Shadow the shot that belongs there: a volley at the front cones, a ball off the glass at the back cones. For the rear cones, turn your hips; don't turn your back on the net. Five full stars with 40 seconds' rest between them.

Goal: One full star in under 20 seconds.

Progression: A partner points at any cone they like, so you react instead of following a pattern.

3. Lateral shuffle shadow

Players: 1 · Time: 8 minutes · Level: 1–3

Set-up: Two cones about seven metres apart, in a line parallel to the net and about 2.5 metres back from it, roughly where you volley from.

How it works: Shuffle sideways to one cone without crossing your feet and shadow a volley. Then shuffle across and shadow the opposite wing at the far cone. Work for 30 seconds, rest for 30, six sets. Keep your hips low so your head stays at the same height; every time you pop up, you lose time.

Goal: Twelve cone touches in 30 seconds.

Progression: Add a split step halfway across on every pass.

4. Braking and stopping

Players: 1 · Time: 8 minutes · Level: 2–6

Set-up: A cone near the net. Start at the back of the court.

How it works: Accelerate to the cone and stop within two or three short steps: low, knees bent, weight on the front of your feet, the last step slightly wider than the others. Hold a hitting stance, completely still, for two seconds, then walk back. Eight repetitions.

Goal: Eight clean stops out of eight, with no extra step to catch your balance.

Progression: Shadow a volley the moment you stop. The Padel School sums up the principle as arriving early and setting your feet before you hit; this drill trains the "set" part.

Padel agility drills with a ball

5. Split-step timing

Players: 2 · Time: 8 minutes · Level: 1–4

Set-up: You at the net with your racket up; a partner on the other side with a basket of balls.

How it works: As the feeder's racket meets the ball, make a small hop, only a couple of centimetres off the ground, and land on the balls of your feet. Then push off and volley the ball back to the feeder. Four sets of 15. The Padel School puts the timing simply: land the split step just as your opponent makes contact. Early and late are the same mistake.

Goal: Twelve of 15 split steps on time, judged by the feeder.

Progression: The feeder hides the direction until the last moment. To make it easier, the feeder calls the side before each ball.

6. Mirror drill

Players: 2 · Time: 8 minutes · Level: 1–4

Set-up: Two players facing each other across the net, each about two metres back.

How it works: The leader moves in short bursts: left, right, forward, back. The follower stays directly opposite. Sets of 30 seconds, swapping the leader each set, six sets in total. Watch the leader's hips rather than the feet, because the hips move first.

Goal: The follower stays within one step of the leader in five sets out of six.

Progression: The leader shadows a shot at the end of each burst and the follower copies the same shot.

7. Volley recovery step

Players: 2 · Time: 10 minutes · Level: 2–4

Set-up: A cone in the centre of your half, about 2.5 metres from the net. You start on it; the feeder stands opposite.

How it works: The feeder alternates balls out towards your forehand side wall and your backhand side wall. Cross over to reach each one, volley it deep, then shuffle back to the cone before the next feed. Four sets of ten. Push back off your outside foot: the first recovery step is the one that matters.

Goal: Touch the cone between every ball in all four sets.

Progression: The feeder plays the next ball as soon as you leave the cone, not when you arrive back.

8. Alternating wall rally

Players: 1 · Time: 10 minutes · Level: 2–4

Set-up: Stand in the middle of the court, about four metres from your back glass.

How it works: Play a forehand into the glass on your right, then a backhand into the glass on your left, and keep alternating. After each shot, shuffle back to the middle. Split-step as the ball hits the glass, then move. Four sets of 90 seconds with 30 seconds' rest. If you find yourself reaching with the arm, you started moving too late.

Goal: Sixteen alternating shots in a row.

Progression: Every fourth ball, volley it against the glass before it bounces. More solo ideas are in our solo padel drills.

Padel fitness and conditioning drills on court

9. Overhead jump and recover

Players: 1 · Time: 8 minutes · Level: 3–6

Set-up: One cone at your volley position and one a few metres behind it.

How it works: From the net cone, move back side-on to the rear cone. Jump off your back foot and shadow a smash, scissoring your legs in the air. Land on your front foot, balanced, and move straight back to the net cone. Three sets of six. The overhead isn't the end of the point, so the recovery is part of the rep.

Goal: Sixteen balanced landings out of 18.

Progression: A feeder sends a real lob. Our padel smash guide covers when a jump smash is the right shot.

10. Court-sprint intervals

Players: 1 · Time: 10 minutes · Level: 2–6

Set-up: Start touching your back glass. A phone timer on the bench.

How it works: Sprint to the net and back, then rest for 20 seconds, the same as the longest gap the rules allow between points. Eight sprints make a set; do two sets with two minutes between them. Stay fast but relaxed, and pace yourself so the last sprint matches the first.

Goal: Time sprint one and sprint eight. Aim for less than half a second between them.

Progression: Add a split step and a shadow volley at the net on every sprint. To make it easier, rest 40 seconds.

Common mistakes in padel agility training

  • Looking at your feet. On court your eyes are on the ball. Train the same way: eyes forward, even on the ladder.
  • Crossing your feet on short moves. Shuffle for small adjustments. The Padel School lists unnecessary crossed feet and too many steps among the signs of inefficient movement. Save the crossover for a ball that is genuinely wide.
  • A big split step. It's a small hop, not a jump. A big one leaves you in the air when the ball arrives.
  • Stopping on straight legs. Brake low, with bent knees and short steps.
  • Going flat out on the first rep. If sprint eight is much slower than sprint one, you trained fatigue, not speed.
  • Training like a runner. Long jogs have their place for general fitness, but points are short bursts. Keep the work short and the rest real.

Frequently asked questions

What are the best padel agility drills?

Drills that combine a split step, a short burst, a balanced stop and a shot: split-step timing, the cone star, the volley recovery step and short court sprints are a good core. Drills that end with a real or shadow shot transfer best to matches.

How can I get faster on a padel court?

Work on your first step and your timing rather than top speed. Time your split step with the opponent's contact, move in short steps, stop in balance and recover straight after each shot. Short intervals with proper rest train that better than long runs.

Do I need an agility ladder for padel?

No. A ladder helps with foot speed, but most drills here need only a few cones or flat markers, and the mirror and split-step drills need nothing at all.

How often should I do padel agility drills?

The Padel Drill Book suggests two short fitness blocks of about 15 minutes a week, and a cool-down at the end of each session. Do them on fresh legs, not straight after a hard match.

Are agility drills the same as padel exercises?

Not quite. Agility drills train movement on court. Strength, mobility and gym work are covered in our padel exercises guide, and the two work well side by side.
