Padel history: from an Acapulco garden to a global sport

7 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 30 Oct 2026

Padel history begins in 1969 in Acapulco, Mexico, where Enrique Corcuera built a small, walled court at his home because he didn't have room for a tennis court, according to the International Padel Federation (FIP). From there it travelled to Spain and Argentina in the 1970s, became a professional sport in the 2000s and is now played in well over 100 countries. Here's the full story, with the dates that matter.

Who invented padel?

Enrique Corcuera, a Mexican businessman, is credited with inventing padel. According to the FIP, he built the first court in 1969 at his house in Las Brisas, Acapulco. He wanted to play a racket sport at home but didn't have space for a full tennis court, so he built a smaller one and enclosed it with walls. The FIP says his wife, Viviana, wrote the first rules.

Not every account agrees on the year. A history published in the Aspetar Sports Medicine Journal gives 1968, in what it calls the unofficial story of the first court; the FIP and the LTA both use 1969. Either way, the walls were the key idea. They kept the ball in play on a small plot of land, and they're still what makes padel different from every other racket sport.

Padel origin: what came before Acapulco

Corcuera didn't invent the idea of a small-court racket game from nothing. The LTA traces similar games back to the 19th century, when passengers on British cruise ships played a version with tennis rackets, and to American paddle games that the LTA says became popular in Washington and New York from the 1910s, such as paddle tennis and, later, platform tennis. These are relatives rather than direct ancestors, and they're the reason padel and paddle tennis are still confused today. Our guide to paddle tennis vs padel explains the difference.

How padel came to Spain

Spain's link to padel starts with Prince Alfonso of Hohenlohe, who had visited Corcuera in Acapulco. He took the game home and built Spain's first two courts at the Marbella Club on the Costa del Sol. The FIP places this in the early 1970s; the LTA dates padel's arrival in Spain to 1974. From there it spread through Spain over the following decades.

Growth was gradual at first. The Aspetar history describes Spain's real breakthrough as coming towards the end of the 1990s. Today the LTA describes padel as Spain's second most popular sport, with an estimated six million active players and more than 20,000 courts. That's also why so much padel vocabulary is Spanish, from the bandeja to the víbora.

How padel came to Argentina

Around the same time, Julio Menditeguy, an Argentine who played at Alfonso's club in Marbella, introduced padel to Argentina. The FIP says the first courts there were built at Club Tortugas and the Mar del Plata Ocean Club. Spelling of his name varies between sources.

Argentina became padel's first great powerhouse. The world's first national padel association, the Asociación Pádel Argentino, was founded there in 1988, according to the FIP. A year later, Mar del Plata coach Jorge Galeotti introduced the first detachable, transportable glass court, which made it possible to stage padel in arenas and take it to new countries.

Rules, federations and the name

The FIP was founded in Madrid on 12 July 1991 by the Argentine, Spanish and Uruguayan associations. The first World Championships followed in 1992. In 1997, Spain and Argentina met in Barcelona to unify the rules and standardise the name "padel". The FIP still publishes the official rules of padel, including the 20 by 10 metre court and the underarm serve. By the end of the 1990s there were 11 national federations; the FIP now lists around 100.

From World Padel Tour to Premier Padel

Professional padel began with the Padel Pro Tour in 2005, which ran for eight seasons. In 2013 it was replaced by the World Padel Tour (WPT), which became the main professional circuit for the next decade.

In 2022, Qatar Sports Investments (QSI) partnered with the FIP to create Premier Padel as a rival tour. Its first Major, the Qatar Major, began in Doha on 28 March 2022. For two seasons the sport had two competing tours. That ended in August 2023, when QSI announced it had acquired the World Padel Tour. The WPT played out its 2023 schedule, and from 2024 the two tours combined under the Premier Padel name, run by QSI and governed by the FIP.

Premier Padel's 2026 calendar, announced in December 2025, had 26 tournaments in 18 countries, including Majors in Doha, Rome and Paris, a Mexico Major in Acapulco, where the sport began, and season-ending Finals in Barcelona. In March 2026 the Qatar Major in Doha was postponed because of the situation in the wider region. Our guide to Premier Padel explains the tournament categories and rankings.

Padel growth today

Padel is often called the world's fastest-growing sport, but the numbers depend heavily on who's counting and how they define a player. Treat these as estimates:

  • Playtomic and Strategy&: the Global Padel Report 2026 counts about 19.4 million players and 58,334 courts worldwide, with 7,898 new courts built in 2025.
  • FIP history page: around 35 million amateur players, in more than 150 countries, with 100 affiliated federations.
  • FIP World Padel Report 2026: about 40 million amateur players in 191 countries and territories, with women making up 47% of participants.
  • Great Britain: the LTA's history page says the number of courts has grown from 51 to 760 since 2020.

The gap between 19 million and 40 million shows how uncertain the counting is. What every source agrees on is the direction: court building and participation have grown very quickly over the past decade. For more on the sport today, see what is padel.

Is padel in the Olympics?

No. As of October 2026, padel is not an Olympic sport. It isn't among the five additional sports the IOC approved for the Los Angeles 2028 Games in October 2023: baseball and softball, cricket, flag football, lacrosse and squash. In October 2026 the FIP declared 20 October World Padel Day and renewed its campaign for padel to be considered for the Brisbane 2032 Olympics.

Padel has reached other multi-sport events. It was added to the programme of the 2026 Asian Games in Aichi-Nagoya, Japan, in November 2025 and confirmed as a medal sport in March 2026, and the first Asian Games padel medals were won in October 2026. Whether that leads to an Olympic place is still an open question.

Frequently asked questions

Who invented padel?

Enrique Corcuera, who built the first court at his home in Acapulco, Mexico, in 1969, according to the FIP. Some accounts give 1968.

Where did padel originate?

In Acapulco, Mexico. It then spread to Spain, through Alfonso of Hohenlohe's courts at the Marbella Club, and to Argentina, through Julio Menditeguy, in the 1970s.

Why is padel so popular in Spain?

Spain was one of the first countries to adopt padel, through the Marbella Club in the 1970s, and it grew steadily until it boomed in the late 1990s. The LTA now describes it as Spain's second most popular sport.

What happened to the World Padel Tour?

Qatar Sports Investments acquired it in 2023. After its 2023 season, it was merged into Premier Padel, which has been the single main professional tour since 2024.

Is padel an Olympic sport?

Not as of October 2026. It isn't on the programme for Los Angeles 2028, and the FIP is campaigning for inclusion at Brisbane 2032. Padel was a medal sport at the 2026 Asian Games.
