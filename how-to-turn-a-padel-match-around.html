How to come back in padel: the turnaround checklist

6 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 8 Nov 2026

To come back in padel, first work out whether you're losing to their good play or your own errors, then change one thing at a time: slow the ball down, lob more, target the weaker opponent and keep it simple on the big points. Panic and random winners rarely turn a match. A short plan, used at every change of ends, often does. Print this checklist or keep it on your phone for your next match.

How to come back in padel: diagnose first

All For Padel's coaching advice on bad patches starts with a question: is the problem the opponents, or your own mistakes? The fix is different for each, so answer it honestly at the next change of ends.

  • Are we losing points to their winners, or to our unforced errors?
  • Is one of us struggling more than the other?
  • Are they at the net most of the time?
  • Is one opponent clearly doing the damage?
  • Are we rushing between points?

Two or three honest answers are enough to choose a plan below.

If your errors are the problem

All For Padel says most unforced errors come from poor preparation or positioning before the shot, not from the contact, and names relaxing too much and lack of movement as the commonest causes.

  • Move your feet on every ball, including the easy ones.
  • Prepare the racket earlier, especially off the back glass.
  • Aim bigger: higher over the net, further inside the lines, more through the middle.
  • Pick one simple pattern, such as playing everything to one opponent, to give your mind a job. All For Padel suggests exactly this to recover concentration.
  • If only one of you is struggling, play more cross-court balls to the opponent in front of you, so your partner gets fewer balls and time to recover.

If the opponents are simply playing better

  • Change the pace. Padel Browser's advice against stronger pairs is to stop matching their pace: higher, deeper, slower balls with more margin, so they have to create the speed. The Padel School recommends slow balls as a way to break a hard hitter's rhythm.
  • Lob more, and lob deep. All For Padel recommends high lobs to unsettle opponents. A deep lob is also how you take the net away from them.
  • Use the conditions. Outdoors, lob into the sun or with the wind, as All For Padel suggests. See playing padel in wind and sun.
  • Slow down the start of each point. All For Padel's tip for both serving and receiving. A more careful serve and a controlled return take away their easy first attack.
  • Target the weaker opponent. Padel Browser suggests sending about 70% of the ball to the weaker player rather than all of it, and giving that plan a full set before abandoning it.

Use every second the rules give you

You're allowed time to think. Use it, but don't abuse it: the FIP rules set the limits.

BreakMaximum (FIP Rule 2)What to do with it
Between points20 secondsTurn away, breathe, agree the next serve or return target in a few words
Change of ends90 secondsDrink, then one diagnosis and one change
After the first game of a setNo rest: play is continuousSwap ends and get straight on with it
End of a set120 secondsChoose the plan, and the sides, for the next set

You change ends after the first, third and every following odd game of a set (FIP Rule 5); our change of ends guide has the details. Remember too that the server must wait until the receiver is ready, so as the receiving pair you can take a moment to settle, within reason.

Can you switch sides to turn it around?

Not in the middle of a set, if you mean who receives on which side. FIP Rule 8.2 says the receiving pair decides who receives first in the first game of each set, and that player receives the first serve of every game in the set; Rule 8.3 keeps the receiving order fixed for the set. The serving order is also set before each set (Rule 6.8). So swapping who plays the right and left as receiver can only happen at the start of a new set.

What you can change within a set:

  • Your serving formation, for example switching to the Australian formation, since the server's partner may stand anywhere (Rule 3).
  • Who covers the middle and the lobs during points.
  • Where you stand to receive: the receiver's position isn't fixed by the rules, as long as the serve bounces before you play it.

Simple comeback plans

Pick one. Run it for at least three games before judging it.

PlanUse it whenWhat you do
Slow and deepThey're out-hitting youHigh, deep balls and lobs only; no drives from the back
Take the netThey're camped at the netLob deep cross-court, follow it in together, bandeja to stay
One targetOne opponent is clearly weakerMost balls to that player, including lobs and returns
Margin firstYour errors are the problemMiddle of the court, middle of the net, no winners from the back

The big points checklist

Comebacks are made on a few points: break points, 40-40 and the first points of a new set. Padel Browser suggests playing for the break rather than winners, and owning the deciding point with the safe version: first serve in, deep central return, no heroics. With golden point scoring, that single deuce point decides the game, so have your plan ready before you reach it.

  1. Agree the serve or return target before you step up.
  2. First serve in, with placement rather than pace.
  3. Return deep through the middle.
  4. Lob if they rush the net.
  5. Let them hit the risky shot.

Keep your heads

Padel Browser's last piece of advice is easy to forget: at changes of ends give one positive, forward-looking instruction, not a post-mortem. Frequently asked questions

How do you come back from losing in padel?

Work out whether the problem is their good play or your errors, then change one thing: slow the pace, lob more, target the weaker opponent or simply aim with more margin. Give the new plan a few games before you judge it.

Can you change sides in the middle of a padel set?

Not as receivers: the receiving order is fixed for the whole set (FIP Rules 8.2 and 8.3). You can change it at the start of the next set, and you can change serving formation or positions during points at any time.

How long can you take between points in padel?

Up to 20 seconds under FIP Rule 2. Changes of ends allow up to 90 seconds, and the break at the end of a set up to 120 seconds.

Should I hit harder when I'm losing?

Usually not. Hitting harder adds errors, and the walls bring hard shots back. Slower, higher and deeper balls tend to work better against a pair who are beating you.

Is it fair to target the weaker player when losing?

Yes. It's a normal tactic at every level. Coaches suggest sending most, not all, of the ball to the weaker player so the opponents can't simply adjust.
