Padel drills for 2 players

6 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 23 Oct 2026

Padel drills for 2 players are the most useful drills you can do, because one partner is all you need to work on every shot in the game. Here are eight partner drills, from cross-court rallies to back-glass feeding and half-court games, each with a clear goal and a way to make it harder.

Why padel drills for 2 players work so well

A match gives you a handful of chances to practise any one shot. A partner drill gives you dozens in ten minutes. With two players you can rally, feed and play competitive games, and you can isolate one thing at a time: a volley, a lob, a ball off the glass.

Two habits make partner drills far more effective:

  • Take turns as the feeder. A good feed is a skill. Feed the ball you'd want to receive, at a pace your partner can control.
  • Agree a score before you start. A target or a game keeps both players focused and makes it obvious when to move on.

Warm up first with a few minutes of easy rallying; our padel warm-up guide has a routine you can run together.

Rally drills with a partner

1. Cross-court counter

Players: 2 · Time: 10 minutes

Setup: Both players at the back, diagonally opposite, each in their own half of the court.

How it works: Rally cross-court only, letting the ball bounce and using the glass whenever it gets past you. Cross-court is the percentage shot because it passes over the lower centre of the net and gives you the longest court to land in.

Goal: A team target of 30 shots, then 50. Start again from zero after any error.

Progression: Every fifth shot must be a lob. The other player lets it bounce and plays it after the glass.

2. Ladder rally, net against back

Players: 2 · Time: 10 minutes

Setup: Half the court. One player at the net, one at the back.

How it works: Rally to a target of two shots, then three, then four. A mistake sends you back to two. The LTA suggests exactly this sort of ladder, with the net-and-back version as the harder step.

Goal: Reach twelve. Swap positions each time you complete a ladder or after five minutes.

Progression: The net player must volley every ball deep, past the service line, so the back player gets practice off the glass.

Net drills with a partner

3. Volley to volley

Players: 2 · Time: 8 minutes

Setup: Both at the net, about two metres back, on the same half of the court.

How it works: Keep a volley rally going with short, compact swings and no winners. Split-step each time your partner hits, so you are ready to move either way.

Goal: Count consecutive volleys as a team. Twenty is good; fifty is excellent.

Progression: One player plays every volley to the other's feet. The receiver must keep it low and back over the net.

4. Feet and fence

Players: 2 · Time: 10 minutes

Setup: One player at the net, the other at the back with a basket of balls.

How it works: The back player feeds a low ball. The net player volleys it deep into the back corners, then volleys the reply to the feet. The idea is to practise the two volleys you need most: deep to push them back, low to stop them coming forward.

Goal: Ten feeds per set. Score one point for a deep volley that reaches the back glass after bouncing, and one for a reply volley that lands before the service line.

Progression: The back player stops feeding and plays the point out after the second volley.

Back-glass and overhead drills

5. Glass and back

Players: 2 · Time: 12 minutes

Setup: The feeder stands at the service line on the same side as the hitter, facing the back glass. The hitter starts a couple of metres in front of the glass.

How it works: The feeder sends slow, deep balls that bounce and hit the back glass. The hitter turns, lets the ball rebound, and returns it to the feeder. The LTA's version of this drill starts by allowing an extra bounce after the glass, then moves to hitting the ball straight after the rebound. Mix forehand and backhand feeds so the hitter can't guess.

Goal: Fifteen controlled returns out of twenty.

Progression: Feed into the corner so the ball comes off the back glass and side glass. Read our back glass guide for the movement: a small step back as the ball passes, then a step forward to hit.

6. Lob and bandeja

Players: 2 · Time: 15 minutes

Setup: One player at the back with a basket, the other at the net.

How it works: The back player lobs. The net player turns side-on, plays a bandeja deep into the court, then moves straight back towards the net. Rotate after fifteen lobs.

Goal: Count bandejas that land beyond the service line and leave the hitter back in position before the next feed.

Progression: Let the net player choose: bandeja on a deep lob, smash on a short one. Add the víbora once the bandeja is reliable.

Competitive games for two

7. Half-court singles

Players: 2 · Time: 15 minutes

Setup: Play only in one half of the court, split lengthways down the centre line.

How it works: Normal padel points, except that you serve straight ahead into the service box opposite you, since the diagonal box is in the other half. Walls count as normal. Add the LTA's yellow card, red card rule if you like: the first mistake in a game is a yellow card, and a second mistake in the same game is a red card that loses it.

Goal: First to six games.

Progression: The server must follow the serve to the net. The receiver wins a bonus point for any lob that forces the server back.

8. Net challenge

Players: 2 · Time: 10 minutes

Setup: Half-court. One player starts at the net, the other at the back.

How it works: The back player starts every point with a feed. The net player tries to keep the net; the back player tries to win it back with a good lob or a low ball at the feet. When the back player's lob gets over the net player, the back player must move forward. The Padel School's advice applies: don't come in behind a weak lob.

Goal: Points are normal. Swap roles after ten points and compare scores.

Progression: The net player may only hit bandejas on lobs, no smashes, which forces patience.

How to build a two-player session

A simple structure that works for most pairs:

  1. Warm-up (10 minutes): mini-rallies, then volley to volley.
  2. Technical block (25 minutes): two drills on one theme, such as glass and back plus cross-court counter.
  3. Overhead block (10 minutes): lob and bandeja.
  4. Game (15 minutes): half-court singles or the net challenge.
Fair feeding

Swap feeder and hitter often, every ten balls or every five minutes. Both players get the same number of reps, and feeding accurately is good practice in itself.

When you have a full four, move on to our padel drills for 4 players. On days when your partner cancels, try the solo padel drills instead.

Frequently asked questions

Can you play padel with only 2 players?

Yes, for practice. The FIP rules are written for pairs (doubles), but two players can drill, rally and play half-court games, which are some of the best ways to improve.

What is the best padel drill with a partner?

The glass and back drill, because the back glass is the skill most new players lack. Pair it with a cross-court rally so you also practise consistency.

How long should each partner drill last?

Around ten to fifteen minutes. Long enough to get into a rhythm, short enough to stay focused.

Do we need a coach to feed?

No. Take turns as the feeder and use a basket of balls. Feed at a steady, controllable pace.

What is half-court padel?

A practice game for two players using one half of the court, split lengthways. Serve straight ahead into the service box opposite you and play normal points.
