Padel recovery: what actually helps after a match or tournament

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 15 Nov 2026

Sore legs the day after padel are normal, especially after a long match, a tournament or your first session in a while. Good recovery means you play better next time and lowers the risk of overuse injuries. Some recovery methods have decent evidence; others are mostly marketing. Here's what works.

General information, not medical advice

This page gives general information. It can't diagnose you or replace an assessment. If you have a health condition, a recent injury or symptoms that worry you, check with a doctor or physiotherapist.

The basics come first

  1. Sleep. Most adults need 7–9 hours. It's when your body does most of its repair.
  2. Refuel. A meal with carbohydrate and protein in the hours after playing.
  3. Rehydrate. Drink regularly; more after long matches in heat.
  4. Cool down. A few minutes of easy movement and stretches. See our 9-minute cool-down.
  5. Easy movement the next day. A walk, an easy bike ride or mobility work usually feels better than total rest.

What the evidence says about recovery tools

A 2018 systematic review and meta-analysis (Dupuy and colleagues) compared recovery techniques after exercise. Massage came out as the most effective for reducing muscle soreness and perceived fatigue. Compression garments and cold-water immersion also showed benefits for soreness, while stretching and active recovery had limited effects on soreness in that analysis.

Soreness or injury?

General muscle soreness (DOMS) usually peaks 1–2 days after playing and affects both sides. Pain that is sharp, in one specific spot, gets worse when you play, or is still there after several days may be an injury. See our guides on calf strain, knee pain and common padel injuries.

Frequently asked questions

How do I recover after a padel match?

Cool down, eat a meal with carbohydrate and protein, drink, sleep well and do some easy movement the next day.

Why are my legs so sore after padel?

Padel involves lots of lunges, braking and side steps, which cause muscle soreness, especially when you've played more than usual or after a break.

Is an ice bath good after padel?

Cold-water immersion can reduce soreness and is useful between tournament days, but it's not necessary after every match.

Does massage help padel recovery?

Yes. In a 2018 meta-analysis, massage was the most effective technique for reducing muscle soreness and perceived fatigue.
