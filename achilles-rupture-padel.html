Achilles rupture in padel: signs and what to do

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 17 Nov 2026

Players who rupture an Achilles almost always say the same thing: it felt as if someone kicked them in the back of the leg, and they turned round to see who. It's one of padel's more serious injuries, it's easy to mistake for something milder, and getting it assessed quickly matters.

Not medical advice

This guide explains common patterns in plain language. It can't diagnose you. If the pain is severe, came from a fall or twist, or isn't settling, get it assessed.

The signs

Mayo Clinic lists these signs of an Achilles tendon rupture:

  • a feeling of having been kicked in the calf
  • a popping or snapping sound at the time of injury
  • pain, possibly severe, and swelling near the heel
  • being unable to bend the foot downward or push off the injured leg when walking
  • being unable to stand on your toes on the injured leg

Some people can still walk, with a limp, which is one reason ruptures get missed. Patient.info stresses that missed ruptures happen and that delayed treatment leads to worse outcomes.

What to do on court

  1. Stop. Don't try to play on or "test it" with hops.
  2. Keep weight off it and get help off the court.
  3. Get assessed the same day at an urgent care centre or emergency department. Don't drive yourself.

NHS 111 Wales also says to go to A&E or call 999 after a leg injury if you heard a crack, the leg has changed shape, the area is numb or tingling, or the skin turns blue, grey or cold.

Who is most at risk?

Mayo Clinic gives the peak age for Achilles rupture as 30 to 40, notes men are up to five times more likely to be affected than women, and lists recreational sports with running, jumping and sudden starts and stops, such as tennis, as a risk. That describes padel and a large share of its players. Mayo also lists steroid injections into the ankle and fluoroquinolone antibiotics as risk factors, so tell your doctor you play padel if either is suggested.

Rupture, tendinopathy or tennis leg?

ProblemOnsetWhere
Achilles ruptureSudden, "kicked", popJust above the heel
Achilles tendinopathyGradual over weeks, stiff in the morningAbout 2–6 cm above the heel
Tennis legSudden snap during push-offMiddle of the calf, inner side

Lowering your risk

Mayo Clinic's prevention advice is to stretch and strengthen the calf muscles, vary your exercises, choose running surfaces carefully and increase training intensity slowly. For padel that means a calf raise plan two or three times a week, a real warm-up before the first sprint, and adding sessions one at a time. The calf and Achilles were padel's most injured area in a 2025 French survey, so this is time well spent.

Frequently asked questions

What does an Achilles rupture feel like?

Like being kicked in the back of the leg, often with a pop, followed by pain near the heel and difficulty pushing off or standing on tiptoe.

Can you walk with a ruptured Achilles?

Sometimes, with a limp, which is why ruptures can be missed. Being able to walk doesn't rule it out.

What should I do if I think I've ruptured my Achilles in padel?

Stop playing, keep weight off it and get assessed the same day. Don't drive yourself.

Who is most at risk of Achilles rupture?

Mayo Clinic gives the peak age as 30 to 40 and says men are up to five times more likely to be affected than women, especially in stop-start sports.

How can padel players prevent Achilles ruptures?

Strengthen the calves, warm up properly and increase training gradually. Nothing removes the risk entirely.
