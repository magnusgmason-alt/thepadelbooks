Padel drills for tennis players

10 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 31 Oct 2026

The best padel drills for tennis players retrain the habits tennis gave you: they make you let the ball come off the back glass, swap power for control, lob on purpose, keep the net with a bandeja instead of a smash, and move with a partner. Below are ten drills that do that, plus a 60-minute session and the mistakes former tennis players make most often.

All ten drills are adapted from The Padel Drill Book, rewritten and shortened for the web. They are the drills from the book that target the gaps tennis players usually bring to the court.

Switching from tennis to padel: what changes

A lot carries over. The Padel School notes that hand-eye skills, volleys and overheads transfer to some degree, and tennis players usually feel at home with a racket quickly. The LTA points out that scoring uses the same games and sets. But the court is smaller (20 × 10 metres against 23.77 × 10.97 for tennis), the serve is underarm after a bounce, and the walls change everything.

  • The glass is your friend. The LTA calls getting used to the back glass the biggest early challenge. The Padel School's advice is to use it as much as possible, especially in training and when warming up.
  • Power can work against you. A hard shot comes further off the glass and hands the opponents an easier ball. Both the LTA and The Padel School say control and placement matter more.
  • Play as a pair. Move forwards and sideways together so you don't leave gaps.

Our padel vs tennis guide covers the full list of differences, including the rules. The drills below turn that list into practice.

Padel drills for tennis players: the back glass

1. Back-glass feed and play

Players: 1 · Time: 15 minutes · Level: 1–3

Set-up: Take a few balls and stand roughly three metres from your back glass. Put a target zone cross-court in the far half.

How it works: Throw the ball hard into the floor so that it rebounds into the glass. Turn side-on and let it come back out to you; move with it, not into the corner. When it has dropped to hip height, play a controlled forehand cross-court into the target. Twenty on the forehand side, then twenty on the backhand side.

Goal: Twelve of 20 into the target.

Progression: Play every exit down the line towards the opponents' side glass. Tennis instinct says take the ball early; here the waiting is the skill.

2. Back-glass exit

Players: 2 · Time: 15 minutes · Level: 2–4

Set-up: The feeder stands near the net on the far side; you start at the back.

How it works: The feeder sends deep balls that bounce about two metres from your glass and come off it around waist height. Turn side-on as soon as the ball passes your service line, then step forward with it as it rebounds and hit once it has dropped to hip height, cross-court into the target. Keep roughly 1.5 metres between you and the glass. Fifteen balls on each side.

Goal: Ten of 15 into the target per side.

Progression: More pace and backspin on the feed, so the ball comes off the glass lower. Our back glass guide explains the footwork.

3. Lob off the back glass

Players: 2 · Time: 15 minutes · Level: 2–5

Set-up: The feeder near the net on the far side; you at the back. The target is the last three metres of the far court.

How it works: The feeder sends the ball into your back glass so it comes out around waist height. Open the racket face slightly and lift from low to high, bending low before the shot and standing up through it. The lob needs to peak well above the reach of a net player's raised racket and land deep. Twenty balls.

Goal: Twelve of 20 in the target.

Progression: The feeder moves to the net and smashes anything short. In tennis the lob is an emergency shot; in padel it is how you win the net back.

Control drills: swap power for placement

4. Net-clearance control

Players: 2 · Time: 10 minutes · Level: 1–3

Set-up: Hang a towel or ribbon on the net post about 1.5 metres above the ground to show a height window. One player at the back on each side.

How it works: Rally from the back. Every ball must cross the net between the tape and the mark. Too high and net players would smash it; too low and it finds the net. Three sets of three minutes. Aim at a point just above the tape and drive through it, and don't let the window drift as you tire.

Goal: Twenty-five balls through the window per set.

Progression: Narrow the window to between one and 1.2 metres.

5. Deep block volley

Players: 2 · Time: 12 minutes · Level: 2–4

Set-up: You at the net; the feeder at the far back line with a basket.

How it works: The feeder drives balls at you. Hold the racket out in front, keep the wrist firm and the grip relaxed, and let the incoming pace do the work: no backswing. Every volley should land in the last three metres, so the opponents have to play it off their glass. Three sets of 12. Tennis players tend to punch volleys for winners; in padel the deep block is the bread-and-butter volley.

Goal: Eight of 12 into the target.

Progression: The feeder mixes in low drives, which you still block deep.

Overheads and the net

6. Bandeja depth

Players: 2 · Time: 15 minutes · Level: 2–4

Set-up: You at the net; the feeder at the far back line lobbing balls that come down near your service line.

How it works: Turn side-on, shuffle back and point your free hand at the ball. Use a slightly open face and a slicing swing, so the ball travels flat and fairly low over the net and lands deep, reaching the back glass without much height. Contact in front of your head. Twenty balls. The bandeja's job is depth and control, so you keep the net; it is not a winner.

Goal: Twelve of 20 in the last three metres.

Progression: After each bandeja, move forward to the net before the next lob. Our guide on how to hit a bandeja covers the technique.

7. Retake the net after a lob

Players: 4 · Time: 15 minutes · Level: 3–5

Set-up: You and your partner at the net; two opponents at the back.

How it works: An opponent lobs over you. Move back, play a deep bandeja and move forward again. While you are behind, your partner drops back a little; then you both walk forward together, at the same speed and on the same line. Play the point out. Ten lobs, then switch roles.

Goal: Back at the net as a pair within two balls on six of ten lobs.

Progression: The opponents may lob again straight away. Stopping at the service line after the bandeja leaves you in no-man's-land.

8. No-smash game

Players: 4 · Time: 12 minutes · Level: 3–6

Set-up: Normal doubles.

How it works: Play normal points, but a flat smash, or any smash that leaves the court, costs the hitter the point. Overheads must be bandejas or víboras that keep you at the net. Play a set to six games. Count your smashes: in this game the number should be zero. The Padel School's advice for tennis players is to stop relying on pace and learn the full set of padel overheads; this game forces the issue.

Goal: Win the set while holding the net.

Progression: Ban víboras too. To make it easier, allow smashes only from within two metres of the net.

Serve and partner play

9. Serve to the T

Players: 1 · Time: 12 minutes · Level: 1–4

Set-up: A basket of balls and a towel about a metre from the T (the point where the centre line joins the service line) in the diagonal box.

How it works: Serve the way the rules require: bounce the ball behind the service line, strike it at or below waist height, keep at least one foot on the ground and one foot behind the line (FIP Rule 6). Aim for the target, with the second bounce reaching the glass low. Three sets of 12 from each side. Tennis players often hit up and long; bend the knees and swing low to high with a touch of slice. Our guide to the serve rules has the details.

Goal: Six of 12 in the target per side.

Progression: Add a partner who returns.

10. Call every ball

Players: 4 · Time: 10 minutes · Level: 1–4

Set-up: Normal doubles.

How it works: Whoever plays the ball must call "mine" before contact; the partner answers "yours" or "go". Play a ball without calling it and you lose the point. Play to 11. Call early, as the ball leaves the opponent's racket; a call at contact is too late to help your partner. Tennis singles players in particular have never needed this habit.

Goal: Zero points lost for no call.

Progression: Also call "out" or "glass" on balls you leave.

Common mistakes tennis players make

Do

Let the deep ball reach the glass, turn early and play it as it drops. Lob to win the net back. Block volleys deep. Keep the net with a bandeja. Call every middle ball.

Don't

Half-volley everything behind the service line to avoid the glass. Hit flat and hard from the back. Rush the net behind a weak ball. Smash every lob. Go for drop shots and winners too early.

The Padel School's first tip for tennis players makes the same point: many new players try to half-volley balls behind the service line rather than letting them come off the glass, and getting comfortable with the glass is what moves your game on.

Frequently asked questions

Is padel easy for tennis players?

The start is usually quick, because hand-eye skills, volleys and overheads carry over. The Padel School warns not to confuse a quick start with mastery: the wall game, positioning and building a point still need to be learned.

Can I use my tennis grip for padel?

Padel coaching generally teaches the continental grip for almost every shot, so you never have to change grip in a fast exchange. It also makes slice and low balls off the glass easier to control. Our padel grip guide explains why.

What is the hardest part of padel for tennis players?

The back glass. The LTA names it as the biggest early challenge, and The Padel School's first tip for tennis players is to practise with the walls.

Is the padel serve like a tennis serve?

No. It is underarm: bounce the ball behind the service line and strike it at or below waist height, with at least one foot on the ground (FIP Rule 6).

Should tennis players take padel lessons?

It helps. The Padel School recommends learning correct glass technique early, through in-person or online coaching, before tennis habits settle in.
