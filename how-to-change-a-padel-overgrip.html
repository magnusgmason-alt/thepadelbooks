How to change a padel overgrip

6 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 8 Nov 2026

Changing a padel overgrip takes about two minutes: peel off the old one, start at the butt of the handle with the tapered end, wind it up the handle with a small, even overlap, then trim and tape the top. Below are the steps in detail, how often to change it, and how to pick between tacky and dry, thick and thin, or a full replacement grip.

Overgrip vs replacement grip: what's on your handle

A padel racket handle has two possible layers. The base grip (or replacement grip) is the thicker, cushioned wrap that comes on the racket. An overgrip is a thin tape wound on top of it. Padel Market describes the overgrip's job as absorbing moisture and stopping the base grip from wearing out early. You change overgrips often and base grips rarely.

You'll only need a new replacement grip if the base grip underneath is torn, flattened or slipping on the handle, or if you want a noticeably different shape or thickness. For everything else, a fresh overgrip is the cheap fix.

What you need

  • A new overgrip (it comes with a strip of finishing tape)
  • Scissors
  • Two minutes and a flat surface

How to put an overgrip on a padel racket

These steps follow Total Padel's guide, with overlap guidance from Wilson's overgrip guide and HEAD's advice on thickness.

  1. Remove the old overgrip. Unwind it from the top and peel off any leftover finishing tape. If the base grip underneath feels sticky, wipe it dry.
  2. Move the wrist cord out of the way. Slide the band that holds the cord down or off, and tie the cord back so it doesn't get wrapped in. The cord is compulsory under the FIP rules, so make sure it's free and untangled when you finish.
  3. Peel the backing. Unroll the new overgrip and remove the plastic film. Keep the finishing tape to one side.
  4. Start at the butt. Stick the tapered end at the bottom edge of the handle, next to the butt cap, so it sits flat.
  5. Wind up the handle. Rotate the racket slowly and keep gentle tension so the tape lies flat without wrinkles. Wilson suggests an overlap of about 1/8 to 1/4 inch, roughly 3 to 6 mm, on each turn. Keep the overlap the same all the way up so the handle has an even thickness.
  6. Mark the finish. When you reach the top of the handle, mark where the overgrip should end.
  7. Trim. Unwind the last turn, lay it flat and cut straight across at your mark, so the end finishes neatly at the top of the handle. HEAD notes some softer overgrips can be torn by hand.
  8. Tape the top. Wrap the finishing tape round the end to secure it, then put the band and cord back in place.

If it goes wrong, HEAD's advice is simple: unwrap it and start again. Overgrips are forgiving for the first attempt.

Overlap sets the size

HEAD notes that the overlap controls thickness. More overlap gives a thicker handle for bigger hands; barely overlapping keeps it slim. Try a few wraps before you commit.

Mistakes that ruin a fresh overgrip

  • Wrapping too loosely. Wrinkles and lumps appear within a session. Pull firmly enough to stretch the tape slightly.
  • Uneven overlap. The handle ends up lumpy, thicker at one end than the other.
  • Wrapping the cord in. You'll have to start again, and a trapped cord can rub.
  • Too many layers on a small hand. A handle that's too fat makes it harder to change grips quickly at the net. Our guide to the padel grip explains why the continental grip needs a relaxed hand.
  • Leaving the plastic film on. It happens more often than you'd think.

How often should you change a padel overgrip?

There's no fixed rule, and sources give different rules of thumb:

SourceSuggested interval
Padel MarketEvery 4 to 6 sessions for club players, sooner if it feels slippery or hard
Padel BrowserEvery 2 to 4 sessions if you play often; weekend players can go a few weeks
Total Padel2 to 3 sessions for intermediate players; professionals may change during a match
HEADWhen it feels worn, and at least once every two months

The practical test matters more than the count. Change it when it looks shiny or discoloured, feels slick or damp, has gone hard, or is starting to unravel at the edges. Many players also put on a fresh one before a tournament or important match. Sweaty hands, hot weather and dry (absorbent) overgrips all shorten the life.

Tacky vs dry overgrips for sweaty hands

Overgrips come in two broad feels:

  • Tacky: slightly sticky to the touch. Padel Browser calls it the default for most players and says it gives confidence in dry or mildly humid conditions. Padel Market recommends tacky grips for dry hands.
  • Dry (absorbent): feels almost papery when new and soaks up sweat, so it stays grippy as your hand gets damp. Both sources recommend dry overgrips for sweaty hands and hot, humid outdoor play. Padel Browser notes they wear out faster than tacky ones.

If your hand sweats a lot, a dry overgrip plus a wristband is usually better than a tacky grip that turns slick halfway through the second set. Drying your grip with a towel between games helps either way. Pack a spare: our padel bag checklist has the rest of what's worth carrying.

Thick vs thin overgrips, and building grip size with layers

Padel Market puts standard overgrips at roughly 0.4 to 0.6 mm thick, with the rule of thumb "thin for better control, thick for comfort". The difference is small per layer but noticeable in the hand.

To make the handle bigger, you have three options:

  1. Overlap more on a single overgrip.
  2. Add a second overgrip on top of the first. Padel Market and Padel Browser both mention layering two overgrips to build handle size, and Padel Browser suggests it for larger hands or more cushioning.
  3. Fit a thicker replacement grip if layering still isn't enough.

Go one step at a time and play a session before adding more. A handle that's too thick tends to make you squeeze, and squeezing tires your forearm. If you've had elbow trouble, read our padel elbow guide before changing your set-up.

Frequently asked questions

Which end of the handle do you start an overgrip?

Start at the bottom, by the butt cap, with the tapered end of the overgrip, and wind up towards the head. Finish at the top of the handle with the finishing tape.

How often should I change my padel overgrip?

Rules of thumb range from every 2 to 6 sessions depending on how much you play and sweat, and HEAD suggests at least every two months. Change it sooner if it feels slick, hard or starts to unravel.

Can I put an overgrip over an old overgrip?

You can, and some players layer two to build handle size. Don't layer over a worn, slippery one just to avoid removing it, as the new grip can shift.

What is the difference between an overgrip and a replacement grip?

A replacement (base) grip is the thicker cushioned wrap fitted to the handle. An overgrip is a thin tape wound on top that absorbs sweat and protects the base grip. Overgrips are changed often; base grips rarely.

Is a tacky or dry overgrip better for sweaty hands?

A dry, absorbent overgrip is usually better for sweaty hands and hot, humid conditions. Tacky overgrips suit dry hands and milder conditions.
