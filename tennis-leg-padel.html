Tennis leg in padel: the calf tear explained

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 17 Nov 2026

You push off to chase a drop shot and feel something hit the back of your calf. Maybe a snap, then sharp pain and a limp. In padel, that's very often "tennis leg": a tear where the inner calf muscle meets its tendon. It's painful and frustrating, but it usually heals without surgery if you treat it sensibly.

Not medical advice

This guide explains common patterns in plain language. It can't diagnose you. If the pain is severe, came from a fall or twist, or isn't settling, get it assessed.

What tennis leg is

Radiopaedia describes tennis leg as a tear of the inner (medial) head of the gastrocnemius, the big calf muscle, at the point where it joins its tendon. The typical mechanism is the knee straightening while the ankle is forced upwards, which is the position of a hard push-off from the back foot. It usually affects active people in middle age and is normally managed without surgery.

Why it's a padel injury

In one study included in the 2023 systematic review of padel injuries (Dahmen and colleagues), tennis leg made up 22.1% of injuries in senior players. The Aspetar review says it is most common in poorly conditioned men over 40, and in a 2025 survey of 645 French players the calf and Achilles were the most injured area overall.

Tennis leg or Achilles rupture?

The first few days

NHS 111 Wales recommends protection, rest, ice (up to 20 minutes every two to three hours, wrapped), compression and elevation for the first two to three days, and avoiding heat, alcohol and massage early on because they can increase swelling. Once walking is tolerable, keep the leg gently moving.

How long until padel?

NHS 111 Wales says most strains feel better within about two weeks but advises avoiding strenuous exercise such as running for up to eight weeks, and severe strains can take months. Work back in stages:

  1. Walking without a limp, stairs without pain.
  2. Calf raises: two legs, then single-leg, until both sides feel similar. Our calf raise plan sets the progression.
  3. Jogging, then side shuffles and gentle changes of direction.
  4. Short sprints, split steps and drills.
  5. A short match, then full play once the calf is fine the next day.

Stop and get same-day help if you suspect an Achilles rupture: a feeling of being kicked in the back of the leg, a pop or snap, and difficulty pushing off or standing on tiptoe on that side. NHS 111 Wales says to go to A&E or call 999 after an injury if you heard a crack, the leg has changed shape, the area is numb or tingling, or the skin is blue, grey or cold. Don't drive yourself.

Frequently asked questions

What is tennis leg in padel?

A tear of the inner head of the gastrocnemius calf muscle where it joins its tendon, usually from a hard push-off.

How long does tennis leg take to heal?

Most strains feel better in about two weeks, but NHS 111 Wales advises avoiding strenuous exercise for up to eight weeks, and severe tears can take months.

Is tennis leg the same as an Achilles rupture?

No. Tennis leg is higher, in the calf muscle; an Achilles rupture is just above the heel and makes standing on tiptoe very hard. A suspected rupture needs same-day assessment.

Who gets tennis leg in padel?

It's most common in middle-aged players, especially poorly conditioned men over 40.

How can I prevent tennis leg?

Strong calves, a proper warm-up before the first sprint, and building up your number of sessions gradually.
