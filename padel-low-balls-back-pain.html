Low balls and back pain in padel: get down without hurting

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 17 Nov 2026

Padel is full of low balls: volleys at your feet, balls dying off the back glass, digs out of the corner. Take them by folding at the waist and your lower back holds the weight of your upper body at a bad angle hundreds of times a session. Take them with your legs and most of that load goes away.

Not medical advice

This guide explains common patterns in plain language. It can't diagnose you. If the pain is severe, came from a fall or twist, or isn't settling, get it assessed.

Why low balls bother the back

In a 2025 survey of 645 French players (Lecler and colleagues), the lower back was where players most often reported pain. The Aspetar review of padel injuries notes trunk problems are more common in padel than in tennis, pointing to padel's extra smashes and volleys. Every low ball you reach for by bending at the waist adds to that load, and it's worst when you're late and have to stretch for it.

Get low with your legs

  • Lower the hips, not the racket. Bend knees and hips together and keep your chest reasonably upright.
  • Widen your base. Feet a little wider than shoulders let you sink without folding forward.
  • Lunge to wide balls. Step towards the ball and drop the back knee rather than reaching sideways with the arm.
  • Hinge, don't round. When you must lean, push the hips back and keep the back's natural shape, the same pattern as a Romanian deadlift.

Footwork buys time

Most back-unfriendly shots happen when you're late. Split-step as your opponent hits, take small adjusting steps, and get to the side of the ball rather than over it. On balls out of the back glass, step back and let the ball come off the wall before you go down to it. Our half-volley guide covers playing the ball at your feet without collapsing at the waist.

If your back is already sore

Keep moving: NHS inform describes keeping active as an essential part of recovery, and most back problems begin to settle within six weeks. Short sessions, extra knee bend and letting your partner take the hard digs help in the meantime.

Contact your GP if your back pain is persistent and unchanging despite self-care, if movement doesn't affect the pain, or if it's worse at night. NHS inform describes cauda equina syndrome as rare but serious. Call 111 (or your local emergency number) if you have back pain with any of these:

  • numbness or pins and needles between the inner thighs or genitals, or around the back passage or buttocks
  • new difficulty starting or stopping urination, or leaking urine
  • loss of bladder or bowel control, or loss of sensation when you go to the toilet
  • changes in erection, ejaculation or genital sensation
  • significant trauma, such as a fall from height, in the last seven days

Frequently asked questions

Why does my back hurt on low volleys in padel?

Usually because you reach down by bending at the waist instead of the knees, so the lower back holds your upper body at a bad angle over and over.

How do I get low in padel without back pain?

Lower your hips by bending knees and hips together, widen your base, lunge to wide balls and get there early so you aren't reaching.

What exercises help me get lower in padel?

Goblet squats, reverse and lateral lunges, single-leg Romanian deadlifts, side planks and hip mobility work.

Are balls off the back glass bad for the back?

They can be if you're late and dig the ball out bent over. Step back, let the ball come off the wall, and get down with your legs.

Should I keep playing if low balls hurt my back?

With mild pain and no warning signs, usually yes, with shorter sessions and more knee bend. Keeping active helps most back pain.
