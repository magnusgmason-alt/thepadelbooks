A 10-minute padel mobility routine for hips, back and shoulders

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 15 Nov 2026

Mobility is your ability to move a joint actively through its full range. In padel that means getting low with a long stride, rotating fully for a groundstroke, and reaching overhead without arching your back. This 10-minute routine covers the four areas padel players tend to get stiff in: ankles, hips, upper back and shoulders.

General information, not medical advice

This page gives general training guidance. If you have a heart condition, a recent injury or any health concern, check with a doctor or physiotherapist before starting.

Mobility vs stretching

Static stretching means holding a position for 20–60 seconds. Mobility work means moving actively through a range, often with control at the end. Before you play, active mobility is the better fit: it prepares joints without making you feel loose and slow. The NHS suggests stretching after exercise, when your muscles are warm, and holding each stretch for about 10–15 seconds.

Neither is a strong injury shield on its own: a 2014 meta-analysis (Lauersen and colleagues) found stretching had no clear effect on sports injuries, whereas strength training had a large one. Mobility is about moving well; combine it with strength training.

The routine

AreaExerciseDose
AnkleKnee-to-wall ankle rocks10 each side
Hip90/90 hip switches8 each side
HipWorld's greatest stretch (lunge, elbow to instep, rotate up)5 each side
HipCossack squat (side-to-side)6 each side
Upper backOpen book rotation, side-lying8 each side
Upper backCat–camel8
ShoulderWall slides10
ShoulderBand dislocates (wide grip)10
Whole bodyInchworm walk-out to a plank5

Move slowly and smoothly. Breathe out as you go into the end of the range. It should feel like a gentle stretch, never sharp pain.

When to do it

  • Before playing, as part of your warm-up, after a couple of minutes of light jogging.
  • On rest days, as a short session in the morning or evening.
  • After a long day at a desk, when hips and upper back are stiffest.

Why it helps your padel

  • Ankle and hip mobility let you get lower for volleys and balls off the glass.
  • Upper-back rotation adds power to groundstrokes without twisting the lower back.
  • Shoulder mobility makes overheads, bandejas and víboras easier on the shoulder. See shoulder pain.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best mobility routine for padel?

A short routine covering ankles, hips, upper back and shoulders, such as ankle rocks, 90/90 hip switches, the world's greatest stretch, open book rotations and wall slides.

Should I stretch before padel?

Before playing, active mobility and a dynamic warm-up are better than long static stretches. Save static stretches for after playing.

Does mobility prevent padel injuries?

Stretching alone has little effect on injury risk. Combine mobility with strength training and a proper warm-up.

How often should I do mobility work?

A few minutes before every session plus one or two 10-minute routines on rest days is plenty for most players.
