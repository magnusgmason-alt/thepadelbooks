Golfer's elbow from padel: inner elbow pain explained

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 17 Nov 2026

Not all padel elbow pain is tennis elbow. If the ache is on the inside of your elbow, the side closest to your body, the likely culprit is golfer's elbow: an overload of the tendons that bend the wrist and turn the palm down. It's much less common than tennis elbow, but padel can trigger it. Here's how to tell them apart and what to do.

Not medical advice

This guide explains common patterns in plain language. It can't diagnose you. If the pain is severe, came from a fall or twist, or isn't settling, get it assessed.

Golfer's elbow vs tennis elbow

Tennis elbowGolfer's elbow
Where it hurtsOutside of the elbowInside of the elbow, near the bony bump
Tendons involvedWrist and finger extensorsWrist flexors and the muscle that turns the palm down
Hurts most whenGripping, lifting the wrist backBending the wrist against resistance, turning the palm down, gripping
How commonThe most common padel injuryMuch rarer: about 9 to 20% of all elbow tendon (epicondyle) diagnoses

The medial epicondyle figure comes from Physiopedia's clinical summary, which also notes the two names "golfer's elbow" and "pitcher's elbow" for the same problem. If your pain is on the outside, read tennis elbow from padel instead.

Symptoms

According to Physiopedia's summary, golfer's elbow typically causes pain just below the bony bump on the inner elbow, sometimes spreading down the little-finger side of the forearm towards the wrist. It's provoked by bending the wrist against resistance and turning the palm down, and often comes with a weaker grip. Pain can start suddenly or build gradually. Tenderness is usually local, without obvious redness or swelling.

Watch for tingling. Numbness or tingling in the ring and little fingers can occur, but it can also mean the ulnar nerve, which runs right next to this area, is irritated. That deserves a proper assessment rather than self-treatment.

Why it can happen in padel

The tendons on the inner elbow work hard when the wrist snaps forward and the forearm turns the palm down. In padel that happens on heavily brushed topspin forehands, wristy flat smashes and víboras, and when you grip the racket hard on defensive blocks. A sudden increase in playing time, as with tennis elbow, is the usual background story. Racket sports such as tennis and racquetball are among the activities Physiopedia lists, although most cases occur outside sport, from forceful, repetitive gripping work.

What helps

Physiopedia describes a three-phase approach:

  1. Calm it down. Stop the activities that provoke it, but not all activity, because complete rest leads to muscle loss. Ice and simple painkillers can help. A counterforce brace is recommended for athletes and can help when returning to sport.
  2. Rebuild. First pain-free movement of the wrist and elbow, then gentle static (isometric) exercises with the elbow bent, then lifting and slow lowering exercises for the wrist flexors as symptoms allow.
  3. Return to padel. Check technique and equipment before ramping back up: relaxed grip, less wrist flick on spins and smashes, and a racket that doesn't need to be squeezed.

Useful exercises from our workout builder: wrist flexion curl, forearm rotation, grip squeeze, plus shoulder work such as band external rotation, since elbow overuse often goes hand in hand with shoulder and shoulder-blade weakness.

When to see someone

See a GP or physio if it isn't settling after a couple of weeks of reduced load, straight away if you have tingling or numbness in the fingers or a weak hand, or if the pain started with a single forceful movement or a fall. Physiotherapy is the main treatment; injections and surgery are reserved for stubborn cases.

Frequently asked questions

What is golfer's elbow in padel?

Pain on the inside of the elbow from overload of the tendons that bend the wrist and turn the palm down. It's less common than tennis elbow.

How do I know if it's golfer's elbow or tennis elbow?

Golfer's elbow hurts on the inner side of the elbow; tennis elbow on the outer side. The table in this article lists the other differences.

Can padel cause golfer's elbow?

It can, especially with wristy topspin forehands and smashes, a tight grip and a sudden increase in playing time.

Is tingling in the little finger part of golfer's elbow?

It can occur, but it can also mean the ulnar nerve is irritated, so get it assessed.

Does a brace help golfer's elbow?

A counterforce brace is commonly recommended for athletes with golfer's elbow and can help when returning to sport, alongside exercise.
