Returning to padel after an injury: a step-by-step plan

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 15 Nov 2026

The most common time to get injured is just after coming back from an injury. You feel fine walking around, play a full match on your first night back, and the calf, knee or elbow goes again. A gradual return protects you. Here's a stage-by-stage plan you can adapt to most padel injuries, plus simple tests to check you're ready.

General information, not medical advice

This page gives general information. It can't diagnose you or replace an assessment. If you have a health condition, a recent injury or symptoms that worry you, check with a doctor or physiotherapist.

Return to sport is a process

The 2016 consensus statement on return to sport (Ardern and colleagues) describes returning as a continuum, not a single day: from returning to participation (training with restrictions), to returning to sport (playing, but not yet at your best), to returning to performance. Decisions should be shared between you and the people treating you, and based on how the body part is functioning, not just the time since the injury.

The 5 stages

StageOn courtMove on when
1. Daily lifeNo padel. Rehab exercisesWalking, stairs and daily tasks are pain-free
2. Basic trainingRehab plus jogging, cycling, light strengthStrength nearly matches the other side
3. Solo hittingWall hitting, feeding drills, controlled movementNo pain during or the day after
4. Drills and ralliesFootwork drills, short rallies with friends, points from set positionsFull-speed movement feels normal
5. Match playOne set, then a full match, then normal frequencyTwo weeks of normal play without symptoms

Readiness checks

  • Legs: hop on the injured leg 10 times without pain and land as well as on the other side; 15+ single-leg calf raises; side shuffles and sprints without hesitation.
  • Elbow or wrist: grip and wrist strength close to the other side; no pain hitting volleys and groundstrokes at moderate pace.
  • Shoulder: full overhead range; no pain with band exercises and shadow smashes.
  • Confidence: you're not thinking about the injury on every shot.

Coming back from a long break

The same principles apply after a holiday, illness or months away: start with one or two sessions a week, keep them shorter, and build up over several weeks. See how often to play padel.

How not to get injured again

  • Keep doing your rehab exercises twice a week for a few months.
  • Warm up every time. See the padel warm-up.
  • Add strength training to your week.
  • Treat new pain early instead of playing through it.

For specific injuries, see ankle sprain, calf strain, hamstring strain, padel elbow and knee pain.

Frequently asked questions

When can I play padel again after an injury?

When daily life is pain-free, strength is close to the other side, and you've progressed through drills and rallies without symptoms. Time alone isn't a good guide.

How do I come back to padel after a long break?

Start with one or two shorter sessions a week and build up over several weeks, alongside strength training.

How do I know I'm ready to return to padel?

Use simple tests: pain-free hopping and calf raises for leg injuries, grip strength for elbow and wrist injuries, and full overhead movement for shoulder injuries.

Why do padel injuries come back?

Usually because players return to full matches before strength and capacity are restored, or stop their rehab exercises too soon.
