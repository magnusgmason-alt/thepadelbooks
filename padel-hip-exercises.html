Padel hip exercises: a 6-week strength and mobility plan

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 17 Nov 2026

In padel your hips do the work you don't notice: they lower you to low balls, push you sideways, turn your trunk on every groundstroke and absorb the lunge. When they're weak or stiff, the knees, groin and lower back pick up the slack. This plan trains strength and mobility together in about 20 minutes, twice a week.

Not medical advice

This guide explains common patterns in plain language. It can't diagnose you. If the pain is severe, came from a fall or twist, or isn't settling, get it assessed.

Why train hips for padel?

The hips are the link between your legs and your racket. Stiff hips push rotation into the lower back; weak hip muscles let the knee drift inwards on lunges; weak adductors leave the groin exposed on wide balls. Strength training is the best-supported prevention method in sport: a 2014 meta-analysis of 25 trials (Lauersen and colleagues) found it cut sports injuries to less than a third. The best specific example comes from football: in a 2019 trial (Harøy and colleagues), adding one Copenhagen adductor exercise to training lowered the risk of groin problems by 41%.

Figure-4 glute stretchHip rotation, so the back doesn't twist for you

The 6-week plan

WeeksTwice a week
1–2Glute bridge 3 × 12 · Split squat (back foot on the floor) 3 × 8 each · Band walk 2 × 10 steps each way · Copenhagen plank, short lever 2 × 15 s each · Both stretches 2 × 30 s
3–4Single-leg bridge 3 × 10 each · Rear-foot-elevated split squat 3 × 8 · Lateral lunge 3 × 8 each · Copenhagen 3 × 20 s · Stretches
5–6Rear-foot-elevated split squat holding weights 3 × 8 · Single-leg Romanian deadlift 3 × 8 each · Lateral lunge with weight 3 × 8 · Copenhagen 3 × 25–30 s · Stretches

Keep the knee in line with your toes on every lunge and split squat. If an exercise causes pain in the hip or groin, regress it or skip it. Build it as a PDF in our free workout builder.

On court

  • Warm up with leg swings, walking lunges with a twist and side shuffles; see our padel warm-up.
  • Use small adjustment steps instead of one huge lunge where you can.
  • Turn from the hips on groundstrokes so your lower back isn't doing the twisting.

Already sore?

Hip pain has many possible causes, and NHS advice is not to try to diagnose it yourself. Keep moving without overdoing it, and read can I play padel with hip pain? or our hip pain guide first.

NHS advice is to call 999 or go to A&E if you have severe hip pain after a fall or injury, can't walk or put weight on the leg, or have tingling or loss of feeling in the hip or leg after an injury. Ask for an urgent GP appointment or contact NHS 111 if severe pain started suddenly without an injury, the hip is swollen and hot, the skin around it has changed colour, or you feel unwell with a high temperature. See a GP if the pain hasn't improved after two weeks of self-care, keeps coming back, affects your sleep or daily life, or you have hip stiffness for more than 30 minutes after waking.

Frequently asked questions

What are the best hip exercises for padel?

Glute bridges, split squats, lateral lunges, band walks, single-leg Romanian deadlifts and Copenhagen planks, plus hip flexor and glute stretches.

Why do padel players need strong hips?

Hips lower you to low balls, push you sideways and turn your trunk. Weak or stiff hips shift load to the knees, groin and lower back.

Does the Copenhagen plank prevent groin injuries?

In a football trial, adding one Copenhagen adductor exercise lowered the risk of groin problems by 41%. It's the best evidence available; padel itself hasn't been studied.

How often should I do hip exercises for padel?

Twice a week for strength, with mobility drills in your warm-up.

Can hip exercises help knee pain?

Hip strength helps keep the knee in line on lunges and landings, which is why hip work is part of most knee programmes.
