Padel drills for kids

6 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 23 Oct 2026

The best padel drills for kids are short, active games where children hit lots of balls and keep score in simple ways. Below are eight fun padel games and exercises for young players, plus the safety basics every parent and coach should know before the first session.

Safety first

The US Padel Association's guide to junior programmes sets the order of priorities as be safe, have fun, learn. That order is a good rule for any session with children.

  • Right-sized racket. HEAD's guidance is roughly 300 g for under-10s and about 330 g for over-10s, with most children moving to an adult racket around 12 or 13. A racket that is too big or heavy makes injury more likely. See our best padel rackets for kids.
  • Wrist cord on. The FIP rules make the racket's safety cord mandatory, worn on the wrist. It stops a racket flying out of a small hand.
  • Eye protection. In a short case report of three padel eye injuries that needed surgery, ophthalmologists at the University of Gothenburg called padel a potentially high-risk sport for eye injuries and recommended goggles. Sports goggles are worth considering for children too, especially in net games.
  • No hitting at people. The FIP etiquette rules ban hitting the ball at opponents when it is not in play. In kids' drills, ban smashing at anyone, full stop.
  • Space and supervision. One group per half-court, balls cleared from the floor, no running on a wet court, and an adult on court.

How to run padel drills for kids

  • Keep everyone hitting. The USPA guide warns against line drills where children queue for one turn. Pair players up instead.
  • Use easier balls for young beginners. The USPA suggests foam or low-compression balls for school clinics; slower balls keep rallies going.
  • Allow two bounces for younger beginners. More contact means more fun and faster learning.
  • Short sessions. Around 60 minutes suits most juniors; 90 minutes is usually too long for beginners and for under-12s.
  • Simple scoring. Count rallies or play first to five before introducing proper padel scoring.

Warm-up games

1. Racket balance relay

Players: 4–8 · Time: 5 minutes

Setup: Two teams at the back glass, a cone at the net for each team.

How it works: Each child walks (or jogs) to the cone and back balancing a ball on their racket, then hands the ball to the next teammate.

Goal: First team to finish wins. A dropped ball means stopping and starting again from that spot.

Progression: Bounce the ball on the racket face while walking, then alternate faces.

2. Bounce count

Players: Any number · Time: 5 minutes

Setup: Each child with a racket and ball, spread out safely.

How it works: Bounce the ball up off the racket as many times as possible. Then down onto the floor. Then alternate up and down.

Goal: A personal best. Everyone counts out loud.

Progression: Use the other face of the racket, or do it while moving sideways.

Rally games

3. Rally ladder

Players: 2 per half-court · Time: 10 minutes

Setup: Pairs on half-courts, standing near the service line on opposite sides of the net.

How it works: Rally gently. First target two shots, then three, then four. Two bounces allowed for younger children. The USPA recommends three to five-shot mini-rallies as the basic unit for juniors.

Goal: Reach a rally of five, then ten. Change partners after each ladder.

Progression: One bounce only, then one player volleys while the other lets the ball bounce.

4. Target zones

Players: 1–3 per feeder · Time: 10 minutes

Setup: Hoops, towels or cones in the opposite court. Small targets score more than large ones. With more than three children, add a second feeder on the other half-court.

How it works: A coach or parent feeds an easy ball; the child plays it towards a target. Then it's the next player's turn. No more than two children should ever be waiting.

Goal: One point for landing in the court, three for hitting a target.

Progression: Call the target before the feed, or let children feed each other.

5. Wall bounce challenge

Players: 2 · Time: 10 minutes

Setup: Both children on the same side. The thrower stands near the service line, the hitter a couple of metres in front of the back glass.

How it works: One child gently throws the ball so it bounces and then hits the back glass. The other lets it come off the glass and taps it back to the thrower. Keep it exploratory: the USPA suggests that wall play for beginners should be playful, not technical.

Goal: Five good returns, then swap.

Progression: Return the ball over the net instead of to the thrower. Older children can read our back glass guide.

Net and serve games

6. Volley catch

Players: 2 · Time: 8 minutes

Setup: One child at the net with a racket, a partner on the other side throwing underarm.

How it works: The thrower sends soft balls at chest height. The volleyer blocks the ball back so the thrower can catch it. Short racket movement, no big swing.

Goal: Ten catches in a row, then swap.

Progression: Throw to alternate sides so the volleyer uses forehand and backhand.

7. Serve skittles

Players: 2–4 · Time: 10 minutes

Setup: Plastic cones or empty plastic bottles (never glass) standing in the diagonal service box.

How it works: Children serve underarm, bouncing the ball first and hitting it at or below waist height, as the rules require. Each knocked-over cone is a point.

Goal: The most cones knocked over in ten serves.

Progression: Serve from the other side of the court too, or move the cones deeper.

Team game

8. Crocodile and fish

Players: 3–6 fish plus a crocodile · Time: 10 minutes

Setup: The "fish" line up at the back on one side. The "crocodile" (an older child or adult) stands at the net on the other side.

How it works: Each fish plays a rally against the crocodile, who hits only soft balls. A fish who wins three shots swims past and joins the crocodile's side; a fish who misses goes back in the queue. With more than three fish, run two crocodiles on two half-courts, so no more than two fish are ever waiting.

Goal: Get every fish across.

Progression: The crocodile may play gentle lobs too.

For ages 10 to 14

Older children can move towards adult-style drills: ladder rallies with one bounce, cross-court rallies and simple doubles points. Start them on the beginner drills and keep sessions playful.

Frequently asked questions

What age can children start padel?

HEAD says children can start once they are strong enough to hold a racket, typically from about age three. The USPA's junior guide suggests starting with an under-12 group (ages 7–12), adding an under-8 group (ages 5–8) as a programme grows.

Are padel drills safe for kids?

Yes, with the basics in place: a racket the right size, the wrist cord on, supervision, no hitting at people and, ideally, eye protection.

What balls should kids use?

Softer, slower balls such as foam or low-compression balls help young beginners keep rallies going. Older children can use normal padel balls.

How long should a kids' padel session be?

About an hour. Shorter for very young children, and keep every activity to ten minutes or less.

What are fun padel games for kids?

Rally ladders, target zones, serve skittles and team games like crocodile and fish. Anything with a score and lots of hitting works.
