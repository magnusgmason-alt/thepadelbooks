How to watch padel and actually learn from the pros

7 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 8 Nov 2026

When you watch padel at the top level, ignore the ball for a while: the real lessons are in where the players stand, how often they lob, how deep their bandejas land and how rarely they rush. Below are nine things to study in pro matches, where to watch Premier Padel in 2026, and the flashy shots you should enjoy from the sofa but leave out of your club game.

Watching is not the same as studying. Most of us follow the ball, cheer the smash and learn nothing. Pick one item from the list below per match, watch one player for a set, and you will see patterns you can take to the club the same week.

How to watch padel like a coach: 9 lessons

1. Where the pair stands, not where the ball goes

Choose one pair and watch only their feet for five points. They are almost always both at the net or both at the back, and they slide sideways together. When one is pulled wide, the other closes the middle. That shape, more than any shot, is why they look like they have so much time. Our doubles positioning guide explains the two home positions.

2. How often they lob

Count the lobs in a game. At the top level the lob is the main way to move opponents off the net and win it back; research summarised in the Aspetar Sports Medicine Journal describes players at the back using it to gain court towards the net. Notice how high and deep the good ones are, and how the lobbing pair moves forward behind them. Then compare with your own club match, where most players lob far less. See our padel lob guide.

3. Bandeja depth and purpose

Watch where the bandeja lands. The aim is usually not to win the point but to keep the net: a controlled, sliced ball deep towards the back glass that comes off without much bounce. Then watch what the player does next: they move forward straight away. Our guide on how to hit a bandeja breaks it down.

4. Who takes the middle

Watch balls down the centre. There is rarely hesitation. One player, usually the one whose forehand or overhead faces the middle, takes it, and the partner has already moved to cover the space. It looks automatic because it was agreed long before the match.

5. Patience

The Padel School says the biggest misconception about the pros is that they play hard and fast all the time. Watch how patiently points are built: slow balls through the middle, lob, re-position, repeat, until someone gets a ball worth attacking. Pace appears only when the opportunity does.

6. How points actually end

Keep a simple tally of winners and errors for one set. A 2022 study by Escudero-Tena and colleagues found that both men and women in professional padel made more errors than winners, and that around six in ten golden points ended in an error. If even the best players lose most points by missing, your priority at club level is obvious.

7. The recovery after an overhead

After a smash or bandeja, watch the next two steps. Good players never admire their shot; they move forward and get their racket up, because the ball often comes back off the glass.

8. The receiver and the server's partner

Watch the return of serve. Notice how often the returner keeps it low, goes through the middle or lobs, rather than going for a winner, and the server's partner shifts according to the plan they made with hand signals before the point. Our serve and return guide covers the options.

9. The player without the ball

For a whole game, watch only the player who isn't hitting. Where do they move when their partner is playing off the glass? When do they step forward? This is where amateurs lose most points, and it is invisible if you follow the ball.

Where to watch Premier Padel in 2026

Premier Padel is the professional world tour; our guide to Premier Padel explained covers how it works. Broadcast rights differ by country and can change from event to event, so treat this as a guide and check before each tournament:

  • Early rounds: Padel Speak's 2026 guide lists rounds one to three on Premier Padel's YouTube channel in most territories, sometimes alongside a local broadcaster.
  • Quarter-finals to the final: on Red Bull TV and/or a regional broadcaster, depending on where you are. Padel Speak's 2026 list includes, for example, Sky Sports and Red Bull TV in the UK and Ireland, Movistar+ and Red Bull TV in Spain, Canal+ in France, Sky Italia in Italy, and beIN Sports and Red Bull TV in the US and Canada.
  • The official source: Padel Browser notes that Premier Padel's own "Where to Watch" page lists the broadcaster for each event by country. If you're unsure, check the official Premier Padel website before the event.

Prices, subscriptions and availability vary by country and change between seasons. Highlights and press conferences are also widely shared on Premier Padel's YouTube channel.

What amateurs should NOT copy

Copy

Positioning as a pair, deep lobs, controlled bandejas, patience, calling the middle, recovering forward after every overhead, the continental grip as your base.

Don't copy (yet)

x3 and x4 smashes as your go-to finish, constant power, low-percentage trick shots, highly personal grips and wrist actions, and the pace of a professional athlete without the conditioning.

The x3 and x4 smash

The por tres sends the ball off the back wall and out over the side; the por cuatro bounces high enough to leave over the back of the court. Our padel smash guide explains both. Pros hit them from very specific positions with years of practice. At club level, trying to finish every lob this way usually means a smash that comes back, a lost net position or a ball into the fence. A deep bandeja and a later attack win more points.

Power for its own sake

Pro pace comes from control and timing, and from years of conditioning. The Padel School notes that the explosiveness you see reflects serious physical preparation. Copy the patience; build the fitness before you copy the speed.

Their personal technique

On The Padel School Podcast, the Farquharson brothers point out that almost all pros learned on the same basics, such as the continental grip and early preparation, and then developed personal variations suited to their size, handedness and style. Those variations may not suit you. Copy the fundamentals they share, not the quirks that make them unique, and practise any grip changes in a low-pressure setting before using them in matches.

Watching live

If a Premier Padel or national event comes near you, go. The Padel School argues that watching live shows you things TV hides: the real height of the lobs, the speed of the recovery to the net and how much of the game is slow and controlled. Sit behind the back glass for a set to see the court the way the players do.

Frequently asked questions

Can watching padel make me a better player?

Yes, if you watch with a purpose. Pick one thing per match, such as pair positioning or lob depth, watch one player, and try to apply it in your next game.

Where can I watch padel for free?

Premier Padel streams early rounds on its YouTube channel in most countries, and Red Bull TV shows later rounds in some territories. Rights vary by country and event, so check Premier Padel's official site.

What should beginners watch in pro padel?

Where the pair stands, how often they lob and how they move forward together after a good lob. Those habits matter more than any single shot.

Should I copy the pros' smashes?

Not as your main finish. The x3 and x4 smashes are low-percentage shots at club level. Copy the deep bandeja and recovery to the net first.

Do pros make more errors than winners?

Yes. A 2022 analysis of professional padel found both men and women made more errors than winners, and most golden points ended in an error.
