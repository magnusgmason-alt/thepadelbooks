Cramps during padel: what causes them and how to prevent them

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 15 Nov 2026

A cramp in the calf or thigh in the third set, or the night after a long tournament, is a common padel complaint. Most people blame dehydration or a lack of salt, but the research suggests fatigue plays a big part. Here's what's going on and what you can do.

General information, not medical advice

This page gives general information. It can't diagnose you or replace an assessment. If you have a health condition, a recent injury or symptoms that worry you, check with a doctor or physiotherapist.

What causes exercise cramps?

A 2009 review by Schwellnus in the British Journal of Sports Medicine examined the evidence for the two main theories. It concluded that the evidence for dehydration and electrolyte loss as the cause of exercise-associated cramps is weak, and that the cramps are more likely linked to altered neuromuscular control in tired muscles. Cramps tend to show up when muscles work harder or longer than usual: long matches, tournament days, or playing at a higher intensity than your fitness allows.

That doesn't mean fluid and food don't matter: you play worse and tire faster if you're dehydrated or underfuelled, which can make fatigue cramps more likely.

How to stop a cramp

  1. Stop and stretch the muscle gently. For the calf, pull the toes towards you with the leg straight. For the hamstring, straighten the knee.
  2. Hold the stretch until the cramp eases, usually within a minute or so.
  3. Massage the muscle gently afterwards.
  4. Carry on carefully. If cramps keep coming back in the same match, the muscle is overloaded; slow down if you can.

How to prevent cramps

  • Build fitness gradually. Muscles that are used to long matches cramp less. See padel stamina.
  • Strengthen calves and thighs: calf raises, squats and lunges. See strength training.
  • Pace yourself in long tournaments: warm up again before each match.
  • Eat a proper meal before playing and snack between tournament matches. See what to eat before padel.
  • Drink regularly, especially in heat.

Cramps at night

The NHS notes that leg cramps at night are common and usually harmless, and recommends regular calf stretches. It suggests seeing a GP if cramps happen often, affect your daily life, last a long time, or come with numbness, swelling or muscle weakness, as well as if they started after taking a new medicine.

Frequently asked questions

Why do I get cramps playing padel?

Mostly because of muscle fatigue: long or intense matches, tournaments, or playing above your current fitness level. Dehydration and low food intake can contribute but are probably not the main cause.

Does salt or electrolytes prevent cramps?

Evidence that electrolyte loss causes exercise cramps is weak. Fluids and food still help you play well, but fitness and pacing matter more.

How do I stop a calf cramp on court?

Stop, straighten the leg and gently pull your toes towards you until it eases, then massage the muscle.

When should I see a doctor about cramps?

If cramps are frequent, last a long time, disturb your sleep a lot, or come with swelling, numbness or weakness.
