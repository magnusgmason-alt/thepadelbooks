Rotator cuff pain from padel

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 17 Nov 2026

Shoulder pain in padel is usually rotator cuff pain. The rotator cuff is a group of four small muscles that keep the ball of the shoulder centred while the big muscles swing the arm. Overload them with too many overheads and they complain. The good news: most rotator cuff pain improves with the right exercise and a sensible return to overheads.

Not medical advice

This guide explains common patterns in plain language. It can't diagnose you. If the pain is severe, came from a fall or twist, or isn't settling, get it assessed.

What the rotator cuff does

The rotator cuff wraps around the top of the upper arm bone and holds it in the shallow shoulder socket as the arm moves. In padel it controls the arm during the fast swing of a smash and slows it down afterwards. The Aspetar review of padel injuries names the supraspinatus, the cuff muscle at the top of the shoulder, as the one most often affected, mostly from repeated overheads such as the smash and bandeja.

How rotator cuff pain feels

The Leicestershire NHS physiotherapy leaflet describes rotator cuff-related pain as felt in the shoulder and upper arm, and painful when lifting the arm away from the body, reaching behind your back, lifting objects or lying on that side. nidirect lists tendonitis, bursitis and impingement among the causes of shoulder pain that's often worse while using the arm. Different labels, similar picture: the cuff and nearby tissues can't currently cope with the load.

Pain that is sudden and severe, after a fall, with an arm you can't lift, or with numbness, is a different situation. See the warning signs below.

Why padel players get it

  • Overhead volume. Dozens of bandejas, víboras and smashes per match. The NHS leaflet explains that pain appears when load rises beyond what the cuff can handle.
  • Big muscles, small stabilisers. The Aspetar review notes the powerful muscles that drive the swing can outgrow the small ones that control the joint.
  • Shoulder-blade control. Poberaj's review links weak muscles around the shoulder blade to rotator cuff problems, and notes repeated overheads can change the shoulder's rotation range.
  • Technique. Hitting from behind the head and swinging with the arm alone, rather than turning the body.

Recovery: keep moving, load gradually

nidirect's NHS advice: stay active and gently move the shoulder, don't stop using it completely, avoid what makes it worse, and do shoulder exercises for six to eight weeks to stop the pain returning. Mild shoulder pain can take four to six weeks to recover fully, and it usually takes about two weeks of self-care before it starts to ease. Pain relief and heat or cold packs can help you keep moving.

The exercises that matter most for the cuff: band external rotation, side-lying external rotation, 90/90 rotation once the shoulder tolerates it, and shoulder-blade work such as the prone Y raise. Our 6-week shoulder plan puts them in order.

Prevention and warning signs

A shoulder programme in the warm-up three times a week cut the odds of shoulder problems by 28% in 660 handball players (Andersson and colleagues, 2017). Pair that with good overhead technique; see shoulder pain when smashing.

See a GP if it doesn't improve after two weeks, if it's very hard to move your arm, or if it started after a fall. Go to an emergency department if:

  • the pain is sudden or very bad
  • you can't move your arm
  • your arm or shoulder has changed shape or is badly swollen
  • you have pins and needles that don't go away, or no feeling in your arm or shoulder
  • your arm or shoulder is hot or cold to touch

Frequently asked questions

What is a rotator cuff injury in padel?

Usually an overload of the small shoulder muscles and tendons that control the arm during overheads, most often the supraspinatus.

How long does rotator cuff pain take to heal?

nidirect says mild shoulder pain can take four to six weeks to recover fully. Exercises should continue for six to eight weeks to stop it returning.

Should I rest a sore rotator cuff completely?

No. Keep the shoulder moving gently and avoid what makes it worse; stopping completely can slow recovery.

What exercises help the rotator cuff for padel?

Band and side-lying external rotation, 90/90 rotation, internal rotation, and shoulder-blade exercises such as prone Y raises and wall slides.

Is a rotator cuff tear the same as rotator cuff pain?

Not necessarily. Sudden, severe pain with an arm you can't lift after an injury can mean a torn tendon and needs urgent assessment.
