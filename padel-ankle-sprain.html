Padel ankle sprain: what to do, how long it takes and how to stop it happening again

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 15 Nov 2026

Rolling your ankle is one of the classic padel injuries: a quick side step, a ball under your foot, or a landing on someone else's shoe. Most sprains get better with good self-care, but many people go back too early and sprain it again. Here's what to do in the first days, how long recovery usually takes, and the exercises that make a repeat much less likely.

General information, not medical advice

This page explains common causes and general self-care. It can't diagnose your pain. If pain is severe, follows a fall or a loud pop, stops you putting weight on the leg, or isn't improving after a couple of weeks, see a doctor or physiotherapist.

The first 48 hours

NHS advice for sprains is to:

  1. Rest. Stop playing and avoid putting too much weight on the ankle at first. Crutches can help if walking hurts.
  2. Ice. An ice pack (or frozen peas) wrapped in a towel, up to 20 minutes every 2–3 hours.
  3. Support. A bandage or ankle support can make it more comfortable.
  4. Raise it. Keep the ankle up on a pillow when you can.
  5. Avoid heat, alcohol and massage for the first couple of days, as these can increase swelling.

Get it checked urgently if you heard a crack, can't put weight on it, the ankle looks misshapen, or your foot is numb or cold.

How long does a sprained ankle take?

NHS guidance says most sprains start to feel better within about two weeks, while more severe ones can take months to fully recover. Being able to walk normally is not the same as being ready for padel: the ankle needs strength, balance and confidence for quick sideways movement.

StageTypical timing (mild sprain)Focus
SettleDays 1–3Protect, ice, raise, gentle toe and ankle movement
MoveDays 3–14Walking normally, range of movement, calf raises, balance
StrengthenWeeks 2–4Single-leg work, band exercises, small hops
Padel-specificWeeks 3–6Side steps, split steps, short rallies, then full play

Timings vary a lot with the severity of the sprain. If you're not making progress, see a physiotherapist.

Rehab: the exercises that matter

A 2009 BMJ trial (Hupperets and colleagues) found that an 8-week home-based balance programme after an ankle sprain reduced repeat sprains. Balance work is the one thing not to skip.

ExerciseDoseProgression
Ankle circles and alphabet2 × each directionDaily from the first days
Calf raises3 × 12Two legs → one leg → slow lowering
Single-leg balance3 × 30 sEyes closed, cushion, catching a ball
Band eversion and inversion2 × 15Stronger band
Side hops and split steps3 × 20 sOnly once pain-free on single-leg hops

Back on court

Before full matches, you should be able to hop on the injured leg without pain, balance on it as well as on the other, and do side shuffles and quick changes of direction confidently. Start with drills and short rallies, then a set, then full matches. A brace or tape can help while you rebuild. Our guide to returning to padel after injury has a full step-by-step plan.

Stop it happening again

  • Keep doing balance work twice a week for at least a couple of months.
  • Clear loose balls off the court.
  • Wear padel shoes with grip that suits the surface.
  • Warm up with side steps and calf raises. See the padel warm-up.

Frequently asked questions

How long after a sprained ankle can I play padel?

Mild sprains often allow a gradual return in a few weeks, once you can hop, balance and change direction without pain. More severe sprains can take months.

Should I put ice on a sprained ankle?

NHS advice is to use an ice pack wrapped in a towel for up to 20 minutes every 2–3 hours in the first days, along with rest, support and raising the ankle.

How do I prevent another ankle sprain?

Keep up balance and calf exercises, clear balls from the court, wear padel shoes and warm up properly. Balance training reduced repeat sprains in a BMJ trial.

Should I wear an ankle brace for padel?

A brace or tape can help while you rebuild strength after a sprain, but it doesn't replace balance and strength exercises.

When is an ankle sprain serious?

If you heard a crack, can't bear weight, the ankle looks deformed, or it isn't improving, get it checked as it may be a fracture or a more serious ligament injury.
