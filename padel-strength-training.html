Strength training for padel: a gym programme for club players

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 15 Nov 2026

Strength training is the single best thing most padel players can add to their week. It makes your lunges and push-offs stronger, your shots more stable, and your body far less likely to break down. You don't need a bodybuilding plan: two 30-minute sessions a week, built around a handful of movements, is enough. Here's what to train, how many sets and reps, and how to fit it around your matches.

General information, not medical advice

This page explains common patterns and general training principles. It can't diagnose you or replace an assessment. If you have a heart condition, a recent injury or any medical concern, check with a doctor or physiotherapist before starting a new programme.

Why strength matters in padel

Padel is a game of short bursts: push-offs, braking, lunges and rotation. Stronger legs and trunk let you produce and absorb those forces with less effort. The injury evidence is just as persuasive. A 2014 meta-analysis (Lauersen and colleagues) found strength training reduced sports injuries to less than a third and overuse injuries by almost half, a far bigger effect than stretching. The WHO recommends muscle-strengthening activity on at least two days a week for all adults.

Sets, reps and effort

For most club players, 2–3 sets of 8–12 repetitions per exercise works well. Choose a weight you could lift two or three more times at the end of each set: hard, but with good form. Rest 60–90 seconds between sets. When all sets feel comfortable, add a little weight or a couple of reps. Progress slowly; tendons adapt more slowly than muscles.

Fitting it around padel

  • Train on days you don't play, or after a light session, not right before a match.
  • Leave a day between heavy leg sessions and important matches.
  • In a busy match week, cut to one session and fewer sets rather than skipping completely.
  • Before a tournament, keep the weights but drop the volume for a few days.

Common mistakes

  • Only training the upper body. Padel starts in the legs.
  • Going too heavy too soon, especially with lunges and calf work.
  • Skipping the forearm and rotator cuff: small muscles, big injury payoff.
  • Training to failure every set. Leave a couple of reps in reserve.

Frequently asked questions

Should padel players lift weights?

Yes. Strength training improves your ability to push off, brake and rotate, and it is one of the best-supported ways to reduce sports injuries.

How many times a week should I do strength training for padel?

Twice a week is a good target for most club players.

What are the best gym exercises for padel?

Squats, split squats or lunges, Romanian deadlifts, calf raises, push-ups and rows, plus anti-rotation core work and rotator cuff and forearm exercises.

Should I lift weights before playing padel?

Not heavy leg work. Train on non-playing days or after a light session so your legs are fresh for matches.

How many sets and reps for padel strength?

2–3 sets of 8–12 reps, stopping each set with two or three reps left in the tank.
