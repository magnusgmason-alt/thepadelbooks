Can I play padel with tennis elbow?

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 17 Nov 2026

Most players with tennis elbow don't need to stop padel completely, but they do need to stop playing the way that caused it. The aim is to keep the elbow below its irritation threshold while you strengthen it. Here's how to judge that, and the changes to grip, shots and schedule that make the biggest difference.

Not medical advice

This guide explains common patterns in plain language. It can't diagnose you. If the pain is severe, came from a fall or twist, or isn't settling, get it assessed.

The NHS starting point

The NHS's first-line advice for tennis elbow is to avoid or reduce the activities that make your symptoms worse. Its other self-care steps:

  • avoid or reduce the activities that make it worse
  • paracetamol, or an anti-inflammatory gel rubbed onto the sore area
  • a hot or cold pack wrapped in a towel for up to 20 minutes every 2 to 3 hours
  • simple exercises such as bending and straightening the arm
  • a forearm strap, or a wrist or elbow brace, from a pharmacy

"Reduce" is the key word. For many padel players that means fewer and shorter sessions for a few weeks, not a total ban.

How to tell if padel is too much

Use the next-morning check, a pain-monitoring approach widely used in tendon rehabilitation:

During padelNext morningVerdict
Little or no painNormalFine, keep going and keep strengthening
Moderate pain, up to about 5 out of 10Back to its usual levelAcceptable, don't increase playing time yet
Strong or rising painWorse than beforeToo much: cut back or take a break

Pain gripping a cup or shaking hands the next day is a good sign you overdid it.

Changes that take load off the elbow

  • Relax your grip. Poberaj's review of padel upper-limb injuries reports that loosening the grip in the follow-through reduces the force at the elbow by nearly 30%.
  • Volley from the shoulder. A short punch with a quiet wrist. Let the incoming pace do the work.
  • Bandeja over smash. Turn your body early and swing across the ball rather than snapping the arm.
  • Hit in front. Late contact makes the forearm rescue the shot.
  • Shorter sessions, no back-to-back days. One hour, then a rest day, while it settles.
  • Check your racket. A lighter, less head-heavy racket and a fresh overgrip so you don't need to squeeze. There's little research on rackets, so treat this as sensible advice rather than proof.

Keep strengthening on off days

Strength work is the treatment, not an optional extra. Our 6-week padel elbow plan builds the forearm and shoulder, and a strap may make play more comfortable meanwhile.

When to stop and get help

See a GP if the pain hasn't eased after at least two weeks of rest and self-care; the NHS says physiotherapy may help if it hasn't improved after six weeks of home treatment. Get it checked sooner if your arm is weak, you have numbness or tingling in your hand, the elbow is swollen and hot, or the pain followed a fall or a sudden injury. Steroid injections can give quick short-term relief, but Physiopedia's evidence summary notes they are linked to worse long-term outcomes than physiotherapy, so they're no shortcut back to court.

Frequently asked questions

Should I stop playing padel with tennis elbow?

Usually not completely. Reduce playing time to a level where pain stays moderate and settles by the next morning, and strengthen on your off days.

How long should I rest tennis elbow from padel?

There's no fixed number. Many players cut back for a few weeks. If it hasn't improved after two weeks of self-care, see a GP.

Will a brace let me keep playing?

A forearm strap may make play more comfortable and the NHS suggests trying one, but it doesn't replace reducing load and strengthening.

Can I play padel after a steroid injection for tennis elbow?

Ask the clinician who gave it. Injections can relieve pain short term, but evidence links them to worse long-term results than physiotherapy.

Is it bad to play through tennis elbow pain?

Playing through strong or worsening pain usually makes it last longer. Moderate pain that settles by the next morning is a reasonable limit.
