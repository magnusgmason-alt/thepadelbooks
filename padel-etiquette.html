Padel etiquette: 12 unwritten rules every player should know

6 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 8 Nov 2026

Padel etiquette is mostly common courtesy for four people sharing a small glass box: arrive on time, warm up cooperatively, return balls gently, call your own faults and leave the court when your hour is up. A few of these habits are actually in the FIP rulebook, such as the 20 seconds allowed between points and the ban on hitting the ball at opponents when it isn't in play. Here are twelve rules, a few written and most unwritten, plus how to behave at an americano.

If it's your very first game, our first time playing padel guide has a short do and don't list. This page goes further, including the situations people argue about.

Padel etiquette before you play

1. Arrive early, finish on time

Court time is booked by the hour, and the next group is usually waiting. Padel Browser's rule of thumb is to arrive five minutes early so you can change shoes and be ready when the clock starts, and to clear the court promptly at the end, taking your bottles and rubbish with you. Post-match analysis happens off court.

2. Share the balls and the booking

Take turns bringing a fresh tube of balls, and take turns booking. Padel Browser also suggests thanking whoever organised the game; in a regular group, the person who always books is doing everyone a favour.

3. Never walk onto, or behind, a court mid-rally

Wait for the point to end, catch someone's eye and then go through. Red Bull's guide is clear: never walk into an active court mid-rally. When you change ends, walk round through the doors rather than stepping over or ducking under the net.

The warm-up

4. Warm up with the opponents, not against them

Competition rules allow a short courtesy rally before play: three minutes under the FIP rules. Socially, the idea is the same. Feed balls the other player can hit, mix in some volleys and lobs, and don't smash. Padel Browser calls smashing in the warm-up probably the most commonly broken unwritten rule, and Red Bull's line sums it up: warm-ups are not mini matches. See padel match times and warm-up for the official timings.

Etiquette during points

5. Make sure the receiver is ready

Don't serve while the returner is still walking into position. The FIP rules back this up: a fault can't be called on a serve struck while the receiver wasn't ready, and once the receiver attempts a return they can no longer claim they weren't ready (Rule 6.11). A glance across the net solves it.

6. Call the score before you serve

Social games have no umpire, so say the score out loud before each first serve. It takes one second and prevents the "wait, was that 30–15?" argument three points later. Agree before you start whether you're playing golden point at deuce; see padel scoring.

7. Call your own faults

If the ball touched you, bounced twice before you hit it, or you touched the net, say so straight away. The rules make the first one clear: if the opponents' ball touches your body or clothing rather than your racket, you lose the point (FIP Rule 13k). Padel Browser adds a simple tie-breaker for the double bounce: if you're unsure whether it bounced twice, give the point to the opponents. On close calls you can't settle, Red Bull suggests replaying the point at the same score.

8. Talk to your partner, not at the opponents

"Mine", "yours" and "leave it" are a normal part of doubles. Shouting while an opponent is playing the ball is not. Under the FIP rules, interfering with an opponent's shot is hindrance: deliberate hindrance loses the point, and an involuntary one means the point is replayed, unless the same pair does it again (Rule 11). Red Bull's practical advice is to keep calls to the moment after the opponent has hit and before you or your partner start your stroke. The details are in our guide to padel interference and hindrance.

9. Stop play straight away for a stray ball

A ball rolling in from the next court is a foreign object entering the court, and the point is replayed (FIP Rule 10b), but only if you call it immediately. If you play on, you lose the right to the let (Rule 10e). Calling it after you lost the rally is bad form and against the rules. Return the stray ball to the next court between their points, not mid-rally.

10. Acknowledge a lucky net cord

Nothing in the rulebook asks you to apologise when your ball clips the net and drops over, or flies off the frame for a winner. It's a custom borrowed from tennis, where, as Tennis.com describes, showing that you recognise a lucky break became the norm. A raised hand is enough in padel too. A long apology isn't needed.

11. Return balls gently to the server

Collect the balls on your side and send them to the server with a roll or a gentle underarm bounce. Padel Browser's guide is specific: not by launching them across the court. The FIP's own etiquette section bans hitting the ball at opponents when it isn't in play.

12. Keep the pace and the celebrations civil

Under the FIP rules, play is continuous: a maximum of 20 seconds between points and up to 90 seconds at a change of ends. Socially, that means a sip of water at the change, not a five-minute chat. Celebrate your own good points by all means; Padel Browser draws the line at taunting, sarcastic clapping or celebrating the other pair's errors. At the end, tap rackets or shake hands with all three other players.

Good etiquette is easier when your game is steady. The Padel Playbook: Beginner & Intermediate covers the serve, the walls and where to stand, and The Padel Drill Book has partner and four-player drills that make a far better warm-up than a smashing contest.

The golden rule

Padel Browser's summary is hard to beat: be fair, be respectful and leave the court better than you found it. A reputation for fair play gets you invited to more games than a big smash.

Padel etiquette at an americano

In a padel americano you change partner every round and keep an individual score, so a few extra habits help the evening run.

  • Be ready when your round starts. The schedule depends on every court starting together.
  • Agree the format out loud. Points total, how the serve rotates, one serve or two. Ask the organiser rather than guess.
  • Count the points together. Call the running score as you go, so the result isn't disputed at the end.
  • Report the result straight away. One player per court reports, so the next round can start on time.
  • Play to the agreed total. Don't stop early because you're winning or losing comfortably.
  • Be a good partner to everyone. You'll play with stronger and weaker players. Keep coaching to a minimum unless your partner asks, and be encouraging after their errors.

Frequently asked questions

Should you apologise for a net cord in padel?

It isn't required by any rule. Many players raise a hand to acknowledge a lucky net cord or frame shot, a custom borrowed from tennis. A short gesture is plenty.

Who picks up the balls in padel?

The rulebook doesn't say. The usual courtesy is that each pair collects the balls on their side and returns them gently to the server, by rolling them or with a soft bounce.

Can you talk during a point in padel?

Yes, to your partner: calls like "mine" and "yours" are normal. Noise aimed at putting off an opponent as they play the ball is hindrance under FIP Rule 11.

What happens if a ball rolls onto the court?

Call a let immediately and the point is replayed (FIP Rule 10b). If you keep playing, you lose the right to the let (Rule 10e).

How long can you take between points?

Under the FIP rules, a maximum of 20 seconds between points and up to 90 seconds at a change of ends. Social games are usually relaxed about it, but keep play moving.

Is it rude to smash in the warm-up?

Yes, in social padel. The warm-up is for everyone to find their rhythm. Practise volleys and bandejas and keep the smashes for the match.
