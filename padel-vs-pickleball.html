Padel vs pickleball: what's the difference?

5 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 23 Oct 2026

Padel and pickleball are the two fastest-growing racket sports of the decade, and people often mix them up. Both are social, easy to start and usually played as doubles. But on court they feel completely different. Here's a side-by-side comparison based on the official FIP and USA Pickleball rulebooks.

Side by side

PadelPickleball
Court20 × 10 m20 × 44 ft (about 6.1 × 13.4 m)
WallsFully enclosed by glass and mesh, walls in playOpen court, no walls
Net88 cm in the middle, 92 cm at the posts34 in (86 cm) in the middle, 36 in at the sidelines
RacketSolid, perforated racket, max 45.5 cm long, wrist strap compulsorySolid paddle, length plus width max 24 in
BallPressurised rubber ball, similar to a tennis ballLight perforated plastic ball
ServeUnderarm after a bounce, at or below the waist, diagonalUnderhand, contact below the waist, diagonal, must clear the kitchen
Special zoneNoneThe "kitchen": 7 ft non-volley zone either side of the net
ScoringTennis scoring (15, 30, 40), sets of six gamesGames to 11, win by 2; traditionally only the serving side scores
FormatAlmost always doublesSingles and doubles

The walls change everything

The biggest difference isn't the racket or the ball, it's the enclosure. In padel, once the ball bounces on your side it can come off the back and side glass and you can still play it. Rallies last longer, and positioning and patience matter more than power. Pickleball has no walls; a ball that gets past you is gone, and the game is decided much more by short, fast exchanges at the net.

Two rules unique to pickleball

  • The two-bounce rule: the serve and the return must each bounce before being played. Only then can players volley.
  • The kitchen: you can't volley while standing in the 7-foot non-volley zone next to the net, and momentum carrying you into it after a volley is a fault.

Padel has no equivalent zone. You can volley right up at the net, as long as you don't touch it.

Scoring

Padel uses tennis scoring: points 15, 30, 40, game; sets to six games with tie-breaks; plus the golden point and Star Point options. Pickleball games are played to 11, win by 2, with side-out scoring, where only the serving side can score. USA Pickleball also allows rally scoring as a provisional option for some events.

How big are they?

  • Pickleball: about 19.8 million players in the US in 2024, according to the SFIA, the fastest-growing US sport for the fourth year running.
  • Padel: about 19.4 million players and more than 58,000 courts worldwide, according to the Global Padel Report 2026 by Playtomic and Strategy&.

Which one should you try?

Choose padel if

you like longer rallies, using angles and walls, and a game where tactics and positioning beat raw power. It's a little more physical.

Choose pickleball if

you want the gentlest possible start, short quick exchanges at the net, and courts that are easy to find in the US.

Honestly? Try both. Many players do. If you're starting padel, our rules guide gets you through your first match.

Frequently asked questions

Is padel harder than pickleball?

Both are easy to start. Padel's walls add a learning curve, but they also make rallies longer and more forgiving once you learn to let the ball come off the glass.

Can you use a pickleball paddle for padel?

No. Padel rackets are a different shape and size, perforated, and must have a wrist strap under the FIP rules.

Is the padel court bigger than a pickleball court?

Yes. A padel court is 20 × 10 m; a pickleball court is 20 × 44 ft, roughly 6.1 × 13.4 m.

Equipment and cost

Both sports are cheap to start. You need a racket or paddle, balls and court shoes. Padel rackets are generally thicker and heavier than pickleball paddles, and padel balls are pressurised, so they lose bounce over time and need replacing more often. Court hire is usually the main cost in both sports, and padel courts are often booked by the hour for four players.

What playing feels like

Padel

Longer rallies, lots of lobs and overheads, and constant movement between the net and the back. Walls give you a second chance on balls that get past you.

Pickleball

Shorter points, lots of soft "dinks" near the kitchen line, and quick reflex exchanges at the net. The ball is light and slow, so rallies can be very tactical.

The learning curve

  • Day one: both are easy. Most people can rally within an hour.
  • First months: in padel, the challenge is learning the walls and the overheads (bandeja, víbora). In pickleball, it's the soft game around the kitchen and the two-bounce rule.
  • Long term: both reward tactics and teamwork more than raw power.

For tennis players

Tennis players usually adapt quickly to both. In padel, the hardest habit to break is chasing balls into the back glass instead of letting them come off. The second is swinging too hard: padel rewards control and placement. In pickleball, the adjustment is the underhand serve, the kitchen and learning the soft game.

Fitness

Both are good exercise. Padel usually involves more running between the back and the net and more overhead shots; pickleball has more short, quick movements in a smaller space. Either way, warm up properly and build up your playing time gradually. See our guide to padel elbow for keeping your arm healthy.

Quick verdict

If you love the idea of walls, lobs and longer rallies, padel is your sport. If you want the gentlest possible entry and a soft, tactical net game, start with pickleball. And if you're curious, there's nothing stopping you from playing both.

Common questions

Can you play both with the same shoes?

Yes, as long as they're court shoes with good lateral support. Padel courts with sand-filled artificial grass grip better with a herringbone or omni sole. Running shoes are a poor choice for either sport, because they aren't built for quick side-to-side movement.

Which is better for older players?

Both are popular with older players. Pickleball's smaller court and slower ball make it very accessible. Padel's walls keep the ball in play, so you spend less time picking up balls and more time hitting them. The best choice is the one you'll enjoy and keep playing.

Which is easier to find a game for?

That depends entirely on where you live. Check the clubs and booking apps near you: in some areas padel courts are everywhere, in others pickleball courts are easier to find.

Do skills transfer between the two?

Partly. Both reward good positioning, teamwork, a compact volley and patience. The walls are unique to padel, and the kitchen and two-bounce rule are unique to pickleball, so expect a short adjustment period whichever way you switch.
