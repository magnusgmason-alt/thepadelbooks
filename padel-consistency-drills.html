Padel consistency drills

9 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 31 Oct 2026

Padel consistency drills train you to keep the ball in play with height, depth and margin, so the other pair makes the mistake first. The nine drills below go from a solo wall rally to four-player games, each with a court diagram and a pass mark, and they all reward the same thing: one more good ball than your opponents.

These drills are adapted from The Padel Drill Book, our 100-drill guide for solo, partner and four-player practice.

How to stop making errors in padel

Most unforced errors come from the same few causes: too much pace, too little margin, and shots chosen from a bad position. The LTA describes padel as a game of consistency, and The Padel School's list of beginner mistakes includes overhitting and losing patience. The fixes are simple to say and take practice to do.

  • Play with margin. HEAD's coaching tips suggest shrinking the court in practice so you aim well inside the lines. The walls keep the ball in play; you don't need to paint the corners.
  • Use the cross-court diagonal. The net is lowest in the middle: 0.88 metres at the centre and 0.92 metres at the posts (FIP). A diagonal ball also has more court to land in than a straight one on a 20 by 10 metre court.
  • Control the height. Too low and you hit the net; too high and the net players smash. A height window over the net is easier to repeat than "hit it well".
  • Slow down. Rally at a pace you can repeat 20 times. Speed is easy to add once the ball goes where you want.
  • Build, then attack. Deep balls first, the finish later. Patience wins more points than power.

A solo consistency drill

1. Backhand wall rally

Players: 1 · Time: 10 min · Level: 1–3

Set-up: Stand about four metres from your back glass on the backhand side, continental grip, front shoulder towards the glass.

How it works: Rally backhands against the glass with one bounce between hits. Use a short swing from low to high and finish with the racket pointing at the glass. Stay side-on through contact; opening the chest early sprays the ball wide. Three sets of two minutes, 30 seconds of rest.

Pass mark: 25 clean contacts in two minutes.

Progression: Two forehands, then two backhands, without stopping the rally.

Cross-court rally drill and other rally drills

2. Cross-court rally

Players: 2 · Time: 12 min · Level: 1–4

Set-up: Two players on opposite diagonals, each a metre in front of their back glass. Each target zone runs from two to five metres in front of the back glass.

How it works: Rally cross-court, landing every ball in the other player's zone and clearing the net by about a metre to a metre and a half. If a ball beats you, let the glass bring it back. Height comes from the swing path, not from hitting up at the sky. Keep the pace at around 60% until you can string 20 together. Switch diagonals after four minutes.

Pass mark: A rally of 15 with every ball in the targets.

Progression: Only balls in the target count as in; anything else ends the rally.

3. Net-clearance control

Players: 2 · Time: 10 min · Level: 1–3

Set-up: Tie a towel or ribbon to the net post at 1.5 metres. That gives you a window between the net tape and the marker.

How it works: Rally from the back of the court, with every ball passing through the window. Choose a point in the air above the net and swing through that point, looking at the window rather than the other player. The window doesn't move late in a long rally, when tired players start to change height. Three sets of three minutes.

Pass mark: 25 balls through the window in a three-minute set.

Progression: Narrow the window to between 1 and 1.2 metres above the court.

4. Depth ladder

Players: 2 · Time: 12 min · Level: 2–5

Set-up: T1 is the last three metres before the far back glass. T2 is a shorter zone just in front of the far service line. F sends medium-paced feeds to A, who stands at the back.

How it works: A plays five balls deep into T1, then five short into T2, then alternates. The preparation is identical every time: a longer, higher follow-through sends the ball deep, a shorter swing with a more open face brings it short. Exaggerate the difference at first. Swap roles after 30 balls.

Pass mark: 12 of 20 in the called target.

Progression: F calls the target just before contact.

Consistency at the net

5. Deep block volley

Players: 2 · Time: 12 min · Level: 2–4

Set-up: A at the net. F stands on the far back line and drives balls at A.

How it works: A holds the racket out in front with a firm wrist and a relaxed grip, and blocks the ball rather than swinging at it. The incoming pace does the work. Every volley should land in the last three metres, so the opponent has to play the next ball off the glass. Three sets of 12.

Pass mark: 8 of 12 blocks in T1.

Progression: F mixes in low drives, and A has to block those deep too. Our volley guide covers the block volley in detail.

6. Low volley under pressure

Players: 2 · Time: 10 min · Level: 3–5

Set-up: A stands about three metres from the net, knees bent, racket at knee height. F drives from the far back line.

How it works: F sends low, fast balls that arrive around A's knees. A opens the face slightly and blocks each one deep, about a metre over the net. A ball below the tape can only be sent up and deep, so don't try to hit down. Stay low after the volley; if you stand up straight away, the next ball catches you out. Three sets of 12.

Pass mark: 8 of 12 landing deep.

Progression: F mixes low drives with high balls, to catch a player who stands up too soon.

Games that punish errors

7. Fifteen-ball rally challenge

Players: 4 · Time: 10 min · Level: 1–4

Set-up: Four players, normal positions.

How it works: Rally co-operatively for 15 balls, counting each one out loud. Keep the ball through the middle at medium height. On ball 16 the point becomes live. If the rally breaks before 15, start again at one. The real test is discipline: resist speeding up at 12 or 13 out of boredom.

Pass mark: Six rallies that reach 15 in ten minutes.

Progression: Raise the co-operative count to 25.

8. No down-the-line

Players: 4 · Time: 12 min · Level: 2–5

Set-up: Mark a lane 1.6 metres wide along each side wall with cones, on both halves of the court.

How it works: Play normal points, but any ball that lands in a lane on the opponents' side loses the point for the hitter. That pushes every shot cross-court or through the middle, where there is more court and the net is lower. Play to 11.

Pass mark: Win the game without losing a point to the lane rule.

Progression: Widen the lanes to 2.5 metres. To make it easier, ban down-the-line only from the back of the court, where it is hardest to play well.

9. Target-zone points

Players: 4 · Time: 12 min · Level: 2–5

Set-up: Mark T1 and T2 in the opponents' two back corners.

How it works: Play normal points. Any point won with a ball that landed in T1 or T2 is worth two. Aim for the corner but accept a bounce in the middle: the players who win this game keep a safe margin instead of chasing the target on every ball. First to 15, then change ends.

Pass mark: Win the game with fewer errors than the other pair.

Progression: Targets only count from overheads.

Common consistency mistakes

  • Going for pace too early. If you can't rally 20 at medium pace, you can't rally 10 at full pace.
  • Aiming at the lines. Aim inside them. The glass and the margin are your friends.
  • Going down the line while defending. From the back, few shots are harder to play well. Go cross-court or lob.
  • Hitting down on low balls. Below the net, lift the ball deep and wait for the next one.
  • Switching off in long rallies. Late in a long rally, tired players start to change their height and target. Keep both the same until the point ends.

Many of these habits are also on our list of common padel beginner mistakes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I stop making unforced errors in padel?

Hit with more margin and less pace, play cross-court and through the middle, and control the height over the net. Drills with targets and a score, like the Cross-court rally and Net-clearance control, make those habits automatic.

Why is cross-court safer in padel?

The net is lower in the middle (0.88 metres) than at the posts (0.92 metres), and a diagonal ball has more court to land in. Down the line from the back is one of the hardest shots to play well.

How high over the net should I hit from the back?

For most rally balls, about one to one and a half metres. Lower and you risk the net; much higher and the net players can attack it, unless you are playing a lob on purpose.

What is the best consistency drill for four players?

The Fifteen-ball rally challenge. It rewards patience, everyone gets plenty of touches, and it ends in a live point so the habit is tested under pressure.

Can I improve consistency without a partner?

Yes. Forehand and backhand wall rallies against the back glass give you hundreds of repetitions in a short session, with a simple score to beat.
