Padel levels explained: what level are you?

8 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 23 Oct 2026

Padel levels explained in one line: there's no single official scale. Most systems run from about 0 or 1 (complete beginner) up to 7 (elite), but booking apps, national federations and clubs each use their own version, and the same number can mean different things. Here's how the main scales work, how they compare, and how to work out your own level honestly.

Padel levels explained: why they matter

Padel is a doubles game played in a small space, so a mismatch ruins it for everyone. If three players are rallying comfortably and the fourth can't return a serve, nobody has fun. That's why booking apps, clubs and leagues ask for your level before you join an open match, a social night or a tournament. A level is simply a shorthand for "who should I be playing with?"

The catch: different organisations use different scales. Always check which one a club or app is using before you pick a number.

Padel level rating: the main systems

Playtomic: 0 to 7

Playtomic, the booking app many players meet first, uses a scale from 0 to 7, with 7 the highest. You get a starting level by answering a short questionnaire about your experience when you create your profile, and you can only do that once. After that, the level changes automatically based on competitive match results: the result itself, your opponents' levels, your partner's level and your "reliability".

Reliability is the key idea. New players have low reliability, so their level moves a lot after each match; as you play more competitive matches, it settles down. Beating higher-rated players lifts you more, and losing to lower-rated players drops you more. Friendly matches don't count. A levelling assessment at a certified Playtomic Leveling Club can set your reliability to 50% straight away.

LTA (Great Britain): 1.0 to 7.0

The LTA uses a self-rating scale from 1.0 to 7.0 in half-point steps, except that 1.5 isn't used. Ratings are grouped into three bands: beginner (1.0, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0), intermediate (3.5 to 5.0) and advanced (5.5 to 7.0). You pick the statement that best describes your game. The advanced band is tied to competition: 5.5 is for nationally ranked players, and 6.0 to 7.0 depend on professional rankings.

World Padel Rating (US): 0 to 21

In July 2024 the United States Padel Association (USPA) announced World Padel Rating (WPR) as its official rating system. WPR uses a wider scale, from 0 to 21, and tracks a confidence level for the accuracy of each player's rating.

Padel levels ABCD: letter categories

Many clubs and tournaments group players into letter categories rather than numbers. In this system D is the lowest and A, or "Open", the highest. One Australian rating guide maps ratings to categories like this:

Rating bandDescriptionTypical category
1.0–2.5BeginnerD to C
3.0–4.0IntermediateC to B
4.5–5.5AdvancedB to Open
6.0–7.0ExpertOpen

Spanish-speaking clubs often use numbered categories instead, and here the order is reversed: a lower number means a better player. In one Spanish club tournament guide, 4ª is for beginners and players who aren't yet consistent, 3ª for intermediate players who keep the ball in play at medium and high pace, and 2ª for advanced players who control direction, pace and depth on both sides. Category systems vary from club to club, so always read the local definitions.

Padel level chart: what each stage looks like

Playtomic describes seven stages of a padel player's development on its 0 to 7 scale, shown in its help pages as a chart from the bottom of the scale to the top. Here they are in plain English, alongside our rough guide to the matching LTA band. Treat that column as a guide, not an official conversion.

Padel level 2 and level 3: what do they mean?

Because scales differ, "level 2" and "level 3" mean slightly different things in different places. In practice, on the 0–7 and 1–7 scales most clubs use:

  • Level 2 is a beginner who can rally a little and serve legally but still finds the walls confusing. Playing off the back glass is the skill that moves you on.
  • Level 3 is a developing player who rallies at a moderate pace, starts to use the glass and knows the basic positions, but still makes plenty of unforced errors. On the LTA scale, 3.0 is the top of the beginner band and 3.5 the start of intermediate.

The jump to level 4 usually comes from three things: regular volleys at the net, a reliable bandeja, and moving with your partner instead of on your own.

What padel level am I? A quick self-check

Answer honestly. It's common to rate yourself a little too high.

  1. Can you rally ten shots in a row cross-court at a gentle pace? If not, you're at the very start of the scale.
  2. Can you return a ball that has come off the back glass, most of the time? If yes, you're past the beginner stage.
  3. Do you volley at the net and lob to win the net back, rather than staying at the back all game?
  4. Can you hit a controlled bandeja when lobbed, and keep your net position?
  5. Do you and your partner move together, and can you change the pace and direction of the ball on purpose?
  6. Do you win matches in local leagues or tournaments against players rated at your level?

Each yes moves you up roughly a stage. If you're unsure between two levels, pick the lower one: playing up feels better than spoiling a game. Better still, book a levelling assessment at a certified Playtomic Leveling Club. It gives you an outside view and a more stable starting rating.

How to move up a level

  • Play competitive matches on the app or in a league, not just friendlies, so your rating reflects your game.
  • Play some matches with stronger players. On rating systems like Playtomic's, beating higher-rated opponents counts for more.
  • Train one skill at a time. Our guide on how to get better at padel sets out a plan, and the beginner drills give you the practice.
  • Cut the errors. At most club levels, the pair that misses less wins. See common padel mistakes.

Frequently asked questions

Is there an official padel level scale?

No single one. Booking apps such as Playtomic, national federations such as the LTA and rating platforms such as WPR each use their own scale. Most run from about 0 or 1 to 7, but WPR goes to 21.

What is a good padel level?

On a 0–7 or 1–7 scale, the middle of the range is a solid club player who uses the walls, volleys and moves with a partner. The top of most scales is reserved for ranked tournament players: on the LTA scale, 5.5 and above.

Is category A or D better in padel?

A, or Open, is the highest; D is the lowest. In numbered Spanish categories it's reversed: a lower number means a better player.

How does my Playtomic level go up?

By winning competitive matches, especially against higher-rated players. Friendly matches don't change your level, and new players' levels move more because their reliability is low.

Should I put myself in a lower level?

If you're between two levels, yes. Starting a little low and moving up is better for everyone than starting too high.
