Padel lessons: what they cost and how to get the most from them

7 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 30 Oct 2026

Padel lessons are worth it for almost every new player: a few sessions with a qualified coach early on fix your grip, your position and your play off the glass before bad habits set in. Prices published in October 2026 include £12 to £22 per person for a shared hour at one Edinburgh club and about $20 to $45 per person in the US, with private lessons costing more. This guide covers formats, prices, how many lessons you need and how to choose a coach.

Are padel lessons worth it?

For most players, yes, and the earlier the better. Padel is easy to start but has a few skills that don't come naturally, especially to people from tennis: the continental grip, a compact swing, where to stand as a pair, and above all how to play the ball after it comes off the glass. The LTA says a coach can help you learn padel's techniques and strategies, and names playing off the glass as one of its three tips for beginners, adding that coaching helps with it.

You can learn by playing with friends, and many people do. The Padel School says your first experience is more likely to be with friends or colleagues than with a coach. The risk is that you groove habits that work against you, such as hitting too hard or volleying everything to avoid the wall, and those are harder to undo after a year of practice.

The short answer

A handful of lessons in your first months is one of the best-value things you can buy in padel. It will usually do more for your game than an expensive racket.

Private vs group padel lessons

Padel lessons come in several formats. Many coaches recommend mixing them.

FormatPlayersBest forTrade-off
Private (1-to-1)1Fixing a specific problem, fast feedbackMost expensive per person; no doubles patterns
Semi-private2–3Friends or a regular partner learning togetherLess individual attention
Group lesson or clinicUsually 4Repetition, doubles positioning, valueCoach's attention is shared
Beginner courseSmall group, weeklyStructured start over several weeksFixed schedule

Padel Browser's guide to choosing a coach suggests private lessons when you want to fix one thing and group lessons for drills and doubles patterns, and says a hybrid, such as one private session a month plus weekly group clinics, is often recommended. Because padel is a game of four, group lessons are particularly useful: you learn where to stand and how to move with a partner, which a 1-to-1 can't fully teach.

What you learn in a first padel lesson

A first lesson is usually a mix of short drills and simple play. Coaches vary, but these topics come up again and again in beginner sessions and courses such as the LTA's Padel Xpress, which covers the basic shots, using the glass and an introduction to tactics:

  • The grip and ready position. Expect to start with the continental grip, the standard padel grip. See our padel grip guide.
  • Forehand and backhand from the back. Compact swings, played at a controlled pace.
  • Volleys at the net. Short, firm blocks rather than big swings.
  • The back glass. Letting the ball come off the wall and playing it as it drops.
  • The serve and the basic rules. Enough to play points.
  • Where to stand. Moving up and back with your partner.

Padel Browser's survey of US intro clinics found every one it checked ran for 60 minutes. They cover scoring, positioning and the fact that a ball coming off the back glass after one bounce is still live, with drills such as serves, volleys and rebounds, and every description promised you'd be playing points by the end. Rackets are usually included and balls always are. If it's your very first time on court, our guide to your first padel session covers what to bring.

How many lessons does it take to learn padel?

There's no official number, and nobody can promise you a level after a set number of hours. A few useful markers:

  • To get started: Padel Browser suggests taking a first lesson before your second or third game, to fix the basics early, and a pack of about five lessons once you've played 5 to 15 matches.
  • To build solid basics: a short course is a good structure. The LTA's Padel Xpress, for example, runs for six weekly sessions for adults new to the game.
  • To add a specific shot: Padel Browser suggests intermediate and advanced players work in blocks of around 6 to 12 weekly lessons focused on one shot or pattern, such as the bandeja.

What matters as much as the number of lessons is what you do between them. A lesson shows you the movement; playing and drilling make it stick. Our beginner padel drills are designed to be practised between sessions.

How to choose a padel coach

Check the qualification

In Britain, the LTA runs the LTA Padel Instructor qualification, a five-day course that prepares coaches to run group lessons for adults and juniors. The LTA's online coach finder lists accredited coaches. Elsewhere, look for national federation or FIP-recognised qualifications. Padel Browser notes that a qualification doesn't guarantee a great teacher, but having none is a warning sign. A tennis coaching badge alone isn't a padel qualification, so ask a tennis coach about their padel experience.

Watch how they run a session

Good signs

The coach asks about your level and goals, gives you one or two clear things to work on, builds from drills to live play, and tells you what to practise next.

Warning signs

The lesson is just the coach feeding balls with no plan, the same comments every week, or corrections without anything specific to try.

Ask three questions

  1. What will we work on first, and why? A good coach can explain a plan in a sentence.
  2. Do you film or analyse points? Video is one of the fastest ways to see your own habits.
  3. What should I practise between lessons? If the answer is vague, the progress will be too.

Frequently asked questions

How much do padel lessons cost?

It varies by country and club. Examples published in October 2026 include £35 to £45 for a private hour and £12 to £22 per person for a group hour in Edinburgh, and roughly $50 to $120 private or $20 to $45 per person in a group in the US.

Are group padel lessons better than private lessons?

They do different jobs. Group lessons are cheaper and teach doubles positioning and movement with a partner. Private lessons give faster, individual feedback on a specific problem. Many players mix both.

How many padel lessons do I need?

There's no fixed number. A first lesson early on fixes the basics, and a short beginner course, such as the LTA's six-week Padel Xpress, gives you a solid start. After that, take lessons in blocks when you want to add a specific shot.

Do I need my own racket for a padel lesson?

Not usually. Intro sessions and beginner courses often provide rackets and balls. Check with the club when you book.

Can tennis players skip padel lessons?

They can play straight away, but a lesson or two is still worth it. The LTA says the back glass is the biggest early challenge for tennis players, and tennis habits like big swings and hard drives often work against you in padel.
