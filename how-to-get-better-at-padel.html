How to get better at padel, fast

6 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 23 Oct 2026

How to get better at padel comes down to four habits: control before power, playing off the back glass, moving with your partner, and practising one skill at a time between matches. That's the short version, and it works at every level. Below is a practical plan for turning court time into real progress, including what to do when you can't get on court.

The big idea: consistency first

At club level, padel matches are won by the pair that makes fewer mistakes. The LTA calls padel "a game of consistency", and every coaching source we checked says some version of the same thing: accuracy and placement matter more than power. So the fastest way to get better is not a new shot. It's missing less.

That shapes everything below. Learn the shots that keep you in the point, practise them until they're boring, and only then add the attacking shots.

How to get better at padel as a beginner: the skills in order

Build your game in roughly this order. Each step makes the next one easier.

  1. Controlled forehands and backhands. Short backswing, contact in front, ball deep and through the middle. Grip matters here; see our padel grip guide.
  2. The back glass. Let the ball come off the wall and play it as it drops. This is the skill that moves most beginners up a level. See how to play off the back glass.
  3. The volley. A short, firm punch from about two to three metres off the net. Our volley guide covers it.
  4. The lob. Your way from the back to the net.
  5. The bandeja. The controlled overhead that lets you keep the net when lobbed.
  6. The serve and return. Not glamorous, but they start every point. See serve and return.

The víbora, the smash off the glass and the chiquita come later. You'll get far more out of a reliable lob and bandeja.

How to practise so it actually works

One skill per session

The LTA's advice is to practise one skill per session. Trying to fix your volley, your lob and your back glass in the same hour usually fixes none of them.

Set targets

Count. Rally cross-court and set a target number of shots without an error. The LTA's "ladder rally" is a good one: rally two shots, then three, then four, and any mistake sends you back to the start.

Practise the glass with a feeder

One player throws the ball at the floor just in front of the back wall; the other lets it rebound and returns it softly. To make it harder, use the corner so the ball comes off two walls. Ten minutes of this every session builds the skill quickly.

Play pressure games

The LTA's "yellow card, red card" game is excellent: play normally, but your first error in a game is a yellow card and a second error in the same game is a red card, which loses you the game. It trains exactly what wins matches.

Play smaller to miss less

HEAD suggests practising on a smaller court area to build margin for error. If you can rally on a tight target, the full court feels easy.

How to get good at padel fast

There's no shortcut past practice, but these speed things up the most.

  • Take lessons early. The LTA recommends a qualified coach for technique and game understanding, and The Padel School notes that many players turn to lessons when their progress stalls. A few early lessons stop bad habits before they set.
  • Play often, and increase gradually. Regular short sessions beat one long one. If you're new to sport, build up your number of games a week gradually so your body adapts, as the sports-science advice in the Aspetar Sports Medicine Journal recommends.
  • Play with slightly better players. They punish your mistakes and show you better positions.
  • Play competitions or leagues. Match pressure exposes what practice hides, and the LTA recommends competitions and ladders for exactly that.
  • Slow down. The Padel School's advice is to be patient, slow down mentally and physically, and think before you hit. Good players seem to have more time; mostly, they just don't rush.

How to get better at padel at home

You can't recreate the glass at home, but you can train the hands, eyes and legs.

  • Racket control: tap a ball up in the air with your racket, alternating faces. The LTA recommends this for touch.
  • Wall volleys: volley against an outside wall to build consistency. Keep the swing short and the racket up.
  • Footwork: practise short side steps and a split step, staying on the balls of your feet.
  • Shadow swings: slow-motion bandejas and volleys in front of a mirror, checking that you turn side-on.
  • Watch padel with a purpose: pick one player and watch only where they stand and when they move forward.
  • Strength and mobility: padel involves lunges, rotation and overheads. Our padel exercises guide covers safe options.

Padel tips and tricks that work at every level

  1. Play through the middle. Balls down the middle make opponents hesitate about who takes them, and the net is lowest there.
  2. Aim at the feet of the net player, so they have to hit upwards.
  3. Use the lob when both opponents are at the net, then move up together.
  4. Racket up at the net, every time.
  5. Move as a pair. If your partner moves, you move.
  6. Only smash if you can get back to the net afterwards. Otherwise, play a bandeja.
  7. Don't stay in no man's land. Be at the net or at the back.
  8. Talk. Call every shared ball.
  9. Warm up properly. Light movement first, then padel-specific movements for arms, shoulders and legs, as the LTA recommends. See our padel warm-up.

Track your progress

Improvement in padel is easy to feel and hard to see. Keep it simple:

  • Count unforced errors in one set a week. If the number falls, you're getting better, whatever the score.
  • Note how many balls off the back glass you return in a session.
  • Record a match on your phone every month and watch your positioning, not your shots.
  • If you use a booking app with ratings, play competitive matches so your level reflects your game. Our guide to padel levels explains how ratings work.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get good at padel?

It depends on how often you play, whether you have coaching and your racket-sport background. Most beginners rally in their first session; becoming a solid club player usually takes months of regular play.

What's the fastest way to improve at padel?

Cut your errors. Play with control, learn the back glass, move with your partner, and get a few lessons early.

Can I improve at padel without a partner?

Yes. Wall volleys, racket control drills, footwork and strength work all help. Some clubs also have ball machines.

Should I take padel lessons?

If you can, yes, especially early. A coach fixes grip, positioning and wall play before habits set, which saves you months.

Does playing more matches make you better?

Matches help, especially competitive ones, but combine them with focused practice. Matches show what's wrong; practice fixes it.
