Shoulder pain when smashing in padel

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 17 Nov 2026

If your shoulder is fine on groundstrokes but complains on every smash, bandeja or víbora, the overhead is your problem, and usually not because of one bad shot. It's the number of overheads, the position you hit them from, and how much of the work your arm does compared with your legs and trunk. Fix those three and most overhead pain settles.

Not medical advice

This guide explains common patterns in plain language. It can't diagnose you. If the pain is severe, came from a fall or twist, or isn't settling, get it assessed.

Why overheads load the shoulder

The Aspetar review of padel injuries describes most padel shoulder problems as overuse injuries from repeated overhead movements such as the smash and bandeja, with the supraspinatus, one of the rotator cuff muscles, the most commonly affected. The same review points out that the big muscles that power the swing can outgrow the small stabilising muscles that control the joint. Poberaj's review of upper-limb injuries adds that repeated overheads can change the shoulder's rotation range and that weak muscles around the shoulder blade contribute to rotator cuff problems.

The technique faults that hurt

FaultWhy it hurtsFix
Hitting from behind your headThe shoulder is forced into an extreme, loaded positionMove your feet back first so the ball is in front
Facing the netNo trunk turn, so the arm makes all the powerTurn side-on early
Smashing everythingMaximum-effort swings, many times a matchBandeja to keep the net; save the flat smash for easy balls
Snapping straight downBig deceleration load on the shoulderSwing across your body, as LTA Padel teaches for the bandeja
Jumping on every overheadTiming gets harder, contact gets lateStay grounded unless you're well set

LTA Padel's bandeja advice is to turn to face the side of the court, let the ball drop to about eye height and swing across the body, taking pace off the ball. The Padel School stresses an early turn, moving the feet into position and contact in front. Both put the work into the legs and trunk.

Count your overheads

The Leicestershire NHS physiotherapy leaflet explains that rotator cuff pain usually appears when load rises beyond what the cuff can currently handle, suddenly or gradually. In padel the spike is easy to spot: a tournament weekend, an hour against a lobbing pair, or a new habit of smashing every ball. When the shoulder is grumbling, cut the number of hard overheads first and let your partner take the middle lobs for a while.

Build an overhead-proof shoulder

In a trial of 660 elite handball players (Andersson and colleagues, 2017), a short shoulder programme three times a week in the warm-up cut the odds of shoulder problems by 28%. It trained rotation range, rotator cuff strength, shoulder-blade strength, upper-back mobility and the legs-to-arm chain. Our 6-week shoulder plan uses the same building blocks; the 90/90 external rotation is the key exercise for the smash position.

Coming back to the smash

  1. Shadow overheads at half speed, pain-free.
  2. Bandejas from easy feeds, a few at a time.
  3. More bandejas and víboras in drills, then in matches.
  4. Flat smashes last, starting with a handful per session.

Move on only when the shoulder is fine during the session and the next morning. If pain persists beyond two weeks, see a GP or physio; get immediate help if:

  • the pain is sudden or very bad
  • you can't move your arm
  • your arm or shoulder has changed shape or is badly swollen
  • you have pins and needles that don't go away, or no feeling in your arm or shoulder
  • your arm or shoulder is hot or cold to touch

Frequently asked questions

Why does my shoulder hurt when I smash in padel?

Usually from repeated overhead load on the rotator cuff, made worse by hitting from behind your head, facing the net and using the arm instead of a body turn.

Is the bandeja easier on the shoulder than the smash?

Generally yes. It's a controlled swing across the body with less pace, which loads the shoulder less than a maximum-effort flat smash.

Should I stop smashing with shoulder pain?

Cut hard smashes first and keep bandejas at a comfortable level. Rebuild the smash last when the shoulder is pain-free.

What exercise helps shoulder pain from smashing?

Rotator cuff work in the overhead position, such as the 90/90 external rotation, plus shoulder-blade exercises like prone Y raises and wall slides.

When should I see a doctor about shoulder pain from padel?

If it hasn't improved after two weeks, if it's hard to move your arm, or straight away if the pain is sudden and severe, the shoulder changed shape or you have numbness.
