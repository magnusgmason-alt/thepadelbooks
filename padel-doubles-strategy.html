Padel doubles strategy: how to play as a team

7 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 23 Oct 2026

The core of padel doubles strategy is that you win and lose as a pair: move together, agree who takes the middle, talk on every shared ball, and build the point until one of you gets the ball to finish. Padel is almost always played as doubles, so these team habits matter more than any single shot.

Padel doubles strategy: one team, one shape

The Padel School's rule for doubles is short: move forward and defend backwards, together. When one partner advances, the other follows; when one drops back, so does the other. If you separate, you open space for the opponents. The FIP rulebook itself is written around pairs, from the coin toss (Rule 4) to who hits the ball (Rule 12: pairs hit alternately).

That team logic shapes everything below: where you stand, who hits which ball, and how you talk.

Padel doubles positioning

There are two home positions for a pair:

  • Both at the net (attack): level with each other, about two to three metres from the net, racket up.
  • Both at the back (defence): a metre or two in front of the back glass, with room to let the ball come off the wall.

Around the service line is a transition area. Pass through it; don't stay there. Our full guide to padel doubles positioning explains both positions and when to switch.

Sliding sideways

Pairs also move left and right together. The Padel School's examples: if you're pulled into a back corner, your partner shifts towards the centre to cover the easy ball down the middle. At the net, if you volley down the line, your partner moves across to close the middle, leaving the opponents only a tight cross-court ball or a lob.

Who takes the middle?

Balls down the centre cause more confusion than any other shot. Agree a default before the match. The Padel School says it's usually the forehand player, though not always.

SituationCommon default
Two right-handers, ball down the middleThe left-side player, whose forehand faces the middle
Lob down the middle at the netThe player whose forehand or overhead side covers it
Volley in the middleOften whoever is further forward, because they can take it earlier
Right-hander and left-handerBoth forehands face the middle, so agree in advance who takes it, and the overheads

The rules give you a reason to sort this out. If both partners hit the ball, simultaneously or one after the other, the pair loses the point (FIP Rule 13o). And if your partner's shot touches you, the point is lost too (Rule 13j). The one exception: if you both swing at once and one hits the ball while the other only hits the partner's racket, it isn't a double hit.

Padel doubles communication

LTA Padel's advice is to call "yours" or "mine" during the point, and at a higher level to tell your partner where the opponents are and where you plan to hit. The Padel School adds: call early, call when you switch sides, and give information, not emotion.

Calls worth agreeing

  • "Mine" / "Yours": who takes the ball. Call early and once.
  • "Switch": a lob has gone over you and your partner is taking it, so you swap sides.
  • "Up" / "Back": move forward or drop back together.
  • Where they are: when you're playing off the back glass, your partner can see the opponents and you can't. The Padel School recommends asking.

Signals before the serve

Many pairs use hand signals behind the back from the net player to plan the serve and whether they'll cross. Padel Browser describes one common set, such as one finger for a serve to the middle and two for wide. These are conventions, not an official code, so agree your own.

Tone matters

The Padel School suggests replacing blame with instructions: not "why did you hit that?" but "high ball next time, let's lob". LTA Padel stresses building your partner's confidence. A tight partner misses more.

Agree simple patterns

The Padel School recommends agreeing a few patterns so you don't have to think mid-rally. Useful ones include:

  • Opponent hits short: attack the middle.
  • Under pressure at the back: default to the lob, then move up together if it's good.
  • One of you pulled wide: the other covers the middle automatically.
  • Lobbed at the net: bandeja if you can, both back if you can't.

Before the match, also discuss who makes tactical calls and who is more confident on lobs and smashes. After it, ask what worked, which patterns won points and where you got exposed.

Playing with a stronger partner

Expect the opponents to target you. HEAD's tactical advice to players is to focus on one opponent, and the weaker player is the obvious choice. Your job is not to play above your level but to be hard to beat:

  • Cut your errors. Keep the ball deep and in play, and lob well. Let your partner finish.
  • Feed your partner the attacking balls. Play towards the opponent who will give a high reply to your partner's side.
  • Agree the middle in their favour if their forehand or overhead covers it.
  • Don't apologise after every point. Reset and play the next one.

Playing with a weaker partner

  • Stay in your own space most of the time. The Padel School notes that each player generally covers their own side. Poaching everything leaves holes behind you.
  • Take more of the middle and the overheads where you can reach them cleanly.
  • Keep it simple. HEAD's advice to keep tactics simple matters most when one partner is under pressure.
  • Encourage. The opponents want your partner to feel isolated; your calls and tone undo that.

Common doubles mistakes

Do

Move as a pair, call every shared ball, cover the middle when your partner is pulled wide, and agree patterns before the match.

Don't

Play one up, one back, go silent on middle balls, chase your partner's balls, or criticise mid-match.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best padel doubles strategy?

Move together, take the net with deep lobs, keep it with controlled overheads, play through the middle, and communicate on every shared ball.

Who should take the middle ball in padel?

Agree before the match. For two right-handers, it's usually the left-side player, whose forehand faces the middle. At the net, whoever is further forward often takes the volley.

What happens if both partners hit the ball?

The pair loses the point (FIP Rule 13o). If you both swing and only one touches the ball while the other hits the partner's racket, play continues.

Should we play one up, one back in padel?

No. It leaves a diagonal gap through the middle. Padel pairs play side by side, both at the net or both at the back.

How do I stop opponents targeting my weaker partner?

You can't stop them, but you can help: cover more of the middle and the overheads you can reach cleanly, keep tactics simple, and keep your partner confident.
