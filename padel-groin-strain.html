Groin strain in padel: why it happens, recovery and prevention

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 15 Nov 2026

A wide lunge for a ball off the side glass, or a sudden change of direction, can overstretch the adductor muscles on the inside of your thigh. That's a groin strain: a sharp pain in the inner thigh or groin, often followed by stiffness and pain when you bring your legs together. Here's what to do, how long it usually takes, and how to make your groin more resilient.

General information, not medical advice

This page explains common causes and general self-care. It can't diagnose your pain. If pain is severe, follows a fall or a loud pop, stops you putting weight on the leg, or isn't improving after a couple of weeks, see a doctor or physiotherapist.

Symptoms

  • Sudden pain in the groin or inner thigh during a lunge, sprint or change of direction
  • Pain when squeezing your knees together or lifting the leg sideways
  • Tenderness, swelling or bruising on the inner thigh
  • Stiffness the next day

A lump in the groin that gets bigger when you cough or strain can be a hernia rather than a strain and should be checked by a GP.

First steps

NHS advice for sprains and strains applies: rest the area and stop playing, use an ice pack wrapped in a towel for up to 20 minutes every 2–3 hours, use support if helpful, and avoid heat, alcohol and massage for the first couple of days. After a few days, start gentle movement within the limits of pain.

Recovery time

Mild strains often settle within a few weeks; more severe ones can take longer. Groin problems also tend to come back if you return before the adductors are strong again, so build up strength before full matches.

Prevention: the Copenhagen plank

In a 2019 trial in male football players, Harøy and colleagues found that a short adductor strengthening programme built on the Copenhagen adduction exercise lowered the prevalence of groin problems. Padel hasn't been studied directly, but the lunging and cutting are similar. Start with the short-lever version (knee on a bench), 2 sets of 15 seconds per side, once or twice a week.

  • Warm up with leg swings, side shuffles and lateral lunges before playing.
  • Build up match time gradually.
  • Keep doing strength work for the hips and adductors. See hip pain and strength training.

When to see a doctor

Get help if you felt a pop and can't walk normally, there's a lot of bruising, the pain isn't improving after two weeks, or you notice a lump in the groin.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a groin strain take to heal?

Mild groin strains often settle in a few weeks; more severe ones can take longer. Return to full padel once you can lunge, shuffle and change direction without pain.

How do you prevent groin strains in padel?

Warm up properly, build up playing time gradually and strengthen the adductors, for example with the Copenhagen side plank.

Can I play padel with a groin strain?

Not while it hurts to lunge or change direction. Playing too early is a common reason groin strains come back.

Is it a groin strain or a hernia?

A strain usually follows a specific movement and hurts on squeezing the legs together. A lump that bulges when you cough or strain may be a hernia and should be checked by a GP.
