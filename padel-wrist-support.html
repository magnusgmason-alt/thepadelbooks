Wrist support for padel: splint, brace or tape?

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 17 Nov 2026

A wrist support can make a sore wrist much more comfortable, both on court and at night. But it doesn't fix why the wrist got sore in the first place. Here's which type to use when, what the NHS recommends, and the changes that make a support unnecessary in the long run.

Not medical advice

This guide explains common patterns in plain language. It can't diagnose you. If the pain is severe, came from a fall or twist, or isn't settling, get it assessed.

What the NHS says

For mild wrist pain, the NHS suggests wearing a splint to support the wrist and ease pain, especially at night, and notes you can get one at most pharmacies and supermarkets. Pharmacists can advise on the best splint, including flexible rubber splints if you still need to use your wrist. The NHS also lists resting the wrist when you can, ice, gentle movement and simple painkillers, and avoiding gripping anything too tightly.

Which support for which job?

TypeBest forPadel use
Rigid or semi-rigid splintResting a sore wrist, especially overnightNot for playing; use at night or during a flare-up
Flexible brace or wrapLight support while you still use the wristCan be worn on court for comfort if it doesn't change your grip
Sweatband or sleeveWarmth and sweatNo real support
TapeShort-term comfortDepends on the person; ask a physio to show you

What matters more than the support

  • Grip size. Rehaboteket's physio guidance lists a grip size that doesn't suit your hand among the triggers of wrist pain in racket sports. A grip that's too thin makes you squeeze; an overgrip is an easy way to build it up.
  • Racket weight. A heavier or head-heavy racket twists more on off-centre hits, so the wrist works harder to hold the face steady.
  • Technique. A quiet wrist on volleys and a body turn on overheads remove most of the repeated load.
  • Strength. Our 6-week wrist plan builds the forearm muscles that protect the wrist.
  • Playing time. Most wrist pain in padel builds gradually, so increase playing time step by step.

Using a support sensibly

  • Wear it while it helps you play comfortably, not as permanent armour.
  • It should not change your grip on the racket or squeeze the hand; take it off if your fingers tingle or swell.
  • Combine it with lower load and strengthening; if you still need it after a few weeks, get the wrist assessed.

When a support isn't enough

See a GP if the pain hasn't improved after two weeks of home treatment, is getting worse or keeps coming back, or comes with tingling or loss of feeling. After a fall, don't strap it up and play: a possible broken wrist needs checking. More in can I play padel with wrist pain?

Frequently asked questions

Should I wear a wrist brace for padel?

A flexible brace can make play more comfortable for some players. It doesn't treat the cause, so combine it with lower load, better technique and strengthening.

Is a wrist splint good for padel wrist pain?

The NHS suggests a splint for mild wrist pain, especially at night. Rigid splints are for rest rather than playing.

Does tape help a sore wrist in padel?

Some players find it comfortable short term. Ask a physio to show you a method that suits your problem.

Can a wrong grip size cause wrist pain?

It can. A grip that's too thin makes you squeeze harder. An overgrip is an easy way to make it thicker.

How long should I wear a wrist support?

Use it while it helps during a flare-up. If you still need it after a few weeks, get the wrist checked.
