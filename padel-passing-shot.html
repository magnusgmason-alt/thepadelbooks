The padel passing shot: 7 ways to beat the net players

6 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 8 Nov 2026

A padel passing shot sends the ball past the net players rather than over them: through the middle, down the line, or low at their feet so they can't volley it cleanly. It is a lower-percentage play in padel than in tennis, because the lob is usually the safer way back to the net and a pass that runs into the back glass can come straight back. Here are seven passing options, when each one is on, and when to lob instead.

Why passing is harder in padel

In tennis, a ball that gets past the net player is usually a winner. In padel it often isn't. The court is enclosed, so a pass that bounces and reaches the glass comes back into play, and the LTA points out that powerful shots are more likely to come back after rebounding off the glass. The net players stand about two metres from the net, cover a 10-metre-wide court between them, and only need to block the ball.

The net itself works against you too: under the FIP rules it is 88 cm high in the middle and 92 cm at the posts, so a down-the-line pass crosses the higher part. That's why the lob is the main way back to the net. The Padel School calls it the most effective way to take the net position, and the LTA says lobbing is one of the most effective counters to opponents who are volleying at the net.

So the passing shot is a tool, not a plan. Use it when the net pair give you a reason.

7 padel passing shots

1. Through the middle

The safest pass. HEAD's tactics advice is that when both opponents are at the net, a ball right down the middle can leave them unsure who should play it, leading to a collision or a free point. It also crosses the net at its lowest point. Keep it low and firm rather than fast.

2. Down the line

Down the line (paralelo) beats a net player who has moved towards the middle. It's the shortest path and crosses the higher part of the net, so your margin is small. It also has to bounce before it reaches the opponents' side glass: a ball that hits their glass or mesh before bouncing loses you the point (FIP Rule 13g). Use it when the gap is obvious, not as a habit.

3. Low at the feet

Not past them, but under them. A ball that dips at the net player's feet forces them to volley upwards, which usually gives you a high ball next. The forehand with light topspin is the tool: HEAD's tip is to lightly brush over the top of the ball so it dips at the opponents' feet rather than bouncing off the back glass. See padel spin for how to shape it.

4. Chiquita, then pass

A two-shot pattern. The chiquita is, in HEAD's words, a soft, slow shot that just clears the net and drops towards the opponent's feet. Their reply has to come up. If it floats, the pass through the middle or down the line is suddenly a high-percentage shot because you're hitting from above the net.

5. The short cross-court angle

When a ball sits up around your service line, angle it short cross-court so it bounces and runs into the opponents' side glass, away from the net player. Angles like this take time to learn, so practise them before you need them. The ball must bounce before their glass, so give yourself a safe margin inside the side.

6. The pass off the back glass

Sometimes the ball comes out of your back glass at a comfortable height and the net pair have crept too close, expecting a lob. A firm, flat drive through the middle or at the feet catches them. The key word is sometimes: if they're well positioned, the lob from the glass is still the better percentage. Our padel lob guide covers that choice.

7. Punish the short overhead

A bandeja that lands short and slow in mid-court sits up for a counter-attack, as our bandeja guide warns. When the overhead pair hasn't got back to the net yet, a low pass at the feet of the player who hit it, or through the gap they left, is often the best ball you'll get all game.

When not to try a pass

  • The ball is below net height. You'd be hitting up to a waiting volleyer.
  • You're off balance or late. Passing needs a clean strike; lob instead.
  • The net pair are well positioned and patient. They will block it back and wait for your error. HEAD's advice is that trying to hit opponents off the court isn't always the best idea.
  • The score says safety. At deuce or golden point, the lower-risk shot usually wins.
A simple rule

Lob by default, pass by invitation. The net players' position and the height of the ball are the invitation.

Passing shot drills

Middle or lob (4 players, 15 min). Two at the net, two at the back. The back pair may only lob or play through the middle; anything else loses the point. Swap every five points. You'll learn which net positions leave the middle open.

Gap call (3 players, 10 min). A feeder at the back sends a waist-high ball to the hitter. Two net players move just before contact: both to the middle, or one to the side. The hitter must go down the line if the side opens and through the middle otherwise. Pass mark: 7 of 10 correct choices that land in.

Frequently asked questions

What is a passing shot in padel?

A shot that goes past the net players rather than over them, usually through the middle, down the line or low at their feet, so they can't volley it cleanly.

Is it better to lob or pass in padel?

Usually lob. The lob pushes the net pair back and lets you take the net, and coaches from the LTA and The Padel School treat it as the main way to do that. Pass when the net players are too far back, leave a gap, or give you a high ball.

Can a passing shot hit the side wall?

Only after it has bounced in the opponents' court. If it hits their glass or mesh before bouncing, you lose the point (FIP Rule 13g).

Down the line or through the middle?

Through the middle is the safer default: the net is lowest there and the opponents may hesitate. Down the line is for when a player has clearly left the side open.

Why do my passing shots keep coming back?

Usually because they are too hard and too deep: the ball reaches the back glass and rebounds for an easy volley or overhead. Aim lower and shorter, at the feet, so it bounces before the glass and stays down.
