Can I play padel with hip pain?

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 17 Nov 2026

A sore hip doesn't automatically mean no padel. NHS advice is to keep moving if you can, just without overdoing it. The questions are what kind of pain it is, how it responds to play, and whether any warning signs mean it needs checking first.

Not medical advice

This guide explains common patterns in plain language. It can't diagnose you. If the pain is severe, came from a fall or twist, or isn't settling, get it assessed.

Check the warning signs first

NHS advice is to call 999 or go to A&E if you have severe hip pain after a fall or injury, can't walk or put weight on the leg, or have tingling or loss of feeling in the hip or leg after an injury. Ask for an urgent GP appointment or contact NHS 111 if severe pain started suddenly without an injury, the hip is swollen and hot, the skin around it has changed colour, or you feel unwell with a high temperature. See a GP if the pain hasn't improved after two weeks of self-care, keeps coming back, affects your sleep or daily life, or you have hip stiffness for more than 30 minutes after waking.

The NHS lists these among common causes but advises against trying to diagnose hip pain yourself. Use the table as a guide to what to ask, not as a diagnosis.

The playing rule

  • Green: mild pain that doesn't build during play and is no worse the next morning. Play, with the changes below.
  • Amber: pain that builds during play or lingers the next day. Shorter sessions, fewer hard lunges.
  • Red: pain that makes you limp or changes how you move. Stop padel for now and keep active in other ways.

How to play with a sore hip

  • Smaller steps. Several adjustment steps instead of one big lunge.
  • Let wide balls go to your partner, or play them off the glass.
  • Shorter sessions, with a rest day between them.
  • Warm up the hips with leg swings, walking lunges and side shuffles.
  • Look after it between sessions: the NHS suggests an ice pack in a towel for up to 20 minutes every two to three hours, painkillers if suitable, soft shock-absorbing shoes, avoiding heavy lifting and low chairs, and gentle hip stretches.

The long-term fix

Hip strength and mobility take load off the joint and the tendons around it. Our 6-week hip plan starts gently, and a 2014 meta-analysis of 25 trials found strength training cut sports injuries to less than a third. If pain keeps coming back, a physiotherapist can work out the cause and tailor it.

Frequently asked questions

Can I play padel with hip pain?

With mild pain that doesn't build during play or linger the next day, and no warning signs, often yes, with smaller lunges and shorter sessions.

Should I rest a sore hip completely?

NHS advice is to keep moving if you can, but without overdoing it.

When should I see a GP about hip pain from padel?

If it hasn't improved after two weeks of self-care, keeps coming back, affects your sleep or daily life, or you're stiff for more than 30 minutes after waking.

When is hip pain an emergency?

Severe pain after a fall, being unable to walk or bear weight, or tingling or numbness after an injury need A&E.

What exercises help hip pain from padel?

Gentle hip stretches at first, then glute bridges, split squats, band walks and Copenhagen planks as pain allows.
