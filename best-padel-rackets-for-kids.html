Best padel rackets for kids and juniors (2026)

5 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 23 Oct 2026

A child playing with an adult racket is like learning to write with a heavy marker: possible, but it slows everything down. The right junior racket is lighter and easier to swing, which means better technique, more fun and less strain on young arms. Here is how to choose one, and five good options for 2026.

The short answer

For most children under about 10, choose a light, round junior racket of around 300 g or less. From about 10, a junior racket of around 310–330 g works well. Strong teenagers can move to a light adult racket around 12–13. Strength and height matter more than age.

How to choose a junior racket

HEAD's guide for parents gives a simple starting point, which matches what most coaches recommend:

Age (roughly)RacketTypical weight
Under 10Shorter junior racket with a thinner profileAbout 300 g
Over 10Junior racket a little shorter than an adult one, adult thicknessAbout 330 g
Around 12–13Light adult racket, full lengthAround 340 g

These ages are only a guide. A tall, strong 9-year-old may be ready for a heavier racket; a smaller 12-year-old may not. HEAD also points out that a racket that's too big or heavy increases the risk of injury, and that a coach is the best person to judge the fit.

Check the actual weight before you buy. Some rackets labelled "junior" weigh 330–350 g, which is heavy for a younger child.

Shape, balance and materials for kids

  • Shape: round is the safest choice for most children. The sweet spot is large and in the centre, so mishits still go over the net.
  • Balance: low or medium. A head-heavy racket is harder to control and tires the arm.
  • Face: fibreglass. It's softer and more forgiving than carbon.
  • Core: soft EVA or foam, for comfort and easy power on slow balls.

The official rules don't set separate limits for junior rackets. Junior models simply stay within the adult maximums: no longer than 45.5 cm, no wider than 26 cm, no thicker than 38 mm, and always with the wrist strap, which is compulsory. See our racket strap rule guide.

When to move up to an adult racket

Move up when your child can swing their junior racket easily for a whole session, plays regularly, and is tall and strong enough to handle a full-length racket. For many, that's around 12–13. Start with a light adult racket of around 340 g, round or soft teardrop, before anything heavier. Our beginner racket guide and upgrade guide list good options. HEAD also advises that adults shouldn't use junior rackets.

Tips for parents

  • Let them try first. Many clubs and coaches have junior demo rackets.
  • Don't buy "to grow into". A racket that's too heavy now will hold back their technique.
  • Add an overgrip if the handle feels too thin or slippery, and replace it regularly.
  • Always use the strap. It stops the racket flying out of a small hand.
  • Shoes matter too. See our guide to padel shoes.

Frequently asked questions

At what age can kids start padel?

HEAD's guide says children can start from about age 3 with suitable equipment. Most junior coaching groups start a little later, once children can track and hit a moving ball.

How heavy should a junior padel racket be?

As a guide, about 300 g for children under 10 and about 330 g for older juniors, then around 340 g for a first light adult racket. Always check the real weight on the product page.

Can a child use an adult racket?

They can, but a full-weight adult racket is usually too heavy and long for younger children. It slows the swing and can strain the arm. A junior racket is the better choice until they're strong enough.

Round or teardrop for kids?

Round for most beginners. A soft teardrop can suit older, more experienced juniors who want a bit more power.

Are there special junior rules?

The FIP rules don't set separate racket limits for juniors. Junior rackets must stay within the same maximum size, and the wrist strap is compulsory.
