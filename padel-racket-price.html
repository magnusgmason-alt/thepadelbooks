Padel racket price: what to pay at every level

7 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 30 Oct 2026

The padel racket price you should expect in 2026 is roughly £55 to £120 (about $90 to $200) for a good first racket, £100 to £160 for an intermediate model, and around £150 to £300 or more ($300 to $450 or more) for an advanced or top-of-the-range racket. Spending more doesn't automatically make you play better. This guide shows what each price band actually buys, when the extra money is worth it, and how to pay less for the same racket.

Padel racket price at a glance

Prices vary by country, retailer and time of year, and the same racket can cost very different amounts in a sale. These are typical ranges from UK and US retailers' published guides in 2026. Use them as a map, not a price list.

TierTypical UK priceTypical US priceWhat you usually get
BudgetUnder £50Few adult rackets under $90Basic materials; one UK retailer advises avoiding this tier because cheap foam can compress within months
Beginner / first racket£55–£120About $90–$120 (entry), $120–$200 (first real racket)Round or soft teardrop shape, fibreglass face, soft EVA or foam core, forgiving feel
Intermediate£100–£160About $200–$300Carbon in the face or frame, more responsive feel, teardrop shapes become common
Advanced / flagshipAbout £150–£310About $300–$450+Higher-grade carbon, firmer cores; less forgiving

The UK beginner and intermediate bands come from Central Sports' 2026 buyer's guide, and the UK advanced band from EverythingPadel's 2026 listings, where advanced models ran from about £150 to £210 and current flagships from Bullpadel and Nox were £305.99 on sale. The US bands come from Padel Browser's check of 361 in-stock rackets at three US retailers in September 2026. It put the true US entry floor at about $90 to $110, called $200 to $300 the enthusiast tier, and found the median racket sat at around $250 to $265.

How much is a padel racket for a beginner?

For most adults starting out, somewhere between £55 and £120, or around $90 to $200 in the US, buys everything you need. In this range you get the features that actually help beginners: a round shape with a large sweet spot, a manageable weight, a low or medium balance and a soft face and core that give the ball an easy push on slow swings.

Padel USA, a retailer, puts it bluntly: for beginners, $200 is more than enough, and overspending early can slow your development. The Padel School makes a similar point about rackets under $100: they're worth it for beginners and casual players, as long as you accept that they won't feel like a premium racket.

If you haven't played yet, you don't need to buy anything. Many clubs hire rackets for a small fee. One UK health-club chain, for example, charges £5 to hire a racket, plus a £10 deposit. Play a few sessions first, then buy.

Cheap vs expensive padel rackets: what you get for more money

A higher price usually pays for three things, all of which matter more as your technique improves.

  • More and better carbon. Entry rackets use fibreglass faces. Mid-range rackets add carbon to the face or frame, and premium rackets use higher-grade or layered carbon. Carbon is stiffer and gives more precise feedback, but it's less comfortable for players who don't yet hit the ball cleanly.
  • Firmer, more advanced cores. Cheaper rackets use soft foams. Premium rackets use firmer rubbers that give more control and power to players who swing fast. On a slow swing, a firm core can feel dead.
  • Durability and finish. Better rackets tend to keep their feel for longer under heavy use. According to The Padel School, budget rackets wear faster if you play often.

What a higher price doesn't buy is forgiveness. Padel Browser warns that a head-heavy diamond with a stiff carbon face punishes a late swing far more than a $150 round racket does. If you're still mishitting regularly, a stiff, head-heavy flagship can make the game harder, and it can be harder on your elbow too. Our guide to choosing a padel racket explains shape, weight, balance and materials in detail.

Are expensive padel rackets worth it?

Sometimes. It depends on how often you play and how consistent your technique is.

Worth it when

You play several times a week, hit the ball cleanly most of the time, know whether you prefer control or power, and have tried the model or a demo of it.

Not yet when

You're in your first months, play once a week or less, still mishit often, or are buying a professional's racket because of who uses it.

For most club players, the best value sits in the middle. Padel Browser calls the $120 to $200 band "the first real racket", and Padel USA says $200 already reaches real performance rackets for intermediate players. If the extra money is the difference between a new racket and a few more hours of coaching, the coaching will usually do more for your game. See our guide to padel lessons.

Padel racket price by level

Complete beginner

Hire for the first few sessions, then buy a round, forgiving racket in the beginner band. Our list of the best padel rackets for beginners has current models, many at around £55 to £70.

Improver and intermediate

Once your strokes are repeatable, usually after six to twelve months of regular play according to Central Sports, it's worth moving up to the intermediate band: a teardrop or controlled round shape with some carbon. See the best padel rackets for intermediate players.

Advanced

Advanced players choose by playing style rather than price. A flagship can make sense here, but last season's version often plays almost the same for much less. Our advanced racket guide lists current options and typical prices.

Juniors

Junior rackets are lighter and usually cheaper. One UK retailer listed a branded junior racket at £79.99 on sale (down from £130) in 2026. Don't hand a child an adult flagship; a light, soft racket protects young arms.

How to pay less for the same racket

  1. Buy last season's model. Brands release new ranges every year, and the previous version usually drops in price. Padel USA says a discounted previous-season pro model can outperform a current mid-range one, and that retailers often clear older stock at the start and end of the year.
  2. Wait for the sales. Many of the prices quoted by retailers in 2026 were sale prices, well below the listed retail price.
  3. Try before you buy. Hire or borrow the model, or a demo, for a full session. The cheapest racket is the one you don't have to replace because it didn't suit you.
  4. Don't pay for features you can't use yet. A firm core and stiff carbon face only help if your swing is fast and clean.

When to replace a padel racket

Rackets wear out gradually. The core loses some of its liveliness, and the frame can crack after hits on the glass or floor. Replace yours if you see a crack, hear a rattle, or notice the ball suddenly feeling dead off the face.

How long that takes depends on how often you play. Justpadel, a retailer, gives these rough guidelines:

How often you playRough lifespan of a high-quality racket
5 to 7 times a week2 to 4 months
3 to 4 times a week4 to 8 months
1 to 2 times a week8 to 16 months

These are guidelines, not rules. A worn grip isn't a reason to replace the racket: an overgrip costs far less and takes two minutes to fit. A protective cover and edge tape help the frame last longer. If you're budgeting for the whole kit, our padel equipment guide covers what else you need.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a padel racket?

In 2026, a good first racket typically costs about £55 to £120 in the UK or $90 to $200 in the US. Intermediate rackets run about £100 to £160, and flagships from big brands can cost £300 or more. Prices vary by country, retailer and season.

Is a £50 padel racket good enough?

It will let you play, but at least one UK retailer advises avoiding rackets under about £50 because low-grade foam can lose its performance within a few months. If you're not sure you'll keep playing, hiring is usually the better option.

Why are padel rackets so expensive?

Premium rackets use more and higher-grade carbon, more advanced core rubbers and more refined construction. Those features help experienced players, but you don't need them to start.

Should a beginner buy an expensive padel racket?

No. Expensive rackets tend to be stiffer and less forgiving. A round, soft racket in the beginner price band will help you more in your first year.

How often should I replace my padel racket?

It depends on how often you play. As a rough guide from one retailer, a high-quality racket used once or twice a week lasts around 8 to 16 months. Replace it sooner if you see a crack or the ball starts to feel dead.
