Padel wall drills: back glass and side wall

10 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 31 Oct 2026

Padel wall drills teach you to let the ball come off the glass and still play a controlled shot, the skill that separates padel players from tennis players faster than any other. Here are ten drills for the back glass and side wall, from solo self-feeds to double walls, the bajada and the contra-pared, each with a diagram and a pass mark.

The drills come from The Padel Drill Book, where the glass has its own chapter of exits, double walls and defence. We've reworded and shortened them here.

How to play off the glass: the short version

Once the ball has bounced on your side, it stays in play if it hits the walls or the mesh; you just have to return it before it bounces a second time (FIP Rule 12). You may also hit it into your own glass so it rebounds over the net, but not into your own mesh (Rules 14b and 13l).

The technique is the same in every drill below, and it matches the LTA's advice on the back glass:

  • Turn side-on early and take the racket back while the ball is still travelling towards the wall.
  • Use small, quick steps to adjust. Don't run straight at the glass.
  • Give the ball room. Most players stand too close and get jammed.
  • Let it drop, then swing low to high, with contact in front of you. Clearing the net is enough; you rarely need pace.

On standard courts the back wall is three metres of glass with a metre of mesh above it, and the side glass runs four metres in from each back wall (LTA court guidance). The drills use those surfaces. For the full technique, read our guide to the back glass.

Back wall drills padel players can do alone

1. Back-glass feed and play

Players: 1 · Time: 15 min · Level: 1–3

Set-up: Stand three metres in front of your back glass with a few balls. T1 is a target zone cross-court on the far side.

How it works: Throw a ball hard into the floor so that, after one bounce, it runs into the glass. Turn side-on, let it come back out to you rather than chasing it into the corner, and move away from the glass with it. When it has dropped to about hip height, play a controlled forehand cross-court into T1. Twenty on the forehand, then twenty on the backhand.

Pass mark: 12 of 20 into T1.

Progression: Play every exit down the line, to a target by the opponents' side glass.

2. Side-glass corner exit

Players: 1 · Time: 15 min · Level: 2–4

Set-up: Stand near your service line on one side. T1 is a deep target on the far side.

How it works: Throw it so that, after the bounce, it strikes the side glass roughly a metre from the corner. Open your chest towards the side wall and wait: the ball moves away from the glass, so let it come to you with your back foot behind it. Play it deep into T1, about a metre over the net. Fifteen balls on each side.

Pass mark: 9 of 15 clean exits into T1 on each side.

Progression: Throw harder so the ball also catches the back glass on its way out.

Back wall drills with a feeder

3. Back-glass exit

Players: 2 · Time: 15 min · Level: 2–4

Set-up: F feeds from near the net on the far side. A starts in the back half of the court.

How it works: F feeds deep, so the ball bounces about two metres from A's back glass and comes off it at waist height. A turns side-on the moment the feed crosses the service line, keeps about a metre and a half from the glass, and steps forward with the ball as it comes out. Hit at hip height, cross-court into T1. Fifteen forehands, fifteen backhands.

Pass mark: 10 of 15 exits into T1 on each side.

Progression: F feeds faster and with backspin, so the ball comes off the glass lower.

4. Recover after the glass

Players: 2 · Time: 12 min · Level: 2–4

Set-up: A recovery cone three metres from the back glass and a metre in from the side line.

How it works: F feeds to the back glass and A plays the exit cross-court. Then A moves forward to the cone and split-steps there. F only feeds the next ball once A has split-stepped. The shot only ends when you are standing on the cone again. Four sets of eight, alternating forehand and backhand sides.

Pass mark: All four sets with A on the cone before every feed.

Progression: F sends the next ball the moment A's shot clears the net, ready or not.

5. Lob off the back glass

Players: 2 · Time: 15 min · Level: 2–5

Set-up: F, standing near the net, feeds so the ball rebounds off A's back glass at about waist height. T1 is the area between the far service line and the far back glass.

How it works: Bend low before the shot, open the racket face and lift the ball from low to high, standing up through it. The lob has to climb well above the reach of a net player's racket and land deep, near the glass rather than near the service line. Twenty balls.

Pass mark: 12 of 20 lobs in T1.

Progression: F stays at the net and smashes anything short. Our lob guide explains height and depth.

Side wall drills and double walls

6. Double-wall read: side then back

Players: 2 · Time: 15 min · Level: 3–5

Set-up: F feeds hard into A's backhand corner: side glass first, back glass second.

How it works: A tracks the ball with the front shoulder and waits about a metre off the back glass. The second wall pushes the ball back towards the middle of the court, so let it come to you, then return cross-court and deep into T1. Twelve balls, then switch to the forehand corner.

Pass mark: 7 of 12 clean returns into T1 per corner.

Progression: F switches between one-wall and two-wall feeds without saying which is coming. The usual mistake is hitting after the first wall, while the ball is still fast and moving away.

7. Back then side exit

Players: 2 · Time: 15 min · Level: 3–5

Set-up: F feeds so the ball bounces close to the corner of A's back glass and then rebounds into the side glass.

How it works: After the first rebound, A turns to face the side glass. The ball runs out along the side wall and slows down, so follow it, keep a racket's length from the wall, and take one step away before you hit. Lob or drive cross-court into T1. Twelve per side.

Pass mark: 7 of 12 clean exits per side.

Progression: The exit has to be a lob over a net player standing on the far service line.

8. Three-ball wall sequence

Players: 2 · Time: 12 min · Level: 3–5

Set-up: F with a basket at the net on the far side, A at the back.

How it works: F feeds three balls without a break: first deep into the middle of the back glass, then into the side glass on the backhand side, then into the forehand half of the back glass. A plays each exit, the last one cross-court. Reset your feet with small steps between balls, and remember that the side glass needs more room than the back glass. Rest 30 seconds after each sequence; eight sequences.

Pass mark: 5 of 8 sequences with three clean exits.

Progression: F shuffles the order of the three feeds for each sequence.

Playing off the glass exercises for advanced players

9. Bajada off the back glass

Players: 2 · Time: 15 min · Level: 4–6

Set-up: F lobs deep from the far side so the ball bounces and hits A's back glass high.

How it works: A turns, keeps clear of the glass and waits for the rebound, using the same timing as a normal exit. As it comes off the wall above shoulder height, A attacks it in the air with a flat or sliced overhead, driving it through the court into T1. Don't hit it at the top of the rebound; wait until it comes out to you. Fifteen balls.

Pass mark: 8 of 15 bajadas into T1.

Progression: F moves up to the net and tries to cut off the bajada with a volley. See our bajada guide for the full technique.

10. The contra-pared

Players: 2 · Time: 12 min · Level: 4–6

Set-up: F feeds a fast, deep ball that gets past A before a normal shot is possible.

How it works: A turns to face the back glass and drives the ball up into it, around two metres high, so it rebounds off the glass and loops over the net into T1. Hit up and firmly, not flat: too low and the ball comes back into the net. Stay below the top of the glass, because hitting your own mesh loses the point (Rule 13l). Twelve on each side.

Pass mark: 5 of 12 that make it over the net and drop into T1. It is an emergency shot, so a lower score is normal.

Progression: Any contra-pared that falls short, F volleys back towards A's feet.

Common mistakes in wall drills

  • Crowding the glass. Standing too close is the most common fault. The ball jams you and you hit it late and high.
  • Hitting too early. A ball still at shoulder height as it leaves the glass is moving fast. Let it drop to your hip unless you are deliberately playing a bajada.
  • Going after the first wall. On a double wall, wait for the second rebound. It brings the ball back to you.
  • Backing into the corner. Your first move after the rebound should be forward, with the ball, not back towards the glass.
  • Trying to win from the back. Off the glass, a deep lob or a controlled ball is the percentage shot. Save the attack for the bajada.

Frequently asked questions

How do you practise the back glass in padel on your own?

Stand about three metres from the back glass, throw a ball down hard so it bounces into the glass, wait for the rebound and send it back over the net. Back-glass feed and play and Side-glass corner exit both work with no partner.

How far from the back glass should I stand?

Further than feels natural. In the feeder drills above, about a metre and a half from the glass as the ball comes out, then step forward with it. If the ball keeps jamming you, move further away.

What is a double wall in padel?

A ball that hits two walls before you play it. Side then back sends the ball out towards the middle of the court; back then side sends it along the side wall.

Can you hit the ball into your own wall in padel?

Yes, into your own glass, as long as the ball then reaches the opponents' court. Hitting your own mesh loses the point (FIP Rules 14b and 13l).

Which wall drill should a beginner start with?

Back-glass feed and play. It needs no partner, teaches you to wait for the ball, and builds the turn-and-step pattern you use on every glass ball.
