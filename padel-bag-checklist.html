What to bring to padel: your bag checklist

6 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 8 Nov 2026

What to bring to padel is short for a casual game: racket, court shoes, a tube of balls, water, a towel and a spare overgrip. For a long session, hot weather or a tournament, a few extras make a real difference. Use the checklists below, from the bare minimum to a full tournament bag, and the guide at the end to choose between a padel bag and a backpack.

What to bring to padel: the essentials

  • Racket, in its cover. If you're hiring, check the club has rackets available.
  • Court shoes, padel or clay-court tennis shoes. If you change at the club, carry them in a separate compartment or bag so sand stays off your clothes.
  • Padel balls. A fresh tube if it's your turn. Our padel balls guide explains how quickly they go flat.
  • Water bottle. Full, and bigger than you think you need in summer.
  • Towel. A small one for your face and grip between games.
  • Spare overgrip. The cheapest fix for a slippery handle mid-session. See how to change a padel overgrip.
  • Phone, keys, card or cash for court fees, in a zipped pocket.

Comfort extras worth packing

  • Wristband or headband to keep sweat off your grip and out of your eyes.
  • Spare socks. Fresh socks after a warm-up or between matches help prevent blisters.
  • A spare shirt for long sessions or hot days.
  • A light layer for cold evenings and outdoor courts. Our guide to what to wear for padel covers clothing.
  • Cap or visor and sunscreen for outdoor courts in sun.
  • Protective glasses, if you wear them. Read our padel eye protection guide for why some players do.
  • A plastic bag for sweaty kit on the way home.

Water and electrolytes

For a normal 90-minute club game, water is enough for most people. Start the session hydrated and drink during breaks rather than trying to catch up at the end.

Electrolytes are more situational. The Australian Institute of Sport's fact sheet lists the cases where electrolyte supplements are useful: exercising in hot environments where large sweat losses can't practically be replaced, when you want to replace large sodium losses (high sweat rates, long sessions or salty sweat), and to speed up rehydration after a big fluid deficit. It also notes that salty food can supply sodium, that there's no consensus on the value of sodium replacement during exercise for performance or health, and that drinking too much fluid, not a lack of salt, is the main cause of serious low blood sodium. In plain terms: if you play long sessions in the heat and finish with white salt marks on your clothes, electrolyte tablets or a sports drink are worth carrying. If you play an hour indoors, they're optional.

Food matters too: our guide to what to eat before padel covers timing and snacks.

First aid basics

You don't need a full medical kit, but a small pouch covers most minor problems on a padel court:

  • Plasters and blister plasters
  • A crepe or elastic bandage
  • An instant cold pack, or ask the club for ice
  • Antiseptic wipes for grazes from the turf
  • Any personal medication you might need, such as an inhaler

For a sprained ankle or strained calf, the NHS advises protecting the injury, resting it, applying ice for up to 20 minutes every 2 to 3 hours, compressing with a bandage and keeping it raised during the first couple of days. It says to call 999 or go to A&E if you heard a crack, the area has changed shape, it's numb or tingling, or the skin looks blue or grey; and to contact NHS 111 if pain or swelling is severe or getting worse, or you can't put weight on it. This is general information, not medical advice, and stop playing if something feels wrong.

Tournament day extras

A tournament can mean several matches and a lot of waiting. On top of the essentials, pack:

  • A second racket, if you have one, in case a frame cracks or the cord breaks.
  • Two or three overgrips, so you can start each match with a fresh one.
  • A second pair of socks and a full change of clothes.
  • Snacks you know agree with you: bananas, sandwiches, bars.
  • More water than usual, plus electrolytes if it's hot.
  • The schedule and draw, saved on your phone, and a charger.
  • A small notebook for a few notes on each opponent.

Our guides to your first padel tournament and a padel match day routine cover what to do with all that waiting time.

Printable checklist

  1. Racket (and a spare for tournaments)
  2. Court shoes
  3. Balls
  4. Water (plus electrolytes on hot or long days)
  5. Towel
  6. Spare overgrip
  7. Wristband or headband
  8. Spare socks and shirt
  9. Cap, sunscreen, light layer
  10. Mini first aid pouch
  11. Phone, keys, payment
  12. Snacks (long sessions and tournaments)

Padel bag vs backpack: which type?

TypeCapacityBest for
Racket coverOne racket and a small itemPlayers who only carry their racket
BackpackAround 30 litres; one racket and the basicsWalking, cycling or public transport
Medium padel bag2 to 3 rackets plus kitMost regular club players
Large padel bag35 to 54 litres; up to about 6 racketsTournament players or anyone carrying for a group

The capacities come from Decathlon's and Padel Market's bag guides. Padel Market notes that two rackets already fill most backpacks and that a large bag is awkward to carry half-empty. Three features are worth paying for:

  • A thermal racket compartment, which Decathlon calls essential for regular players and Padel Market calls practically essential if you leave your bag in the car or play in extreme weather. See our padel racket care checklist for why heat matters.
  • A separate, ventilated shoe compartment to keep sand and smells away from clean clothes.
  • Padded shoulder straps if you'll carry it far.

Frequently asked questions

What should I bring to my first padel game?

Court shoes, comfortable sportswear, water and a towel. Most clubs hire rackets and many provide balls, so check when you book.

What should be in a padel bag?

Racket, shoes, balls, water, a towel, a spare overgrip and a wristband, plus spare socks, a light layer and a small first aid pouch. Add a spare racket, snacks and extra grips for tournaments.

Do I need electrolytes for padel?

Not usually for a normal indoor game. They're most useful for long sessions in the heat or if you sweat heavily. Water is fine for most club matches.

Is a padel backpack big enough?

For one racket and the basics, yes. If you carry two rackets, shoes and a change of clothes, a medium padel bag is more practical.

Do I need a thermal padel bag?

If you leave your bag in the car or play in very hot or cold weather, a thermal racket compartment is worth having. If you go straight from home to the court, it matters less.
