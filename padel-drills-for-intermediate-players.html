Padel drills for intermediate players

6 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 23 Oct 2026

The most useful padel drills for intermediate players train decisions as well as shots: when to come to the net, how to keep it with the bandeja, and how to defend off two walls. These eight drills add movement, targets and pressure to the basics, so your technique holds up in real matches.

What padel drills for intermediate players should train

At intermediate level you can rally, volley and use the back glass. What usually holds you back is what happens between the shots: drifting into no man's land, coming in behind a weak lob, or hitting a bandeja and then standing still to watch it. The Padel School's intermediate match analysis picks out exactly these errors.

So these drills share three features:

  • Movement after every shot. You don't score unless you recover.
  • Targets. Depth and direction count, not just getting the ball back.
  • Decisions. Many drills give you two options and score you on picking the right one.

Net and transition drills

1. Lob, read, go

Players: 2–4 · Time: 12 minutes

Setup: The drilling pair at the back (with two players, the single back player makes the decision). A feeder (or the other pair) at the net.

How it works: The net side feeds a low ball. The back player lobs. If the lob clears the net players and pushes them back, the back pair moves forward together, past the service line and into net position. If the lob is short, they stay back and defend. The Padel School puts it simply: when your lob is not good, don't come to the net.

Goal: Score one point for a good lob followed by moving up, one for a weak lob followed by staying back. Lose a point for the wrong decision.

Progression: Play the point out after the decision.

2. Bandeja chain

Players: 2 · Time: 15 minutes

Setup: The hitter at the net, the feeder at the back with a basket. Place a towel about two metres in front of the opposite back glass.

How it works: The feeder lobs five balls in a row. After each bandeja, the hitter must move forward again into net position before the next lob arrives. Intermediate players often need several bandejas in a row before they can finish a point, so this trains patience.

Goal: Count bandejas that land between the towel and the glass. Ten out of twenty is a good score.

Progression: Mix in short lobs. The hitter must smash those and play a bandeja on the deep ones. Smash the easy balls only.

3. Feet, deep, finish

Players: 2 · Time: 10 minutes

Setup: The volleyer at the net, the feeder at the back.

How it works: The feeder plays three balls in sequence: a low ball to the feet, a medium ball at body height and a high, easy ball. The volleyer plays the first back deep and low, the second into a corner, and puts the third away.

Goal: A full sequence counts only if all three volleys hit their zone. Aim for six out of ten.

Progression: The feeder randomises the order so the volleyer has to read each ball.

Defence drills

4. Double-glass defence

Players: 2 · Time: 12 minutes

Setup: The defender in a back corner. The feeder at the net on the other side.

How it works: The feeder sends balls into the corner so they come off the back and side glass, starting at a gentle pace and building up. The defender reads the rebound, takes small steps, and replies with a lob or a low cross-court ball. The LTA's back-glass drill also moves into the corner so the ball comes off two walls.

Goal: Seven out of ten replies that are either a deep lob or a ball landing before the feeder's service line.

Progression: Alternate corners so the defender also practises the backhand double-glass exit. Our back glass guide covers the basics.

5. Smash defence

Players: 2 · Time: 10 minutes

Setup: The defender at the back, the feeder at the net hitting controlled overheads.

How it works: The feeder hits medium-paced overheads deep into the court. The defender lets the ball come off the back glass and replies with a lob. Coaches stress that the ball is already travelling towards the net after the rebound, so you need little extra power: focus on control.

Goal: Count lobs that land beyond the feeder's service line. Swap after ten balls.

Progression: Increase the pace of the overheads gradually.

Precision drills

6. Low and deep

Players: 2 · Time: 10 minutes

Setup: Mark two target zones with towels or cones in the opposite court: one deep in each back corner, about two metres from the glass. HEAD's precision drills use markers on the court floor in the same way. The feeder stands at the back of the opposite court, as opponents who have been pushed back would.

How it works: This is a drive control drill for when both opponents are back. The feeder sends balls to the hitter's forehand side; the hitter plays flat forehands that skim the net and land in a target zone, pinning the opponents to the glass so your pair can move forward. A drive that floats high sits up for them, so the ball must cross the net low.

Goal: Six out of ten that cross low and land in a target zone.

Progression: Do the same with slice backhands, then mix forehand and backhand.

7. Half-court error game

Players: 2 · Time: 10 minutes

Setup: A narrowed court: half the court, with the side lines moved in a metre using cones.

How it works: A smaller target makes errors more costly, which is the point. HEAD recommends a smaller court for cutting out unforced errors. The first bounce must land inside the cones; after that the walls play as normal.

Goal: Our own scoring: an unforced error costs two points. First to eleven.

Progression: Add the yellow card, red card rule from the LTA: the first mistake in a game is a yellow card, and a second mistake in the same game is a red card that loses it.

Pressure game

8. Three-ball net challenge

Players: 4 · Time: 15 minutes

Setup: One pair at the net, one pair at the back.

How it works: The back pair gets three feeds each point: a low ball, a lob and then a live point. The net pair must hold the net through all three. If the back pair wins the net at any stage, they swap ends.

Goal: The net pair scores a point for each three-ball sequence they survive at the net. First to seven.

Progression: The net pair may not smash. Only bandejas and víboras, to train control over power.

Footwork underpins everything

Split-step every time your opponent hits, and set your feet before you swing. The Padel School's line sums it up: arrive early, set your feet, then hit.

Frequently asked questions

What should intermediate padel players practise most?

The bandeja, the transition to the net, and defence off the glass. These decide most intermediate points, much more than power.

How do I stop getting stuck in no man's land?

Commit. After a good lob, move all the way to the net; after a weak one, stay back. The lob, read, go drill trains exactly this decision.

How often should intermediate players drill?

Once a week alongside your matches is a good start. Even 30 minutes of focused drills makes your matches more productive.

Should intermediate players learn the víbora?

Yes, once the bandeja is reliable. Use the bandeja chain first, then add víboras on medium lobs.
