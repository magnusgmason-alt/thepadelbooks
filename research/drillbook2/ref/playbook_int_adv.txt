WELCOME · WHY THIS EDITION

You already play.
Now play it right.
You can rally, you know what a bandeja is, and you win your
share of club matches. Then you meet a pair who never
seem to miss, always seem to be at the net, and make you hit
the shot you least want to hit. This book is about becoming
that pair.
The first edition of The Padel Playbook covered padel from the first
lesson. Readers told us they wanted more of the second half: tactics,
advanced shots, patterns and training. So we rebuilt the book for
players who are past the basics.
There is no chapter on how to hold a racket or how to keep score.
There are chapters on the víbora, the x3, how to defend a doublewall lob, how to beat a left-hander, how professional pairs build
points and how to train with a plan.
THE PROMISE

Every chapter gives you something to change in your next match:
a position, a target, a pattern or a habit.

The service line sits only 3.05 m from the back glass. That short
distance is why the back of the court is a defensive zone: balls that
land deep reach the glass quickly, and the player who understands
the rebound has time; the player who doesn't is rushed.
The net is 88 cm at the centre and 92 cm at the posts. Low balls
through the middle cross the lowest part of the net, which is one
reason the middle is the safest target in padel.
WHY IT MATTERS

Padel is a game of space. Every tactic in this book is about taking
space from your opponents, mostly the net, and giving them balls
they can only defend.

LEVEL · WHAT SEPARATES LEVELS

It's not the big shots.
Players usually believe the next level needs a harder smash.
Watch a level 5 match against a level 3 match and the differences
are elsewhere.
01

Fewer free errors
Advanced players lose most points to good shots, not to their
own mistakes.

02

Better lobs
Deep, high lobs that push opponents back to the glass and let the
pair take the net.

03

Net time
Advanced pairs spend far more of each point at the net.

04

Shot choice
They pick the safe option under pressure and attack only from
good positions.

05

Movement as a pair
Both players move together, so gaps rarely appear.

06

Walls as an ally
They let the ball come off the glass instead of rushing it.

THE TRUTH

At club level, the pair that makes fewer errors and owns the net
wins most matches. Everything else is decoration.

LEVEL · THE INTERMEDIATE PLATEAU

Why most players
stop at level 3.5.
The most common plateau in padel sits between levels 3 and 4.
Players can rally and enjoy the game, but they lose to the same
type of pair every week.
01

They attack from the back
Hitting hard from behind the service line gives the opponents at the
net an easy volley.

02

They don't lob
Without a lob, a pair stuck at the back can never retake the net.

03

They smash every high ball
A smash from the wrong position is a gift; the bandeja is usually the
right answer.

04

They play shots, not points
Each ball is hit on its own merits, without a plan for the next two.

WAY OUT

Learn three things: the deep lob, the bandeja to the side glass,
and volleying through the middle. Those three moved more of our
readers up a level than anything else.

LEVEL · THE ADVANCED STEP

What level 5 players add.
01

Variety on the overhead
Bandeja, víbora and an x3 or x4 when the ball is right. Opponents
can't sit on one shot.

02

Defence that turns
From the back they play chiquitas, lobs and low balls that let them
climb to the net again.

03

Targeted serving
Serve to the glass, the T or the body, then a first volley with purpose.

04

Scouting
They find the weaker player, the weaker side and the weaker shot in
the first games.

05

Tempo control
They slow the game down with lobs and speed it up only when the
ball allows.

06

Match management
They adjust on change of ends, at Star Point and when the score
is close.

RULES · SCORING FORMATS

Deuce, golden
point and Star Point.
Padel uses tennis scoring, but three different formats now decide
what happens at 40–40. Ask which one applies before every match.

Advantage

Traditional

Play advantage points until one pair is two points clear.

Golden point Punto de oro

At 40–40, one deciding point. The receiving pair chooses
which player receives.

Star Point

FIP format from 2026

Advantage is played at the first deuces; if the game reaches
deuce again, a single deciding point is played. Receivers
choose who receives.

TACTICAL CONSEQUENCE

Under golden point and Star Point, the receiving pair should send
their stronger returner to the side the server likes least. Decide this
before the match, not at 40–40.
Professional circuits and federations update formats regularly. Club leagues often keep
golden point.

RULES · THE SERVE

What makes a legal serve.
01

Position
Behind the service line, between the centre line and the side wall,
with at least one foot on the ground.

02

Bounce and contact
Drop the ball and let it bounce behind the service line, then strike it
at or below waist height.

03

Direction
Diagonally into the opponents' service box. It may then hit the glass
but not the mesh.

04

Two attempts
A fault on the first serve gives a second serve.

05

Let
If the ball touches the net and lands in the correct box without
touching the mesh, the serve is replayed.

06

Order
Partners alternate serving each game; receivers keep the same
side for the whole set.

Some competitions have trialled or adopted variations, such as serving without
bouncing the ball first. Check the rules of each event.

RULES · WALLS AND FENCE

What the walls allow,
and what they don't.
01

Your shot must bounce first
Your ball must land in the opponents' court before touching any of
their walls or fence. A ball that hits their glass or mesh directly loses
you the point.

02

After the bounce, anything goes
Once it has bounced in their court, the ball may hit any wall or
fence and remains in play until it bounces a second time.

03

Using your own walls
After the ball has bounced on your side you may hit it against your
own back or side wall to send it over: the contrapared.

04

Mesh is live
A ball that bounces and then hits the mesh is in play, but the
rebound is unpredictable.

05

The body
If the ball touches you or your clothes, you lose the point.

RULES · PLAYING OUTSIDE

The ball leaves the court.
Many modern courts have open exits on each side of the net. If your
opponent's shot bounces and then leaves the court through an exit
or over the fence, you may run outside and play it back, as long as it
hasn't bounced a second time.

A

D

B
C

An x3: the smash bounces and leaves over the side fence. The defender runs out of the
exit to retrieve it, where the court and rules allow.

01

Check the court and event
Some clubs and leagues forbid playing outside. Agree it before
the match.

02

Re-entering
The return must come back into the opponents' court; it may not
touch anything outside before crossing.

RULES · THE NET AND THE OPPONENT'S SIDE

Fine lines that cost points.
Touching the net

01

Touching the net or posts with your body or racket while the ball is
in play loses the point.

Crossing the net

02

You may not hit the ball before it has crossed to your side. If a ball
spins back over the net after bouncing on your opponents' side,
you may reach over to play it, without touching the net.

Double hit

03

Hitting the ball twice in one stroke loses the point.

Hindrance

04

Shouting or moving to distract opponents during their shot can be
called as a hindrance.

Ball hitting the opponents' fence directly

05

Point to the opponents, even at speed.

Ball hits a light or the roof

06

Indoors, it usually loses the point for the hitter. Local rules vary.

IN MATCHES

Most club disputes are about whether the ball touched the mesh
or the body. Call your own side fairly; the reputation is worth more
than the point.

GEAR · RACKET SHAPES

Round, teardrop or diamond.
Round

Control

Low balance, large sweet spot near the centre. Forgiving,
good for defence, volleys and touch.

Teardrop

All-round

Medium balance, a sweet spot slightly higher. The
most versatile choice for intermediate and many
advanced players.

Diamond

Power

High (head-heavy) balance, a sweet spot near the
top. More power on overheads, less forgiving on volleys
and defence.

Many advanced drive-side players use a round or teardrop racket
for control and consistency. Revés players, who finish more points
with overheads, often move to a teardrop or diamond.
OUR ADVICE

Don't buy a diamond because the professionals use one. If your
volleys and defence are not yet consistent, a diamond will cost
you more points than its smash wins.

GEAR · WEIGHT AND BALANCE

Small numbers,
big difference.
355–365 g

365–375 g

375 g+

LIGHTER: SPEED, CONTROL

MEDIUM: MOST ADULTS

HEAVIER: POWER, STABILITY

Balance is the distance from the handle to the racket's centre of
mass. Low balance (under about 255 mm) feels lighter and faster;
high balance (over about 265 mm) feels heavier in the head and
adds power.
01

Overgrips
Each one adds roughly 5–8 g at the handle, which lowers the
balance slightly.

02

Lead tape
A few grams at the top increases power and stability; at the sides it
enlarges the sweet spot. Add 2–4 g at a time.

03

Elbow and shoulder
Heavier and head-heavy rackets put more load on the arm. If you
have pain, go lighter, lower balance and softer.

GEAR · SHOES

The most
underrated purchase.
Padel is played on sand-filled artificial grass. Running shoes
slip; tennis hard-court shoes grip too much and can strain
knees and ankles.
01

Herringbone sole
The standard sole pattern for padel: grips in sand while letting you
slide slightly.

02

Omni or clay soles
Some brands make specific soles; herringbone or mixed patterns
suit most courts.

03

Lateral support
Padel is mostly side-to-side. Look for a firm heel and
side reinforcement.

04

Cushioning
More cushioning for heavy schedules and hard courts with
little sand.

05

Replace them
When the pattern is worn flat at the ball of the foot, usually every 4–
8 months for frequent players.

GEAR · BALLS, GRIPS AND BAG

The small things that matter.
Balls

01

Padel balls have lower pressure than tennis balls. Open a new can
for matches; balls lose bounce within a few sessions.

Heat and altitude

02

Balls fly faster and bounce higher in heat and at altitude. Some
brands make altitude versions.

Overgrips

03

Change every few sessions in hot weather. A slightly thicker handle
reduces grip tension.

Wrist strap

04

Required by the rules. Always wear it.

Bag checklist

05

Two rackets, spare overgrips, new balls, water, an electrolyte drink,
towel, spare shirt, tape and sunglasses for outdoor courts.

ADVANCED TIP

Carry two identical rackets. If one breaks or a grip slips in a
match, you change without changing your game.

SIDES · THE TWO ROLES

Two sides, two jobs.
In padel, partners keep the same side for the whole set. At
intermediate level and above, the side you play shapes your
shots, your tactics and even your racket.
Drive

Right side (derecha)

Revés

Left side (izquierda)

For a right-hander, the forehand is in the middle. The drive
player builds the point: consistent, patient, a reliable lob and
return, and the one who keeps the ball in play.

For a right-hander, the forehand is on the side glass and
overheads are taken on the forehand side. The revés player
usually finishes more points, with more overheads and
more aggression.

NOT A HIERARCHY

Neither side is the 'better player' side. Many of the best pairs in the
world are built around an outstanding drive player who makes
the revés player's attack possible.

SIDES · PLAYING THE DRIVE

The drive player's job.
01

Return consistently
Most serves go to the drive player first in each game. Return low to
the feet or lob deep; avoid giving a high ball.

02

Build the point
Volley deep and through the middle, keep the ball low and wait for
the right moment.

03

Lob the revés player's forehand away
Lobs cross-court over the opposite revés player's backhand side
are hard to punish.

04

Cover the middle on the forehand
When both players can reach a middle ball, the forehand usually
takes it. For a right-handed pair, that is often the drive player's job
on balls towards the left half.

05

Be the stable one
Low error rate is the drive player's main weapon.

SIDES · PLAYING THE REVÉS

The revés player's job.
01

Own the overheads
High balls through the middle are often taken by the revés player

TACTICS · THE CORE IDEA

Whoever owns the net wins.
At every level above beginner, the pair at the net wins most
points. Tactics in padel are mostly about two questions: how do
we take the net, and how do we keep it?
01

Taking the net
Serve and follow in, deep lobs, chiquitas, and volleys after a
weak return.

02

Keeping the net
Bandejas and víboras that don't give the defenders time, and
volleys through the middle.

03

Losing the net
Smashing from the wrong place, volleying short, or both players not
retreating on a good lob.

SCORE YOURSELF

After a match, estimate how often you won the net back after
losing it. Upper-intermediate pairs manage it several times a
game; plateaued pairs rarely do.

PRO · HOW PROFESSIONALS PLAY

Slower than you think.
Watch a Premier Padel match and the first thing you notice is

FITNESS · WHAT PADEL DEMANDS

The body behind the game.
Short bursts

01

Points last a few seconds to over a minute, with many changes
of direction.

Lateral movement

02

Most steps are sideways, with lunges to low balls.

Rotation

03

Every stroke uses the trunk; the back and obliques work constantly.

Overhead load

04

Repeated overheads stress the shoulder and elbow.

Endurance

05

Matches often last 90 minutes or more, sometimes several per day
in tournaments.

WHY IT MATTERS

Most padel injuries among club players are in the elbow,
shoulder, calf, knee and lower back. Many are preventable with a
warm-up and two short strength sessions a week.

FITNESS · THE WARM-UP

Ten minutes before you play.
2 min easy jog or skipping around the court.
10 leg swings forward and sideways each leg.
10 walking lunges with rotation.
10 arm circles forward and back, then 10 band pull-aparts if you have
a band.
10 bodyweight squats.
Side shuffles and crossover steps, 2 lengths each way.
5 short sprints from the back glass to the net.
3 minutes of mini-padel at the net.
Volleys, lobs and a few overheads before match play.
Cold courts and cold muscles are a common factor in calf strains in winter. Warm up
longer when it's cold.

FITNESS · ELBOW AND SHOULDER

The overhead joints.
Padel elbow

01

Pain on the outside of the elbow, usually from grip tension, late
contact or a heavy, stiff racket.

What helps

02

Lighter and lower-balance racket, softer core, thicker grip, relaxed
hand, wrist strengthening and contact in front.

Shoulder

03

Repeated overheads with poor rotation load the shoulder.
Strengthen the rotator cuff and use the legs and trunk.

Warning signs

04

Pain that lasts after play, night pain or weakness. See a
physiotherapist early.

ADVANCED PLAYERS

The víbora and x3 add high-speed load. Build them into training
gradually, not all in one week.

FITNESS · CALF, KNEE AND BACK

The lower body.
01

Calf strain
Sudden pain in the calf when pushing off, often in older players and
in cold conditions. Warm up longer, strengthen calves, and replace
worn shoes.

02

Knee
Twisting on grippy courts and deep lunges load the knee.
Strengthen hips and thighs; use shoes with the right sole.

03

Ankle
Sprains from landing on balls or partners' feet. Clear stray balls
from the court.

04

Lower back
Rotation and bending to low balls. Bend the knees, not the back;
strengthen glutes and core.

This book doesn't replace medical advice. If pain persists, see a qualified professional.

MENTAL · WHAT PRESSURE DOES

Why good players
miss easy balls.
Under pressure, muscles tighten, swings shorten and decisions
speed up. Players start hitting harder and choosing riskier
targets, exactly when the safe shot matters most.
01

Tight grip
Kills feel on volleys and lobs. Grip pressure should be about 3–4 out
of 10.

02

Rushed decisions
Players hit before the ball comes off the glass.

03

Hero shots
The x3 from the service line at 30–40.

04

Blame
Pairs turn on each other after errors.

THE GOAL

Not to feel no pressure, but to play the same patterns under
pressure that you play when relaxed.

MENTAL · THE BETWEEN-POINT ROUTINE

Twenty seconds that matter.
01

React
Accept the point. A short positive or neutral gesture,
whatever happened.

02

Recover
Turn away, walk slowly, breathe out longer than you breathe in.

03

Plan
Agree the next point with your partner: target, pattern, who
covers what.

04

Ritual
Same routine before every serve or return: bounce, breath, look at
the target.

Routines give the mind something to do other than replaying the last
error. Professional players use them on almost every point.
Practise the routine in training, not only in matches, so it is automatic when it counts.

MENTAL · RESETTING AFTER ERRORS

One error is one point.
01

Label it

02

Next ball cue
Choose a simple cue word: 'middle', 'deep', 'split'.

03

Partner first

04

No match analysis mid-point

After two errors in a row from the same shot, play the safest
version of it for the next three points: higher lob, slower bandeja,
middle volley.

How good players behave.

Body shots

Celebrate fairly
Celebrate your winners, not the opponents' errors.
