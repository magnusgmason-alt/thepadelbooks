Common padel mistakes beginners make, and how to fix them

7 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 23 Oct 2026

The most common padel mistakes all have the same root: playing padel like tennis. Beginners hit too hard, run away from the glass, stand in the wrong place and try to win points that the walls will simply hand back. The good news is that each one has a simple fix, and fixing even three of them will win you more games than any new shot.

Why common padel mistakes happen

Padel looks simple, and in many ways it is. But the walls change what a good shot is. A hard, deep drive that wins the point in tennis comes off the back glass in padel and sits up for the opponents. A ball you'd chase in tennis comes back to you in padel if you wait. Almost every mistake below comes from instincts that work in other sports and don't work here.

Coaches agree on the list remarkably well. The mistakes below appear again and again in coaching advice from The Padel School and the LTA.

Mistakes with the ball

1. Hitting too hard

The number one padel beginner mistake. Power sends the ball long into the glass, where it rebounds nicely for the opponents, or straight out of the court. The Padel School lists hitting too hard first among the most common tactical errors, and one of the LTA's three beginner tips is less power and more control.

Fix: play at about 70% pace and aim for placement: deep, low and through the middle. Save real power for high balls near the net.

2. Going for winners too early

Most beginner points end in errors, not winners. If you go for the line from the back of the court, you'll lose more points than you win.

Fix: make it your job to put one more ball back than the other pair. Wait for a short, high ball before you attack.

3. Never lobbing

Tennis players treat the lob as a last resort. In padel it's the main way to get the opponents off the net and move forward yourself.

Fix: when both opponents are at the net and you're at the back, lob deep over them, then move up with your partner. See our lob guide.

4. Smashing at the wrong time

Smashing while moving backwards, or smashing every high ball, usually ends with the ball bouncing off the glass and coming back. The Padel School's rule of thumb: only smash when you know you can regain your net position.

Fix: on lobs you can't attack comfortably, play a controlled bandeja and stay at the net, or let the ball come off the back glass. Keep the full smash for balls you can hit in front of you.

Mistakes with the walls

5. Running away from the glass

Many beginners hit the ball before it reaches the back wall because they're nervous of it. That means hitting low, rushed shots from awkward positions.

Fix: let the ball bounce, come off the glass and drop, then play it. Getting comfortable near the glass is uncomfortable at first; it's still the skill that separates beginners from improvers. Our back glass guide breaks it down.

6. Preparing late off the glass

The Padel School's back-glass advice: the racket should go back as the ball is about to hit the glass, not after it comes out. Wait for the rebound and you're always late.

Fix: turn side-on and take the racket back at the height of the ball as it travels towards the wall. Low ball, low racket; high ball, high racket.

7. Hitting the ball behind your body

Crowding the wall, or standing too close to the ball's path, means contact happens beside or behind you.

Fix: give yourself more space than feels natural, then step in so you meet the ball in front of your body.

Mistakes with position

8. Standing in no man's land

The area around the service line is the hardest place on court to play from. A ball at your feet there is almost impossible to attack.

Fix: be either at the net or at the back. At the back, start about a step behind the service line. At the net, stand about two to three metres from it. Pass through the middle, but don't stay there.

9. Standing too close to the net

Hugging the net feels aggressive but leaves you open to every lob.

Fix: stay about two to three metres back, racket up. You can still step forward into volleys, and you can cover the lob. See our volley guide.

10. Not moving with your partner

One player at the net, one at the back: the diagonal gap between you is the easiest target in padel.

Fix: imagine a rope between you. If your partner moves forward, so do you; if they're lobbed back, you go back too. If they shift left, you shift left. More in padel positioning for doubles.

Mistakes with preparation and teamwork

11. Racket down, feet flat

Beginners often wait with the racket by their knees and their weight on their heels. By the time the ball arrives, there's no time to prepare.

Fix: racket up in front of you, knees soft, weight on the balls of your feet. Use short side steps and stay on your toes, as the LTA recommends. Turn sideways early as the ball comes.

12. Not talking

Silent pairs leave balls down the middle or collide going for them.

Fix: call every shared ball: "mine", "yours", "go". Agree before the match who takes lobs and balls down the middle.

Two rule mistakes beginners make

Legal

Hitting the ball into your own glass so it rebounds over the net and bounces on the other side. Volleying any ball except the serve.

Point lost

Your shot hits the opponents' glass before bouncing. Touching the net with your racket or body. Letting the ball hit you. Volleying the serve.

Also: the wrist cord is compulsory, and a serve that bounces in the box and then hits the mesh is a fault. Our rules guide covers the rest.

Padel tips for beginners: fix them in practice

You can't fix twelve things at once. Pick one per session, which is also the LTA's advice for practice.

  • For hitting too hard: play a game where any ball that hits the back glass without bouncing loses you two points.
  • For wall fear: have a partner throw balls at the floor just in front of the back glass, and return each one softly.
  • For consistency: rally cross-court and count. Try for ten in a row, then fifteen, then twenty.
  • For position: after every shot in a practice match, check: are we level?

Frequently asked questions

What is the most common padel mistake?

Hitting too hard. Power sends the ball into the glass, where it comes back to the opponents. Control and placement win more points at every club level.

Why do I keep losing points off the back glass?

Usually because you prepare too late or stand too close. Take the racket back as the ball approaches the glass, give yourself space, and hit the ball in front of you.

Where should I stand as a beginner?

Either at the back, about a step behind the service line, or at the net, about two to three metres from it. Avoid staying in the middle of the court, and stay level with your partner.

Should beginners smash?

Only on easy, high balls you can hit in front of you near the net. On other lobs, a controlled bandeja or letting the ball come off the glass is safer.

How long does it take to stop making beginner mistakes?

It varies with how often you play and whether you have coaching. Fixing one habit at a time, with a lesson or two, speeds things up a lot.
