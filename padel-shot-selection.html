Padel shot selection cheat sheet

6 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 8 Nov 2026

Good padel shot selection comes down to one question: what is this ball, from this position, allowed to do? From the back you mostly lob and reset, in the middle you build, and at the net you attack the feet, the middle and the angles. The tables below give you the percentage choice for every common situation, so you can make the decision before the ball arrives.

Padel shot selection: three rules behind every choice

  1. Height decides attack or defence. The Padel School's rule of thumb: a ball between your ankles and knees is for defending with a soft ball, waist to chest allows a controlled shot with some aggression, and above the shoulders is for attacking.
  2. Position decides how much risk you can take. Babolat's model splits the court into three zones: the back (last two metres before the glass) where you stay in the rally, the middle where you build, and the net where you finish.
  3. Height before speed. Bounce's strategy guide sums up percentage padel in those three words. When in doubt, a high deep ball beats a fast flat one.

The lob direction rule from our lob guide: cross-court has the most margin, down the line is hard to smash but has the least margin, and a high lob down the middle is the easiest to attack.

The Padel School's lob breakdown fits this table: short lobs get the víbora or attacking overhead, central lobs the bandeja or víbora, deep lobs go off the back glass.

Ball off the back glass

ReboundBest shotWhy
Low reboundDeep cross-court ballMost margin, keeps you neutral
High reboundLobPushes the net pair back
High, sitting rebound with opponents backBajada (overhead off the glass)A rare chance to attack from the back
Ball stuck behind your hipSafe, high resetYou can't control it, so don't force it

The first, second and last rows follow Bounce's back-glass advice: attack only when the ball comes far enough forward to hit in front of your body. Start your swing as the ball is about to hit the glass, as The Padel School recommends. Remember the rules are on your side here: you may play the ball off your own glass, but never off your own mesh (FIP Rules 14b and 13l).

Babolat's rule: the closer you are to a side wall, the more cautiously you should play. Our guide to the padel side wall covers double-wall rebounds.

How to use this cheat sheet

Don't try to memorise every row. Pick the one table that matches where you lose most points, play a match with that table in mind, and add another next week. Before each shot, ask two quick questions: where am I, and how high is the ball? Those two answers point you to the right row. If you're still unsure when the ball arrives, the default is always the same: high, deep and through the middle or cross-court.

Percentage padel in one paragraph

Most club points are lost, not won. Pick the shot with the most margin for the ball you've been given: higher over the net, more central, deeper. Attack only balls above the shoulder or sitting up at waist height near the net. Save the risky shots for when you're in balance and in the right zone. Bounce lists trying to win from the defensive zone and smashing every overhead among the commonest mistakes; avoiding those two alone will win you points.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most important shot in padel?

The lob. It's the main way the defending pair wins back the net, and it's the safest choice whenever you're at the back and under pressure.

When should I attack in padel?

When the ball is above your shoulder, or sitting up at waist height near the net, and you're in balance. Low balls are for defending with a soft, controlled shot.

Where should I hit the ball in padel?

From the back, deep and usually cross-court or high through the middle. At the net, at the opponents' feet, through the middle between them, or to an angle that makes the next ball float.

Should I smash or play a bandeja?

Play the bandeja on most lobs and recover to the net. Smash only when the ball is high, slow and clearly finishable, because a smash that comes back off the glass often costs you the net.

Can I hit the ball into my own side glass?

Yes. Playing the ball against your own glass so it then crosses the net is legal (FIP Rule 14b). Hitting your own mesh fence is not (Rule 13l).
