Padel plyometrics and footwork drills for a faster first step

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 15 Nov 2026

Most points in padel are won or lost in the first step: how quickly you react, push off and arrive balanced. Plyometrics train your muscles and tendons to store and release energy quickly, and footwork drills turn that into padel movement. Here's a safe progression from beginner to advanced.

General information, not medical advice

This page gives general training guidance. If you have a heart condition, a recent injury or any health concern, check with a doctor or physiotherapist before starting.

Before you start

Plyometrics put high load through the calves, Achilles and knees. Build a base first: you should be able to do around 15 single-leg calf raises and 10 controlled split squats per leg without pain. If you have current knee, ankle or Achilles pain, sort that first. See knee pain and ankle pain.

The progression

LevelDrillDose
1Pogo hops (small, quick, stiff ankles)3 × 15
1Split step to a partner's clap, then a step in the called direction3 × 8
2Lateral line hops, two feet3 × 15 s
2Skater hops, stick each landing for 1 s3 × 6 each side
3Lateral bound into a short sprint3 × 4 each side
3Drop-step and sprint back (lob recovery)3 × 4 each side
3Reaction drill: partner points, you shuffle and shadow the shot4 × 15 s

Quality over quantity: land softly, knees over toes, and stop when your contacts get slower or noisier. Rest 60–90 seconds between sets.

The split step

The split step is padel's most important footwork habit. Just as your opponent hits, make a small hop and land on the balls of both feet, slightly wider than your hips. You're loaded and ready to push off in any direction. Practise it until it happens on every shot.

How often?

  • 1–2 short sessions a week (10–15 minutes) is enough.
  • Do them fresh, after a warm-up, not at the end of a hard match.
  • Leave 48 hours between plyometric sessions and heavy leg days.
  • Over 40? Stay at levels 1–2 longer and progress slowly. See padel over 40.

Frequently asked questions

What are plyometrics for padel?

Jumping and bounding exercises, such as pogo hops, skater hops and lateral bounds, that train fast push-offs and change of direction.

How can I get faster on the padel court?

Master the split step, train short plyometric and footwork drills once or twice a week, build leg strength, and work on reading the ball earlier.

Are plyometrics safe for older padel players?

Yes, if you build a strength base first, start with small low-impact hops and progress slowly. Skip them if you have current joint or tendon pain.

How often should I do footwork drills for padel?

One or two 10–15 minute sessions a week, done fresh after a warm-up.
