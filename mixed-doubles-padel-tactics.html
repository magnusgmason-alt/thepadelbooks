Mixed doubles padel: 9 tactics that work

6 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 8 Nov 2026

Mixed doubles padel is won the same way as any doubles match: choose sides by strengths, own the middle together, target with a plan and keep the respect that makes people want to play again. The extra ingredient is that level and playing style often differ more within a mixed pair, so roles matter even more. Here are nine tactics, plus the formats that make mixed padel easy to find.

1. Choose sides by strengths, not by gender

The standard rule for two right-handers is that the player with the stronger overhead and more attacking game goes left, where the forehand and smash face the middle, and the more consistent builder goes right. Our guide to left side vs right side explains the roles.

In mixed padel, don't assume the man goes left. Ask who has the more reliable overhead, who is more patient, and who is left-handed. All For Padel's coach says personal comfort matters more than the traditional profiles, and a pair with a lefty has its own answer (see our guide to left-handed padel). Play a set each way if you're unsure.

2. Agree who takes the middle before the first point

Balls down the centre cause more arguments than any other shot, and in mixed pairs one partner sometimes takes everything out of politeness or habit. Decide your default: for two right-handers, usually the left-side player's forehand takes it. Then call every shared ball, early, with one word. Padel Addict's coaching advice is to agree these roles before the match and keep communicating; it's the single biggest factor in whether covering your partner's side helps or hurts.

3. Target the weaker player, not a gender

Targeting is normal padel. The smart way to do it is to target the player who is actually weaker today, which may not be who you assumed. Padel Browser suggests identifying the weaker player in the first few games from real signs: who drifts off the back glass, who avoids backhand volleys, whose overheads land short. Then send them most of the ball, around 70%, rather than all of it, because a pair that sees one target adjusts quickly.

That approach is better tactics and better manners. Plenty of mixed matches are lost by pairs who peppered the woman and found she was the steadier volleyer.

4. When you're the target, play the long game

If the ball keeps coming to you, take it as a compliment to your partner and keep it simple:

  • Return deep and through the middle, where there's most margin.
  • Lob deep to push the net pair back; a lob reset beats a risky drive.
  • Vary height and pace so the opponents can't groove their attack.
  • Tell your partner, "They're coming to me," so they can lean towards the middle.

5. Cover the middle when your partner is under pressure

The partner who isn't being targeted should drift towards the centre and take the middle balls they can reach in balance, particularly at the net. Padel Addict's advice is that this happens above all at the net, and that if your partner is defending their line, you shouldn't be afraid to come into their side. Its warning is just as important: take balls that are clearly your partner's and you leave your side open, hit worse shots and create friction.

6. Make the lob your shared weapon

The lob works for every player in a mixed pair, whatever their power. A deep lob from either of you moves both opponents back and gives you the net. If the opponents' strongest player is a big smasher, lob the other one, or lob only when you can get it deep. Padel Browser's point that depth matters more than height applies here.

7. Serve and return for placement, not pace

An accurate serve to the body or into the side glass that keeps the receiver low is worth more than a fast serve in the middle of the box. On the return, aim deep through the middle or low at the feet of a server rushing in, and use the lob against an aggressive net player. Our guide to the padel serve and return covers the details.

8. Keep the respect: mixed padel etiquette

Mixed padel is mostly social, and people come back to groups where they feel respected. A few habits help:

  • Aim smashes at the space, not the face. Hitting at a player during a point is legal and a ball that touches them loses the point (FIP Rule 13k), but at close range in a social game the feet, the middle and the open court are better targets anyway.
  • Never hit the ball at anyone when it's not in play. The FIP's etiquette guidance bans it outright.
  • Don't coach your partner mid-match unless asked. One positive tactical note at the change of ends is plenty.
  • Call your own side honestly and acknowledge net cords and frame shots.

Our full guide to padel etiquette covers the rest.

9. Use the formats built for mixed padel

Finding mixed games is easier than ever:

  • Mixed open matches. Playtomic lets the first player in an open match set the gender option, and one choice is "Mixed", where each team consists of a man and a woman. The setting can't be changed once the match is created.
  • Mixed americanos and mexicanos. In a mixed americano the rotation keeps one man and one woman in each pair while everyone scores individual points. A mexicano re-draws the pairings from the standings each round, which keeps matches closer when levels vary; our guide to padel tournament formats compares them.
  • Club mixed leagues and socials. Ask your club; many timetable them, and they're a good way to find a regular mixed partner.

Train for mixed doubles padel as a pair

Mixed pairs improve fastest when they practise their roles together: who takes the middle, who covers the lob, how you move when one of you is pulled wide. Frequently asked questions

Who should play on the left in mixed padel?

The player with the stronger overhead and more attacking game, if you're both right-handed. That's not automatically the man. If one of you is left-handed, the lefty usually plays the right.

Is it OK to target the woman in mixed padel?

Target the weaker player on the day, whoever that is, and do it most of the time rather than every ball. Choosing a target by gender is poor tactics as well as poor form.

Are the rules different for mixed doubles padel?

No. Mixed matches use the same FIP rules as any doubles match. Only the format, such as mixed open matches or mixed americanos, controls who partners whom.

How do I find mixed padel games?

Look for mixed open matches on booking apps, mixed americanos and socials at your club, and club mixed leagues. Our guide to finding padel players covers more options.

What is a mixed americano?

An americano where each pair is one man and one woman. Partners rotate every round and each player collects the points their pair wins; the highest individual total wins.
