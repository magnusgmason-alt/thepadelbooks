What is padel? The sport explained

6 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 23 Oct 2026

What is padel? It's a racket sport, usually played as doubles, on a smaller, enclosed court where the glass walls are part of the game. You serve underarm, score like tennis, and let the ball come off the walls like squash. It's quick to learn, very social, and one of the fastest-growing sports in the world.

What is padel sport? The short answer

Padel is a racket sport for four players, two on each side of a net, played on a 20 × 10 metre court that is completely enclosed by glass walls and metal mesh. The ball can bounce off the walls and stay in play, which makes rallies long and gives the game its own tactics. The official rules are set by the International Padel Federation (FIP), founded in Madrid in 1991.

If you've never seen it, picture doubles tennis played inside a glass box, with solid rackets and an underarm serve.

What is padel tennis? How the game works

You'll sometimes hear padel called "padel tennis", because the scoring and the basic shots come from tennis. The main rules:

  • The serve is underarm. You bounce the ball behind the service line and hit it at or below waist height, diagonally into the opposite service box. You get two attempts.
  • One bounce per side. After the ball bounces on your side, it can hit the walls or mesh and you can still return it, as long as you hit it before it bounces twice.
  • Your shot must land first. The ball has to bounce on the opponents' floor before touching their walls.
  • You can use your own glass. Hitting the ball into your own glass so it rebounds over the net is legal, provided it then bounces on the other side.
  • Tennis scoring. 15, 30, 40, game; sets to six games; best of three sets. At 40–40 you play advantage, a golden point or the Star Point, depending on the event.

Where padel comes from

According to the FIP, padel was invented in 1969 at Las Brisas in Acapulco, Mexico, by Enrique Corcuera. He didn't have room for a tennis court at his home, so he built a smaller one enclosed by walls. That became the first padel court. In the early 1970s Prince Alfonso of Hohenlohe took the game to Spain, building courts at the Marbella Club, and an Argentine friend of his, Julio Menditeguy, introduced it in Argentina. Spain and Argentina became the sport's two great strongholds, and most padel vocabulary, from bandeja to víbora, is Spanish.

The FIP itself was founded on 12 July 1991 by the Argentine, Spanish and Uruguayan associations. It now has more than 100 national federations.

How big is padel?

Estimates vary with who's counting and how they define a player, so treat these as ranges rather than exact numbers:

  • Playtomic's Global Padel Report 2026, produced with Strategy&, counts 19.4 million players, 58,300 courts and nearly 20,000 clubs worldwide.
  • The FIP's own World Padel Report 2026 puts the figure at around 40 million amateur players in 191 countries and territories, with more than 91,000 courts, and says women make up 47% of participants.

Whichever number you take, padel has grown very quickly. Playtomic's report says nearly 22 new courts opened every day around the world in 2025.

Is padel popular in the US?

It's growing fast from a small base. The Sports & Fitness Industry Association (SFIA) included padel in its participation report for the first time in 2026, and its data shows about 1,073,000 Americans played padel in 2025. Of those, 238,000 played eight or more times. The United States Padel Association (USPA) says just over 1,000 courts were open across 31 states as of April 2026.

Playtomic's report says the US added 250 new clubs in 2025, with growth concentrated in Florida, Texas, California and some East Coast cities. The USPA expects the country could reach 10,000 courts and more than 10 million players by 2030. That's a projection, not a fact, but it shows how quickly the sport is expanding from a small base.

Is padel the same as pickleball?

No, although people mix them up. Both are social, beginner-friendly and usually played as doubles. The differences are big:

  • Walls: padel is played inside glass walls that are part of the game. Pickleball courts are open.
  • Ball and racket: padel uses a pressurised rubber ball and a thick, perforated racket. Pickleball uses a light plastic ball with holes and a flat paddle.
  • Scoring: padel uses tennis scoring; pickleball games go to 11.
  • The kitchen: pickleball has a non-volley zone next to the net. Padel doesn't; you can volley right at the net as long as you don't touch it.

Is padel an Olympic sport?

Not yet. As of October 2026, padel is not on the Olympic programme. The FIP is campaigning for padel to be included at the Brisbane 2032 Olympic Games, and renewed that call when it declared 20 October World Padel Day in 2026.

Padel has, however, been part of several major multi-sport events: the 2023 European Games and, in 2026, the South American, Mediterranean and Asian Games. The Olympic Council of Asia confirmed padel as an official sport at the Aichi-Nagoya 2026 Asian Games in November 2025.

How to pronounce padel

In English, Dictionary.com gives PAD-uhl, with the stress on the first syllable, so it sounds like "paddle". That's also how it's usually said in the UK.

In Spanish the word is written pádel, and the accent on the a puts the stress on the first syllable too: PA-del, with a short, open a and a clear e. You'll also hear pa-DEL, with the stress on the second syllable, from many players in the US. Nobody will correct you on court, whichever you choose. Just don't spell it "paddle": paddle tennis is a different game.

Why people love it

  • It's easy to start. Underarm serve, small court, short rallies from the first session.
  • It's social. Four players, a small court and plenty of talking.
  • It rewards brains over power. The walls keep the ball in play, so positioning and patience win more points than hitting hard.
  • Mixed levels work. A stronger and a weaker player can pair up and still have a good game.

Frequently asked questions

What is padel in simple terms?

A doubles racket sport on a small, enclosed court, where the ball can bounce off the walls. It uses tennis scoring and an underarm serve.

Is padel a type of tennis?

It's a separate sport with its own federation and rules, though it borrows tennis scoring and many tennis strokes. The walls make it play very differently. See padel vs tennis.

Is padel an Olympic sport?

No. As of October 2026 it isn't on the Olympic programme. The FIP is campaigning for inclusion at Brisbane 2032.

Where was padel invented?

In Acapulco, Mexico, in 1969, by Enrique Corcuera, according to the FIP.

Can you play padel with two people?

Yes, for practice or on a narrow singles court, but the sport is designed for doubles and nearly all matches are two against two.
