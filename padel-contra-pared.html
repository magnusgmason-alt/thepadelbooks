The padel contra pared: hitting off your own back wall

6 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 30 Oct 2026

The padel contra pared, or contrapared, is a shot where you hit the ball into your own back glass so it rebounds up and over the net. It's the escape route when a ball has got past you and you can't play it any other way. Coaches treat it as a last resort, not a weapon, but every player needs it. Here is when to use it, how to hit it safely, and what the rules allow.

What is the padel contra pared?

Contra pared means "against the wall". Instead of hitting the ball towards the opponents, you hit it backwards into your own back glass. The ball rebounds off the glass, rises over your head and travels back over the net. HEAD describes it as a defensive shot played after your opponents have lobbed you, which needs plenty of height and depth, and Spanish glossaries such as Total Padel and PadelUSA define it the same way: hitting the ball off the back wall so it then clears the net.

Don't confuse it with ordinary wall play. Normally you let the ball bounce, come off the back glass and drop, and then hit it towards the net. That's the salida de pared, covered in our back glass guide. The contrapared is what you do when that option is gone, because the ball is past you and too close to the glass to let it come out.

Is the contra pared legal?

Yes. Under FIP Rule 14b, a return is correct if the ball hits a wall of your own court first and then bounces in the opponents' court. Two limits apply:

Allowed

You hit the ball into your own back glass (or side glass), it rebounds over the net and bounces in the opponents' court.

Point lost

You hit it into your own mesh (Rule 13l), it drops on your side, or it flies over the net and hits the opponents' glass or mesh before bouncing (Rule 13g).

That last point matters. Padel Addict's advice is that the contrapared must clear the net without hitting the glass on the opposite side, which would lose the point. Our rules article can you play the ball off your own wall? covers every case.

When to use the contra pared

Both Padel Addict articles describe it the same way: a last-moment resource, to use "as a last option" when the ball has passed you and you can't get to it any other way. Typical moments:

  • You've been lobbed at the net and the ball is dropping deep, too close to the glass to let it rebound and come out.
  • A passing ball has beaten you and you're reaching back for it near the back wall.
  • A ball hits the side glass but won't reach the back glass, so there's no back-wall rebound to wait for.

If you can let the ball come off the glass and play it normally, do that. It's safer and gives you far more options. The contrapared is for when the glass has taken that choice away.

Two types: lob and flat

TypeHowWho it's forResult
Lob contrapared (globo)Open racket face, hit high on the glassBeginners and intermediates; the most common versionA high ball back over the net players, buying time
Flat contrapared (recta)More closed face, hit at medium or low height on the glassAdvanced playersA lower ball that forces the net players to volley rather than smash

Padel Addict suggests the flat version against opponents who smash well, because a high, sliced lob contrapared can give a strong smasher exactly the ball they want.

How to hit a contra pared, step by step

  1. Read it early. As soon as you see the ball will get past you and land close to the glass, commit to the contrapared. A late decision is how players end up hitting the glass at the wrong height.
  2. Continental grip. The same grip you use for volleys and overheads, so you don't need to change.
  3. Get behind the ball. Turn and move back towards the glass so the ball is in front of you as you face the wall, with enough room to swing.
  4. Open the face for the lob version. The racket face points up towards the higher part of the glass.
  5. Swing from low to high. Padel Addict's advice is a well-marked shot with enough power to clear the net, using an upward, bottom-to-top racket movement so the ball doesn't come out flat.
  6. Aim high on the glass. For the lob version, strike the highest point of the glass you can. The higher the contact, the higher and safer the rebound.
  7. Finish with the racket tip up, then turn straight away to see where the ball has gone and recover your position.

How hard to hit is the hardest part. Too soft and the ball drops on your side. Too hard and it flies over the net and hits the opponents' glass without bouncing. Our back glass guide puts it simply: hit up with enough power to clear the net, but not so much that the ball carries into their glass.

Conditions change the shot

Padel Addict lists several. On wet glass the ball comes off higher than normal. A wet ball is heavier and travels less. Wind can make the lob fall short or carry too far. And in the sun the ball is livelier, so you need less force. Adjust before the match, not during the point.

After the contra pared

A good lob contrapared buys you time, nothing more. The net players will often have an overhead. As the ball goes over, move back into a solid defensive position with your partner and get ready for a smash or a bandeja. If the contrapared lands deep, you may be able to win the net back on the next ball.

Common mistakes

  • Using it too often. It's a last resort. If you play it on balls you could have let come off the glass, you're giving away the point.
  • Hitting low on the glass. The ball comes out flat and fast, often into your own body.
  • Hitting the mesh. On the back wall the mesh starts above the glass. Hitting your own mesh loses the point.
  • Over-hitting. The ball clears the net and hits the opponents' glass before bouncing.
  • Not looking after the shot. Turn and find the ball immediately.
Safety first

Padel Addict calls the contrapared one of the most dangerous shots for players without enough skill, because a low or mid-glass contact can send the ball straight back into your body or face. Practise it slowly, with high contacts, and consider eye protection. See our padel equipment guide.

How to practise it

Stand two or three metres from the back glass, facing it. Toss the ball gently against the glass at head height and play a soft, upward contrapared over the net. Only when you're consistent, have a partner feed lobs over you from the net so you have to turn, run back and choose: let it come off the glass, or play the contrapared. Learning to choose is as important as the shot itself.

Frequently asked questions

What is a contra pared in padel?

A shot hit into your own back glass so the ball rebounds over the net. It's used when the ball has got past you and you can't let it come off the glass normally.

Is hitting your own wall legal in padel?

Yes, under FIP Rule 14b, as long as the ball then bounces in the opponents' court before touching their walls or mesh. Hitting your own mesh is not allowed.

Is contrapared one word or two?

Both spellings are common: contrapared and contra pared. They mean the same shot.

Should beginners use the contra pared?

Learn the lob version as an emergency shot, with high contact on the glass. Most of the time, beginners should focus on letting the ball come off the glass instead.

Can you play a contra pared off the side glass?

Yes, the rules allow any of your own glass walls. Most contraparedes are played off the back glass, because that's where balls that have passed you end up.
