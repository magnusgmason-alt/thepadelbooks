Padel core exercises: 8 moves for rotation, power and a healthy back

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 15 Nov 2026

Every padel shot passes through your trunk. Your legs push, your trunk turns and transfers the force, and your arm finishes the job. A strong, controlled core gives you more power with less effort and takes load off your lower back and shoulder. These eight exercises train the core the way padel uses it: resisting rotation, producing rotation and holding steady while your limbs move.

General information, not medical advice

This page explains common patterns and general training principles. It can't diagnose you or replace an assessment. If you have a heart condition, a recent injury or any medical concern, check with a doctor or physiotherapist before starting a new programme.

What "core" means for padel

For padel, the core is more than your abs. It's the muscles around your trunk and hips that keep your spine stable and pass force from the ground to the racket. The Aspetar review of padel injuries notes that trunk and lower back problems are common in padel, partly because of the repeated rotation and overhead shots. Training the core to control rotation is a sensible part of keeping your back healthy.

The 8 exercises

ExerciseDoseWhy it helps
Front plank3 × 30–45 sBasic trunk stiffness
Side plank3 × 20–30 s eachSide-to-side control, protects the lower back
Dead bug3 × 8 each sideKeeps the back steady while arms and legs move
Pallof press (band)3 × 10 each sideResists rotation, like stabilising before a shot
Half-kneeling cable or band chop3 × 8 each sideControlled rotation from high to low
Band lift (low to high)3 × 8 each sideRotation pattern of a lob or groundstroke
Bird dog3 × 8 each sideBack extensors and hip control
Medicine ball rotational throw (against a wall)3 × 6 each sideExplosive rotation for overheads

Start with the first five. Add the chop, lift and throws once planks and Pallof presses feel solid.

How to do them well

  • Keep your ribs down and your lower back neutral, not arched.
  • Breathe normally. If you hold your breath, the hold is too hard.
  • On rotation exercises, turn from the hips and trunk together, not just the arms.
  • Quality beats duration: stop when your form slips.

When to do them

Add 3–4 of these to the end of each strength session, or do a 10-minute core circuit twice a week. Light planks and dead bugs also fit into your warm-up. For a full programme, see strength training for padel.

Core work and back pain

Core training is one part of looking after your back, alongside general strength and sensible playing load. If your back already hurts, see our guide to padel back pain, and get any pain with numbness, weakness or leg symptoms checked.

Frequently asked questions

Why is core strength important in padel?

Your trunk transfers force from your legs to the racket and controls rotation, so a strong core adds power and takes load off your lower back and shoulder.

What are the best core exercises for padel?

Planks, side planks, dead bugs, Pallof presses, chops and lifts, and medicine ball rotational throws.

How often should I train my core for padel?

Two or three times a week, usually at the end of a strength session.

Can core exercises help padel back pain?

They can be part of it, alongside general strength and managing your playing load. Pain with numbness, weakness or leg symptoms needs checking.
