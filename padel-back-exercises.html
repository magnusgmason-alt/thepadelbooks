Padel back exercises: a 6-week core and hip plan

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 17 Nov 2026

A padel back rarely needs more stretching. It needs a trunk that stays in control while you twist, bend and reach, and hips that move well enough that your spine doesn't have to. This plan does both in about 20 minutes, twice a week, with a mat and a resistance band.

Not medical advice

This guide explains common patterns in plain language. It can't diagnose you. If the pain is severe, came from a fall or twist, or isn't settling, get it assessed.

Why train the back for padel?

Across the studies in Dahmen and colleagues' 2023 systematic review, the lower back accounted for roughly 9% to 28% of padel injuries. In a 2025 survey of 645 French players (Lecler and colleagues), it was the place players most often reported pain. The Aspetar review of padel injuries links that load to rotation, extension, jumping and smashes.

Exercise is the best-supported prevention tool in sport. A 2014 meta-analysis of 25 trials (Lauersen and colleagues) found strength training cut sports injuries to less than a third, while stretching programmes showed no clear benefit. There is no padel-specific back trial yet, so this plan applies that general evidence to padel's movements.

The exercises

ExerciseWhat it does for padel
Dead bugKeeps the trunk still while arms and legs move
Bird dogBack and hip control without load on the spine
Side plankSide-to-side control for lunges and wide balls
Pallof pressResisting rotation, so hips and trunk turn as one
Half-kneeling band chopControlled rotation through the hips and upper back
Glute bridgeHip extension, taking work away from the lower back
Single-leg Romanian deadliftHinging at the hips with a neutral back, the pattern for low balls
Half-kneeling hip flexor stretchHip extension range for overheads
Open bookUpper-back rotation, so the lower back twists less

The 6-week plan

WeeksTwice a week
1–2Dead bug 3 × 6 each side · Bird dog 3 × 6 each side · Side plank on knees 3 × 20 s each side · Glute bridge 3 × 12 · Open book 2 × 8 each side · Hip flexor stretch 2 × 30 s each side
3–4Dead bug 3 × 8 · Bird dog 3 × 8 · Side plank 3 × 20–30 s · Pallof press 3 × 10 each side · Glute bridge 3 × 15 · Open book 2 × 8
5–6Dead bug 3 × 10 · Side plank 3 × 30–40 s · Pallof press 3 × 12 · Band chop 3 × 10 each side · Single-leg Romanian deadlift 3 × 8 each side · Hip flexor stretch 2 × 30 s

Move slowly and breathe normally; these are control exercises, not endurance contests. If an exercise reproduces your back pain, swap it for an easier one or ask a physiotherapist. Over 40? Spend longer in weeks 1 to 2.

Turn it into a habit

Use the dead bug, bird dog and glute bridge as a five-minute pre-match activation, and do the full plan on non-padel days. In our free workout builder, the "Back & core" quick-start set adds these exercises to a PDF with the dose for your level.

Exercises help, but so does how you move on court. Read getting low without hurting your back and back pain from smashing.

Already sore?

NHS inform calls keeping active an essential part of recovery from back problems, and most flare-ups begin to settle within six weeks. Gentle versions of these exercises are usually fine; see can I play padel with back pain? for how to adjust your padel.

Contact your GP if your back pain is persistent and unchanging despite self-care, if movement doesn't affect the pain, or if it's worse at night. NHS inform describes cauda equina syndrome as rare but serious. Call 111 (or your local emergency number) if you have back pain with any of these:

  • numbness or pins and needles between the inner thighs or genitals, or around the back passage or buttocks
  • new difficulty starting or stopping urination, or leaking urine
  • loss of bladder or bowel control, or loss of sensation when you go to the toilet
  • changes in erection, ejaculation or genital sensation
  • significant trauma, such as a fall from height, in the last seven days

Frequently asked questions

What are the best back exercises for padel?

Dead bugs, bird dogs, side planks, Pallof presses and glute bridges for trunk and hip control, plus hip flexor and upper-back mobility.

How often should padel players do core exercises?

Twice a week is enough to make a difference, with a short activation before matches.

Is stretching enough to prevent back pain from padel?

Probably not on its own. A meta-analysis of 25 trials found strength training reduced sports injuries substantially, while stretching programmes showed no clear benefit.

Can I do back exercises with back pain?

Usually gentle ones, yes. Keeping active helps most back pain. Swap any exercise that reproduces your pain.

Do I need equipment?

A mat and a resistance band for the Pallof press and band chop. A light weight helps later for the single-leg Romanian deadlift.
