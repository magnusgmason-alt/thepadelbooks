Left-handed padel: a guide for lefties and their opponents

6 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 8 Nov 2026

In left-handed padel the usual rule is simple: a lefty plays the right side of the court, so their forehand and overhead cover the middle, and a lefty with a right-handed partner gives the pair two forehands through the centre. Being left-handed is also an advantage in itself, because most of your opponents rarely face your spin. This guide covers the best side, every pairing, how to play against left-handers, and the left-handed pros worth watching.

Why left-handers stand out in padel

Most padel players are right-handed, so most players spend their time reading right-handed spin. Padel Addict's coaching article on left-handers makes the point that a lefty doesn't hit like a righty: their spin goes the other way, so the rebounds come out differently, especially off the side glass and on double-wall balls. Opponents who are used to one pattern have to adjust, and many never quite do.

The other advantage is geometry. The middle of the court is where many shots are played, and left-handers change who covers it.

Best side for a left-handed padel player

The right side, in almost every case. The Padel School says that left-handers, being in the minority, almost always play on the right, because from there the balls down the centre are on their forehand and smash side. All For Padel's coach agrees that the ideal set-up puts the left-hander in the theoretical drive (right) zone.

That gives a lefty on the right a different job from a right-handed drive player. A right-hander on the right is the builder, with the backhand facing the middle. A left-hander on the right has the forehand and overhead in the middle, so The Padel School suggests they look to attack through the middle with smashes and bandejas. Our guide to left side vs right side explains the traditional roles.

Lefty and righty pairs: forehands in the middle

This is the classic and most recommended pairing: right-hander on the left, left-hander on the right. Both forehands face the centre. All For Padel calls it an advantage because both players can cover the centre with the forehand, and Padel USA says it maximises pressure and attacking options.

Making forehands-in-the-middle work

The weakness is the middle itself. Padel USA calls that space the "divorce zone", where doubt between partners creates gaps. With two forehands pointing at it, you need a clear rule: for example, the player whose side the ball is drifting towards takes it, or the player at the net takes it over the player behind. On lobs down the middle, both of you have your overhead side there, so agree who goes before the match. Our guide to partner communication has simple calls.

What about forehands outside?

Putting the lefty on the left and the righty on the right gives both players their forehand along the side glass and both backhands in the middle. It can suit a pair whose backhands are strong and who love attacking down the line, but it makes the centre a target, and we didn't find a coaching source that recommends it as a default. Try it only if the standard set-up clearly isn't working for you.

Two left-handers together

All For Padel says a pair of left-handers works much like two right-handers, mirrored: the forehand position is on the left and the backhand position on the right. So the more attacking lefty plays the right side, with their forehand and overhead covering the middle, and the more consistent lefty plays the left.

How to play against left-handers

Padel Addict and Padel USA give consistent advice:

  • Expect the rebound to come out the other way. Don't wait for the bounce you're used to. Move your feet and follow the ball, especially on double-wall rebounds.
  • Keep your swing short. A compact backswing lets you adjust when the spin surprises you.
  • Don't lob down the middle. A lefty on the right has their forehand and overhead there. Padel Addict advises cross-court lobs that avoid the centre, or a fast lob down the line.
  • Go to the backhand. Padel USA notes that a lefty's backhand is usually less comfortable, and deep balls to that side can force awkward replies.
  • Use the middle to cause doubt. Against a lefty-righty pair, balls through the centre can still create hesitation between the two forehands.
  • Shift a little. Padel Addict suggests adjusting your position a few centimetres towards the lefty's dominant side rather than standing where you would against a right-hander.
Your position matters

Padel Addict notes that if you play the right side, your natural cross-court rally goes to a lefty's backhand, but with little margin; if you're unsure, use a cross-court lob that stays away from the centre. If you play the left, cross-court rallies tend to be more comfortable for you.

Notable left-handed padel players

We've only listed players whose left-handedness we could confirm in reliable sources:

  • Arturo Coello (Spain). HEAD's athlete profile describes him as left-handed ("zurdo"), and Padel Addict calls him the best left-handed player on the professional circuit. Wikipedia lists his position as drive (right side), alongside his partner Agustín Tapia.
  • Juan Martín Díaz (Argentina). Known as "el zurdo Díaz" in the Argentine press. With the right-handed Fernando Belasteguín he formed the world's number-one pair for 13 years, from 2002 to 2015, according to Mar del Plata's La Capital.
  • Álex Ruiz (Spain). Described by Zona de Padel as one of the most respected left-handed players on the World Padel Tour and Premier Padel circuits, listed on the drive side.

Watch any of them on the right side and you'll see the forehands-in-the-middle pattern in action. Our guide to Premier Padel explains the pro tour.

Training tips for lefties

Your partners and coaches are probably right-handed, so most drills are set up for them. Ask for feeds to your forehand from the middle of the court, not just down the line, and practise overheads from the right side, which is where your finishing shots will come from. Frequently asked questions

Which side should a left-handed padel player play?

Usually the right side. From there a left-hander's forehand and overhead face the middle of the court, where many balls are played.

Is it good to have a left-handed partner in padel?

Yes, if you set up well. With the righty on the left and the lefty on the right, both forehands cover the middle. You just need a clear rule for who takes the balls and lobs down the centre.

How do you beat a left-handed padel player?

Expect the spin and rebounds to come out the other way, keep your swing short, avoid lobbing down the middle into their forehand, and play deep to their backhand.

Is Arturo Coello left-handed?

Yes. His racket sponsor HEAD describes him as left-handed, and he plays the drive (right) side with Agustín Tapia.

Where do two left-handers play?

As a mirror image of two right-handers: the more attacking player on the right with the forehand in the middle, and the more consistent player on the left.
