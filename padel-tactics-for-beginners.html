Padel tactics for beginners: the simple rules that win points

7 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 23 Oct 2026

The best padel tactics for beginners are simple: keep the ball in play, move with your partner, win the net with a deep lob, and play through the middle of the court. You don't need clever shots to beat most club pairs. You need fewer errors and better positions than they have, and this guide shows you how to get both.

Why tactics matter more than technique at first

LTA Padel puts it bluntly: at every level, most rallies are won and lost by mistakes. That's good news if you're new. You can't build a perfect bandeja in a week, but you can decide today to stop giving away free points.

Padel also rewards position more than power. The walls bring hard shots back into play, so a big hit that isn't perfectly placed often just returns to your opponents at a comfortable height. A pair that stands in the right place and plays the percentage ball will beat a pair of hard hitters more often than you'd think.

So the padel strategy for beginners below is mostly about two things: making fewer errors, and being in the right part of the court when you hit.

Padel tactics for beginners: the eight rules

1. Get the ball back, one more time

Your first tactic is consistency. HEAD's coaching advice is to keep the ball in play and let the opponents make the errors, and that's the right default for new players. Aim well inside the court, over the middle of the net where it's lowest, and give yourself margin. A rally you keep alive is a rally the other pair can still lose.

2. Move as a pair

Think of you and your partner as tied together. When one goes forward, the other follows. When one is pushed back by a lob, you both go back. The Padel School's point is that if one of you stays back while the other goes up, a big diagonal gap opens through the middle, and good opponents aim straight at it. Side by side, the gaps are small.

That means no "one up, one back" like many social tennis doubles. Both at the net, or both at the back. Our guide to padel positioning in doubles goes into the details.

3. The net is where points are won

The pair at the net usually controls the point. From there you can see the whole court and choose between the feet, the middle, the body or an angle. From the back you have fewer options and you're mostly defending. LTA Padel compares it to a goalkeeper stepping off the line: you look bigger and the opponents have less to aim at.

So your simple game plan is: from the back, look for a way to get forward; at the net, try to stay there.

4. Lob deep to get to the net

When both opponents are at the net and you're at the back, hitting harder rarely works. They're close, quick and expecting it. A deep lob over their heads forces them to turn and run back, and gives you and your partner time to move forward together. That's why the lob is one of the most important shots in padel, not a sign of weakness.

The key word is deep. A short lob sits up for a smash. Aim higher and longer than feels natural, and cross-court gives you the most room for error.

5. When in doubt, play down the middle

The middle is the low-risk target. The net is lowest there, you're far from the side walls, and the opponents may hesitate over who takes it. LTA Padel also points out that hitting through the middle tends to bring the ball back through the middle, which makes it easier for your pair to cover. If you don't know where to hit, the middle is rarely wrong.

6. Let the glass help you

Under the FIP rules, once the ball has bounced on your side it stays in play off the walls, as long as you return it before it bounces a second time (Rule 12). New players often rush to hit the ball before it reaches the back glass. Wait. Let it come off the wall, let it drop, and hit it in front of you. You get more time and an easier ball. Our back glass guide shows the footwork.

When you're the one attacking, aim deep balls at the glass rather than the mesh. The glass gives a predictable rebound; the mesh can do anything.

7. Don't try to win the point at the back

Babolat's coaching splits the court into zones. Near the back glass your priority is staying in the rally, not finishing it: lobs, high deep balls and slow cross-court shots. Big swings from deep in the court are where beginners lose most of their points. Save your attacking shots for when you're at the net and balanced.

8. At the net, set up before you finish

The Padel School's advice on patience applies to everyone: don't go for the winner too early. When you feel the point is there, play one more good ball first, a deep volley or a ball at their feet, and finish on the next one. Even professionals rely on bandejas and lobs far more than smashes.

Padel tactics for the serve and return

The serve is underarm and has to be hit at or below waist height (FIP Rule 6.4), so it's not a weapon in the tennis sense. Its job is to get you to the net.

  • When you serve: serve deep with a little slice, aim for the side glass or the middle, then move forward straight away. Your partner already starts at the net.
  • When you return: don't go for winners. A deep lob over the server's partner or a low, soft ball at the incoming server's feet is enough.
  • Second serve: safe and deep. A double fault is a free point (FIP Rule 13q).

Padel strategy tips: what to do when you're losing

If a match is going badly, don't change everything. Change one thing:

  • Lots of unforced errors? Slow down and aim bigger, safer targets.
  • Getting smashed? Your lobs are short. Lob higher and deeper.
  • Getting passed down the middle? You and your partner are too far apart, or nobody is calling the ball.
  • Losing every net exchange? Lob more and play fewer drives at net players.

HEAD also suggests finding the opponents' weaker side early. Backhands are often weaker than forehands, so test them and keep going there if it works.

The beginner's game plan in one line

Keep it in, move together, lob deep, take the net, play the middle, and let the glass give you time.

Talk to your partner

Call every ball that could be for both of you: "mine", "yours". LTA Padel adds a more advanced habit: tell your partner where the opponents are and what you're planning. And keep it positive. A partner who feels criticised plays tight. Encourage after mistakes, and save the analysis for the change of ends.

Common tactical mistakes beginners make

Do

Move forward together after a good lob. Play the middle when unsure. Let the ball come off the back glass. Keep the second serve safe.

Don't

Stand one up, one back. Smash every lob. Go for winners from the back of the court. Stand still around the service line, where balls land at your feet.

The area around the service line deserves a special mention. It's a place to pass through, not to stand in. A ball at your feet there is one of the hardest shots in the game.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most important padel tactic for beginners?

Consistency. Most rallies at every level are decided by errors, so getting one more ball back in play wins more points than any single shot.

Should beginners play at the net in padel?

Yes. The pair at the net usually controls the point, so learn to move forward with your partner after a good lob or serve, and stand around two to three metres from the net.

Where should I aim in padel as a beginner?

Down the middle when in doubt, deep with your lobs, and at the opponents' feet when they're at the net. Leave the sharp angles for later.

Should I smash every lob?

No. A hard smash that isn't perfect often comes back off the back glass. Smash only the easy, short balls and play a controlled overhead such as a bandeja on the rest.

Is it a good tactic to hit every ball to the weaker player?

It's a common and legitimate tactic, and many coaches recommend finding the opponents' weaker player or weaker side early. Just stay consistent while you do it.
