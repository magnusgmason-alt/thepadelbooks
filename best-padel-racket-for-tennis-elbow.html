Best padel racket for tennis elbow: what to look for

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 17 Nov 2026

If your elbow hurts, the right racket can take some load off it. Light, low balance, round and soft is the combination retailers and brands most often recommend. But a racket supports recovery rather than treating it, so pair the change with the habits and exercises that actually fix tennis elbow.

Not medical advice

This guide explains common patterns in plain language. It can't diagnose you. If the pain is severe, came from a fall or twist, or isn't settling, get it assessed.

Why the racket matters

Tennis elbow, or padel elbow, is an overload of the tendons on the outside of the elbow. A 2023 systematic review of padel injuries (Dahmen and colleagues) found the elbow the most commonly injured site, with lateral epicondylitis the most frequently reported diagnosis. Every shot sends force and vibration up the arm, and a heavy, head-heavy or very stiff racket asks the forearm muscles to work harder to control it. Retailers and brands consistently recommend a few features for sensitive arms.

The checklist

FeatureLook forWhy
WeightThe lighter end of the rangeMost adult rackets are roughly 350 to 380 g; lighter is easier to swing and control
BalanceLow (towards the handle)Less leverage on the forearm, quicker at the net
ShapeRoundLarge, central sweet spot, usually low balance, more forgiving off-centre
CoreSoft EVA or foamAbsorbs more shock and vibration
FaceFibreglass or a softer carbonLess stiff feel than hard carbon faces
GripA fresh, not-too-thin overgripYou don't have to squeeze to hold the racket

What the sources say

  • Soft cores: Padel USA calls soft rackets the safer choice for tennis elbow because they absorb more shock, Decathlon says soft cores reduce vibration and ease wrist strain, and Justpadel links a soft core to comfort for players with arm or elbow pain.
  • Shape: Central Sports says diamond rackets can increase arm fatigue, and round rackets have the largest, most forgiving sweet spot, so off-centre hits jar the arm less.
  • Weight: HEAD says beginners and intermediate players usually prefer 340 to 360 g, and Tennis Warehouse's guide treats lighter rackets as easier on the arm.

There's no clinical trial comparing padel rackets for tennis elbow, so treat this as sensible equipment advice rather than a medical treatment.

The racket is only half the story

Changing rackets without changing anything else rarely fixes an elbow. The bigger levers are:

  • Grip pressure. Poberaj's review of upper-limb padel injuries notes that loosening the grip in the follow-through reduces the force at the elbow by nearly 30%.
  • Load. The NHS's first advice for tennis elbow is to reduce the activities causing the pain. Fewer, shorter sessions while it settles.
  • Strength. Forearm and shoulder exercises over several weeks; see our 6-week padel elbow plan.
  • Technique. Late contact and wristy volleys load the elbow; a coach can spot both quickly.

And remember, much tennis elbow comes from work and daily life rather than sport; see tennis elbow from padel.

Before you buy

  1. Try before you buy: hire or borrow a round, soft racket for a full session.
  2. Compare weights on the spec sheet and pick from the lighter end.
  3. Check the balance: hold the racket at the throat; a low-balance racket won't feel head-heavy.
  4. Put on a fresh overgrip from the start; Padel Market describes its job as absorbing moisture and protecting the base grip.
  5. If pain persists after two weeks of rest and self-care, the NHS suggests seeing a GP; physiotherapy may help if it hasn't improved after six weeks.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best padel racket for tennis elbow?

Look for a light, low-balance, round racket with a soft EVA or foam core and a softer face. That combination absorbs more shock and is easier to control.

Are soft padel rackets better for tennis elbow?

Soft cores absorb more shock and vibration and are the usual recommendation for sensitive arms, but weight, balance, grip and technique matter too.

Is a diamond racket bad for tennis elbow?

Diamond rackets are head-heavy with a small, high sweet spot, which can increase arm fatigue. Most players with elbow pain do better with a round shape.

How heavy should a padel racket be for tennis elbow?

Pick from the lighter end of the usual 350 to 380 g range; HEAD says many beginner and intermediate players prefer 340 to 360 g.

Will a new racket cure my tennis elbow?

No. It can reduce the load, but recovery comes from managing how much you play, strengthening the forearm and improving grip and technique.
