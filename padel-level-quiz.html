    How the padel level quiz works

    Each answer carries points on the 0 to 7 scale. The skill questions (rally, back glass, side glass, serve, volley, overheads, lob and positioning) count most, and the back glass, volley and overhead count a little more, because they separate levels more clearly than anything else. How long and how often you play counts less: plenty of players have played for years without moving up.

    Two honest limits apply. If balls off the back glass still beat you, the quiz won't put you above 3.0, and if your volleys are not yet reliable, it won't put you above 3.5. That matches how matches really go: opponents find the weakness quickly.

    Not an official rating

    There's no single official padel scale. Booking apps such as Playtomic set your level from competitive match results, and federations use their own systems. Use this quiz as a self-check for choosing matches, lessons and training. Our guide to padel levels compares the main scales.

    What each padel level looks like

    LevelStageWhat it looks like
    0–0.5BeginnerYou're at the start. Learning the grip, the underarm serve and how the ball comes off the glass will make the biggest difference fastest.
    1–1.5LearningYou can rally a little and you know the basics. The walls are still the main puzzle, and that's normal at this stage.
    2–2.5DevelopingRallies are getting longer and the back glass is less scary. Next step: volleys you trust and a clear plan with your partner.
    3–3.5CoordinatedYou play the walls, rally well and move with your partner. This is where the bandeja and smarter lobs start to win matches.
    4–4.5SolidA strong club player: consistent, tactical and comfortable at the net. Gains now come from decisions under pressure and fine overheads.
    5–5.5AdvancedYou control most rallies and choose your shots on purpose. Tournament-level details matter now: depth, variety and patterns with your partner.
    6–7Top tierCompetition level. Your edge comes from specific, high-intensity training and match play against players at your level or above.

    The stage names follow the seven development stages Playtomic describes for its 0 to 7 scale. The descriptions are ours.

    How to move up a padel level

    The result shows the three areas where you scored lowest. Start there. A level goes up when your weakest shot stops costing points, not when your best shot gets better. Fifteen minutes of focused drills before or after a match does more than another hour of match play.     Frequently asked questions

    What padel level am I as a beginner?

    If you've played fewer than a handful of times, you're around level 0 to 1 on the 0 to 7 scale. Most new players who can keep a slow rally going and return easy balls off the back glass sit around 1.5 to 2.

    What is an average padel level?

    Most regular club players sit somewhere around 2 to 4 on the 0 to 7 scale. Levels 5 and above are usually reserved for strong tournament players.

    Is this quiz the same as my Playtomic level?

    No. Playtomic sets a starting level from its own questionnaire and then changes it based on competitive match results. This quiz gives an honest self-assessment on a similar 0 to 7 scale, which is useful for choosing matches, lessons and training, but it isn't an official rating.

    Should I pick a lower or a higher level?

    If you're between two levels, pick the lower one. A match that's slightly too easy is still fun; a match that's too hard isn't, for you or for the other three players.

    How can I move up a level in padel?

    Train the area that holds you back most, not the one you already enjoy. For most club players that's the back glass, the volley or the bandeja. Short, scored drills between matches help far more than matches alone.
