Premier Padel explained: how the pro tour works in 2026

7 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 30 Oct 2026

Premier Padel is the professional world tour of padel: the circuit where the best players on the planet compete for prize money and FIP ranking points, from February to December, in cities across Europe, the Americas, the Middle East and Africa. Since 2024 it has been the only top-level tour. This guide explains the tournament categories, how ranking points work, what happened to the World Padel Tour, and how you can watch the matches.

What is Premier Padel?

Premier Padel, officially the Qatar Airways Premier Padel Tour for sponsorship reasons, is the professional circuit for the world's best men and women. It is governed by the International Padel Federation (FIP), backed by Qatar Sports Investments (QSI) and supported by the players' association, the PPA. Men's and women's draws run side by side at almost every event.

It isn't the same thing as the padel you play at your club, but the rules are the same. The pros play under the FIP rulebook, with a few tour-specific choices, such as the Star Point scoring format introduced in 2026. If you're new to the sport, start with what is padel.

From World Padel Tour to Premier Padel

For a decade the top circuit was the World Padel Tour (WPT). It ran from 2013 to 2023, replaced the earlier Padel Pro Tour, and was managed by Setpoint Events, a subsidiary of the Spanish brewer Damm.

Premier Padel was created as an alternative. Its first season was 2022, and its first tournament was the Qatar Major in Doha, from 28 March to 2 April 2022. That first year had eight events: four Majors (Doha, Rome, Paris and Monterrey) and four P1s (Madrid, Mendoza, Giza and Milan). For two seasons there were two rival top-level tours.

On 24 August 2023, QSI announced it had bought the World Padel Tour from Damm. Both tours finished their 2023 schedules, and from 2024 the circuits were combined under the Premier Padel name, run by QSI and governed by the FIP. The World Padel Tour ended after eleven seasons. P2 tournaments and a season-ending Finals were added in 2024. For the longer story of the sport, see our padel history guide.

Premier Padel tournament categories

Every Premier Padel event belongs to a category. The higher the category, the more ranking points (and prize money) are on offer. These are the winner and finalist points for each category under the ranking system the FIP introduced in 2025, as listed by Padel Alto; Red Bull's 2026 season guide gives the same figures:

Draws are big. For 2025 the FIP set Major main draws at 48 pairs for men and 40 for women, with qualifying rounds before that, though it noted some draws may vary.

The Premier Padel 2026 season

The 2026 calendar originally listed four Majors, 10 P1s, 11 P2s and the Finals, starting with the Riyadh P1 in February and ending in Barcelona in December. Then it changed:

  • 16 March 2026: the Qatar Major, due in Doha from 6 to 11 April, was postponed. The organisers cited "the unprecedented situation in the wider region". It was not rescheduled, and the Qatar Major did not take place in 2026.
  • 7 May 2026: Premier Padel and the FIP upgraded two events, for the 2026 season only. Pretoria (27 July to 2 August) went from P2 to P1, and Kuwait (26 to 31 October) went from P1 to Major.

So the 2026 Majors are Italy (Rome, June), Paris (September), Kuwait (late October) and Mexico (Acapulco, November). The Finals take place at the Palau Sant Jordi in Barcelona in December.

Calendars change

Dates, venues and categories have moved during the season, so check the official Premier Padel calendar before you book tickets or travel. This page reflects the calendar as of late October 2026.

How Premier Padel ranking points work

There's one official world ranking: the FIP ranking. It's an individual ranking, so each player has their own points even though they play in pairs. According to the FIP, three rules shape it:

  1. Your best 22 results count. Results from Premier Padel and the CUPRA FIP Tour are combined, and only a player's 22 best results are used. You can play more events, but the weaker results don't count.
  2. Points last 52 weeks. The ranking runs on a continuous 52-week cycle. A player must match or beat last year's result in the same week to keep those points. If they don't, the old points drop off and the new result replaces them.
  3. Bigger events, bigger points. A Major winner earns twice as much as a P1 winner, and more than three times as much as a P2 winner.

This is why "defending points" matters so much. A pair that won a Major last year has 2,000 points to defend in that week. Lose early and they can drop places in the ranking even while playing well elsewhere.

The CUPRA FIP Tour

Below Premier Padel sits the CUPRA FIP Tour, run by the FIP itself. Since 2025 it has four categories, with these winners' points: Platinum 300, Gold 150, Silver 80 and Bronze 40. Its results count towards the same FIP ranking, and the ranking decides who gets into each draw.

The FIP Race and the Finals

Alongside the ranking there's the FIP Race, which counts results across the calendar year and decides who plays the Finals. Sixteen men and sixteen women qualify, which means eight pairs per draw. The FIP Race also uses each player's best 22 results of the year.

Who are the best Premier Padel players?

Rankings move week to week, so treat this as a snapshot. In mid-September 2026, the FIP listed thirteen players already qualified for the Barcelona Finals. The six men were Coello, Tapia, Chingotto, Galán, Lebrón and Augsburger. The seven women were Triay, Brea, González, Josemaría, Ustero, Sánchez and Fernández.

In the men's draw, two pairs have shared most of the 2026 titles: Agustín Tapia with Arturo Coello, and Alejandro Galán with Federico Chingotto. In the women's draw, Delfina Brea and Gemma Triay, and Bea González and Paula Josemaría, have won the most events. For the current order, check the live FIP ranking, because pairs split and re-form during the season.

Premier Padel scoring: what's different

The pros play under the same FIP rules as you. The big difference in 2026 is what happens at deuce. Instead of golden point or unlimited advantage, Premier Padel uses the Star Point, approved by the FIP General Assembly in November 2025. It was first used at the FIP Bronze Melbourne in January 2026 and made its Premier Padel debut at the Riyadh P1 in February.

It works in three stages from 40-40: win the first advantage point and you win the game; lose it and you go back to deuce. The same goes for the second advantage. If the score reaches deuce a third time, one deciding point, the Star Point, settles the game. Our guides to golden point and padel scoring compare all three systems.

How to watch Premier Padel

In February 2024, Premier Padel signed a multi-year partnership with Red Bull, running until at least the end of 2027. Under that deal:

  • Red Bull TV shows matches live and free from the quarter-finals onwards, in territories without another broadcast partner.
  • Premier Padel's YouTube channel streams the earlier rounds, rounds one to three, in those same territories.
  • Local broadcasters hold the rights in some countries. Padel Speak's 2026 guide lists, for example, Movistar+ in Spain, Sky Italia in Italy, Canal+ in France and beIN SPORTS in the USA, Canada and Australia, with coverage of different rounds.

Rights differ by country and can change between seasons, so check what's available where you live. Watching the pros is one of the best ways to learn: notice how often they lob, how patient they are off the glass, and how rarely they smash a ball that isn't ready. Frequently asked questions

What is Premier Padel?

Premier Padel is the professional world padel tour, governed by the International Padel Federation and backed by Qatar Sports Investments. Since 2024 it has been the only top-level circuit, after QSI bought the World Padel Tour and combined the two tours.

Does the World Padel Tour still exist?

No. QSI bought the World Padel Tour in 2023, both tours finished that season, and from 2024 they were unified under the Premier Padel name.

What is the difference between a Major, a P1 and a P2?

They're tournament categories. A Major is the biggest, worth 2,000 points to the winners. A P1 gives the winners 1,000 points and a P2 gives them 600. The Finals, for the best eight pairs of the year, are worth 1,500 to the winners.

How many results count in the FIP ranking?

A player's best 22 results from Premier Padel and the CUPRA FIP Tour, over a rolling 52-week period.

Where can I watch Premier Padel for free?

In countries without a local broadcaster, Red Bull TV shows the quarter-finals onwards live and free, and Premier Padel's YouTube channel streams the early rounds. Elsewhere, a local broadcaster may hold the rights.

Do the pros play golden point?

Not in 2026. Premier Padel uses the Star Point: two advantage points, then one deciding point at the third deuce.
