Hip pain from padel: causes, exercises and when to get it checked

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 15 Nov 2026

Padel asks a lot of your hips: deep lunges for low balls, sudden stops, and constant rotation for groundstrokes and overheads. If your hips are stiff or weak, that load can turn into pain on the outside of the hip, in the front, or deep in the groin. Here are the common causes, what you can do yourself, and the exercises that make your hips more robust.

General information, not medical advice

This page explains common causes and general self-care. It can't diagnose your pain. If pain is severe, follows a fall or a loud pop, stops you putting weight on the leg, or isn't improving after a couple of weeks, see a doctor or physiotherapist.

Where does it hurt?

AreaCommon causes in padel players
Outside of the hipIrritated tendons of the gluteal muscles or bursa; often worse lying on that side
Front of the hipHip flexor strain or tightness; pain when lifting the knee or lunging
Inner thigh / groinGroin (adductor) strain, especially after a wide lunge
Deep in the hip jointJoint problems such as early osteoarthritis or hip impingement, more common with age
Buttock, spreading down the legOften coming from the lower back

What you can do

NHS advice for hip pain is to rest it, avoid activities that make it worse, use an ice pack wrapped in a towel for 20 minutes several times a day, take painkillers such as paracetamol or ibuprofen if suitable, wear flat comfortable shoes, and lose weight if you are overweight. Try to stay as active as you can within the pain: complete rest usually isn't helpful for long.

On court

  • Warm up with leg swings, walking lunges and side shuffles before playing.
  • Use smaller adjustment steps instead of one huge lunge where you can. Good positioning helps.
  • Build up match time gradually, especially after 40.
  • Finish with a cool-down for the hips.

When to get help

The NHS advises going to A&E if hip pain follows a fall or injury and the leg looks deformed, is badly bruised or swollen, or you can't move or put weight on it, and getting urgent advice if you have hip pain with a high temperature or feel unwell. See a GP if pain hasn't improved after two weeks, keeps coming back, or is stopping you doing normal activities.

Frequently asked questions

Why do my hips hurt after padel?

Deep lunges, sudden stops and rotation load the hip muscles and tendons. Common causes are irritated gluteal tendons on the outside, hip flexor strain at the front, and groin strain on the inside.

What exercises help hip pain from padel?

Mobility work such as 90/90 switches and hip flexor stretches, plus strength exercises like bridges, side planks, split squats and band walks.

Can I keep playing padel with hip pain?

If it's mild and settles quickly, reduce your play and keep exercising. Stop and get it checked if it's getting worse, you're limping or it hurts at night.

Is padel bad for your hips?

Not for most people. The movement is good for the hips if you build up gradually, warm up and keep them strong and mobile.
