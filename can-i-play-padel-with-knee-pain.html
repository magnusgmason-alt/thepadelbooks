Can I play padel with knee pain? A simple decision guide

4 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 17 Nov 2026

Short answer: sometimes yes, sometimes absolutely not. A knee that aches a bit from overuse can often keep playing at a lower load, while a knee that swells, locks or gives way needs to come off court and get checked. Here is a simple way to decide before your next match, and how to adjust your padel so a niggle doesn't turn into months out.

Not medical advice

This guide explains common patterns in plain language. It can't diagnose you. If the pain is severe, came from a fall or twist, or isn't settling, get it assessed.

Step 1: check for red flags first

Don't play, and get advice from NHS 111 or your local equivalent, if:

  • your knee is very painful
  • you can't move it or put any weight on it
  • it is badly swollen or has changed shape
  • it locks, gives way or clicks painfully (painless clicking is normal)
  • you have a very high temperature, or feel hot, cold or shivery, with redness or heat around the knee

Also stop if you felt a pop at the moment of injury followed by quick swelling, or if the knee gave way. Mayo Clinic notes that a torn meniscus can follow a forceful twist, often with a pop, swelling or stiffness, and catching or locking.

Step 2: rate your pain honestly

If there are no red flags, the most practical tool is a pain-monitoring rule used in tendon and knee rehabilitation (tested for patellar tendinopathy by Sprague and colleagues in 2021):

During and right after padelNext morningWhat it means
0 to 2 out of 10NormalGreen: carry on, keep doing your strength work
Up to about 5 out of 10Back to its usual levelAmber: acceptable, but don't add more load yet
Above 5, or rising during playWorse than beforeRed: the session was too much; cut back next time

The next-morning check is the most useful part. Pain that is back to normal by the next day means your knee coped. Pain or stiffness that is worse means it didn't.

Step 3: if you play, change the load, not just your hopes

  • Play shorter. One hour instead of two. Skip tournaments and Americanos for a few weeks.
  • Leave a day between sessions so you can do the next-morning check.
  • Cut the jumps. Swap jumping smashes for bandejas and stay down on overheads.
  • Move with small steps. Take extra adjusting steps instead of long lunges, and turn with your feet rather than twisting on a planted foot.
  • Warm up properly. Ten minutes of easy movement, mini-band work and split steps before you hit hard. Our padel warm-up has a routine.
  • Check your shoes. Worn-out soles that stick or slip add twisting load. See padel shoes.

Step 4: keep strengthening on non-padel days

Rest from padel shouldn't mean rest from everything. Strength training is the best-supported way to protect against sports injuries: a 2014 meta-analysis (Lauersen and colleagues) found it reduced injuries to less than a third. For front-of-knee pain, the 2016 consensus statement (Crossley and colleagues) recommends combining hip and knee exercises. When "playing through it" goes wrong

The usual story: you play through a mild ache, it's a bit worse the next day, you play again because it eased once you were warm, and three weeks later you can't do stairs without pain. Tendon and kneecap pain both behave this way, because they often warm up and feel fine during play. That's why the next-morning rule matters more than how it feels mid-match.

See a GP or physio if the knee hasn't improved within a few weeks, or if the pattern keeps repeating despite cutting back. In many areas of the UK you can refer yourself to NHS physiotherapy without seeing a GP first.

Frequently asked questions

Is it OK to play padel with a sore knee?

With a mild overuse ache and no red flags, often yes, at reduced load. Keep pain to a moderate level during play and make sure it settles by the next morning.

Should I play padel with a knee brace?

A sleeve can make the knee feel more comfortable, but it won't fix the cause. If your knee feels unstable, get it assessed rather than relying on a brace.

Can padel make knee pain worse?

Yes, especially lots of jumping, deep lunging and long sessions on consecutive days. Reducing those while you build strength usually helps.

Should I use ice or heat after padel?

The NHS suggests an ice pack wrapped in a towel for up to 20 minutes every two to three hours for a sore knee.

When should I see a doctor for knee pain from padel?

Straight away if it's very painful, swollen, locks, gives way or you can't take weight. Otherwise, if it hasn't improved within a few weeks.
