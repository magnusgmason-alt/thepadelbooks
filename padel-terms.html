Padel terms: the glossary every player needs

6 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 30 Oct 2026

Padel terms come mostly from Spanish, because the sport grew up in Spain and Argentina: bandeja, víbora, chiquita, bajada, por tres. Once you know twenty or so words, coaching videos, pro commentary and club chat all make sense. This glossary covers the court, the shots, scoring and formats, and the slang you'll hear on court, with definitions that match our shot guides.

Why padel terms are mostly Spanish

Padel was invented in Mexico in 1969 and grew up as a sport in Spain and Argentina. The shot names come from there. English speakers do have their own words for some shots (lob for globo, smash for remate), but the shots with no tennis equivalent, like the bandeja and the víbora, kept their Spanish names everywhere.

You don't need to speak Spanish to use them. Pronounce them roughly as written and everyone will understand. The racket, by the way, is a pala in Spanish, and the ball is a pelota.

Padel shot terms: the basics

  • Derecha / drive: the forehand. In Spanish padel, "drive" often refers to the forehand or the forehand side of the court.
  • Revés: the backhand.
  • Volea: a volley, hit before the ball bounces.
  • Bote pronto: a half-volley, played just after the bounce.
  • Globo: the lob, high over the net players. In padel it's the main way to win back the net.
  • Chiquita: "little one". A soft, low ball from the back that drops at the net players' feet so they have to volley upwards.
  • Dejada: a drop shot that lands just over the net, often played as a soft volley. See the padel drop shot.
  • Dormilona: "sleepy". A very soft drop shot that barely bounces on the other side.
  • Cruzado / paralelo: cross-court and down the line.
  • Cortado / liftado / plano: slice (backspin), topspin and flat.

Padel overhead terms

Padel has more names for overheads than any other racket sport, because the glass changes what an overhead needs to do.

  • Remate: the smash in general.
  • Bandeja: "tray". A controlled, sliced overhead hit at around head height, whose job is to keep you at the net when you're lobbed. It wins the position rather than the point. See how to hit a bandeja.
  • Víbora: "viper". A faster overhead hit around head height, brushing the outside of the ball for side spin and slice, so it skids low off the side glass.
  • Por tres (x3): "by three". A smash that bounces, hits the back wall and goes out over the side, named after the 3 m height of the side enclosure.
  • Por cuatro (x4): "by four". A flat, powerful smash that bounces high enough to go out over the back of the court, which is 4 m high.
  • Rulo: a topspin smash played diagonally towards the mesh, where the bounce is unpredictable. The LTA describes it as a lower-risk option than a flat smash.
  • Gancho: "hook". Definitions vary: PadelUSA describes it as a shot between a bandeja and a smash, hit with a hooking motion, while The Padel Weekly calls it a softer overhead to hold the net. Ask your coach what they mean by it.
  • Cuchilla: "knife". A cross between a bajada and a víbora, with side spin.

Padel wall terms

  • Salida de pared: "coming off the wall". Playing the ball after it rebounds off your back or side glass. It's the most important wall skill: see how to play off the back glass.
  • Bajada (de pared): "descent". An attacking shot from the back of the court, played after a lob has bounced and come high off your back glass, from around shoulder height or above.
  • Contrapared: "against the wall". Hitting the ball into your own back glass so it rebounds over the net, usually as a last resort when the ball has got past you. It's legal under FIP Rule 14b, as long as it then bounces in the opponents' court. See the contra pared guide.
  • Boast: borrowed from squash. An angled shot into your own side glass that then crosses the net.

Scoring and format terms

  • Golden point (punto de oro): one deciding point at 40-40 instead of advantage. See golden point padel.
  • Star Point: the format the pro tours use in 2026. Two advantage points are played; if it reaches deuce a third time, one deciding point settles the game.
  • Iguales / ventaja / juego / desempate: deuce, advantage, game and tie-break.
  • Americano: a social format where partners and opponents rotate and everyone keeps an individual score.
  • Mexicano: like an Americano, but after the first round the pairings are set by the live leaderboard.
  • King of the court: a multi-court format where winners move up a court and losers move down.

Padel slang you'll hear on court

Every club has its own slang, and these words come from Spanish courts. Their meanings vary a little from glossary to glossary, so take them as the common usage, not official definitions.

  • Nevera: "the fridge". Hitting almost every ball to one opponent, so the other player is frozen out of the game. The LTA lists it as a tactic.
  • Willy: a shot hit between the legs, usually with your back to the net.
  • Cadete: a shot hit behind the back.
  • Moco: a slow, safe shot that just pushes the ball over to the other side with little risk of a mistake.
  • Tapar: to block an opponent's smash or bandeja.
  • Chancletazo: a quick, flat, hard hit.
  • Amago: a fake: shaping up for a smash and playing a soft ball instead.

The ten padel terms to learn first

If you only learn ten, learn these, in roughly the order you'll need them:

  1. Glass and mesh: your own glass is playable, your own mesh isn't (FIP Rule 13l).
  2. Golden point: agree before the match whether you're playing it.
  3. Globo: the lob, your way back to the net.
  4. Volea: what you hit at the net.
  5. Salida de pared: letting the ball come off the glass.
  6. Bandeja: the overhead that keeps the net.
  7. Chiquita: the low alternative to the lob.
  8. Víbora: the attacking overhead.
  9. Bajada: attacking a lob that comes high off your glass.
  10. Por tres: the smash that leaves the court over the side.

Frequently asked questions

What does bandeja mean in padel?

Bandeja is Spanish for "tray". It's a controlled, sliced overhead hit at around head height, used to stay at the net when the opponents lob you.

What is the difference between a bandeja and a víbora?

Both are overheads hit around head height. The bandeja cuts under the ball for control and keeps the net; the víbora brushes the outside of the ball for side spin and pace, so it stays low off the side glass.

What does por tres mean?

"By three". It's a smash that bounces, hits the back wall and goes out over the 3 m side enclosure. Por cuatro is the version that goes out over the 4 m-high back of the court.

What is a chiquita in padel?

A soft, low shot from the back of the court that dips at the feet of the net players, forcing them to volley upwards so you can move forward.

What is a contrapared?

A shot hit into your own back glass so it rebounds over the net. It's legal if the ball then bounces in the opponents' court first, and it's usually a last resort.

Is it padel or paddle?

Padel. Paddle tennis and platform tennis are different sports, and padel is always spelled with one d and an e.
