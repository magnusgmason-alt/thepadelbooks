Padel calories burned: what the evidence says

6 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 23 Oct 2026

There's no official figure for padel calories burned, but a reasonable estimate for an hour of padel is roughly 300 to 600 kcal for most adults, depending on your body weight and how hard you play. Here's where that range comes from, why it varies so much, and what the research says about padel as a workout.

Padel calories burned: the short answer

For most adults, an hour of padel probably burns somewhere around 300 to 600 kcal. A lighter player in a relaxed social game sits at the bottom of that range; a heavier player in a hard, competitive match can go above it.

Treat any single number you see online with caution. Calorie burn depends on your weight, the intensity of the game, how long the points are, and how much time you spend picking up balls and changing ends. That's why we give a range and show the working.

Where the numbers come from

The standard reference for the energy cost of activities is the Compendium of Physical Activities, last updated in 2024 and published in the Journal of Sport and Health Science. It gives each activity a MET value: how many times more energy it uses than sitting at rest.

The 2024 Compendium has no entry for padel. It does list tennis, which is the closest well-studied racket sport:

Compendium activity (code)MET
Tennis, doubles (15685)4.5
Tennis, doubles (15680)6.0
Tennis, general, moderate effort (15675)6.8
Tennis, singles (15690)8.0
Tennis, general, competitive (15676)8.0

Because padel is played as doubles, the doubles tennis values (4.5 to 6.0) are a sensible starting point for a social game, with the moderate and competitive values (6.8 to 8.0) as a guide for harder matches. This is an approximation: padel and tennis aren't the same sport, and no padel-specific MET value has been published in the Compendium.

Padel calories per hour: estimates by weight

The Compendium's conversion is: kcal per minute = MET × 3.5 × body weight in kg ÷ 200. Multiply by 60 for an hour. Using the tennis values above:

Body weightEasy social game (4.5 MET)Steady game (6.0 MET)Hard, competitive (8.0 MET)
60 kgabout 285 kcalabout 380 kcalabout 505 kcal
70 kgabout 330 kcalabout 440 kcalabout 590 kcal
85 kgabout 400 kcalabout 535 kcalabout 715 kcal

These are estimates per hour of continuous play at that intensity. Real sessions include warm-up, breaks at change of ends and time between points, so your actual figure for "an hour at the club" will usually be lower than the table suggests. MET values also include the energy you'd burn anyway at rest.

Why your number varies

  • Body weight. A heavier person uses more energy for the same movement. That's built into the formula.
  • Intensity. Long rallies and lots of running burn more than short, slow points.
  • Level. Research shows points and match profiles differ between levels. Mellado-Arbelo and Baiget's 2022 systematic review found professional players had the longest game durations, and that distance covered and speed actually decreased as standard increased, likely because better players position and anticipate better.
  • Rest. Padel is intermittent. In studies of professional play, average point duration ranged from about 9 to 17 seconds, with similar or longer gaps between points (Guijarro-Herencia et al., 2023).
  • Fitness and age. Individual metabolism varies, which no table can capture.
What about fitness trackers?

Watches estimate calories from heart rate and movement. They're useful for comparing your own sessions over time, but their absolute numbers are estimates too. Don't treat them as more precise than the ranges above.

Is padel a good workout?

For most people, yes. The research describes padel as a moderate-intensity, intermittent sport:

  • Heart rate. A 2023 review in Frontiers in Sports and Active Living found heart rate during play was consistently around 70 to 80% of maximum across studies (Guijarro-Herencia et al.).
  • Oxygen use. A study of elite young male players found oxygen consumption during simulated competition stayed below 50% of their maximum, with average heart rate around 74% of maximum (Carrasco et al., 2011).
  • Overall load. Mellado-Arbelo and Baiget's review concluded that physiological responses were mostly moderate, that perceived effort ranged from moderate to relatively high, and that work-to-rest ratios confirm padel's stop-start nature.

The NHS recommends adults do at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity activity a week, or 75 minutes of vigorous activity. The NHS doesn't list padel, but it does list doubles tennis as a moderate-intensity example. Given the research above, regular padel can be a good way to work towards that target, especially because it's social and people tend to keep playing.

To get more out of it physically, warm up properly, play longer rallies and add some strength work. Our guide to padel exercises covers that, and our padel warm-up helps you start safely.

Padel vs tennis calories

We couldn't find a peer-reviewed study that measured calorie burn in padel and tennis side by side, so be wary of articles that claim one burns a precise amount more than the other.

What we can say: the Compendium values for doubles tennis (4.5 to 6.0 MET) are lower than for singles tennis (8.0 MET). Padel is a doubles game on a smaller court, so a typical padel session is probably closer to doubles tennis than to singles. On the other hand, the walls keep the ball in play, which can mean more rallying and less time collecting balls. Overall, expect a broadly similar calorie burn to doubles tennis, with intensity mattering far more than the sport. Our padel vs tennis guide compares the two in more detail.

How to burn more calories playing padel

  • Play longer. Ninety minutes at a steady level burns more than an intense half-hour.
  • Raise the intensity. Play opponents who push you, so rallies last longer.
  • Try singles or drills. More shots per player and more running. See padel singles.
  • Keep breaks short. The FIP rules allow up to 20 seconds between points (Rule 2). Casual games often take much longer.

Frequently asked questions

How many calories does 1 hour of padel burn?

Roughly 300 to 600 kcal for most adults, based on tennis MET values from the Compendium of Physical Activities. Lighter players and relaxed games are at the lower end; heavier players and hard matches can go higher.

Is there an official MET value for padel?

No. The 2024 Compendium of Physical Activities doesn't list padel, so estimates use related sports such as doubles tennis.

Does padel burn more calories than tennis?

There's no reliable head-to-head study. Padel is probably closer to doubles tennis than singles tennis, and intensity matters more than which sport you play.

Is padel good for losing weight?

It can help as part of regular activity, because it's moderate exercise people enjoy and stick with. Weight loss also depends on diet and total activity, not one sport.

Is padel cardio?

Yes. Studies show heart rate around 70 to 80% of maximum during play, with an intermittent, mostly aerobic profile.
