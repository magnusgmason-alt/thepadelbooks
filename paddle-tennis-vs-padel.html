Paddle vs padel: what's the difference?

6 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 30 Oct 2026

Paddle vs padel: padel is the doubles racket sport played on a glass-walled 20 × 10 metre court, while "paddle" usually means paddle tennis, a separate American game played on an open court with no walls. The names sound almost identical, which is why people mix them up, and there's also platform tennis, POP tennis and pickleball to confuse things further. Here is how to tell them apart.

Padel or paddle: which spelling is right?

The sport on the glass court is padel. That's the spelling used by the International Padel Federation (FIP) and by national federations around the world. In Spanish it's written pádel.

The confusion is understandable, because the name did come from English. According to Padel Addict's summary of the Real Academia Española dictionary entry, pádel comes from the English "paddle tennis", with a spelling that matches how Spanish speakers say it. In 2018, two Spanish sports scientists, Javier Courel-Ibáñez and Bernardino Javier Sánchez-Alcaraz, published a paper calling for one agreed term for teaching and research, and proposed "padel", the FIP's spelling, to avoid confusion with other racket sports.

Searching for a court?

Search for "padel" not "paddle". "Paddle" will bring up paddle tennis, platform tennis, pickleball and even paddleboarding.

Pronunciation is a separate question. Many English speakers say padel exactly like "paddle", and that's fine. Our guide to what padel is covers the different ways people say it.

Padel in thirty seconds

Padel was invented in 1969 in Acapulco, Mexico, by Enrique Corcuera, according to the FIP. It's played almost always as doubles on a 20 × 10 metre court enclosed by glass and metal mesh. You serve underarm, score like tennis, and once the ball has bounced on your side you can play it off the walls. The racket is solid and perforated, with a compulsory wrist cord, and the ball is pressurised, like a slightly smaller tennis ball. The full rules are in our padel rules guide.

What is paddle tennis?

Paddle tennis is an American sport that is much older than padel. It was invented in 1898 by Frank Peer Beal, an Episcopal minister in Albion, Michigan, and Beal persuaded New York's parks department to lay courts in Washington Square Park in 1915.

  • Court: 50 × 20 feet (about 15.2 × 6.1 m), with no walls.
  • Net: 31 inches (about 79 cm) high in the middle.
  • Paddle: solid with no strings; it may be perforated.
  • Ball: a tennis ball with reduced pressure.
  • Serve: one underarm serve per point.

It's basically a scaled-down tennis game. Once the ball goes past you, it's gone, because there's nothing to bounce off.

What is POP tennis?

POP tennis is the modern name for paddle tennis. In 2015 and 2016 the United States Paddle Tennis Association rebranded the sport as POP tennis and renamed itself the International POP Tennis Association.

According to the association, POP tennis is played with a solid, perforated paddle shorter than a tennis racket and a low-compression ball, at 50% to 75% of the compression of a normal tennis ball, for lower bounces and longer rallies. You get one underarm serve per point, served diagonally, and games use tennis scoring with advantage. Courts can be on almost any surface, from hard courts to sand. Like paddle tennis, there are no walls in play.

What is platform tennis?

Platform tennis is the sport most like padel among the "paddle" games, because the fence is in play. It was developed in 1928 at Fox Meadow Tennis Club in Scarsdale, New York, by James Cogswell and Fessenden Blanchard, who wanted a way to play outdoors in winter.

Under the rules of the American Platform Tennis Association (APTA):

  • Court: 44 × 20 feet (about 13.4 × 6.1 m), on a raised deck with a playing area of 60 × 30 feet (about 18.3 × 9.1 m).
  • Screens: a 12-foot (about 3.7 m) wire-mesh screen surrounds the deck, with 8 feet between each baseline and the back screen.
  • Serve: only one serve is allowed in doubles. A fault loses the point.
  • Ball and paddle: a rubber ball with flocking, and a paddle up to 18 inches long and 12 inches wide.
  • Scoring: tennis-style games and sets, with a tie-break at 6–all.

Because the court is a raised deck, the space underneath can hold heaters, so with lights the courts stay playable even in cold winter weather. Platform tennis was designed as a winter game, and that's still its great selling point.

Platform tennis vs padel

Both let you play the ball after it has bounced and come off the surround, but the details differ in ways that matter.

Padel

Solid glass walls plus mesh. After the bounce you can play the ball off any wall, and you may hit it into your own glass so it rebounds over the net, as long as it then bounces on the opponents' side. Two serves.

Platform tennis

Taut wire screens. After the bounce you can play the ball off the screen, but your return must go into the opponents' court: under the APTA rules you can't hit it into the screen so that it rebounds over. One serve in doubles.

The court and equipment are different too. A padel court is 20 × 10 metres with a net 88 cm high in the middle; a platform tennis court is smaller, about 13.4 × 6.1 metres inside a larger deck. Padel uses a pressurised ball that looks like a tennis ball; platform tennis uses a spongy rubber ball.

And pickleball?

Pickleball is the other sport people confuse with padel, because you play it with a "paddle". It's played on an open court with no walls, using a light plastic ball with holes, and it has a non-volley zone next to the net called the kitchen. Our full guide to padel vs pickleball compares the two in detail.

One quick way to keep them apart: in pickleball, paddle tennis and platform tennis you hold a paddle. In padel you hold a racket, even though it has no strings.

Which one should you try?

If you want the glass-court doubles game that is growing fast around the world, that's padel. If your club has a heated platform court, platform tennis is a great winter game. If you want a simple game on an open court, try POP tennis or pickleball.

Frequently asked questions

Is it padel or paddle?

The glass-court sport is padel, the spelling used by the International Padel Federation. In Spanish it's pádel. "Paddle" usually refers to paddle tennis or platform tennis.

Is paddle tennis the same as padel?

No. Paddle tennis is an American game from 1898, played on an open 50 × 20 foot court with no walls. Padel is played on an enclosed 20 × 10 metre court where the walls are in play.

What is the difference between platform tennis and padel?

Platform tennis is played on a smaller raised deck surrounded by wire screens, with one serve in doubles and a rubber ball. Padel uses glass walls, two serves and a pressurised ball, and you can play the ball into your own glass.

Is POP tennis the same as paddle tennis?

Yes. POP tennis is the name the United States Paddle Tennis Association gave the sport in 2015 and 2016, before renaming itself the International POP Tennis Association.

Why is padel called padel?

The Spanish word pádel was adapted from the English "paddle tennis", according to the Real Academia Española dictionary. The sport kept the Spanish spelling as it spread internationally.
