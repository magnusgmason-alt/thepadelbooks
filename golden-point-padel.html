Golden point in padel: how it works and who chooses the side

7 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 30 Oct 2026

The golden point in padel is a single deciding point played at 40–40: there's no advantage, so whoever wins that one point wins the game, and the receiving pair chooses which of them receives it. It's one of three scoring options in the official FIP rules, alongside classic advantage and the Star Point that the professional tour has used since 2026. Here is how it works, where it's used, and how to play it well.

What is golden point in padel?

Padel uses tennis scoring: 15, 30, 40, game. The only question is what happens when both pairs reach 40. Under classic advantage scoring you need two points in a row to win the game, so a game can go back and forth for a long time. The golden point removes that: at 40–40, the next point wins the game.

Rule 1 of the FIP Rules of Padel lists three ways to score a game: advantage, the Star Point and the golden point. The rule doesn't say who picks; in practice the league or tournament sets it in its own regulations, and in a friendly game it's up to the four of you, so agree before the first serve.

Golden point padel rules, step by step

  1. The score reaches 40–40. Someone calls "golden point".
  2. The receiving pair chooses. They decide whether the serve will come to the player on the right or the player on the left.
  3. The server serves to that player. The server serves diagonally into the chosen box, with the usual serve rules and two attempts.
  4. One rally decides the game. Win it and the game is yours. Lose it and it goes to the other pair.

Who chooses the side on golden point?

The receiving pair. They choose whether to receive from the right or the left, which in practice means choosing which of the two partners returns the golden point. The Padel School puts it simply: the returning pair decides who receives the serve on golden point.

Two limits apply under Rule 1:

  • No swapping positions. The FIP rules say the receiving pair can't change positions to receive the deciding point. Each player stays on the side they've been playing. The choice is which box receives, not a chance to move your best returner to the other side.
  • Mixed matches. In mixed doubles, the player who receives the deciding point must be the same sex as the server, so there's no choice to make.

Whatever you decide, make the choice before the server is ready and announce it clearly, so nobody argues about it afterwards.

Make the choice count

The serving pair starts the point and usually follows the serve to the net, so the choice of side is the receivers' one chance to shape the golden point before it starts. Use it deliberately rather than out of habit.

The golden point keeps matches shorter and more predictable in length, and it makes every 40–40 dramatic. The downside is that one rally can decide a game you have mostly controlled. Advantage gives the better pair more chances to prove it, but games can drag on. For the full scoring system, including tie-breaks, see padel scoring.

The Star Point: golden point's 2026 replacement on the pro tour

For the 2026 season, professional padel adopted a middle ground called the Star Point. According to the FIP, it works like this:

  1. First deuce: play an advantage point as normal. Win the next point after advantage and you win the game; lose it and it's 40–40 again.
  2. Second deuce: play a second advantage, in the same way.
  3. Third deuce: the next point is the Star Point. Whoever wins it wins the game, exactly like a golden point.

The key dates, from the FIP and Premier Padel:

  • 28 November 2025: the FIP General Assembly approved the Star Point unanimously as an option in Rule 1.
  • 5–11 January 2026: first used at the FIP Bronze Melbourne, the first stop of the CUPRA FIP Tour.
  • February 2026: Premier Padel debut at the Riyadh Season P1, the first event of the 2026 season.

The FIP says it applies from 2026 in Premier Padel, the CUPRA FIP Tour, FIP Promises and the amateur FIP Beyond events. When Premier Padel and the FIP announced their changes for 2027 in June 2026, those covered rankings and draws, not scoring, so as of October 2026 the Star Point remains the pro format. Check your league or tournament rules: the Star Point is an option, not compulsory, and many club events still play golden point.

A short history of the golden point

The golden point became famous when the World Padel Tour adopted it from its 2020 season, the first major professional circuit to do so, according to Padel Addict. When Premier Padel launched in 2022, it chose classic advantage scoring after consulting the players. The FIP's sporting director, Guillermo Alcaide, who wasn't involved at the time, later said he understood the vote was close, perhaps around 55–45.

The debate never went away. Fans liked the drama of the golden point; many players preferred advantage, which gives the stronger pair more chances. The Star Point is the compromise: advantage first, then a golden-point-style decider.

How often does it decide a game? A 2023 study by Escudero-Tena and colleagues analysed 2,752 points from 69 quarter-final, semi-final and final matches at six World Padel Tour events in 2021. It recorded 205 golden points in men's matches and 189 in women's, about one point in seven in both cases.

Golden point tactics

When you're receiving

  • Choose your best returner. Pick the partner who is returning best on the day, not automatically the "better" player. If the server has been hurting one side all match, take that into account.
  • Think about the server's pattern. If one of you struggles with the serve into the side glass, receive with the other.
  • Make the return. Don't go for a winner. A deep, low return or a lob that gets the serving pair off the net turns the point into a rally.

When you're serving

  • Get the first serve in. A double fault on golden point hands over the game for nothing. Take a little pace off if you need to.
  • Placement over power. LTA Padel recommends varying speed and direction rather than going for big serves, and says a serve into the side glass is effective at all levels.
  • Don't hit it too hard. The Padel School points out that a harder serve comes off the glass faster and can give the receiver an easier ball. Aim for a pace that makes them wait for the glass while you stay accurate.
  • Follow it to the net. Serve and move forward with your partner. More on that in our serve and return guide.

For both pairs

Keep the ball in play and stay patient. On a single deciding point, the pair that makes fewer unforced errors usually wins it. Talk before the point: which side receives, and what the plan is on the return.

Should you play golden point in your social games?

If your court time is limited, yes. Golden point stops one game from eating fifteen minutes, so you finish more sets in your booking. If you have plenty of time and want the most "fair" result, play advantage. If you want to play like the pros, play the Star Point. Whatever you choose, agree it before you start, and use the same rule for the whole match.

Golden point is also common in quick social formats such as king of the court. See padel tournament formats for how clubs use it.

Frequently asked questions

What is golden point in padel?

A single deciding point played at 40–40 instead of advantage. Whoever wins it wins the game.

Who chooses the side on golden point?

The receiving pair. They choose whether to receive from the right or the left, so they decide which partner returns. They can't swap positions to do it, and in mixed matches the receiver must be the same sex as the server.

Is golden point still used in professional padel?

Not as it stands. In 2026 Premier Padel and the FIP tours use the Star Point: two advantages, then a single deciding point at the third deuce. Many leagues and clubs still play golden point.

What is the difference between golden point and Star Point?

Golden point decides the game at the first 40–40. The Star Point allows two advantage attempts first and only plays a deciding point at the third deuce.

Do you get two serves on golden point?

Yes. The normal serve rules apply, including a second serve after a fault. See padel service faults.
