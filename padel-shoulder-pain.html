Padel shoulder pain: a 10-minute routine and the technique fixes

6 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 8 Nov 2026

Padel shoulder pain is mostly an overhead problem: every bandeja, víbora and smash loads the rotator cuff, and a match against good lobbers can mean dozens of them. The best-supported fix is a short, regular shoulder programme, plus technique that lets your legs and trunk share the work. Below is a 10-minute band routine with sets and reps, the technique changes that matter, and a step-by-step plan for getting back to overheads.

Not medical advice

The routine below is for healthy shoulders and for players with mild, settling niggles. If your shoulder already hurts a lot, or pain started after a fall, see a physiotherapist or doctor first.

Why overheads load the shoulder

The Aspetar review of padel injuries describes shoulder problems in padel as mainly overuse injuries from repeated overhead movements such as the smash and bandeja, with the supraspinatus, one of the four rotator cuff muscles, the most commonly affected. Across the studies in the 2023 systematic review of padel injuries, the shoulder accounted for roughly 7% to 34% of injuries.

An NHS physiotherapy leaflet from Leicestershire Partnership NHS Trust explains the mechanism simply: rotator cuff-related pain usually appears when the load on the shoulder rises beyond what the rotator cuff can currently handle, either suddenly or gradually. In padel that's the week you play three Americanos, the match where you get lobbed for an hour, or the new habit of smashing every ball.

Shoulder pain after padel: what it usually feels like

Rotator cuff-related pain is felt in the shoulder and upper arm and can be painful when lifting the arm away from the body, reaching behind your back, lifting objects or lying on that side. Pain that's worse while you use the arm is typical of tendon and bursa problems. Pain that started suddenly and badly, or after a fall, is a different situation; see the red flags at the end.

The evidence for a shoulder routine

There's no padel-specific trial yet, but there is a good one in another overhead sport. In a randomised trial of 660 elite handball players (Andersson and colleagues, British Journal of Sports Medicine, 2017), a short shoulder programme done three times a week as part of the warm-up lowered the risk of shoulder problems by 28%. It targeted internal rotation range, external rotation strength, shoulder-blade strength, thoracic mobility and the kinetic chain. The routine below follows the same building blocks.

The 10-minute padel shoulder routine

Do it three times a week, ideally before you play. You need a light resistance band and a fence or post to anchor it.

MinutesExerciseSets × repsWhy
0–1Open book: lie on your side, knees bent, rotate the top arm and chest open1 × 6 each sideThoracic rotation, so the shoulder doesn't do all the turning
1–3Band external rotation, elbow tucked at your side2 × 12 each armRotator cuff strength
3–4Cross-body stretch: pull the arm gently across your chest2 × 20 s each armBack-of-shoulder flexibility for internal rotation
4–6Band pull-apart, arms straight in front2 × 15Shoulder-blade muscles
6–7Wall slide: forearms on a wall, slide up into a Y2 × 10Shoulder-blade control overhead
7–8Band external rotation at 90°: elbow at shoulder height, rotate the forearm up2 × 10 each armRotator cuff in the overhead position
8–9Split-stance band row with a trunk turn2 × 10 each sideLinking legs, trunk and arm
9–10Shadow bandejas, then smashes, at half pace10 of eachRehearses the movement before you load it

Keep the band light enough to finish every set with clean form. Add resistance only when the last reps feel easy. For a full strength programme beyond these ten minutes, see our padel exercises guide, and fold the routine into your padel warm-up.

Technique fixes that take load off the shoulder

Two mainstream coaching sources agree on the basics of a shoulder-friendly overhead. LTA Padel's advice for the bandeja is to turn your body to face the side of the court, hit when the ball has dropped to about eye height and swing across your body rather than straight down, taking speed off the ball. The Padel School's version is to turn early, move your feet into position and make contact in front.

  • Move your feet first. Hitting from behind your head because you didn't get back far enough forces the shoulder into an awkward, overloaded position.
  • Turn side-on early. The trunk turn supplies much of the power, so the arm doesn't have to.
  • Not every lob needs a smash. A controlled bandeja keeps your net position with far less effort. Deep lobs can be left to come off the glass; see playing off the back glass.
  • Ration your smashes. Save full-power smashes for balls you can genuinely finish.

If you already have padel shoulder pain

nidirect, the NHS information service for Northern Ireland, advises keeping the shoulder gently moving rather than stopping using it completely, avoiding activities that make the pain worse, and using pain relief and heat or cold packs if needed. It says mild shoulder pain can take four to six weeks to recover fully. Rotator cuff-related pain can take longer: the Leicestershire NHS leaflet says it can take a minimum of 12 weeks to improve, with further improvement up to 24 weeks, and that you may see no change in the first six weeks.

The same leaflet says exercise is the best treatment, and that some pain during exercise is acceptable as long as it settles shortly afterwards. If pain is still raised a few hours later, reduce the intensity.

How to return to overheads

Agree the details with a physiotherapist if you're seeing one. A sensible order, moving to the next step only when the shoulder is no worse the next morning:

  1. Daily life is comfortable. You can reach overhead and sleep without the shoulder waking you, and the band routine feels fine.
  2. Groundstrokes and volleys only. Play rallies from the back and at the net, and let your partner take the overheads, or let lobs bounce.
  3. Soft bandejas from feeds. Ten to twenty controlled bandejas at half pace, from easy lobs.
  4. Full bandejas and víboras. Build up the number and the pace over a few sessions.
  5. Serves at match pace. The padel serve is hit at or below waist height, so it asks less of the shoulder, but build the volume gradually.
  6. Smashes last. Start at three-quarters pace and keep the count low for the first matches.

When shoulder pain needs a professional

See a GP if the pain hasn't started to improve after two weeks, if it's very hard to move your arm, if the pain began after a fall or injury, or if it persists after physiotherapy ends. nidirect says to go to the nearest emergency department if:

  • the pain is sudden or very bad, or you can't move your arm
  • your arm or shoulder has changed shape or is badly swollen
  • you have pins and needles that don't go away, or no feeling in the arm or shoulder
  • your arm or shoulder is hot or cold to touch

Frequently asked questions

Why does my shoulder hurt after padel?

Usually from repeated overheads that load the rotator cuff beyond what it's used to, often after a jump in playing time or a match full of lobs. Arm-only technique adds to the load.

Can I play padel with rotator cuff pain?

Mild, settling pain may allow play without overheads while you build strength. If pain builds during play or lasts into the next day, reduce your load and get advice.

How often should I do shoulder exercises for padel?

Three times a week is a good target. That's how often the handball trial that cut shoulder problems by 28% ran its programme, as part of the warm-up.

Is the bandeja easier on the shoulder than the smash?

Generally yes. It's hit with less pace, at about eye height and with the swing travelling across the body, so it asks less of the shoulder than a full-power smash.

How long does padel shoulder pain take to heal?

Mild shoulder pain can take four to six weeks. Rotator cuff-related pain often takes 12 weeks or more with regular exercise, according to NHS physiotherapy guidance.
