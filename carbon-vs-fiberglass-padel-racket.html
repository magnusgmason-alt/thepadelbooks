Carbon vs fiberglass padel racket: which suits your game?

6 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 8 Nov 2026

In the carbon vs fiberglass padel racket question, fibreglass faces are softer, more elastic and more forgiving, while carbon faces are stiffer, more precise and generally more durable. That's why most first rackets use fibreglass and most advanced rackets use carbon. But the face is only one layer: the core, shape and balance often matter more, and the 3K, 12K and 18K labels don't mean what many shoppers assume.

A note on spelling: British retailers write fibreglass, American ones fiberglass. It's the same material.

The feel, power, forgiveness and durability rows follow Justpadel's materials guide, which sums it up as carbon for advanced players and fibreglass for beginners and recreational players. The Padel School describes the faces the same way: fibreglass softer and more forgiving, carbon stiffer and more precise.

What fibreglass feels like

A fibreglass face flexes a little more at impact. The ball stays on the face fractionally longer and comes off with an easy push, even when your swing is short or late. That's exactly what most players need in their first year, when contact is inconsistent and the ball often arrives from an awkward angle off the glass. The trade-off is that when you do swing hard, a fibreglass face doesn't add as much punch to smashes and attacking volleys.

What carbon feels like

Carbon is stiffer. It gives clearer feedback, a more precise response and more speed when you accelerate through the ball. It also tends to last longer under heavy use. But a stiff face is less forgiving: a mishit feels harsher, and more vibration reaches the hand when you miss the sweet spot. Carbon rewards clean technique rather than creating it.

What do 3K, 12K and 18K carbon mean?

The K number is about the carbon thread, not the racket's quality. In composites, the K is the number of thousands of individual carbon filaments bundled into each tow: 3K is about 3,000 filaments, 12K about 12,000 and 18K about 18,000, as Easy Composites explains. Padel brands weave those threads into the sheet that forms the face.

What that does to feel is where sources part company:

  • Justpadel describes 3K as the softest and most comfortable and 18K as the stiffest and most responsive.
  • PadelPROShop describes 3K as drier and more direct with clear feedback, 12K as the tolerant middle and 18K as firmer and more demanding, with more vibration outside the sweet spot.
  • Padel Browser calls the idea that a higher K means a stiffer, more powerful racket "mostly wrong". It describes 3K as firm and precise and 18K as more elastic, and points out that the K only describes the top sheet: the number of layers, the resin and the backing matter more.

The point they all agree on: K is not "better or worse" on its own. Don't pay more just for a higher number. A flashy 18K face over a single thin layer can play softer than a plain 3K face backed by several layers of carbon and fibreglass.

Hybrid faces: carbon and fibreglass together

Many rackets mix the two materials, for example a carbon frame with a fibreglass face, or carbon and fibreglass layers within the face. Justpadel notes faces are made of carbon, fibreglass or a combination of the two, and Padel Browser mentions fibreglass as a backing layer behind carbon in some rackets. In practice, hybrids sit in the middle: a bit more stability than pure fibreglass, a bit more comfort than full carbon. They suit improvers who are outgrowing a soft first racket.

Which is better for beginners?

Fibreglass, or a soft hybrid. The Padel School advises beginners and early intermediates to prioritise forgiveness, control and comfort: a round shape, a soft core and more forgiving materials. Our guide to choosing a padel racket and our round vs diamond comparison cover the shape and balance side.

Which is better for elbow pain?

Face material is only part of the answer. Justpadel ties arm comfort mainly to a soft core, and Padel Browser recommends prioritising a soft core and round shape over the carbon weave if your arm is sensitive, and avoiding stiff, head-heavy frames. PadelPROShop adds that more rigidity tends to send more vibration to the arm. A soft core with a fibreglass or soft hybrid face is the comfortable combination. If you already have pain, equipment changes are one piece; see our padel elbow guide and a professional if it persists.

Durability: does carbon last longer?

Generally, yes. Justpadel rates carbon as more durable than fibreglass. But the core usually wears out before the face: rubber compresses with every hit, and that's what makes a racket feel dead. Our guide to how long a padel racket lasts explains the signs.

Price differences

Material is one of the main things you pay for. Padel Browser's check of 361 in-stock rackets at three US retailers in September 2026 found:

  • Entry ($60 to $120): fibreglass faces, soft EVA cores, usually round.
  • First real racket ($120 to $200): carbon appears in the face or frame; fibreglass faces are still common.
  • Enthusiast ($200 to $300): 12K or 18K carbon frames and prior-season flagships, plus some fibreglass models.
  • Pro-level ($300 to $450+): carbon faces, stiffer frames and more customisation.

Prices vary by country, retailer and season. Our padel racket price guide has UK ranges too.

Choose fibreglass if

you're in your first year or two, you play once or twice a week, you still mishit regularly, or you've had arm or elbow trouble.

Choose carbon if

you hit the ball cleanly most of the time, you want more precision and punch on volleys and overheads, and you play often enough to value durability.

Frequently asked questions

Is carbon or fiberglass better for a padel racket?

Neither is better for everyone. Fibreglass is softer and more forgiving, which suits beginners and recreational players. Carbon is stiffer, more precise and more durable, which suits players with consistent technique.

What does 3K, 12K or 18K mean on a padel racket?

It's the number of thousands of carbon filaments in each thread of the weave: roughly 3,000, 12,000 or 18,000. It describes the top sheet only, and sources disagree on how it changes feel, so don't treat a higher number as automatically better.

Is fiberglass better for tennis elbow?

A soft fibreglass face can help, but the core matters more. A soft core, round shape and low balance are the usual recommendations for sensitive arms. If you have pain, see a professional as well as changing equipment.

Do carbon padel rackets last longer?

Carbon faces are generally more durable than fibreglass, but the rubber core wearing out is usually what ends a racket's life, whatever the face is made of.

Are carbon padel rackets more expensive?

Usually. In US retailer data from 2026, entry rackets under about $120 mostly had fibreglass faces, carbon appeared from around $120 to $200, and full carbon faces were typical at $300 and above. Prices vary by country and season.
