Padel court cost: what it really takes to build one

8 min readBy The Padel AcademyUpdated 30 Oct 2026

A padel court cost in the UK runs from roughly £45,000 to £80,000 for a single court, and the LTA's own 2025 budget for one floodlit outdoor court, groundworks included, is about £71,000 before VAT and fees. In the US, a 2026 industry estimate puts a new court at about $39,000 to $100,000. Where you land in those ranges depends on the ground, the type of court, lighting, and whether you put a roof over it. This guide breaks down every line, with sources, so you can budget properly before you ask for quotes.

Padel court cost at a glance

Prices vary by country, site and supplier, and they change quickly as the sport grows. Treat these 2025 and 2026 figures as a starting point for your budget, not a quote.

That last range looks wide because it is. LTA Padel says the figure depends on the number of courts, lighting, drainage and site preparation. The rest of this guide explains what pushes a project towards one end or the other.

How much does a padel court cost to build? The line items

The LTA's 2025 guidance note splits a one-court outdoor project into two roughly equal halves:

  • The court and lights: about £36,000. This is the steel frame, glass, mesh, artificial turf, net and floodlights.
  • The ground: about £35,000 for the ring beam, court base, ducting and drainage.

In other words, the hidden work under the court can cost as much as the court you see. MyBuilder's UK price guide breaks it down differently but tells the same story:

ItemTypical UK cost (MyBuilder)
Court materials£20,000 to £30,000
Installation labour£15,000 to £25,000
Groundworks and foundation preparation£5,000 to £10,000
Lighting£3,000 to £6,000
Fencing and surrounds£2,000 to £4,000

For comparison, a Spanish supplier, Stock Padel, gave these component ranges in 2024: €5,000 to €10,000 for ground preparation, €10,000 to €20,000 for the structure and enclosure, €8,000 to €15,000 for the artificial turf, €2,000 to €5,000 for lighting, €500 to €1,000 for the net and posts, and €1,000 to €3,000 for licences and permits. Spain has a mature padel industry, which is one reason its figures sit lower than UK and US quotes.

Padel court construction cost: what drives the price

Five things move a quote more than anything else.

  1. The ground. A flat, well-drained site with an existing hard base is cheap to build on. A sloping field that needs digging out, a new concrete slab and drainage is not. Sports Venue Calculator says ground and foundation works can be anywhere from nothing to around 40% of an outdoor project.
  2. Court type. Standard courts cost less than panoramic ones (more below).
  3. Lighting. Floodlights add thousands to the build, but without them an outdoor court is unusable on winter evenings, which is when many people want to play.
  4. Cover. A canopy or building can more than double the cost of a single court.
  5. Number of courts. Building several at once spreads the fixed costs. In the LTA's table, one outdoor court costs about £71,000, while four cost about £268,000, or roughly £67,000 each.

Panoramic vs standard padel courts

On a standard court, the glass panels are held in place by vertical metal posts. On a panoramic court, most of those posts are removed and the glass is joined with special connectors, so players and spectators get a clear view through the walls. Super-panoramic designs extend the effect along part of the side walls, leaving posts only around the doors.

Panoramic courts cost more because of the sports-grade glass and more careful assembly. Suppliers rarely publish the difference, so ask for both options in the same quote. For a private or club court, a standard court plays exactly the same under the rules. A panoramic court mainly makes sense where people will watch, such as a show court or a club that hosts events.

Indoor vs outdoor padel court cost

An outdoor court is the cheapest way in. Covering it changes the budget completely:

  • Indoor courts: MyBuilder and Padel Market both quote £60,000 to £80,000 for the court, and that's before the cost of the building around it.
  • Canopy: the LTA budgets about £95,000 for a canopy over one court, including its foundations and drainage and a 3 m rain screen. A canopy over two courts comes to about £170,000.
  • Height: the LTA's guidance asks for at least 6 m clear at the ends of the court and 8 m above the net line under a front-to-back canopy. A low barn or warehouse may not be usable at all.

What you get for the extra money is year-round play, no rain cancellations and, according to Sports Venue Calculator, lower long-term maintenance than an outdoor court.

Space needed for a padel court

The playing area is 20 m by 10 m, as set out in the FIP rules. Our guide to padel court dimensions covers every line, wall and net measurement. The footprint you need is a little bigger:

  • One court: the LTA gives a minimum external area of 20.80 m by 10.80 m.
  • Two courts: 20.80 m by 22.3 m, allowing a 1.5 m gap between them.
  • Access: for wheelchair access, the LTA says the space between the net post and the enclosure must be at least 1.2 m on each side.
  • Height: floodlights need at least 6 m of clear space above the playing area.

If you want the option of playing the ball outside the court, the court needs extra safety space around it. See out-of-court play for why. Most clubs and home courts don't build for this.

Backyard and home padel court cost

A home padel court costs about the same as any other single court, because the structure, glass and turf are identical. The variables are your garden and your neighbours.

  • Ground: lawns usually need digging out, a new base and drainage. That's the groundworks line, which runs from MyBuilder's £5,000 to £10,000 up to the LTA's £35,000 for base, drainage and ducting, depending on the site.
  • Planning: the LTA's guidance says building a padel court will require formal planning consent from the local authority, and that contractors usually leave this cost out of their quotes. Indoor structures and canopies also need planning approval. Rules differ by country and council, so check locally before you pay a deposit.
  • Noise and light: the LTA says that if a home is within 50 m of the court, specialist noise and light surveys are likely to be needed. Padel is loud, and talking to neighbours early is part of the LTA's advice.

Running costs after you build

The build is not the last bill. Budget for these from day one:

  • Maintenance: MyBuilder puts routine maintenance at £400 to £500 a year. The LTA's weekly checklist includes brushing the turf so the sand infill stays even, checking drainage, inspecting the glass for cracks or chips, and checking the net and posts.
  • Bolts and fixings: inspect and tighten every 6 to 12 months, per the LTA. Skipping the schedule can invalidate court and canopy warranties.
  • Resurfacing: the LTA suggests resurfacing about every five years, depending on use.
  • Sinking fund: the LTA recommends setting aside £1,500 per court per year to cover the surface, lamps and lighting columns.
  • Electricity: LED floodlights cost roughly £1 per court per hour to run, according to the LTA.

If you're weighing up building versus paying to play, our guide to how much padel costs covers court hire prices in the UK, US and Spain.

How to get an accurate quote

  1. Measure the site and note slope, drainage and access for delivery lorries.
  2. Decide the spec: standard or panoramic, lights or not, open or covered.
  3. Ask at least three builders to quote on the same spec, with groundworks priced separately.
  4. Check what's excluded: planning, surveys, VAT and electrical connection are the usual gaps.
  5. Ask about maintenance and warranty terms before you sign.

For technical standards, LTA Padel points venues to the SAPCA Padel Code of Practice and its own construction guidance note, which also asks for floodlights to be designed and installed to the SAPCA code. Once the court is built, start with our guide to what padel is for new players.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to build a padel court?

In the UK, a single outdoor court typically costs £45,000 to £80,000, and the LTA's 2025 budget for one floodlit outdoor court with groundworks is about £71,000 before VAT and fees. A 2026 US estimate is about $39,000 to $100,000. Prices vary by country, site and date.

How much space do you need for a padel court?

The playing area is 20 m by 10 m. The LTA gives a minimum external area of 20.80 m by 10.80 m for one court, plus at least 6 m of clear height for floodlights.

Do you need planning permission for a padel court?

In the UK, the LTA's guidance says padel courts require formal planning consent from the local authority, and canopies and indoor structures need approval too. Check with your own council before committing.

Is a panoramic padel court worth it?

It plays the same as a standard court but gives spectators a much better view, and it costs more. It's worth it for show courts and event venues; most private courts don't need it.

How long does a padel court surface last?

The LTA suggests resurfacing about every five years, though heavy use wears turf faster. Regular brushing and keeping the infill level help it last.

What does a padel court cost to run each year?

Plan for routine maintenance of around £400 to £500 a year, plus the LTA's recommended £1,500 per court per year sinking fund for surface and lighting replacement, and about £1 per hour for LED lights.
